###### Abstract.

Multi-agent large language model (LLM) systems can exhibit emergent
behaviors through interaction, yet whether a similar phenomenon exists
for _reasoning alignment_ remains largely unexplored. We study whether
agent interaction produces additional reference-grounded reasoning
alignment beyond matched individual self-refinement, and introduce REMAP
(Reference–Entailment–Matrix for Agentic Process), a framework that
quantifies this effect through Semantic Coverage and
interaction-specific alignment gains. Across diverse model families and
two multi-hop reasoning benchmarks, we observe broadly positive
interaction gains, particularly on reasoning content that depends on
integrating evidence distributed across agents. These gains are
consistently associated with independently measured peer-information
recovery, while further analysis reveals composition-dependent
heterogeneity effects and asymmetric information transfer across agent
capability levels. Motivated by these findings, we further introduce a
fact-level multi-agent communication framework that replaces full
reasoning-trace exchange with compact, gap-aware, and selectively routed
information. Our experiments show that compact fact exchange improves
reasoning alignment and task performance while reducing communication
overhead, whereas more selective communication exposes a trade-off
between acquiring distributed information, retaining local content, and
communication efficiency. Overall, our results show that collective
emergent alignment can be quantified, linked to information-transfer
patterns, and used to guide more effective multi-agent communication.

## 1. Introduction

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly work in multi-agent
systems (MAS) wu2023autogen; hong2024metagpt, where they exchange
information, discuss candidate solutions, and revise their reasoning
li2023camel; qian2024chatdev. A central motivation is _emergent
collective behavior_: interaction can help a group combine partial
information into outcomes that isolated agents may not reach
chen2023agentverse. Previous work has observed emergent coordination in
generative-agent simulations park2023generative and improved reasoning
and factual accuracy through multi-agent debate du2024debate;
liang2024debate. These findings motivate a closer look at how the
interaction changes reasoning and whether the agentic process
contributes to emergent behavior in reasoning alignment.

Reasoning alignment and final-answer quality capture complementary
aspects of collaboration smit2024mad; wang2026consistency;
turpin2023unfaithful. Gains may also come from voting li2024more,
repeated sampling wang2023selfconsistency, or individual refinement
madaan2023selfrefine; shinn2023reflexion. Existing reasoning-alignment
metrics mainly evaluate individual traces golovneva2023roscoe;
prasad2023receval; xia2024reasoneval, while MAS evaluation commonly
focuses on task accuracy, consensus, or communication efficiency
zhu2025multiagentbench; li2024sparse. A quantitative gap remains: how
can we determine whether interaction adds reference-aligned reasoning
beyond what the same agents obtain through independent self-refinement?
We define this additional, reference-grounded gain in reasoning
alignment as collective emergent alignment.

As shown in Figure
[1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Collective Emergent Alignment: Measuring and Amplifying Interaction-Induced Reasoning Alignment in Multi-Agent LLMs"),
we introduce REMAP, a Reference Entailment Matrix framework for Agentic
Process to quantify this effect. REMAP constructs a bidirectional
natural language inference (NLI) matrix between reference units and the
reasoning units generated by each agent. Each reference unit contributes
its best generated match, yielding a _Semantic Coverage_ score $`C`$.
The matched initial-to-final coverage changes give the
interaction-specific contrast:

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
E_{\Delta}=G_{\mathrm{MAS}}-G_{\mathrm{SELF}},\qquad G_{X}=C_{\mathrm{final}}^{X}-C_{\mathrm{init}}^{X}.
``` |  |

A positive $`E_{\Delta}`$ means that MAS gains more reference coverage,
or loses less, than matched self-refinement. An ownership-aware variant,
$`E_{\Delta}^{\mathrm{non}}`$, evaluates only reference units supported
by evidence initially assigned to peers. This separates changes on
peer-supported information from changes on an agent’s local content. We
validate the measurements against answer quality and deterministic
gold-item recovery computed independently of the NLI scorer.

REMAP then connects measurement to the design of communication. We
examine peer-information recovery, heterogeneous composition, and
owner–receiver directionality. The analyses show associations between
collective gains and recovered peer information, together with
composition-dependent gains and exploratory directional differences.
These observations motivate a fact-level MAS framework that organizes
communication around the information available to each receiver.

We organize the study around three research questions:

- •
  RQ1: How can we quantify whether reference-grounded reasoning
  alignment emerges from multi-agent interaction beyond matched
  self-refinement?
- •
  RQ2: What information-transfer patterns are associated with collective
  emergent alignment, particularly peer information recovery,
  heterogeneous/homogeneous composition, and owner–receiver
  directionality?
- •
  RQ3: Can these findings guide more effective fact-level communication
  that improves reasoning alignment and task performance while reducing
  communication overhead?

Our contributions are threefold: (1) We introduce REMAP, a
reference-entailment-matrix framework for measuring reasoning alignment
throughout agent trajectories. (2) We empirically characterize
collective emergent alignment across seven model families and two
multi-hop reasoning benchmarks. (3) We introduce and evaluate a
fact-level communication framework guided by these findings, connecting
peer-information acquisition, task performance, and communication
efficiency.

## 2. Related Work

Evaluating Reasoning Alignment. The Structured Alignment Score
wang2026cotlens compares generated and reference reasoning through
semantic-relation matrices. ROSCOE golovneva2023roscoe includes
reference-based and reference-free diagnostics. ReCEval
prasad2023receval evaluates step correctness and informativeness using
claims, prior steps, and context. REVEAL jacovi2024reveal benchmarks
relevance, evidence attribution, and logical correctness. The
NLI-hypergraph audit of Kohli et al. kohli2026referencefree checks how
support relations compose within a trace. REMAP builds on
reference-based evaluation and applies it separately to each agent’s
states along matched self-refinement and MAS trajectories.

Evaluating Multi-Agent Reasoning. MultiAgentBench zhu2025multiagentbench
evaluates milestone progress and coordination. Who&When zhang2025whowhen
locates failure-responsible agents and steps, while MAST cemri2025mast
classifies system-design, inter-agent, and verification failures. CARA
assesses inter-agent consistency. Its CARA-GT variant measures
expert-reference alignment and coverage across debate rounds
wang2026consistency. Together, these studies support process-level
analysis zhu2026raffles, including reference-based MAS evaluation.
````
