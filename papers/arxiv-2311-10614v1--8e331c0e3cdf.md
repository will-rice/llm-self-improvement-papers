# A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest

###### Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs), despite their great power in language
generation, often encounter challenges when dealing with intricate and
knowledge-demanding queries in specific domains. This paper introduces a
novel approach to enhance LLMs by effectively extracting the relevant
knowledge from domain-specific textual sources, and the adaptive
training of a chatbot with domain-specific inquiries. Our two-step
approach starts from training a knowledge miner, namely LLMiner, which
autonomously extracts Question-Answer pairs from relevant documents
through a chain-of-thought reasoning process. Subsequently, we blend the
mined QA pairs with a conversational dataset to fine-tune the LLM as a
chatbot, thereby enriching its domain-specific expertise and
conversational capabilities. We also developed a new evaluation
benchmark which comprises four domain-specific text corpora and
associated human-crafted QA pairs for testing. Our model shows
remarkable performance improvement over generally aligned LLM and
surpasses domain-adapted models directly fine-tuned on domain corpus. In
particular, LLMiner achieves this with minimal human intervention,
requiring only 600 seed instances, thereby providing a pathway towards
self-improvement of LLMs through model-synthesized training data.

## 1 Introduction

![Refer to caption](2311.10614v1/illustrative.png)

Figure 1: Illustration of the LLMiner approach for enhancing Large
Language Models (LLMs) as domain-specific chatbots.

Large Language Models (LLMs) have significantly advanced the fields of
natural language understanding and generation. Despite these
advancements, training LLMs as dynamic AI agents capable of answering
complex, domain-specific, and knowledge-intensive questions remains a
significant challenge [Abbasian et al. (2023)](#bib.bib1); [Ge et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib8). Current methods to integrate knowledge into LLMs
primarily involve either modifying model weights with manually curated
knowledge [Hase et al. (2021)](#bib.bib9); [Meng et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib16); [Mitchell et al. (2022)](#bib.bib17); [Dai et al.
(2021)](#bib.bib4) or continuous pre-training with a language modeling
objective on domain-specific texts ([Jang et al., 2021](#bib.bib12);
[Jin et al., 2021](#bib.bib13); [Qin et al., 2022](#bib.bib20)).
However, these methods often fall short in addressing complex queries
that extend beyond simple fact retrieval ([Zhong et al.,
2023](#bib.bib28)), and they tend to rely on the mere memorization [Zhu
and Li (2023)](#bib.bib30); [Wang et al. (2021)](#bib.bib25) of factual
content, which proves insufficient for delivering insightful responses
to specialized questions.

This paper introduces a novel approach for empowering LLMs with
domain-specific expertise through an autonomous knowledge mining process
([fig. 1](#S1.F1 "In 1 Introduction ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest")).
We propose LLMiner, an LLM agent that autonomously extracts knowledge
directly from domain-specific texts and structures it in conversational
format. During inference, LLMiner first analyzes the importance of the
sentence within a given context, and then employs this analysis in a
chain-of-thought reasoning process to generate pertinent questions.
Subsequently, it structures answers by synthesizing information from
both the original text and the formulated questions. This procedure is
repeated for each sentence in the document, thereby creating a
multifaceted knowledge in instructional formats from the raw text.
Unlike previous work largely depends on the LLM’s inherent ability to
structure knowledge from raw text, LLMiner offloads this knowledge
structuring process to the training phase. We feed in multifaceted
knowledge as training examples to enable LLMs to develop a more
comprehensive understanding of domain-specific content. These alleviate
the problems including the model’s failure to apply memorized text data
to related queries [Zhu and Li (2023)](#bib.bib30); [Sun et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib22); [Wang et al. (2021)](#bib.bib25), and its tendency
to reiterate Wikipedia content without effectively responding to the
query [Sun et al. (2023)](#bib.bib21).

For domain-specific chatbot training, we utilize LLMiner generated data
as two forms of augmentations: instructional formats for fine-tuning
chatbots, and lists of answers that provide alternative perspectives to
the original text. These augmentations, combined with the OpenAssistant
Conversation Dataset (OASST), equip the chatbot with both conversational
ability and domain-specific knowledge. In our findings, we observe
that 1) The instructional data enhances the ability of extracting
factual knowledge from raw text. 2) the list of answers as augmentation
can enhance the understanding of document reflected in answering
factual-based queries.

A key feature of LLMiner is its minimal reliance on human intervention,
requiring only a small set of seed instances generated by prompting
GPT-4 to adapt an LLM to the knowledge mining process. This capability
allows LLM to be easily adapted as a tool to help any AI agent to
continuously evolve by integrating new domain knowledge. Our
contributions are highlighted in the following:

1.  1.  We develop an LLM-based system for automatic knowledge mining,
        harnessing the model’s inherent natural language processing
        capabilities to extract and organize domain-specific knowledge.
2.  2.  The data mined by LLMiner offers a multi-perspective view of
        document content, facilitating a deeper comprehension of the
        original content.
3.  3.  Our method demonstrates a pathway for LLM self-improvement using
        model-synthesized training data, enabling continuous adaptation and
        growth in line with the expanding landscape of domain knowledge.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Question Generation

Automatic question generation has been introduced to synthesize data for
tasks such as question answering ([Narayan et al., 2023](#bib.bib18);
[Lewis et al., 2021](#bib.bib15); [Honovich et al., 2022](#bib.bib10);
[Honovich et al., 2021](#bib.bib11); [Alberti et al., 2019](#bib.bib2)),
retrieval tasks ([Dai et al., 2022b](#bib.bib6)) or dialogue
systems ([Wang et al., 2023](#bib.bib26); [Dai et al.,
2022a](#bib.bib5)). However, previous works use documents or specific
spans within them as answer without re-writing. Furthermore, they
require labeled QA data to train question generation. Models such PAQ
requires training each model for each step in the generation pipeline,
while our methodology harnesses a single LLM throughout the generation
procedure.

### 2.2 Instruction-tuning

Recent endeavors to enhance LLMs have revolved around instruction tuning
in QA [Taori et al. (2023)](#bib.bib23); [Sun et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib21); [Zhou et al. (2023)](#bib.bib29) or conversation
style [Köpf et al. (2023)](#bib.bib14); [Chiang et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib3). These efforts predominantly aim at aligning models
with human preferences rather than bolstering knowledge acquisition,
which requires large amount of knowledge-intensive quality QA data. Our
paper presents such an effort in extracting high quality QA data from
raw text as supervised data to encode knowledge into LLMs.

### 2.3 Chain-of-thought Reasoning

Chain-of-thought reasoning methods are proposed to improve the
generation quality in various tasks [Wei et al. (2022)](#bib.bib27).
Techniques like ReciteLM [Sun et al. (2022)](#bib.bib22) have been
introduced, enabling LLMs to recite pertinent information during
question answering (QA). However, these methodologies are tethered to
the necessity of finely crafted in-context examples and exhibit
shortcomings in incorporating external knowledge efficiently ([Sun
et al., 2022](#bib.bib22)).

## 3 Methodology

In this section, we outline our approach for training a Large Language
Model (LLM) into a specialized knowledge miner, which we refer to as
LLMiner. This model is designed to autonomously mine relevant
Question-Answer (QA) pairs from specialized text corpora. These mined QA
pairs are subsequently utilized to fine-tune the LLM, enhancing its
proficiency in addressing domain-specific queries. The subsequent
subsections will delve into three pivotal aspects of our methodology: 1)
Preparing seed data using GPT-4 for training LLMiner, 2) Fine-tuning a
smaller LLM to align with the mining task, and 3) Employing LLMiner to
mine knowledge in instructional format.

### 3.1 Preparing Seed Data with GPT-4

Our aim in training LLMiner is to enable it for versatile knowledge
mining scenarios, capitalizing on the robust language understanding
abilities inherent in pre-trained LLMs. To this end, we compile a
diverse corpus that includes $`300`$ randomly sampled passages from
Wikipedia, along with $`50`$ each from TREC-COVID, NFCorpus, ArguAna,
FEVER, DBPedia, and SCIDOCS [Thakur et al. (2021)](#bib.bib24), making
for a total of 600 instances.

As illustrated in
[fig. 2](#S2.F2 "In 2.3 Chain-of-thought Reasoning ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest"),
for each passage, we start by randomly selecting a sentence to serve as
the focal point. We then employ three prompts to generate an analysis, a
question, and an answer, respectively. In each generation process, we
adhere to a set of guidelines, along with a specific formatting approach
as input for GPT-4 [OpenAI (2023)](#bib.bib19). The full list of prompts
is included in
[appendix A](#A1 "Appendix A GPT-4 Prompts for LLMiner Seed Data Generation ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").

##### Significance Analysis

We prompt GPT-4 to assess the significance of the selected sentence
within the broader context of the entire passage. The guidelines for
this analysis include factors such as clarity, importance, knowledge
addition, and relevance to the overall content. The analysis is a
chain-of-thought reasoning for organizing knowledge in the paragraph
with the picked sentence as a focal point, serving as the initial step
for question proposal. If the sentence is deemed unclear or
uninformative, GPT-4 is required to output the specific guideline that
has not been met, and we then bypass the subsequent steps for such
sentences. These exceptions are used as training data to refine the
model’s filtering capabilities.

##### Question Proposal

If the sentence is determined to be significant, GPT-4 will be
subsequently prompted to formulate questions aimed at encapsulating the
insights obtained from the analysis. The guidelines specify that
questions should be self-contained, answerable, and insightful. Given
that the final deliverable from LLMiner consists solely of QA pairs
without accompanying passage context, it is crucial that the questions
are formulated to be self-contained. To ensure this, our prompts direct
the model to avoid using context-dependent phrases such as "from the
document" or "in the report."

##### Answer Generation

Lastly, GPT-4 proceeds to generate an answer to the previously
formulated question. This phase engages the model in a reading
comprehension task, adhering to guidelines that ensure the answer is
factual, concise, and self-contained. By rephrasing or summarizing the
information present in the original sentence, the model effectively
reorganizes the information in the passage that aligns with the
perspective of the generated question.

By following this three-step procedure, we gather a set of 600 training
instances from GPT-4 including the analysis, question and answer, which
will be used to align an open-source LLM for the knowledge mining task.
GPT-4 substitutes for human labeling efforts in this context.

### 3.2 Training for Model Alignment

Utilizing GPT-4 for the above knowledge mining comes with two primary
drawbacks:

1.  1.  The computational overhead for large-scale inference is significant.
2.  2.  Each generative step necessitates detailed prompts that outline the
        guidelines for generating appropriate output.

To address these issues, we employ a secondary, smaller LLM (LLaMa-7b)
that we fine-tune to align with the GPT-4-generated seed data. The
fine-tuning employs a simplified prompt (detailed in
[appendix B](#A2 "Appendix B LLMiner Prompts for Training and Inference ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest")),
with a document and a highlighted sentence as input. The model outputs
either an analysis or an analysis followed by a QA pair, depending on
whether the sentence is important. This process effectively clones
GPT-4’s responses using more straightforward prompts. The fine-tuning
serves as a behavior cloning process to mimic the responses generated by
GPT-4 using more concise and straightforward prompts.

### 3.3 Inference with LLMiner

During the inference stage, we process each incoming passage by
iterating through all its sentences, treating each one as a "highlighted
sentence" for the model to focus on. We use the same prompt as in the
training phase of LLMiner for inference with each highlighted sentence
within the passage. While we generate an intermediate analysis, we opt
not to use it as knowledge representation. Instead, our primary goal is
to mine knowledge in instructional format that can serve as training
data for subsequent model fine-tuning. Additionally, the answers from
LLMiner output also function as augmented knowledge in text form, adding
richness and diversity to the original text corpus, as illustrated in
Figure
[fig. 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2 Training for Model Alignment ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").

## 4 Domain-Specific Text Collection

This section outlines the methodologies for gathering domain-specific
text and crafting test data. The objective is to facilitate the
development of a specialized chatbot, tailored to user-defined topics
such as "Artificial General Intelligence" or "Traditional Medicine in
Southeast Asia." We delineate the processes for constructing a
domain-specific corpus and formulating testing datasets.

### 4.1 Construction of a Domain-Specific Corpus

We employ two open-source tools, Wikipedia-API and GPT-4, to assemble a
domain-specific corpus based on user-defined topics.

##### Initial Topic Expansion

As a first step, we utilize GPT-4 to generate hypothetical Wikipedia
titles related to the user-specified topic, as detailed in
[fig. 10](#A3.F10 "In Appendix C Prompts for Building Domain Corpus ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").
These titles, potentially non-existent on Wikipedia, act as initial
search queries for the Wikipedia-API. Users may also contribute
additional keywords to enhance these seed titles.

##### Wikipedia-Based Retrieval and Selection

With the generated hypothetical titles and user-provided keywords in
hand, we utilize the Wikipedia-API to retrieve actual Wikipedia titles.
Subsequently, GPT-4 is used to assess relevance, following the prompt
specified in
[fig. 11](#A3.F11 "In Appendix C Prompts for Building Domain Corpus ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").
Articles corresponding to the relevant titles are incorporated into our
domain-specific text corpus.

### 4.2 Testing Dataset Creation

This subsection elaborates on the approach to simulate real-world user
interactions with the chatbot and the process of testing dataset
creation, focusing on a scenario where the trainer customizes a chatbot
for a specific topic, and users interact with it for inquiries within
that domain.

##### Simulating User Queries

To mimic user interactions, we engage annotators to propose questions
that reflect potential user queries within the chosen topic. We aim to
compile around 30 human-crafted questions per topic. These questions are
designed to be factually based, targeting the chatbot’s knowledge in the
domain text. In addition, annotators are responsible for creating
concise reference answers, typically one or two sentences long, for each
question.

##### Independence from Training Corpus

Annotators are allowed to utilize external resources like Google or
ChatGPT to ensure the accuracy and relevance of their questions and
answers. However, it’s crucial to note that they do not have access to
the actual text corpus or augmented data compiled by the chatbot
trainer. This constraint ensures that the testing questions and
responses are independent of the chatbot’s training data, thereby
allowing for an unbiased evaluation of the chatbot’s performance in
responding to unanticipated queries.

## 5 Chatbot Training Methodology

To train our specialized chatbot, we adopt a mixed-training approach
that combines four distinct types of data:

1.  1.  Raw Text: This consists of the domain-specific text that we have
        gathered from Wikipedia, serving as a foundational source for domain
        expertise.
2.  2.  Conversation Data: We leverage the OpenAssistant Conversations
        Dataset (OASST) [Köpf et al. (2023)](#bib.bib14) as a source of
        dialog interactions. To ensure quality, we only include entries with
        an overall score greater than 0.5, resulting in approximately 17k
        conversations.
3.  3.  QA Pairs: These are the question-answer pairs that we have mined
        using the methodologies described earlier. This data enriches the
        model’s capability to respond to user queries in a focused manner.
4.  4.  Augmented Knowledge: In addition to using full QA pairs, we also
        include the list of mined answers as standalone elements to serve as
        augmented knowledge in text form. This approach aims to enhance the
        chatbot’s reservoir of domain-specific information, as depicted in
        [fig. 3](#S3.F3 "In 3.2 Training for Model Alignment ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").

By integrating these diverse data sources, we aim to create a robust and
versatile chatbot capable of fielding domain-specific queries with a
high degree of accuracy and contextual relevance. Since the mixing ratio
of these different data types is not the primary focus of the paper, we
employ uniform sampling for the data types as our design choice. The
training details are included in
[appendix D](#A4 "Appendix D Training Hyperparameters ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").

## 6 Experiments and Evaluations

This section delves into the empirical validation of LLMiner’s
effectiveness by examining the quality of the generated Question-Answer
(QA) pairs. Additionally, we assess the performance of a chatbot that
utilizes LLMiner, benchmarking it against several baseline models. These
evaluations serve to substantiate the advantages of our method over
prior approaches.

### 6.1 Performance Evaluation of LLMiner

We assess LLMiner’s capabilities by comparing its output to a baseline
knowledge miner that lacks chain-of-thought analysis. This baseline is
modeled after existing methodologies as cited in [Dai et al.
(2022b)](#bib.bib6); [Dai et al. (2022a)](#bib.bib5); [Lewis et al.
(2021)](#bib.bib15). For the sake of a balanced comparison, both LLMiner
and the baseline are trained using the same underlying model
architecture and pipeline, albeit with distinct prompts, as detailed in
Appendix
[appendix E](#A5 "Appendix E Baseline Miner Prompts for GPT-4 and Knowledge Minder Training ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").

#### 6.1.1 Qualitative Analysis

An illustrative example of LLMiner’s performance is presented in Figure
[fig. 2](#S2.F2 "In 2.3 Chain-of-thought Reasoning ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").
The figure underscores how LLMiner emphasizes key pieces of information
in the sentence while linking them to a broader context, thereby
enriching the quality of the mined QA pairs.

### 6.2 Chatbot Performance Evaluation

We assess the performance of a chatbot trained on the LLaMa-7B model
under various configurations:

- •
  Chatbot (Baseline): Trained exclusively on the OASST conversation
  dataset.
- •
  Chatbot + Passage: Serves as a major baseline and combines
  domain-specific passages with the OASST dataset.
- •
  Chatbot + Passage + QA: Integrates QA pairs generated by LLMiner along
  with domain passages and the OASST dataset.
- •
  Chatbot + Passage + QA + Augmentation: Includes an augmented list of
  answers for knowledge enrichment, in addition to the components of the
  previous configuration.

We employ GPT-4 as a judge to rate the quality of the generated answers.
The evaluations are conducted using a Likert scale ranging from 1 to 5,
with prompts specified in Appendix
[appendix F](#A6 "Appendix F GPT-4 Judge Prompts ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").
The results of this evaluation are presented in Table
[table 1](#S6.T1 "In 6.2 Chatbot Performance Evaluation ‣ 6 Experiments and Evaluations ‣ A Self-enhancement Approach for Domain-specific Chatbot Training via Knowledge Mining and Digest").

Our findings indicate that the incorporation of QA data from LLMiner can
help learning domain knowledge from raw text corpus, which enhances the
quality of chatbot to answer factual-based questions. Furthermore,
mixing additional the training data with augmented knowledge (rewritten
version of original passage by the list of answers) offers more
perspectives of the original text, which improves the model’s
performance across three of the four topics assessed.

## 7 Conclusion

We propose a methodology for transforming a Large Language Model (LLM)
into a specialized knowledge miner, namely LLMiner. Our approach
leverages the power of GPT-4 to prepare seed data and fine-tunes a
smaller LLM to perform the knowledge mining task efficiently. The mined
Question-Answer (QA) pairs serve not only as enriched data for
fine-tuning but also as a resource for domain-specific queries.
Experimental evaluations demonstrated the effectiveness of our approach
in both data mining quality and chatbot performance. Notably, the
inclusion of QA pairs and additional text augmentations led to
significant improvements in chatbot performance across multiple topics.
Our findings underscore the potential of using LLMs as robust,
domain-specific knowledge miners and shed light on the self-improvement
of LLM training with automomous data mining.
