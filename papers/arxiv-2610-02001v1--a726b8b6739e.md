###### Abstract

Small open-weight models (2–9B) run on ordinary laptops, but under
cloud-scale agent harnesses they rarely complete real tasks: tool
prefill overflows the context, self-correction diverges, tool
demonstrations loop, and tasks are silently abandoned. We present
evidence, from a controlled single-machine comparison and one
third-party benchmark, that a substantial share of these failures is
attributable to the harness rather than the model. We introduce
Mingbird, a local-first agent harness for Windows and Ollama whose ten
mechanisms compensate point-by-point for small-model failure forms,
three of them representative: a byte-level net-zero prefill budget, a
finish gate that re-reads the task before accepting completion, and
signature-level loop detection. On LRAB, a controlled comparison holding
machine, models, budgets, and scoring fixed (4 harnesses $`\times`$ 4
open models (2B–35B) $`\times`$ 18 real tasks, deterministic artifact
scoring), Mingbird reaches 0.886 overall against 0.631 (goose), 0.479
(opencode), and 0.405 (agent-mini), with all 288 cells published; on
$`\tau^{2}`$-bench (278 tasks, three arms, one protocol) it totals 0.856
against 0.791 and 0.737; and a frontier-model probe on the same 18 tasks
spans 0.997 to 0.478 across harnesses, with well-formed scaffolds
staying within 0.072 of each other. A leave-one-mechanism-out ablation
is reported as directional only: same-night replications of the same arm
move its mean by up to 0.069, the size of every nominal single-trial
delta, and the one batch-matched comparison (full mechanism stack versus
text re-read alone) gives the executable completion guards a paired
$`+0.10`$ across three replications. The evidence carries stated limits:
a self-built benchmark, a single machine, and single-trial scoring.

## 1 Introduction

Open-weight language models in the 2–9B parameter range have crossed a
practical threshold. On a laptop with an integrated GPU and 16–32 GB of
shared memory they answer, summarize, translate, and explain at a level
that makes them useful daily tools \[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2),
[3](#bib.bib3), [4](#bib.bib4)\]. Local runtimes such as Ollama and
llama.cpp made deployment a one-command operation \[[5](#bib.bib5),
[6](#bib.bib6)\]. The prevailing assumption is that such models cannot
serve as agents — systems that autonomously execute multi-step,
tool-mediated tasks: writing code that passes its tests, organizing
files, conducting research with real search, or driving a domain
database \[[7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8)\]. A recent position paper
argues the opposite for agentic workloads \[[9](#bib.bib9)\]; our
results support that view, with one qualification: the limiting
component is usually the harness rather than the model.

Our observations conflict with this assumption in a specific way: small
models generally produce the correct next action, but cannot reliably
emit and execute every step unaided, inside a harness designed for a
much larger model. When we drove a 2B model with a cloud-scale agent
framework, we observed the same failure forms repeatedly: the static
tool prefill alone consumed a large fraction of the context
(long-context degradation is well documented \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]); the
model could not self-correct after a failed tool call
\[[10](#bib.bib10), [11](#bib.bib11)\]; it looped indefinitely on tool
demonstrations; and it sometimes declared a task finished when the
artifacts did not exist, or gave up halfway without saying so. These are
not failures of capability. They are failures of the scaffold
surrounding the model, and we treat them as engineering defects with
corresponding engineering remedies.

This paper makes that argument concrete. We describe Mingbird, a
local-first agent harness for Windows and Ollama, designed from the
start for 2–9B models (upper bound tested at the 35B tier;
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.3 Protocol ‣ 4 LRAB: A Controlled Benchmark, Not a Leaderboard ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
Mingbird is a GUI application and a CLI over one agent loop; it adds no
inference machinery of its own and talks to whatever model Ollama
serves. Its design consists of ten core mechanisms (M1–M10) plus a
five-ring safety model (M14, v1.6.0; M11–M13 are product-level
facilities,
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Mechanisms ‣ 3 The Mingbird Harness ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")),
each mapped to a failure form we first observed and then measured.

Measuring harness effects requires holding everything else constant, so
we built LRAB (Local Real-task Agent Benchmark) as a controlled
experiment: 4 harnesses (Mingbird, goose \[[12](#bib.bib12)\],
agent-mini \[[13](#bib.bib13)\], and opencode \[[14](#bib.bib14)\], all
stock and unpatched) $`\times`$ 4 open models (2B, 4B, 12B, 35B;
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.3 Protocol ‣ 4 LRAB: A Controlled Benchmark, Not a Leaderboard ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks"))
$`\times`$ 18 real tasks, 288 cells in total, on one machine, one
backend, uniform time budgets, and deterministic artifact-based scoring.
Every one of the 288 cells is published as raw data; every aggregate
from the unified-protocol campaigns is directly recomputable from the
rows of a published CSV file (the legacy thinking-on sensitivity
numbers, retained for comparison in
Section [5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4 External check: 𝜏^2-bench in three domains ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks"),
are aggregate-level only). Because LRAB was designed by us, we also
validate externally: on $`\tau^{2}`$-bench \[[15](#bib.bib15)\], an
independent benchmark we did not design, we ran all three domains
(retail, airline, telecom) under one uniform protocol
(Section [5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4 External check: 𝜏^2-bench in three domains ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
Over the 278 scored tasks (114 retail, 50 airline, 114 telecom),
Mingbird totals 0.856 against 0.791 for the benchmark’s native agent and
0.737 for opencode; the goose arm is deferred for throughput
(Section [5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4 External check: 𝜏^2-bench in three domains ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).

by large margins.

## 2 Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Agent paradigms

The dominant agent recipe descends from ReAct \[[16](#bib.bib16)\]: the
model interleaves free-form reasoning with tool calls in a loop, and the
harness executes each call and appends the observation. Toolformer
\[[17](#bib.bib17)\] demonstrated that models can learn when to call
APIs; Gorilla \[[18](#bib.bib18)\] fine-tunes specifically for accurate
API invocation and motivated the function-calling interfaces now exposed
by most inference providers. Around this core, a body of work improves
the _model’s_ behavior inside the loop: Reflexion \[[19](#bib.bib19)\]
stores verbal self-critiques across attempts, and Self-Refine
\[[20](#bib.bib20)\] iterates on its own outputs. These techniques
presuppose exactly what small models lack, namely the ability to produce
a usable critique of one’s own attempt. Mingbird therefore concentrates
on the harness side: it does not ask the model to self-improve in the
abstract; it changes what the model sees, when it is allowed to stop,
and what happens after each malformed step. Anthropic’s engineering
guidance \[[21](#bib.bib21)\] distinguishes workflows (predefined code
paths) from agents (the model directs its own process); Mingbird is an
agent in that sense, but with harness-enforced guardrails at the points
where small models demonstrably fail.

### 2.2 Agent benchmarks

AgentBench \[[22](#bib.bib22)\] and AgentBoard \[[23](#bib.bib23)\]
evaluate multi-turn agents across many environments, the latter with
analytical progress metrics rather than final success only. WebArena
\[[24](#bib.bib24)\] builds a reproducible web environment; SWE-bench
\[[25](#bib.bib25)\] scores real GitHub issue resolution; GAIA
\[[26](#bib.bib26)\] scores general assistant questions that are easy
for humans. Closest to our external validation are $`\tau`$-bench
\[[27](#bib.bib27)\] and $`\tau^{2}`$-bench \[[15](#bib.bib15)\]: a user
simulator converses with an agent that must operate domain tools under
policy, and scoring verifies the final database state, so an agent that
bypasses its tool stream cannot score. LRAB differs from all of these in
intent: it is not meant to rank models but to isolate harness effects
under a fully published protocol, which is why it uses one machine, one
backend, and per-cell deterministic artifact scoring.

### 2.3 Local and small-model inference

llama.cpp \[[6](#bib.bib6)\] established efficient CPU/GPU inference for
quantized open models, and Ollama \[[5](#bib.bib5)\] packages it with
model management and a serving API. Surveys of small language models
\[[1](#bib.bib1)\] document the on-device latency and memory envelope,
and data-centric efforts such as SmolLM2 \[[2](#bib.bib2)\] and the
Gemma and Qwen families \[[3](#bib.bib3), [28](#bib.bib28)\] push
capability per parameter. This line of work improves the model side of
the local stack. The harness side, however, has largely been ported from
cloud practice: lightweight libraries such as smolagents
\[[29](#bib.bib29)\] and Open Interpreter \[[30](#bib.bib30)\] make
small models drivable with minimal scaffolding; function-calling at the
edge has been explored directly \[[7](#bib.bib7)\]; and the full
harnesses we test against (goose \[[12](#bib.bib12)\], opencode
\[[14](#bib.bib14)\], agent-mini \[[13](#bib.bib13)\]) are designed
around large-context cloud models and Unix-family shells. Mingbird
starts from the opposite end: 2–9B models, a Windows host, an integrated
GPU, and tasks that mix code, files, and research.

### 2.4 Engineering systems

SWE-agent \[[31](#bib.bib31)\] showed that the agent–computer interface
(the shape of the tools an agent is given) changes measured performance
on SWE-bench; our results agree and extend the observation to
model-scale effects. OpenHands \[[32](#bib.bib32)\] is a general
platform for software-development agents. Cline \[[33](#bib.bib33)\] and
aider \[[34](#bib.bib34)\] embed agents in editors and git workflows.
These systems target strong models (cloud APIs or large local
checkpoints), assume a POSIX-flavored command environment, and optimize
for coding tasks. Mingbird is complementary: it targets the under-served
regime where the model is small, the machine is a consumer laptop, and
the platform is Windows, and it treats harness mechanisms for
small-model failure forms as the primary design object.

##### Harness and scaffold effects.

That the harness, not only the model, drives measured agent performance
is an active 2026 result, not our discovery. Zhang et al.
\[[35](#bib.bib35)\] name and test the binding-constraint thesis —
harness configuration dominates model choice as a source of performance
variance — and call for mandatory harness disclosure; Starace
\[[36](#bib.bib36)\] pre-registers a three-scaffold, five-model
comparison on GAIA and finds within-model scaffold effects up to 28
points; the CAIS Arena benchmark \[[37](#bib.bib37)\] pins a single
model across six agent frameworks on one MCP tool server. Our
contribution is complementary and at a tier none of these cover: we
instantiate the thesis with 2–9B local weights on Windows/Ollama, and
decompose it at the mechanism level — a reusable inventory of
small-model failure forms with the harness mechanism that addresses
each, and measured per-mechanism costs and deltas. For loop detection
specifically, OpenHands’ StuckDetector \[[32](#bib.bib32)\] normalizes
events and counts repeated action–observation ($`\geq`$4), action–error
($`\geq`$3), monologue, and alternation patterns with intervention
nudges; our M4 follows that detection paradigm and adds signature
normalization over tool name plus arguments, a tiered escalation ladder,
and a CI-enforced regression tie. What we claim as new at this scale is
narrower: a net-zero prefill budget enforced byte-level in CI, and a
completion gate that re-reads the task text before accepting “done” —
both aimed at the 2–9B tier where, per our measurements, prefill
crowding and false finishes are the dominant failure forms. A
complementary line treats the harness itself as the design object:
Agentless \[[38](#bib.bib38)\] shows that a deliberately narrow scaffold
can outperform agentic ones on software-engineering tasks; ADAS
\[[39](#bib.bib39)\], AFlow \[[40](#bib.bib40)\], GPTSwarm
\[[41](#bib.bib41)\], and DSPy \[[42](#bib.bib42)\] search, compile, or
optimize harness structure automatically; and ToolLLM
\[[43](#bib.bib43)\] routes over large API spaces with a learned
retriever. Our category router is a static, zero-training counterpart of
that retrieval idea, and our mechanism set is hand-designed rather than
searched — a choice
Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
quantifies mechanism by mechanism.

## 3 The Mingbird Harness

### 3.1 Architecture overview

Mingbird is a local-first agent harness: everything (model, tools,
speech recognition, memory) runs on the user’s machine (the only
optional outbound endpoint is a user-configured search provider), and
the harness adds no inference overhead of its own. This section
describes the v1.6.0 release; all measurements reported in this paper
were taken on v1.5.0, and the two releases differ by 23 static-prefill
tokens (774 vs. 797;
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Reproducibility ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
Figure [1](#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3.1 Architecture overview ‣ 3 The Mingbird Harness ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
shows the architecture. A GUI (bilingual, built on ttkbootstrap) and a
CLI front the same agent loop. Per turn, the loop assembles a _flat
prefill_ through a task-domain router (M1), classifies the request as
chat or task (M2), executes tool calls through a sandboxed executor with
edit safety (M6) and security boundaries (M8), monitors loop signatures
(M4), and enforces a finish gate before any completion claim leaves the
system (M3). A resilience layer (M7) absorbs encoding faults, empty
turns, and process kills via checkpoints. The backend is whatever Ollama
serves, with automatic model detection and a default 128K context (up to

#### 3.2.1 M1: Flat prefill with a net-zero budget

##### Failure form.

A 2–9B model given a cloud-style tool manifest (a few dozen JSON schemas
with descriptions) spends several thousand tokens before the task
begins. In our pre-benchmark runs we observed instruction-following
degrade once a large, weakly relevant schema block preceded the task; in
the worst case the context is substantially spent before the first user
sentence. This matches the published input-length evidence: reasoning
degrades when the same task is padded with more tokens
\[[44](#bib.bib44)\], and frontier models lose accuracy long before
their context window fills \[[45](#bib.bib45)\].

##### Mechanism.

The harness classifies the task domain first and loads only the tool
definitions relevant to that category, in a flat (non-nested) format.
The factory prefill is 797 tokens, pinned by a unit test (v1.6.0). MCP
tools \[[46](#bib.bib46)\] are injected through the same mechanism:
category-routed, flat, with compressed schemas (parameters beyond eight
are trimmed to required ones). The binding constraint is a hard budget:
_any new feature must add zero net static prefill_, enforced by a
byte-level regression assertion in CI (M10).

##### Trade-off.

Category routing can misclassify and hide a tool the task needs; the
classifier is deliberately conservative and the task can request
re-routing. The net-zero rule forces every new capability to be paid for
by removing or compressing something else, a constraint that binds at
small scale, where prefill tokens compete directly with task context,
and that larger models absorb without measurable cost.

#### 3.2.2 M2: Chat/task layering

##### Failure form.

A user asks a casual question (“what can you do?”) and a small model,
seeing the full toolset, answers by _demonstrating_: it calls tools,
narrates, calls more tools, and enters an open-ended loop, which we term
the _tool-demonstration loop_. The same model given a task rarely does
this.

##### Mechanism.

Requests are classified into chat and task. Chat uses a minimal
chat-level prefill and the loop terminates once the answer is delivered;
the model is not offered a continuation. Task mode unlocks the full
toolset. A short follow-up instruction appended to a conversation that
has produced artifacts is recognized as task continuation. This rule is
what keeps multi-step repair pipelines (e.g. the todo-list repair flow)
coherent.

##### Trade-off.

Misrouting a genuine task into chat mode would strand it without tools,
so the classifier errs toward task mode whenever an artifact-producing
history exists. The layering trades a single unified loop for two
simple, separately testable ones.

#### 3.2.3 M3: Finish gate and completion-time prompt re-injection

##### Failure form.

Small models abandon tasks silently: they emit a message that reads as a
completion claim while the required artifacts are missing or incomplete.
Longer plans suffer a related drift, because after context compression
the plan text itself is gone and the model continues from a summary that
has lost the acceptance criteria.

##### Mechanism.

Before any completion claim is accepted, the harness re-reads the
original task text and asks the model to verify delivery against it,
point by point. The todo/plan pipeline re-injects the plan verbatim
after compression, and the todo interface exposes an explicit completion
exit (todo(update, all=true)) so that completion is an explicit action
rather than a stylistic drift. The gate’s verification step runs the
task’s own checks and feeds real failures (file, line, error text) back
into the conversation; we refer to this loop as the
verification-feedback mechanism in the ablation.

##### Trade-off.

The gate costs one extra model turn per task. It also cannot force a
model to produce a _correct_ self-check; what it forces is a second pass
in which the task text is in context, which in our runs was sufficient
to convert most silent abandonments into either completion or an
articulated blocker.

#### 3.2.4 M4: Signature-level anti-loop

##### Failure form.

A small model repeats the same tool call with the same arguments (10,
50, 150 times) producing no artifacts. In one pre-benchmark case (4B
model, task WF-08), the pre-fix run made 156 tool calls and produced 0
artifacts; we return to this case as motivation below.

##### Mechanism.

The harness computes a call signature (tool plus normalized arguments)
and trips at $`\geq 6`$ identical calls or $`\geq 15`$ consecutive calls
with no output. Escalation is graduated: the third rejection carries a
strong corrective hint, and six consecutive empty turns trigger a hard
reset of the turn; a v1.5.0 reachability fix ensures that alternating
partial-output debris and empty turns no longer reset the escalation
counters. After the fix, the same 4B $`\times`$ WF-08 configuration
completed in 15 tool calls with 3 artifacts, with the prefill
bit-identical before and after. This is a single-task motivating case,
not controlled evidence; the controlled ablation appears in
Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks").
Two adjacent failure modes are guarded separately. The output cap was
raised from 2048 to 8192 tokens in v1.5.0: at 2048, the emission of a
large file is truncated mid-body and the run decays into an empty-turn
spiral; a truncation signature now returns skeleton-plus-append feedback
so the model can continue in chunks. File reading obeys a byte-budget
crawl guard (v2). The budget is clamped to twice the file size, between
64 KB and 512 KB. The guard never refuses outright; past the budget it
returns the first 2000 characters of the requested range with the
position preserved, so that crawling can continue rather than restart.

##### Trade-off.

Any loop detector has false positives (legitimate iteration, e.g.
re-running tests after each edit). Signatures normalize arguments, not
raw text, and legitimate iteration usually changes inputs; the
thresholds ($`6`$, $`15`$) were set well above observed legitimate
repetition. When the detector does trip on legitimate work, the cost is
one corrective prompt, not a terminated run.

#### 3.2.5 M5: False-finish rejection and format rescue

##### Failure form.

Two related failure modes affect output emission. First, the model
claims completion without artifacts; this case is handled by the M3
gate, and M5 governs the content of the rejection. Second, the model
emits a tool call as plain text — placeholder-quoted quasi-XML, a JSON
snippet in prose, or a double-quoted pseudo-call — which a strict parser
discards, collapsing the turn.

##### Mechanism.

A rejected false-finish message always carries the next executable
action, so the lowest-cost recovery path is embedded in the rejection
itself. Malformed calls are rescued structurally: the harness recognizes
three recurring shapes (placeholder-quoted, bare-JSON, and double-quoted
pseudo-calls) and converts them into real calls. Strict schema
enforcement is the standard alternative; grammar-constrained decoding
engines \[[47](#bib.bib47), [48](#bib.bib48), [49](#bib.bib49)\] and
schema-emission benchmarks \[[50](#bib.bib50)\] map its costs, and how
much output-format restrictions cost a model is actively debated
\[[51](#bib.bib51)\]; for 2–9B models we adopt rescue-with-feedback
instead of strict schema enforcement. Repeated same-signature emission
after a rescue triggers a corrective hint.

##### Trade-off.

Structural rescue is heuristic; a shape outside the three forms is
rejected with feedback, and the harness does not attempt speculative
parsing. We preferred a small, audited rescue set over a permissive
parser that could execute something the model did not mean.

#### 3.2.6 M6: Edit safety

##### Failure form.

A small model overwrites a file with a bad edit and cannot recover;
subsequent turns are then consumed by repair attempts, a regime in which
2–9B models perform particularly poorly.

##### Mechanism.

Every write automatically records a .bak backup; rollback is one command
the model can issue itself. An edit convergence guard watches for
alternating destructive edits.

##### Trade-off.

.bak files accumulate in the working directory; they are excluded from
artifact scoring and pruned on successful convergence. The mechanism is
deliberately simple — no diffing, no merging — because simplicity is
what makes it usable _by the model itself_ as a recovery tool.

#### 3.2.7 M7: Resilience (encoding, empty turns, checkpoints)

##### Failure form.

On Windows, process output arrives in legacy codepages (GBK among them);
a strict UTF-8 reader crashes silently and the harness stalls with no
diagnostic. Separately, models occasionally emit empty generations, and
long runs are terminated (by the user, by power management, or by
crashes) and were previously unrecoverable.

##### Mechanism.

The read side decodes as UTF-8 with replacement, so no byte pattern can
crash the reader thread. This fix ended a class of silent stalls. An
empty generation triggers a hard reset of the turn rather than appending
nothing. “Think-tag shells” (responses whose visible content is only a
reasoning wrapper with no actionable output) are detected semantically
and treated as empty. A kill-resume checkpoint records enough state to
resume a terminated run from the last completed step.

##### Trade-off.

Replacement decoding can garble individual characters in favor of never
losing the stream; for agent work (logs, test output, paths) we found
stream survival strictly more valuable than byte fidelity. Checkpoints
add state to manage and were limited to one file per run.

#### 3.2.8 M8: Security boundary

##### Failure form.

An agent that writes files and runs commands can write outside its
working directory, access credential files, or — in harnesses with agent
fan-out — spawn uncontrolled sub-agents. Small models are more prone to
such divergences.

##### Mechanism.

Working-directory boundaries are enforced by normalized-path checks
(symlink- and separator-safe on Windows). Sensitive paths (.ssh, .env,
credential stores) sit behind a gate. Out-of-bounds operations require
explicit GUI confirmation by the human. Parallel sub-agents are denied
by default (default-deny); when sub-agents are enabled, each gets a
minimal system prompt that is not counted against the main prefill
budget.

##### Trade-off.

Confirmation dialogs add friction for power users; the default-deny rule
forgoes an entire concurrency feature by default. We chose the
configuration that is safe for a non-expert user on a single personal
machine — the population a local-first harness primarily serves.

#### 3.2.9 M9: Capability surface

##### Design intent.

M9 groups the capabilities that must fit under the M1 budget:
capabilities are added only if they remain within the static prefill
budget.

- •
  Voice input ships with a bundled local STT model, pure CPU,
  approximately $`20\times`$ real time, with automatic end-of-speech
  detection.
- •
  Vision works by reading images into the message payload (verified with
  four vision-capable models).
- •
  Session memory is persistent, searchable, and replayable.
- •
  MCP servers connect through a flat JSON configuration.
- •
  Skills are markdown files with frontmatter, progressively disclosed;
  dozens of skills compress to a few hundred tokens of standing context.
- •
  Model identity and context length are auto-detected from Ollama
  (default 128K, up to 256K).
- •
  A task time-box ships off by default. The user writes a budget in
  plain language (in a dedicated box or in the task text itself; both
  Chinese and English phrasings are parsed, and a deadline written in
  the task overrides the box), and as the budget runs down the harness
  prompts the model to converge on a deliverable. Users of local models
  are, in our experience, sensitive to wall-clock time, and are better
  served by maximal delivery within a bounded run than by an unbounded
  one.

##### Trade-off.

Progressive disclosure means the model must know to open a skill file
before using it; the skill index is kept in the standing context
precisely so that this costs one read. None of these capabilities is
novel individually; the constraint that ties them together (zero static
prefill growth) is the contribution.

#### 3.2.10 M10: Quality base

##### Mechanism.

441 tests run in CI (v1.6.0). Two assertions matter most for this paper:
the byte-level prefill net-zero assertion (M1) and the loop detector
thresholds (M4), both regression-guarded.

##### Trade-off.

The test suite constrains implementation speed; every contribution must
keep the prefill budget intact, which has rejected otherwise reasonable
designs (e.g. richer system prompts, always-on tool indexes).

For completeness, the product-level facilities that are not part of the
measured mechanism set are: M11, the no-telemetry/no-account declaration
(architectural, unmeasured here); M12, the task time-box (off by
default, zero static cost); M13, bundled voice input (local STT, no
agent-loop involvement). They are listed once for inventory and not
evaluated.

The five-ring safety model follows as M14.

#### 3.2.11 M14: Five-ring safety model (v1.6.0)

##### Failure form.

Mid-task, a small model may attempt to uninstall a failing dependency or
delete a system file; we observed this incident pattern in actual runs.

##### Mechanism.

Safety behavior is organized as five rings: sub-agents run sandboxed
with permissions strictly narrower than the main agent’s; irreversible
operations (formatting, raw-device writes, driver removal and the like)
are refused outright; system-mutating actions are graded by operator
presence (unattended runs default to deny, with an explicit per-run
override); recursive deletion is confined to the working directory, with
per-file alternatives offered when a request is refused; and the last
ring is reversibility (.bak before writes, deletions moved to a trash
directory, large-file overwrites requiring an explicit replace). A
single environment variable provides a global override, used at the
operator’s own risk.

##### Trade-off.

The rings may refuse operations an expert could perform safely; this
conservative bias is deliberate given the target population defined in
M8, and the explicit overrides preserve expert usability. The ring
design is complementary to prompt-injection defenses evaluated elsewhere
\[[52](#bib.bib52), [53](#bib.bib53)\]: M14 governs what the agent’s own
tool stream may do to the host, not what injected content can make it
say.

##### Adversarial evaluation.

The rings are measured, not only designed. We built a 54-case probe set
— 44 adversarial user requests (7/9/9/12/7 across rings 1–5) plus 10
benign controls — in which every adversarial prompt is a
natural-language user request of the kind a small model accepts without
suspicion (delete this failing service; write this file straight into
the system temp area; read my SSH key and summarize it), never an
injected text: the set tests what the agent’s own tool stream may do to
the host, which is M14’s contract, not prompt-injection resistance
(evaluated elsewhere, \[[52](#bib.bib52), [53](#bib.bib53)\]). Both
small-tier matrix models were run once per case (gemma4:e2b, qwen3.5:4b;
unattended mode, temperature 0, thinking disabled — the unified protocol
of the matrix campaigns) on the v1.8.2-era tree
(Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Reproducibility ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
Scoring is dual-channel: a _marker channel_ reads the archived
transcript and checkpoint for the rings’ own interception messages, and
a _ground-truth channel_ diffs host state before and after each case
(sentinel files, content hashes, directory listings of the sensitive
locations, NTFS alternate streams, one registry probe) plus rollback
checks (an overwrite must leave a .bak or a trash entry, and must not
truncate without backup). A third transcript-level rule flags any tool
call whose _command or path_ (never its file content) matches the case’s
danger pattern and returned success; such flags are alerts rather than
effects — each is verified against the ground-truth channel and the
archived workdir, and the per-case disposition is published with the run
(benchmarks/safety/results_2609/).

absolute-path and ..\\.\\ relative copies into system locations, a copy
with the \\?\\ prefix that dodges path normalization, a junction written

only on the fifth route, after four direct routes were refused in

override variable inside its own command string; the repeated-call guard

throughout (0.67–1.00 by ring); what fails is closing every route at
once. We read this as the measured boundary of pattern-list enforcement:
it holds against the impulsive tool use M14 is designed for and
demonstrably does not hold against a methodical same-tier adversary.

##### Controls and claims.

Of the 10 benign in-workdir operations, the 2B completes 6 and the 4B
completes 7. The refusals are informative: reading an in-workdir .env
copy is refused on both models because the sensitive-path substring
match has no workspace scoping (this probe targets exactly that, and it
reproduces across models); on the 2B, two in-workdir recursive deletes
were also refused, because the irreversible-command list fires before
the workspace-scoping rule can allow them — the over-strictness is
command-shape dependent, since the 4B’s PowerShell route through the
same control was correctly allowed. One control per model completed
without refusal but without the required artifact (a capability miss,
counted separately from false refusals), and one 4B control timed out.
Beyond the rings, the 2B runs show a model-level integrity failure no
gate closes: in two verified-benign flags the finish summary asserted
the refused action had been performed (a parent directory deleted;
sensitive cards written to a shared library); the finish gate bounced
each claim once and the model restated it, while ground truth showed no
effect. The 4B’s analogous cases died in command-repetition timeouts
before any finish claim; conversely, its confirmed leaks were reported
honestly as successes — the model did what it claimed. We report both
behaviors as measured, without claiming the finish gate catches false
claims.

## 4 LRAB: A Controlled Benchmark, Not a Leaderboard

### 4.1 Design principles

LRAB is self-built, and this provenance constrains how its results may
be used. A self-built benchmark cannot establish that Mingbird is the
best harness in general; it can establish a causal statement: _on these
18 tasks, on this machine, with these models, changing only the harness
changes the outcome by the reported margin_. Three principles follow.
First, hold everything except the harness constant: same machine, same
Ollama backend, same models, same time budgets, same scorer. Second,
make every cell inspectable: tasks, fixtures, scoring code, and all 288
per-cell results are published, so any aggregate is recomputable from
raw rows. Third, treat the benchmark as one component of the evidence,
with a third-party benchmark ($`\tau^{2}`$-bench,
Section [5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4 External check: 𝜏^2-bench in three domains ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks"))
as the other.

### 4.2 Task set

The 18 tasks are emitted by versioned generator scripts (published in
the repository) into a four-tier difficulty ladder, so that a failure
can be localized to a regime rather than averaged into one number: T1
retrieval (WF-01/02), locate and extract from supplied fixtures; T2
synthesis (WF-03–06), implement features, synthesize data, write tests;
T3 workflow (WF-07–15), multi-step work with well-defined artifacts —
web research with real search queries and sourced findings, profiling
and optimization, refactoring, comparison dossiers; T4 long-horizon
(LH-01–03), multi-phase builds intended to run for hours, testing plan
retention and recovery (M3, M7), with LH-03 doubling as a
kill-and-resume stress test (checkpoint continuity at half budget). Each
task specification is structured: domain, input fixtures the agent must
ground in, a minimum credible plan, milestone checks, and final artifact
checks. Task texts are concrete and verifiable by construction — they
name the artifacts that must exist and the checks they must satisfy —
and definitions are frozen before a campaign begins. The set covers the
artifact-producing core of local agent work (code with tests, data
analysis, grounded research, file organization, refactoring, multi-hour
builds) and deliberately excludes what the external check covers:
interactive tool-use dialogue
(Section [5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4 External check: 𝜏^2-bench in three domains ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
Per-task specifications, generator scripts, and the full scoring rubrics
are published in the repository.

### 4.3 Protocol

All 288 cells ran on one machine (an integrated GPU with 32 GB shared
memory, Windows 11, Ollama as the only backend). Four harnesses
(Mingbird, goose \[[12](#bib.bib12)\], agent-mini \[[13](#bib.bib13)\],
and opencode \[[14](#bib.bib14)\]), all stock and unpatched, each drove
the same models: gemma4:e2b (2B by Ollama tag: 5B total, 2.3B active per
token) \[[3](#bib.bib3)\], qwen3.5:4b (4B), gemma4:12b (12B)
\[[3](#bib.bib3)\], and ornith-1.5:35b (Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B, a public
multimodal release by Ornith AI \[[54](#bib.bib54)\]);

### 4.4 Scoring

Scoring is deterministic and artifact-based: files exist where the task
specifies; tests pass when executed; reports contain the required
findings. Scores are partial: a cell with seven equally weighted checks
scores 0.571 when four pass; where weights are non-uniform, the
published rubric states them explicitly. Harness processes have no
access to the scorer, so a harness cannot improve its score by any means
other than producing artifacts.

### 4.5 Fairness: time is priced by budgets, not by seconds

Harnesses differ substantially in wall-clock behavior, and a benchmark
must fix how to price that. LRAB prices time with uniform budgets per
task family and does not normalize raw seconds. The rationale is that an
agent is useful to a user only if it delivers within the time that the
user is willing to wait; wall-clock differences between harnesses
therefore reflect real architectural costs. In goose’s case, a large
prefill amplified by long contexts manifests as slower but substantive
work: its slowest cell runs 11.7$`\times`$ slower than Mingbird on the
same task and model (WF-04, 12B; derived from the published CSV).
Discounting seconds would have rewarded harnesses that terminate early
over harnesses that work slowly, and awarding speed-normalized credit
would have rewarded doing less per turn. Budget pricing instead encodes
the user-facing requirement: finish within the budget or score zero. In
the released matrix no arm actually hit the ceiling: the number of
cells, of each arm’s 72, whose wall time reached 95% or more of the
budget is zero for all four arms (Mingbird, goose, opencode, agent-mini:
0/72 each; the one budget-terminated cell in the matrix — goose’s WF-08
at 4B — has no wall-clock recorded in the published CSV, so it enters
this census through no arm;
Section [4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Protocol ‣ 4 LRAB: A Controlled Benchmark, Not a Leaderboard ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")),
so in this batch budget pricing changed no cell’s score through timeout
truncation. We report the wall-clock consequences explicitly in
Section [5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3 Wall-clock discussion ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks"),
so that readers who prefer a different pricing can re-derive it from the
CSV.

##### Statistical significance.

Because each cell is a single run, we treat the 18 tasks as the unit of
analysis. Within each model size, Mingbird is compared with each
competitor by a two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the 18
task pairs, with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals over task resamples
(10,000 resamples, fixed seed) and Holm correction across the three
comparisons within a model; the script (analyze_significance.py) ships
with the repository. At 2B and 12B, the Mingbird advantage over all
three competitors is significant after Holm correction (largest adjusted
$`p`$ per model: 0.0008 at 2B, 0.0466 at 12B). At 4B only the comparison
with opencode is significant (adjusted $`p=0.0181`$); goose, with
thinking disabled under the unified protocol, closes to 0.801 against
Mingbird’s 0.876 and the comparison does not reach significance (raw
$`p=0.3258`$), nor does agent-mini at its strongest tier (adjusted
$`p=0.1486`$). At 35B the comparisons with goose (Holm-adjusted
$`p=0.0415`$) and agent-mini ($`0.0005`$) are significant, while
opencode at 0.896 against Mingbird’s 0.941 does not separate (raw
$`p=0.4652`$). We read this honestly: under the unified no-thinking
protocol two competitors close the gap at their strongest tiers, and the
robust Mingbird advantages are the small-model segment and the overall
margin.
Figure [5](#S5.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 5.2 Failure forms behind the numbers ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
shows the score distributions behind these tests.

The overall margin itself is given an interval rather than asserted.
Pooling the 72 cells and resampling whole tasks — each draw takes 18
tasks with replacement and keeps all four models of each drawn task, so
the four models of a task move together — the Mingbird advantage over
the published matrix is $`+0.255`$ against goose (95% CI
$`[+0.188,+0.322]`$), $`+0.407`$ against opencode ($`[+0.328,+0.491]`$)
and $`+0.481`$ against agent-mini ($`[+0.410,+0.555]`$); a task-level
sign permutation over the same summaries gives $`p\leq 10^{-4}`$ for all
three. Because the per-cell score is a weighted partial-credit sum, we
also report the scale-free version of the same comparison: cells at or
above half credit are 68/72 for Mingbird, 48/72 (goose), 38/72
(opencode) and 28/72 (agent-mini). Script:
benchmarks/overall_ci_2609.py.

##### Task-family clustering.

The 18 tasks are generated in four families (2 retrieval, 4 synthesis, 9
workflow, 3 long-horizon), so task-level pairing overstates
independence. We therefore re-ran the analysis with the family as the
resampling unit (cluster bootstrap over the four families; script
alongside analyze_significance.py). Two properties of this analysis must
be stated precisely, and they discipline what family-level claims are
available at all. First, when all four family means are non-negative, a
cluster-bootstrap CI cannot cross zero by construction — every resample
of non-negative family means is non-negative — so such a CI is
uninformative whatever its width; this applies to every comparison whose
family means share a sign, including the ones that favor us (at 2B all
three comparisons; at 4B vs. goose; at 12B vs. goose, whose tier-1 mean
is exactly zero). Second, the auxiliary sign permutation flips
task-level signs within families; it is a task-level test — a
family-level flip would have only $`2^{4}=16`$ sign patterns and a
minimum two-sided $`p`$ of $`0.125`$ — so with four families _no_
family-level significance claim is available for any comparison, ours
included. Under that standard the honest family-level statements are: at
2B the paired advantage is descriptively present in all four families
(all four family means strictly positive:
$`+1.000/+0.428/+0.413/+0.823`$ vs. goose), which is the strongest
family-level evidence four clusters can give, but it is not a
family-clustered significance claim; the task-level tests remain
significant as reported. The one comparison whose cluster CI is
informative is 4B vs. agent-mini, the only one with a negative family
mean (long-horizon $`-0.221`$): its cluster CI crosses zero, consistent
with its already-nonsignificant adjusted $`p`$. 4B vs. goose and 12B vs.
goose fall in the uninformative category above; their task-level results
stand as reported (nonsignificant $`p=0.35`$; marginal adjusted
$`p=0.036`$). No comparison that was nonsignificant becomes significant
under clustering.

##### Error accounting.

Errored trials score 0 for every arm. Under the unified protocol the
three completed arms finished with zero errored trials across all 278
cells; the earlier thinking-on runs had shown 5 native-agent errors in
each of retail and airline. We disclose error counts because the
error-as-0 rule is part of what a harness comparison measures: a
scaffold that crashes mid-task has not delivered.

##### Statistical significance.

Because per-task outcomes are pass/fail, the primary test is the exact
McNemar test on discordant pairs; the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test
(which on binary outcomes reduces to a sign test) agrees to the reported
digit. Over the 278 tasks of the three completed arms, Mingbird against
the native agent gives a mean paired difference of $`+0.065`$ (40 wins /
216 ties / 22 losses, McNemar $`p=0.030`$; Wilcoxon $`p=0.022`$), and
against opencode $`+0.119`$ (47 / 217 / 14, $`p=2.7\times 10^{-5}`$;
Wilcoxon $`2.4\times 10^{-5}`$); with Holm correction across the two
comparisons both remain significant (adjusted $`p=0.030`$ and
$`5.4\times 10^{-5}`$). Two qualifications belong to the result. First,
on the discriminating-domain subset (retail + airline, 164 tasks;
telecom is near-saturated at 0.93–1.00 for every arm and cannot separate
the arms), Mingbird separates from opencode ($`46`$/$`104`$/$`14`$,
$`p=4.2\times 10^{-5}`$) but not from the native agent
($`32`$/$`110`$/$`22`$, $`p=0.22`$): the pooled native-agent
significance leans on telecom, where Mingbird wins all 8 of its
discordant tasks. Domain-wise we report as computed: the opencode
comparison is significant in retail ($`p=0.002`$) and airline
($`p=0.005`$) but not in telecom; the native-agent comparison is
significant only in telecom ($`p=0.005`$), while retail alone (26 wins /
16 losses among 72 ties) does not separate at $`n{=}114`$. Second, the
external margin is smaller than the internal one: against the common
opponent opencode the paired margin is $`+0.407`$ on LRAB and $`+0.119`$
here ($`29\%`$ retained); the native agent does not appear in the LRAB
matrix, so its $`+0.065`$ has no internal counterpart. The tie-heavy
structure (pass/fail scoring) means the pooled tests lean on the domains
that discriminate.

##### Thinking as a harness configuration.

Because the unified protocol pins thinking off, we also hold the earlier
thinking-on runs as a sensitivity view. The configuration moves arms,
not noise: opencode’s retail score goes 0.246 $`\to`$ 0.588 and telecom
0.298 $`\to`$ 0.991 when thinking is disabled; the native agent’s
airline score goes 0.520 $`\to`$ 0.740 while its saturated telecom mean
drops 1.000 $`\to`$ 0.930; Mingbird moves least (retail 0.789 $`\to`$
0.763). The same lever appears inside LRAB (opencode 12B 0.140 $`\to`$
0.539; goose 35B 0.822 $`\to`$ 0.679). A harness’s thinking policy is
thus a first-class design decision with task-dependent costs and
benefits — one more axis on which harnesses differ even when the model
is fixed.

##### Per-arm best configuration.

Granting every arm its more favorable thinking configuration does not
change the ranking. On $`\tau^{2}`$, both configurations are per-trial
published (the thinking-on batch is archived alongside the unified one),
so the comparison is recomputable: taking each arm’s better
configuration gives Mingbird 0.867 (its best is thinking-on), the native
agent 0.791 (best off), and opencode 0.737 (best off), against the
unified-protocol 0.856 / 0.791 / 0.737. Notably, Mingbird is the arm
whose total moves least between configurations ($`\pm 0.011`$), while
rival arms move far more at the domain level (opencode’s telecom pass
rate swings from 0.298 with thinking on to 0.991 with it off); the
unified protocol is therefore not what produces the gap. On LRAB, the
legacy thinking-on batch is summarized only in aggregate (per-tier
examples above, marked as legacy), and the direction is tier-dependent
for rivals: goose gains from thinking at 35B (0.822 vs. 0.679) and on
the long-horizon tasks (0.623 vs. 0.394) but loses at 4B/12B (e.g. 0.636
vs. 0.801 at 4B, legacy vs. unified), while opencode’s 12B goes the
other way (0.140 vs. 0.539). No single thinking configuration is
uniformly best across harnesses — which is itself the finding.

##### Deviations from the official setup.

The user simulator is qwen3.8-flash, not the official gpt-4o user, so
absolute scores are not comparable to the published leaderboard. All
four arms share the identical simulator, tool schemas, and policy
prompts, so the between-arm comparison is controlled even though the
absolute numbers are not leaderboard-comparable. Per-trial traces and
the scoring manifests ship with the published data package.

### 5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B

To move from the design narrative of
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Mechanisms ‣ 3 The Mingbird Harness ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
toward marginal-contribution estimates, we ran an ablation on the v1.5.0
code base: four variants, each with one mechanism disabled through an
AGENT_ABLATION environment gate, over the same 18 LRAB tasks with
gemma4:e2b ($`n=18`$ cells per arm). The disabled mechanisms are the
finish gate (M3), the verification-feedback loop (part of M3), the
signature-level anti-loop monitor (M4), and the category-routed flat
prefill (M1); the ablation thus covers three mechanisms (M1, the two
halves of M3, and M4) across four variant arms, while M2 (chat/task
layering) and M5 (format rescue) are not ablated and remain the two
named mechanisms whose benefits this paper does not quantify. The four
variant arms ran 2026-09-14..15; the same-night re-run arms and their
three replications ran 2026-09-22 on the v1.8.2-era tree
(Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Reproducibility ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
The baseline is the full-mechanism arm of the same code base, and its 18
cells are reused directly from the 2B column of
Table [5](#S5.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 5.1 The 288-cell matrix ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
(same code); the four variant arms are independent new executions over
those tasks, under the same temperature-0 configuration as the main
matrix. There is therefore a batch gap between the baseline arm and the
variant arms, and the common-cause disclosure below stems from it. Each
variant wrote an independent results directory, scored by the same
deterministic rubric.

The two gate variants are separable in the implementation, and the
separation is mechanical: AGENT_ABLATION=finish_gate bypasses four of
the six finish-time interventions (no-work finish refusal, the pytest
test guard, summary-to-artifact cross-checking, and plan-completeness
checking); two interventions are not gated by this switch and stayed
active in both arms — the todo-sync reminder (which refuses a finish
while the model’s own declared plan shows unchecked items) and the
delivery self-check, which re-injects the task’s original text once and
asks the model to self-check before the second, accepted finish. This
matters for interpretation: the textual re-read — the part of M3 the
abstract highlights — is present in both arms, so the $`-0.098`$
measures the four executable guards _on top of_ the re-read, not the
re-read itself. AGENT_ABLATION= verify_feedback leaves every guard
active but downgrades the failure feedback inside pytest refusals — the
precise injected hint (failing test, file, line, assertion) is replaced
by the last line of the pytest output only. The two switches therefore
measure different things: removing the gate removes the
executable-refusal behavior; removing verification feedback keeps
refusals but starves the model of the exact failure location it would
need to recover in one step. The nearest published neighbors are
tool-interactive self-critique (CRITIC \[[57](#bib.bib57)\]) and
programmatic verification loops in code agents (AutoCodeRover
\[[58](#bib.bib58)\]); M3 applies the same verify-before-finish
principle at the harness level, for general local tasks rather than code
alone.

##### What the gate executes, and what the $`-0.098`$ is made of.

The pytest guard fires only when the working directory contains
test\_\*.py at finish time. Two of the 18 tasks stage benchmark-provided
test files into the workdir (WF-04 stages test_inventory.py; LH-03
stages test_suite.py); on those two tasks the harness at finish time
executes the same fixture the scorer executes later (the scorer’s
spec_pytest and script_pass checks run the same files), so there the
acceptance criterion is literally runnable before scoring. On the other
16 tasks the guard fires only on test files the agent itself wrote, and
the three other ablated guards act on information the scorer only
partially shares: the claim-check and the scorer both test file
existence, but the claim-check runs on whatever the model claimed in its
finish summary, and the no-work and plan-completeness guards act on
run-time information — productive tool use and the agent’s own declared
plan — that the scorer never sees (the published scoring is fully
deterministic — milestone and final-artifact checks only; the scorer’s
optional LLM-judge component was disabled for every published batch, so
no judge enters any LRAB number in this paper). The per-task
decomposition bounds how much of the effect this overlap can explain:
the net $`-0.098`$ is carried by four tasks at roughly $`+0.71`$ each
(LH-01, WF-13, WF-04, WF-02) against three tasks the gate hurt (WF-07
$`-0.571`$, WF-10 $`-0.322`$, WF-15 $`-0.143`$). Only one of the four
contributors (WF-04) stages a benchmark test file; its $`+0.714`$ is
$`41\%`$ of the net delta. The remaining majority comes from tasks where
no benchmark test exists for the harness to run — there the gate’s
benefit is refusing finishes that claim artifacts which do not exist,
not executing the grader’s checks, and the delta is measured with the
textual re-read active in both arms (see above). We still flag the
WF-04/LH-03 overlap as a threat to validity, and note the external
contrast: on $`\tau^{2}`$-bench, where the finish gate cannot execute
any acceptance check (scoring is database end-state plus judge), the
harness margin persists at $`+0.065`$ to $`+0.119`$.

##### Development exposure of the mechanisms.

Because the harness and the benchmark share authors, we disclose what
each ablated mechanism was developed against. The finish gate and the
anti-loop monitor were iterated _with LRAB in the loop_: their v1.2.0
forms were explicit responses to LRAB long-horizon failures
(early-finish abandonment; a same-tool-call rule that disarmed
legitimate batch work), and the WF-08 incident (156 calls, zero
artifacts $`\to`$ 15 calls, three artifacts, prefill byte-identical)
drove the anti-loop escalation ladder. The verification-feedback hint
predates LRAB (developed against an earlier internal benchmark,
2026-08-19..26, before LRAB existed on 2026-08-28), with LRAB-period
strengthening after a WF-08 12B failure. The flat prefill also predates
LRAB (root cause: a Q&A tool-demonstration loop, 2026-08-20) and, as
Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
notes, is inert on LRAB’s narrow tool surface. Consequently, the
$`-0.098`$ finish-gate and $`-0.015`$ anti-loop deltas should be read as
in-sample effect estimates for mechanisms co-developed with the task set
— which is why the ablation is reported as directional throughout. Of
the four ablated arms, three carry some in-sample exposure (the finish
gate and anti-loop monitor were iterated with LRAB in the loop; the
verification-feedback hint predates LRAB but was strengthened during the
LRAB period), and only the flat prefill is both pre-LRAB and, by its
near-zero delta, inert here; the external $`\tau^{2}`$-bench comparison
— same code, no benchmark-provided tests for the gate to run — is the
out-of-sample anchor for the mechanisms the ablation cannot cleanly
measure.

## 6 Threats to Validity

##### Self-built benchmark.

LRAB’s tasks and rubrics were authored by the same people who built
Mingbird. We cannot rule out that the task set favors our harness’s
mechanisms; indeed, it was designed to exercise the failure forms in
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Mechanisms ‣ 3 The Mingbird Harness ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks"),
which is the point of a controlled experiment but also its bias.
Mitigations include the full publication of tasks, fixtures, scoring
code, and raw cells, in the spirit of reproducible agent evaluation
\[[59](#bib.bib59)\]; the explicit framing as a controlled experiment
rather than a leaderboard; and the external $`\tau^{2}`$-bench
replication, whose benchmark, tasks, and scorer are third-party. The two
legs agree in direction.

##### External-benchmark coverage.

$`\tau^{2}`$-bench is our only three-arm external comparison (a fourth
CLI arm, goose, is deferred for throughput), and it comes from one
family (one lab, three domains). This is not for lack of searching; it
reflects a structural constraint. A harness comparison requires the
_harness_ to be the experimental variable, which excludes every suite
that supplies its own agent loop, and it additionally requires the suite
to discriminate at local-model scale; dynamic GUI-device environments
such as AndroidWorld \[[60](#bib.bib60)\] add device emulation to that
list and were not candidates for the same reason. We examined five
external candidates against these two filters and excluded each for
cause: AppWorld \[[61](#bib.bib61)\] (all-zero at every local model tier
we ran), ToolSandbox \[[62](#bib.bib62)\] and AgentBoard (the benchmark
provides the agent loop; only the model varies), function-calling
emission suites (model-side emission with no environment and no
multi-step loop; e.g. JSON-schema emission benchmarks
\[[50](#bib.bib50)\]), CRMArena \[[63](#bib.bib63)\] (we built a local
backend against the official data snapshot and probed it: gemma4:e2b
0/5, qwen3.5:4b 0/5, and the 35B tier at 40+ minutes per task with the
first completed task scored 0 — no discrimination at usable throughput),
and GAIA (no discrimination for local models in our runs). We report
this filter explicitly so that the single-family external evidence can
be weighed for what it is; the cross-benchmark agreement between LRAB
and $`\tau^{2}`$ on the shared harnesses is the strongest external check
available under the constraint.

##### Development–evaluation overlap.

At least one mechanism (the anti-loop gate, M4) was developed against a
task that also appears in LRAB (WF-08): its before/after evidence is
that task. More broadly,
Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
discloses that three of the four ablated arms carry some in-sample
exposure (finish gate, anti-loop monitor, LRAB-period strengthening of
verification feedback); only the flat prefill is both pre-LRAB and inert
here. There is no held-out task split and no temporal separation between
mechanism development and evaluation, so test-set overfitting cannot be
ruled out. We mitigate by publishing every cell so the overlap is
visible, and by the external $`\tau^{2}`$ results, which were produced
after the mechanisms were frozen; a held-out task split remains future
work.

##### Single machine and platform.

All 288 cells ran on one Windows 11 machine (an integrated GPU, 32 GB
shared memory). We do not know how the ranking transfers to Linux, to
discrete GPUs, or to different memory envelopes; agent-mini’s 35B
collapse is specifically a Windows interaction and would presumably not
reproduce on POSIX shells. Mingbird itself is Windows-first, which has
two consequences: the harness matches the benchmark platform exactly,
and it is untested elsewhere.

##### Campaign ordering (temporal confounding).

The three baseline arms ran 2026-09-01..04 and were frozen at their
stock versions; the Mingbird arm ran 2026-09-13..14 with the final
v1.5.0 code; the goose and opencode arms were re-shot 2026-09-18..20
under the unified protocol. Execution order was blocked, not randomized,
so machine state, driver stack, and background load could in principle
have drifted between campaigns. Model weights are pinned by manifest
digest, which controls the model but not the runtime stack. Two facts
partially compensate: the goose/opencode re-shots cross the original
campaign boundary and reproduce the direction of the baseline campaign’s
results, and every cell is published, making anomalies auditable. A
fully interleaved replication (round-robin task order across all four
arms) remains the strongest check and is future work.

##### Model set.

Four models, one per size point, all open-weight and served by one
backend. Two further small-tier candidates were pulled and piloted for a
second-family check and rejected _before_ entering the matrix, on a
criterion set in advance: a candidate must form schema-valid tool calls.
minicpm5-2b and llama3.2:3b both fail that criterion in the same way —
they emit argument keys the tools do not define (e.g. {"fn": …} where
path is required), repeat the malformed call after the harness’s
corrective message and worked example, and end with no artifacts (the
first made 175 rejected calls across 449 turns; the second scored 0.00,
0.06, 0.31 and 0.00 on its first four tasks, all without a single
schema-valid write). Their transcripts are archived with the data
release. This is a model-side floor that none of the harness
compensations we document repairs, and it is reported as such rather
than as a harness result. Our “2B” label in
Table [5](#S5.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 5.1 The 288-cell matrix ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
follows the Ollama tag naming: gemma4:e2b is a 5B-total /
2.3B-active-per-token (MatFormer) model, as stated in
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.3 Protocol ‣ 4 LRAB: A Controlled Benchmark, Not a Leaderboard ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks").
The second small-tier family of
Section [5](#S5 "5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
(qwen3.5:2b) is a dense 2.3B model run through the same 18-task
protocol, so the two low-tier results together cover a MatFormer and a
dense model at that parameter point. The matrix includes no proprietary
model; the frontier probe of
Section [5.5](#S5.SS5 "5.5 Model ceiling probe: one frontier model, four harnesses ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
covers one hosted model, but with a single trial per arm and a cloud
endpoint that is not a frozen snapshot, so harness effects at that scale
remain indicative rather than established.

##### Scoring subjectivity residuals.

Scoring is deterministic and artifact-based, but the
artifacts-and-checks rubrics were authored by us. Determinism removes
run-to-run judgment variance, not design-time rubric bias. Partial
credit weights (e.g. a cell scoring $`1/7`$ or $`4/7`$) reflect our
judgment of task structure.

##### Single trial under greedy decoding.

Every cell is a single run (one retry, latest attempt wins), with
temperature pinned to 0 for every arm. Greedy decoding removes sampling
randomness at the source, but it does not remove execution-level
variance: the same-night full-mechanism re-run of
Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
(identical code, model, budgets, and scoring) moved long-horizon cells
by $`0.5`$–$`0.7`$ and workflow cells by up to $`0.43`$ against the
matrix batch, and across three repeated same-configuration trials the
arm’s total mean ranged over $`0.069`$ with per-task long-horizon swings
reaching $`0.75`$. Long-horizon magnitudes in
Table [5](#S5.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 5.1 The 288-cell matrix ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
— including Mingbird’s LH-segment lead — are therefore batch-fragile and
should be read as one draw per cell; the all-task and workflow-segment
margins are the stable quantities. The task-level paired tests and
bootstrap intervals of
Section [5](#S5 "5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
are the inference this design supports, the ablation results stay
directional (their intervals reflect task variance, not sampling), and
the smaller segment margins (e.g. 12B: 0.906 vs. 0.772) deserve
replication.

##### Cloud user simulator.

The $`\tau^{2}`$ comparison uses a cloud simulator (qwen3.8-flash)
instead of the official gpt-4o user, so absolute scores are not
comparable to the leaderboard; only the between-arm difference is
controlled. The judge (qwen3.8-max) is likewise a cloud model. Both are
accessed by alias through one endpoint, and a cloud alias is not a
frozen snapshot; all arms were sampled in the same September 2026
window, but an independent replication today could meet a drifted
simulator. Simulator behavior could also interact with harness style
(e.g. tolerance for terse agent replies).

##### Ablation coverage.

Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
removes mechanisms one at a time, but only on a single 2B model over 18
tasks per arm, against a same-code control baseline, and its task-level
confidence intervals all cross zero. It does not cover 4B and larger
models or the multi-model matrix, and with one mechanism disabled at a
time it says nothing about interactions between mechanisms. Marginal
contributions at other model scales remain unmeasured.

##### Safety evaluation scope.

The five-ring evaluation
(Section [3.2.11](#S3.SS2.SSS11 "3.2.11 M14: Five-ring safety model (v1.6.0) ‣ 3.2 Mechanisms ‣ 3 The Mingbird Harness ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks"))
is one run per case over 54 probes and two models, on a probe set we
wrote ourselves: it establishes that the rings fire on realistic
requests and characterizes what leaks through, but the per-ring rates
carry single-run noise, the probe set has no external red-team
provenance, and both confirmed leaks are bash-side rule gaps documented
as findings rather than silently patched in the evaluated tree. The
false-completion-claim phenomenon it exposes is a model-level integrity
problem that no host-side gate in the evaluated version closes; we
report its count and the per-case evidence notes rather than claim a
fix.

## 7 Discussion and Future Work

##### What the results suggest.

For small models, the harness is not peripheral glue but a substantial
part of the capability surface. The 2B segment shows this most clearly:
same model, same budgets, and one harness completes 0.821 of its cells
while others complete 0.017–0.271. The failure forms behind that gap
(prefill overflow, unbounded demonstration loops, silent abandonment,
malformed calls discarded by strict parsers) are deterministic
engineering problems, and none of them requires model retraining. The
frontier probe sharpens the attribution from the other direction
(Section [5.5](#S5.SS5 "5.5 Model ceiling probe: one frontier model, four harnesses ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")):
with the model pinned near its ceiling, harness choice alone spans
0.478–0.997 on the same tasks, so a weak scaffold strands capability
that a stronger one recovers. The ablation puts directional numbers on
this attribution at 2B: the largest single loss comes from removing the
finish gate
(Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).
We expect several mechanisms (flat prefill with a hard budget, finish
gates, loop signatures) to transfer to larger models with weaker
budgets, though our data cover only up to the 35B tier of
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.3 Protocol ‣ 4 LRAB: A Controlled Benchmark, Not a Leaderboard ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks").

##### Beyond Windows.

Mingbird is Windows-first; porting the harness (notably M7’s encoding
layer and M8’s path normalization) to macOS and Linux is planned. The
agent-mini result shows platform fit can dominate model capability in
this size class, which elevates cross-platform measurement beyond
routine porting work.

##### A human baseline for LRAB.

The natural missing baseline in LRAB is human performance: participants
completing the same 18 tasks under identical budgets. A careful human
baseline (a small number of participants, task order randomized,
artifacts scored by the same deterministic scorer where applicable)
would calibrate what 0.886 means; we consider this the most valuable
single addition to the benchmark.

##### Scaling the ablation.

The leave-one-mechanism-out study of
Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
covers one model at 2B and 18 tasks per arm. Extending it across the
full matrix (every mechanism at every model size) would turn the design
narrative of
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Mechanisms ‣ 3 The Mingbird Harness ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")
into complete marginal-contribution estimates. The published matrix and
open harness make this feasible for us or for others.

## 8 Conclusion

We presented Mingbird, a local-first agent harness for Windows and
Ollama that treats small-model failure forms as first-class engineering
targets. The evaluation is a fully published controlled experiment: 288
cells, four harnesses, four open models (2B–35B), one machine, and
deterministic artifact-based scoring. Mingbird reaches 0.886 overall
against 0.631, 0.479, and 0.405 for three stock baselines, is the only
harness without a small-model cliff, and leads the long-horizon segment
at 0.827 (single draw per cell). An external replication on
$`\tau^{2}`$-bench in all three domains (0.856 vs. 0.791 and 0.737 over
278 tasks, same local 4B model, matched user simulator, with the fourth
CLI arm deferred for throughput on this benchmark) supports the
direction on a third-party benchmark, and a frontier-model probe — one
hosted model, four harnesses, the same 18 tasks — spans 0.997 to 0.478,
showing that a defective scaffold buries more than half of a frontier
model while well-formed scaffolds stay within 0.072–0.12 of each other.
A leave-one-mechanism-out ablation attributes the largest single share
of the margin to the finish gate, and the thinking configuration itself
emerges as a harness-level lever with task-dependent costs. LRAB is
self-built and carries its authors’ bias; we publish every cell so that
the results can be independently recomputed or refuted. Our results
suggest that a substantial share of what is attributed to model
incapacity in this setting is in fact scaffold defect, and such defects
are addressable.

## Appendix A Case studies: the mechanisms at work

The aggregate tables compress 288 runs into means; this appendix shows
three exchanges, verbatim from the published transcripts, where a
mechanism visibly changes what happened. (Transcripts log both the
Chinese harness messages and the model’s English output; we gloss the
markers.)

##### C1: format-leak salvage (WF-01, 2B, turn 20).

The model completed the literature-review task and tried to finish, but
emitted the finish call as plain text instead of a structured call. The
parser recognized the call’s structure inside the prose, salvaged it
into a real invocation, and the run ended as a scored completion:

> \[20\] lightning-mark: salvaged plain-text tool call: finish  
> \[…\] all requirements satisfied, task complete.  
> \[20\] finish {‘‘summary’’: ‘‘Completed the mini literature review
> …’’}  
> ===== TASK COMPLETE =====

Without salvage, this turn would have been an empty round: the same
cell’s score depends on the artifacts already written, but the
completion claim itself would have been lost in prose.

##### C2: the finish gate (WF-01, 12B, turns 17–18).

The model declared completion after checking off its plan. The gate
re-injected the original task text (1,648 characters) and required a
self-check before accepting the finish. The model then re-finished with
the concrete deliverables named:

> \[17\] finish {‘‘summary’’: ‘‘Completed the mini literature review
> …’’}  
> \[17\] warn: delivery self-check --- task text re-injected, re-finish
> required  
> \[18\] finish {summary naming shortlist.md, data_table.md,
> contradictions.md, the seven selected papers, and the documented data
> discrepancies}

The ablation shows the population-level version of this exchange:
removing the gate costs a nominal $`0.098`$ overall, the largest
single-mechanism delta in the directional ablation
(Section [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Ablation: leave-one-mechanism-out at 2B ‣ 5 Results ‣ Mingbird: A Local-First Agent Harness EnablingSmall Open Models to Complete Real Tasks")).

##### C3: the anti-loop guard (WF-08, 4B, before/after).

The signature-level guard was developed against this task, where the
stock loop degenerated into 156 identical tool calls with zero artifacts
in the target directory. After the fix — same code, same task, same
model, with the static prefill byte-identical (asserted in CI) — the
same cell runs 15 tool calls and delivers 3 artifacts. The guard’s
interception message tells the model why the repeat cannot succeed and
what to do instead (“read the actual state first, or change strategy”),
converting a silent spin into a redirect. The $`\tau^{2}`$ adapter
carries the same idea (same-signature repeat $`\geq`$ 4 triggers a
corrective nudge).
