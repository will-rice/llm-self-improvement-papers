# SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

###### Abstract

OPSD provides dense supervision for agentic reinforcement learning
through privileged information feedback. However, existing privileged
information often lacks explicit task-progression planning, while
teacher–student log-probability gaps reflect shifts in model preference
rather than true task progress. To address these limitations, we propose
Subgoal-Guided Graph-Regularized OPSD (SGR-OPSD), a framework that
combines subgoal supervision with interaction-derived evidence for
turn-level credit assignment. Specifically, SGR-OPSD uses LLM-generated
subgoal plans to provide dense action-level guidance, converting them
into advantage weights via a constrained, KL-regularized objective. To
ground credit in observed outcomes, we construct a Subgoal Progress
Graph that estimates empirical returns from sampled transition
frequencies. The graph compares empirical action returns against the
state average to derive preferences that calibrate the teacher’s credit
weights. Evaluated on ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA across two model
scales (Qwen2.5-3B and 7B), SGR-OPSD consistently outperforms GRPO and
strong self-distillation baselines—improving success rates over GRPO by
up to 7.4 percentage points and reaching 85.8% on ALFWorld with
Qwen2.5-7B. Ablation studies confirm that subgoal guidance and
graph-based weight calibration are key to these performance gains.

## 1 Introduction

![Refer to caption](2611.02414v1/Figure1_QV_editable.png)

Figure 1: Overview of SGR-OPSD. (a) Subgoal-guided OPSD produces credit
weights, which are jointly calibrated using subgoal progress graph
evidence. (b) The figure shows a real example in which calibration
shifts favorable credit from the sink to the dining table.

Reinforcement learning (RL) enables LLM-based agents to solve complex
tasks through multi-turn interactions, ranging from household
manipulation to online shopping ([Shridhar et al., 2020](#bib.bib2);
[Yao et al., 2022](#bib.bib3)). Effective training requires fine-grained
supervision to evaluate intermediate decisions within a trajectory.
On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) ([Zhao et al., 2026a](#bib.bib11))
provides dense feedback by conditioning a teacher model on privileged
training-time information to score student-sampled actions ([Lv et al.,
2026](#bib.bib12); [Lan et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)). This feedback
complements the sparse outcome rewards used in methods like Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib8)),
enabling the student to internalize inference-unavailable guidance. OPSD
thus offers a promising framework for dense supervision in agentic RL.

Existing methods supply privileged information through
trajectory-derived skills or reference answers, integrating teacher
feedback via advantage reweighting or auxiliary distillation
objectives ([Wang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7); [Yang et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib9); [Lu et al., 2026](#bib.bib10)). AgentOPSD further
accumulates likelihood evidence across turns, while TASPO maps
action-level support into bounded advantage weights ([Wang et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib1); [Yang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib4)). However, two key
gaps remain between such supervision and true task progress. First,
privileged guidance often lacks an explicit, ordered plan for the task
at hand. For “cool bread and place it on the countertop”, a structured
plan—locate the bread, cool it, then place it—makes the required
progression explicit. Second, teacher–student log-probability gaps
measure shifts in model preference rather than progress made through
real interactions; even plan-conditioned feedback can incorrectly favor
actions that stray from successful continuations. Thus, effective credit
assignment requires both task-specific planning information and a
mechanism that grounds its influence in execution evidence. To bridge
these gaps, our approach uses subgoal plans to structure teacher
guidance and uses evidence from the Subgoal Progress Graph to calibrate
the resulting credit.

We propose SGR-OPSD, illustrated in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
An LLM generates a fixed subgoal plan from the task description alone,
providing the scoring teacher with explicit task-progression guidance.
We derive a constrained KL-regularized rule that converts action-level
teacher–student likelihood evidence into bounded exponential weights on
GRPO advantages. The rule accounts for advantage signs and enforces a
fixed token-weighted budget within each trajectory, establishing a
teacher reference for turn-level credit. To calibrate these weights, we
construct a _Subgoal Progress Graph_ from student rollouts and derive
action preferences from empirical $`Q-V`$: each action’s discounted
return relative to the visit-weighted state average. A joint objective
incorporates these preferences while regularizing the allocation toward
the teacher reference, connecting plan-guided supervision with evidence
of task progress. The trained student requires neither the plan nor the
graph at inference. We evaluate SGR-OPSD on ALFWorld, WebShop, and
Search-QA with Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B. On the 7B model, it reaches
85.8% success on ALFWorld and 78.2% on WebShop, improving over GRPO by
7.4 and 7.6 percentage points, respectively.

Our contributions are as follows:

- •
  We propose leveraging subgoal plans as privileged supervision and
  derive a constrained KL-regularized formulation to allocate turn-level
  credit via bounded exponential weights.
- •
  We develop a Subgoal Progress Graph that derives action preferences
  from empirical return estimates to calibrate teacher-reference
  weights, while preserving advantage signs and a fixed token-weighted
  budget per trajectory.
- •
  Extensive experiments across three environments and two model scales
  demonstrate the superiority of SGR-OPSD, outperforming GRPO by up to
  7.6 percentage points and achieving an 85.8% success rate on ALFWorld.

## 2 Related Works

### 2.1 On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLMs

Self-distillation derives the teacher from the student and conditions it
on privileged training-time information [Wang et al.
(2026a)](#bib.bib7); [Wang et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib1). Existing works
integrate diverse privileged signals via distinct mechanisms: Skill-SD
leverages trajectory-derived skills [Wang et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib7),
RLSD scales token updates using log-probability gaps between an
answer-conditioned teacher and the student [Yang et al.
(2026a)](#bib.bib9), and SDAR gates SkillBank guidance alongside RL [Lu
et al. (2026)](#bib.bib10). However, these privileged signals often
contain irrelevant noise and lack explicit task-progress planning. To
address this, SGR-OPSD uses a fixed, LLM-generated subgoal plan derived
solely from the task description as privileged information and applies a
constrained KL-regularized objective to transform action-level evidence
into bounded turn weights.

### 2.2 Credit Assignment for Multi-Turn Agents

While GRPO provides trajectory-level advantages under outcome rewards,
it fails to distinguish the contributions of individual turns ([Shao et
al., 2024](#bib.bib8)). Recent lines of work refine this coarse outcome
signal: AgentOPSD accumulates privileged likelihood evidence via
recursive belief updates ([Wang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib1)), TASPO
converts action-level support into bounded advantage weights ([Yang et
al., 2026b](#bib.bib4)), and AlignOPSD aligns sibling contexts to
allocate credit across spans and turns ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)).
Although such teacher likelihood preferences offer dense feedback, they
lack explicit measurements of task progress. To bridge this gap,
SGR-OPSD constructs a Subgoal Progress Graph from student rollouts,
leveraging graph-derived action comparisons to calibrate turn-level
credit from a subgoal-conditioned teacher.

### 3.1 Background

We consider multi-turn tasks where an agent interacts with an
environment to complete an instruction $`x`$. Given a task $`x`$, the
rollout policy $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ samples $`K`$
trajectories $`\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}`$. Each trajectory $`\tau_{i}`$
consists of $`N_{i}`$ turns indexed by $`t`$, with $`k`$ indexing
individual tokens within a response. At turn $`t`$, given context
$`h_{i,t}^{S}`$, the agent generates a response $`y_{i,t}`$ containing
action $`a_{i,t}`$ and receives the next observation.

Outcome-based policy optimization assigns each turn a base advantage
$`A_{i,t}`$, where positive values reinforce the sampled turn and
negative values discourage it. Self-distillation supplements this sparse
outcome feedback by re-scoring student-sampled trajectories using
privileged information. Both scoring branches share the pre-update
parameters $`\theta_{\mathrm{old}}`$, so their likelihood difference
reflects the effect of privileged information.

### 3.2 Subgoal-Guided Credit Weights

Subgoal-conditioned supervision. Subgoal plans make the intermediate
requirements of task completion explicit. We employ a fixed, ordered
plan $`z(x)`$, generated by DeepSeek-V3.1 ([DeepSeek-AI,
2024](#bib.bib14)) solely from the task instruction $`x`$, as privileged
information. $`z(x)`$ combines task-specific intermediate goals and
ordering constraints without requiring prior task interactions or
successful trajectories
(Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Privileged Information and Subgoal Generation ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(b)
illustrates how this plan conditions teacher scoring to yield
subgoal-guided teacher–student log-likelihood gaps.

Specifically, the teacher branch receives the privileged context
$`h_{i,t}^{T}`$ augmented with $`z(x)`$, whereas the student operates on
the standard context $`h_{i,t}^{S}`$. Token probabilities for branch
$`B\in\{T,S\}`$ are defined as
$`\pi^{B}_{i,t,k}=\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(y_{i,t,k}\mid h_{i,t}^{B},y_{i,t,<k})`$,
with both branches retaining identical response prefixes $`y_{i,t,<k}`$.
Summing the token-level log-probability differences over the token
positions $`\mathcal{A}_{i,t}`$ of the executed action yields the
action-level gap:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
d_{i,t}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_{i,t}}\bigl(\log\pi^{T}_{i,t,k}-\log\pi^{S}_{i,t,k}\bigr).
``` |  | (1) |

The gap $`d_{i,t}`$ measures how the plan changes the action’s
log-likelihood, providing dense action-level evidence for credit
assignment.

Constrained KL weighting. Subgoal support should strengthen positive
updates and soften negative ones. We derive turn weights by balancing
signed teacher evidence against KL regularization toward unit weights:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
w^{\mathrm{ref}}=\arg\max_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\bigl\{\mathcal{U}(w)-TD_{p}(w\|\mathbf{1})\bigr\},
``` |  | (2) |

where $`w=(w_{i,t})_{i,t}`$ collects the turn weights and
$`w^{\mathrm{ref}}`$ is their optimized teacher reference. The feasible
set $`\mathcal{W}`$ bounds each weight by
$`1-\epsilon\leq w_{i,t}\leq 1+\epsilon`$ ($`0\leq\epsilon<1`$), fixes
$`w_{i,t}=1`$ when $`A_{i,t}=0`$, and imposes a token-weighted budget
for each trajectory:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{t=1}^{N_{i}}p_{i,t}w_{i,t}=1,\qquad p_{i,t}=\frac{m_{i,t}}{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{i}}m_{i,r}},
``` |  | (3) |

where $`m_{i,t}`$ counts effective response tokens in turn $`t`$. Thus,
$`p_{i,t}`$ weights turns by their token share, and the budget
redistributes emphasis while keeping the weighted mean at one. Within
this budget, the signed teacher-support term is

|     |                                                        |     |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math

Equation [1](#S3.E1 "In 3.2 Subgoal-Guided Credit Weights ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")

### 3.3 Subgoal Progress Graph

To complement teacher preferences with outcome evidence, we merge
matched states across sampled rollouts into a *Subgoal Progress Graph*.
Transition counts define empirical values:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{Q}(v,a)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[r+\gamma I\widehat{V}(v^{\prime})\mid v,a],`$ |  | (7) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{V}(v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\widehat{\pi}(a\mid v)\widehat{Q}(v,a).`$ |  |  |

Here $`v,v^{\prime}`$ are current and successor nodes, $`r\in\{0,1\}`$
is the success reward, and $`\gamma\in(0,1)`$ discounts continuation.
The expectation averages recorded transitions;
$`\widehat{\pi}(a\mid v)`$ is the fraction of visits to $`v`$ taking
action $`a`$. The indicator $`I`$ permits continuation only for
nonterminal transitions with remaining budget and a successor recorded
as a source node.

The relative graph signal is

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\delta_{i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\widehat{Q}(v_{i,t},a_{i,t})-\widehat{V}(v_{i,t}),`$ |  | (8) |
|  | $`\displaystyle g_{i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{clip}(\delta_{i,t},-b_{G},b_{G}).`$ |  |  |

Here $`v_{i,t}`$ is the pre-action node, $`b_{G}>0`$ bounds the signal,
and positive $`\delta_{i,t}`$ indicates above-average empirical return.

Graph-derived action comparisons. The Subgoal Progress Graph determines
action ordering; calibration translates these preferences into bounded
weight adjustments. Beyond node matching for value estimation,
comparisons require matching plans, student contexts, and remaining
budgets, with all associated visits scoreable and having nonzero
advantages:

These constraints preserve the token-weighted budget and weight bounds,
with zero-advantage turns unchanged. Graph comparisons therefore
redistribute emphasis within each trajectory; trajectories without
eligible comparisons retain their teacher reference exactly.

### 3.4 Policy Optimization

The calibrated weights redistribute outcome-based credit through

|     |                                         |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \widetilde{A}_{i,t}=A_{i,t}w_{i,t}^{*}.

## 4 Experiments

We evaluate SGR-OPSD across three interactive environments and two model
scales to examine whether subgoal-guided, graph-calibrated credit
improves policy learning.

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Benchmarks and metrics. For ALFWorld ([Shridhar et al.,
2020](#bib.bib2)), we evaluate success on the 140-task seen split across
six household task categories: Pick, Look, Clean, Heat, Cool, and Pick2.
For WebShop ([Yao et al., 2022](#bib.bib3)), we evaluate product search
and selection on 500 goals, reporting task score and full-task success
rate. SearchQA follows Search-R1 ([Jin et al., 2025](#bib.bib16)); we
report exact-match accuracy on 51,713 questions across NQ, TriviaQA,
PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle. Together,
these environments test sequential state changes, constraint
satisfaction, and evidence gathering.

Baselines. We compare with Vanilla and Skill-Prompt as training-free
references, GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib8)) as the outcome-based
learner, and OPSD ([Zhao et al., 2026b](#bib.bib15)), GRPO+OPSD,
Skill-SD ([Wang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7)), RLSD ([Yang et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib9)), SDAR ([Lu et al., 2026](#bib.bib10)), and
AgentOPSD ([Wang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib1)) as self-distillation
baselines. Qwen3.5-Plus and DeepSeek-V3.1 provide additional
closed-source model references.

Training configuration. We use the Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbones and carry out three independent training
runs with seeds $`\{0,42,2026\}`$. We train SGR-OPSD on 8 NVIDIA A100
GPUs for 150 policy updates, with eight rollouts per task and a learning
rate of $`10^{-6}`$. The maximum interaction lengths are 50, 15, and 4
turns for ALFWorld, WebShop, and SearchQA, respectively. We set
$`\epsilon=0.1`$, $`T=T_{G}=1`$, $`\beta=2`$, $`\mu=0.02`$, and
$`\gamma=0.1`$. We provide subgoal-generation prompts in
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Privileged Information and Subgoal Generation ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
and environment-specific training and evaluation settings in
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Experimental Details ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

### 4.2 Main Results

Stronger multi-turn task completion. SGR-OPSD leverages task-specific
subgoals and Subgoal Progress Graph to calibrate turn-level credit
redistribution. As shown in
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.2 Subgoal-Guided Credit Weights ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),
this method consistently improves task completion across both model
scales, achieving the highest ALFWorld macro-average and top performance
on both WebShop metrics among all baselines. At 7B scale, it achieves
85.6% success on ALFWorld and 78.2% on WebShop. The performance gain
over GRPO holds across both scales, with improvements of 7.4–7.6 pp on
ALFWorld and 5.7–7.6 pp on WebShop success, underscoring the benefit of
augmenting outcome feedback with structured credit allocation.

Gains beyond existing Self-distillation methods. The advantage persists
against GRPO+OPSD and the Skill-guided methods Skill-SD, SDAR, and
AgentOPSD on both evaluated environments at each model scale. Relative
to the strongest baseline for each backbone, SGR-OPSD improves the
ALFWorld macro-average by 2.0–5.3 pp and WebShop success by 4.1–5.5 pp
Its leading 7B results on Cool and Pick2 sub-tasks provide concrete
examples: these tasks require completing an object-state change or
collecting two distinct objects before final placement. The results
support the full method’s effectiveness on tasks whose success depends
on satisfying intermediate requirements.

Generalizability to retrieval-based reasoning. SearchQA tests the method
in a different action space, where progress depends on retrieving
evidence and resolving information dependencies. SGR-OPSD improves
macro-average accuracy over GRPO by 1.2 and 2.1 points at 3B and 7B,
respectively. It achieves the highest macro-average at both scales,
exceeding the strongest baseline by 1.2 points at 7B, with the best 7B
results on TriviaQA and MuSiQue. These gains show that the approach also
benefits retrieval-based reasoning beyond environment manipulation.

### 4.4 Subgoal Guidance Analysis

Task-specific requirements. Subgoal plans instantiate workflow rules
with the task’s objects, destination, and required states.
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.4 Subgoal Guidance Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
illustrates this distinction for cleaning a soapbar and placing it on a
countertop. The Skill rule specifies a general clean-task workflow; the
Subgoal plan names the soapbar and countertop and makes placement
conditional on the soapbar being clean and held. The contrast is
concrete: the Skill rule refers to a “target object” and “final
location,” while the plan specifies which object must reach which
destination and in what state. These explicit completion conditions give
teacher scoring a task-specific reference for assessing sampled actions.
Through the teacher–student likelihood gap, the plan supplies credit
evidence tied to the instruction’s intermediate requirements.

Using a fixed Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct checkpoint, we test one pair per
ALFWorld task with 32 continuations per action from matched states,
contexts, and budgets. Mean discounted returns determine the reference
ranking; the graph and pairs are frozen before sampling these
independent continuations. Graph-preferred actions also achieve 2.8 pp
higher continuation success.

Credit calibration analysis. Calibration translates this progress
evidence into better credit allocation

## 5 Conclusion

We introduce SGR-OPSD, which combines subgoal-conditioned teacher
evidence with calibration by the Subgoal Progress Graph to allocate
credit across agent turns. It improves over GRPO at both 3B and 7B,
reaching 85.6% ALFWorld macro-average success and 78.2% WebShop success
at 7B, supporting structured credit assignment.

## Limitations

Evaluation scope. We evaluate SGR-OPSD on three benchmarks using
Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct. Its applicability to other model families and
open-ended environments requires further evaluation, particularly when
observations and action spaces differ from those studied here.

Fixed subgoal guidance. Subgoal plans are generated from task
instructions before interaction and remain fixed across turns. They may
omit requirements or fail to accommodate new information revealed by the
environment. Updating plans from observed feedback is a natural
extension of the current framework.

Rollout-dependent graph evidence. The Subgoal Progress Graph estimates
empirical action values from recorded transitions and outcomes;
comparison coverage depends on exploration and the availability of
matched action visits. When eligible comparisons are absent, the method
retains the teacher reference. Graph scores summarize progress within
sampled rollouts rather than establish true action values; broader
exploration and more reliable state matching are directions for
extending this evidence.

### A.2 DeepSeek-V3.1 Generation Prompts

We query DeepSeek-V3.1 ([DeepSeek-AI, 2024](#bib.bib14)) through a
chat-completion interface with temperature 0 and a maximum output of
1024 tokens. Each initial request contains the environment-specific
system prompt reproduced below and a user message containing only the
task instruction $`x`$ (the question text for Search-QA). Outputs that
fail validation are retried with the previous response and validation
feedback. The deployment model identifier is deepseek-v31-meituan. Plans
are cached by task and generation configuration and reused unchanged;
ALFWorld and WebShop generate them before the first rollout for a new
instruction, while Search-QA precomputes the scheduled training
questions. The external model generates guidance, and the rollout policy
supplies both likelihood scores.

The response is a JSON object containing 2–8 subgoals, each with
nonempty goal and precondition fields. The teacher receives the numbered
rendering j. \<goal\> Preconditions: \<precondition\>. The complete plan
is supplied at each turn, without stage-dependent filtering, and is
absent from the student’s acting context and evaluation prompts.

## Appendix B Implementation Details of Credit Allocation

This appendix specifies the scoring, weighting, and graph conventions
used by the allocation in
Section [3](#S3 "3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

### B.1 Scoring Context and Action Tokens

The teacher prompt prepends the cached task plan to the student prompt
and is left-truncated to the environment-specific context limit.
Consequently, $`h_{i,t}^{T}`$ and $`h_{i,t}^{S}`$ can retain different
parts of the interaction context. Both branches use the same pre-update
actor and score the original sampled response IDs. Action-token
probabilities remain conditioned on earlier reasoning tokens;
Equation [1](#S3.E1 "In 3.2 Subgoal-Guided Credit Weights ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
is not a probability marginalized over reasoning.

The action mask selects original response tokens that overlap the
trimmed executable-action span, including tokens crossing its byte
boundaries. The span is not retokenized. A turn without a scoreable
action receives $`d_{i,t}=0`$ and is excluded from graph comparisons.
Its outcome penalty and full-response policy update remain active. Such
a turn still participates in its trajectory’s allocation budget, so its
multiplier need not remain one.

### B.2 Training Mass and Unique Visits

Training batches can contain padding copies of an environment turn. The
allocator represents each unique turn once, after checking agreement of
its copies’ evidence and base advantages. Its mass $`m_{i,t}`$ is the
number of effective response tokens summed across all training copies.
These copies contribute to the policy loss and hence to $`p_{i,t}`$, but
do not create additional graph visits. The allocated advantage is
broadcast back to all copies.

Base advantages retain the reward normalization and invalid-action
penalties of the training recipe in
Section [4.1](#S4.SS1 "4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
The allocator does not replace them by a common trajectory advantage.
Accordingly, positive, negative, and zero advantages can occur within
one trajectory. The multiplier budget
$`\sum_{t=1}^{N_{i}}p_{i,t}w_{i,t}=1`$ does not imply conservation of
the sum of advantages or the optimizer’s gradient norm.

### B.3 Graph Construction and Pair Eligibility

State and action identities. Each task’s group of eight student rollouts
forms a separate Subgoal Progress Graph at each update. Recorded visits
contribute transition counts once, excluding training padding copies.
ALFWorld node keys combine observation-memory anchors with
physical-state signatures hashing simulator facts and entity metadata.
WebShop keys combine formatted observations with product identity, page,
page name, and selected options. These descriptors support graph
estimation without entering teacher or student prompts. Within a node,
actions are grouped by their exact executed strings.

SearchQA states and actions. The graph ID hashes the update,
rollout-group ID, and question ID. A node represents the exact formatted
student context: the question, interaction count, and ordered executed
queries with their returned evidence. The four-turn history window
covers preceding interactions within the four-turn budget. Nodes merge
only when their context strings match within the graph. Different query
histories remain separate even if they retrieve the same passage. The
pre-action state signature hashes the question ID and context; the
student-prompt hash checks the scoring input after prompt processing.

Actions use the projected executable string: \<search\>query\</search\>
or \<answer\>answer\</answer\>. Projection removes surrounding
reasoning, standardizes the wrapper, and trims payload-boundary
whitespace without semantically normalizing queries or answers. Gold
answers and reward labels are excluded from node keys and state
signatures.

Empirical graph values. Let $`N(v,a)`$ count recorded visits taking
action $`a`$ at node $`v`$, and $`N(v)=\sum_{a}N(v,a)`$.
Equation [7](#S3.E7 "In 3.3 Subgoal Progress Graph ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
uses $`\widehat{\pi}(a\mid v)=N(v,a)/N(v)`$: $`\widehat{Q}`$ averages
immediate rewards and discounted successor values over recorded
transitions, and $`\widehat{V}`$ averages action values by visit
frequency. Their difference measures an action’s empirical return
relative to the state average.

For ALFWorld and WebShop, the recorded binary success reward (0 or 10)
is divided by 10 before value estimation; SearchQA uses binary terminal
exact-match reward directly. Thus all three environments use
$`r\in\{0,1\}`$ and $`\gamma=0.1`$, with success assigned to the
terminating transition. Terminal transitions, exhausted budgets, and
successors absent from the recorded source-node set have zero
continuation value; immediate rewards are retained. Failed or
budget-truncated episodes remain in the transition counts, with zero
terminal success reward.

Values are initialized to zero and iterated until the maximum absolute
change is below $`10^{-12}`$, with at most 200 iterations; failure to
converge raises an error. Cycles are handled by these discounted
fixed-point updates. Clipping is applied after subtracting
$`\widehat{V}`$ from $`\widehat{Q}`$, as in
Equation [8](#S3.E8 "In 3.3 Subgoal Progress Graph ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning");
there is no subsequent reward rescaling. All visits to a state–action
pair share its empirical signal. The values summarize continuation
behavior pooled by node; the additional context and budget checks below
restrict comparisons, not the value-estimation pool.

Pair eligibility and averaging. Within each node–plan bucket, every
visit associated with the two candidate actions must be scoreable, have
nonzero base advantage, and agree on the pre-action state signature,
student-prompt hash, and remaining step budget. ALFWorld signatures use
simulator facts and entity metadata; WebShop signatures use recorded
session state and available actions. Success reachability is not an
additional eligibility condition. We retain $`(a,b)`$ when
$`\bar{g}(a)>\bar{g}(b)`$, excluding ties. The vector $`H_{a}`$ averages
unique visits equally within each trajectory, then equally across
trajectories. Although identical state–action visits share a graph
score, this averaging also defines their mean credit correction. Graph
scores supply pair ordering, while $`\mu`$ sets the correction margin.
For each eligible pair $`e`$, the comparison weight is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\rho_{e}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_{v}|\,\lVert b_{e}\rVert_{2}^{2}},
``` |  | (16) |

where $`\mathcal{E}_{v}`$ contains the eligible pairs in the same
node–plan bucket. This weight normalizes comparison count and
coefficient scale; it is not a bootstrap reliability estimate.

### B.4 Optimization and Fallback

For a nonzero advantage, differentiating the teacher objective before
clipping gives $`s_{i,t}d_{i,t}-T\log w_{i,t}-\nu_{i}=0`$. The resulting
exponential tilt is clipped to the prescribed interval, and the
trajectory multiplier $`\nu_{i}`$ is found by bisection to satisfy the
budget. Zero-advantage turns retain $`w_{i,t}=1`$. This solves the
bounded allocation directly, rather than clipping weights and
subsequently renormalizing them outside their bounds.

Joint solver. We use float64 Nesterov-accelerated projected gradient
descent, initialized at the teacher correction $`c^{\mathrm{ref}}`$,
with monotone restart. Let $`F(c)`$ denote the joint objective,
$`D=\operatorname{diag}(p)`$ the token-mass metric, and $`\Pi_{D}`$ its
projection onto the bounded, per-trajectory budget constraints. The step
size is $`1/L`$, with

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L=\frac{T_{G}}{1-\epsilon}+\beta\bigl\|M^{\top}\operatorname{diag}(\rho)M\bigr\|_{\infty},
``` |  | (17) |

where $`B`$ has rows $`b_{e}^{\top}`$, $`M=|B|D^{-1/2}`$, and the matrix
infinity norm is the maximum row sum. The momentum coefficient is
$`(\sqrt{L}-\sqrt{m})/(\sqrt{L}+\sqrt{m})`$ with
$`m=T_{G}/(1+\epsilon)`$. An extrapolated step that increases the
objective beyond $`10^{-13}(1+|F(c)|)`$ is replaced by the ordinary
projected-gradient step and momentum is reset. Outside the feasible box,
extrapolation uses the KL term’s quadratic extension matching its value,
gradient, and curvature at the clipped boundary.

We stop when

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L\bigl\|c-\Pi_{D}(c-D^{-1}\nabla F(c)/L)\bigr\|_{\infty}\leq 10^{-8},
``` |  | (18) |

with at most 5,000 iterations. Teacher-budget bisection uses 100
iterations; each metric projection uses 55. A solution must be finite,
have maximum trajectory-budget error at most $`10^{-10}`$ and bound
violation at most $`10^{-12}`$, and increase the objective over the
teacher initialization by at most $`10^{-9}`$. Failure of convergence or
these checks raises an error and stops the update; it does not silently
substitute teacher weights.

No-comparison case. A trajectory with no eligible graph term retains its
teacher reference exactly. Within a trajectory containing graph
comparisons, the budget can also adjust turns that do not participate in
a pair. The soft hinge term encourages pairwise margins but does not
guarantee that every graph comparison is satisfied. The experimental
allocation hyperparameters are reported in
Section [4.1](#S4.SS1 "4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

## Appendix D Progress Verification and Credit Calibration

### D.1 Independent Progress Verification

Agreement with independent outcomes. Preferences from the Subgoal
Progress Graph agree with independent continuation returns on 49/60
matched ALFWorld action pairs (81.7%), compared with 30/60 (50.0%) for
teacher evidence. Graph-preferred actions also achieve higher mean
discounted return and continuation success
(Table [10](#A4.T10 "Table 10 ‣ D.1 Independent Progress Verification ‣ Appendix D Progress Verification and Credit Calibration ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
They succeed in 1,756/1,920 continuations, compared with 1,702/1,920 for
their alternatives. At the task level, success is higher for 22 pairs,
lower for nine, and tied for 29. On the 31 pairs with unequal success
rates, graph preferences agree with the success ordering on 22 pairs
(71.0%), compared with 16 (51.6%) for teacher evidence. Return agreement
evaluates all 60 pairs and captures both completion and its timing.

Independent continuation protocol. The evaluation fixes
Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct at update 150, selected by training-validation
success (82.03%), and constructs graphs from eight rollouts for each of
140 ALFWorld tasks. Candidate actions must be legal and share the exact
simulator-state signature, student-prompt hash, and remaining budget.
The sampling protocol requires an absolute graph-score difference above
$`10^{-8}`$ and positive recorded reliability for at least one source
visit. The latter requires that its state–action pair occur in at least
two trajectories, have nonzero empirical advantage, and appear in at
least 16 of 64 trajectory-bootstrap replicates. Each replicate resamples
the eight trajectories with replacement. These criteria select the
evaluation pool; allocation uses the objective and pair weights defined
in
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Implementation Details of Credit Allocation ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

The pool contains 311 action pairs across 94 tasks. Using selection seed
20261027, we choose one pair uniformly within each task, reserve eight
disjoint tasks for replay checks, and select 60 formal tasks
proportionally across the remaining task categories. The state and
action manifest is frozen before continuation sampling. For each pair,
we restore the recorded state and history, force either action, and
sample 32 continuations per action with the remaining interaction
budget. Sampling uses temperature 1, top-$`p`$ 1, no top-$`k`$
restriction, and at most 512 response tokens per turn. Request seeds
differ between graph construction and continuation evaluation; the
inference-engine initialization seed is 0. The resulting 3,840
continuations are excluded from graph estimation and pair selection.

Outcome metrics. For each action, we average the independent discounted
returns $`\sum_{j\geq 0}\gamma^{j}r_{j}`$, with $`\gamma=0.1`$, binary
terminal success reward, and $`j=0`$ denoting the forced action. Ranking
agreement compares a predicted gap’s sign with the difference between
these two sample means. All 60 empirical return differences exceed the
outcome sign tolerance $`10^{-12}`$. Graph gaps use tolerance
$`10^{-8}`$; teacher and credit gaps use $`10^{-6}`$. Success is also
measured without discounting.

### D.2 Credit Calibration Analysis

Improved outcome alignment. Calibration using Subgoal Progress Graph
preferences increases agreement with independent returns from 8/21
(38.1%) to 16/21 (76.2%) on the same directly eligible pairs, with
Wilson intervals \[20.8%, 59.1%\] and \[54.9%, 89.4%\]
(Table [12](#A4.T12 "Table 12 ‣ D.2 Credit Calibration Analysis ‣ Appendix D Progress Verification and Credit Calibration ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Across all 60 pairs, calibration yields eight additional correct
rankings: nine corrections and one change from correct to incorrect. The
paired gain is 13.3 percentage points over all 60 pairs (95%
task-bootstrap interval \[3.3, 23.3\]). Thirty-five pairs involve
zero-advantage visits and remain tied under both reference and
calibrated credit; four further pairs fall outside the exact
direct-comparison conditions. The table reports correctness and ties for
both the complete set and the directly eligible subset.

Retrospective scoring and allocation. At a fixed checkpoint, we
recompute credit weights from the saved graph-building rollouts of the
60 formal tasks using
Equations [2](#S3.E2 "In 3.2 Subgoal-Guided Credit Weights ‣ 3 Method ‣ SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
````
