# AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation for PEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery

Barbara Su    Fangshuo Liao    Anastasios Kyrillidis
Affiliation: Department of Computer Science Affiliation: Rice University
Email: [{bs82, Fangshuo.Liao, anastasios}@rice.edu](mailto:)

###### Abstract

Fine-tuning large language models with LoRA ([Hu et al.,
2022](#bib.bib12)) requires choosing a rank $`r`$ before training
starts. Existing approaches either extract rank-1 components
sequentially ([Liao et al., 2024](#bib.bib16); [Vandchali et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21)), freezing each component’s error permanently into
every subsequent residual, or optimize the full low-rank factorization
jointly ([Zangrando et al., 2025](#bib.bib38); [Shinwari and Usama,
2025](#bib.bib48)) with guarantees that describe only the joint update,
not individual rank-1 directions. We present AdaPaD (Adaptive Parallel
Deflation), which trains all rank-1 components simultaneously: each
worker refines its component against a deflation target built from the
latest estimates of all predecessors, and as those estimates improve,
the targets improve too. We call this property _self-correction_:
deflation errors converge to zero over rounds rather than persisting as
fixed residuals. On top of this backbone, AdaPaD adds _advance learning_
(private pre-training before activation) and _per-module dynamic rank
discovery_ (importance-based growth until a shared budget is exhausted),
making the rank distribution an output rather than an input. We prove
that every component’s error decays exponentially after a warm-up
period, with a generalization bound that splits into a vanishing
algorithmic term and an irreducible statistical floor. Empirically,
AdaPaD is competitive with adaptive-rank LoRA baselines on GLUE with
DeBERTaV3-base at matched parameter budgets, and competitive with
fixed-rank LoRA on Qwen3-0.6B SQuAD/SQuAD v2 while deploying an adapter
that is on average $`30.7\%`$ smaller.  [
](https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ParallelLinearRegression-C604/README.md)

## 1 Introduction

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is the standard way to adapt
large pretrained models to downstream tasks. Among the many
approaches ([Houlsby et al., 2019](#bib.bib36); [Li and Liang,
2021](#bib.bib35); [Ben Zaken et al., 2022](#bib.bib37)), Low-Rank
Adaptation (LoRA) ([Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib12)) stands out for its
simplicity: for a pretrained weight
$`\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}`$, LoRA freezes $`\mathbf{W}`$ and
trains a low-rank update
$`\Delta\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{\top}`$ with
$`\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}`$,
$`\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}`$ and rank $`r\ll\min(m,d)`$ a
hyperparameter. LoRA is effective in practice, but leaves a fundamental
design choice unresolved: how should $`r`$ be chosen?

In practice $`r`$ is set by hand and applied uniformly to every
layer ([Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib12); [Hayou et al., 2024](#bib.bib34);
[Liu et al., 2024](#bib.bib33)), yet the appropriate rank varies across
modules, tasks, and training stages. Adaptive-rank methods address this
in different ways: AdaLoRA ([Zhang et al., 2023b](#bib.bib28)) prunes
from high rank using SVD importance, IncreLoRA ([Zhang et al.,
2023a](#bib.bib29)) grows from low rank by activating reserve
components, DyLoRA ([Valipour et al., 2023](#bib.bib30)) trains across a
range of ranks, and SoRA ([Ding et al., 2023](#bib.bib31)) learns sparse
gates. Recent theoretical work analyzes the joint
$`\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{\top}`$ update: dEBORA ([Zangrando et al.,
2025](#bib.bib38)) uses bilevel Frank-Wolfe with rank-identifiability
guarantees, and ARD-LoRA ([Shinwari and Usama, 2025](#bib.bib48)) adds
learnable per-head scaling with a stationary-point theorem. Both treat
the full low-rank matrix as one object; we instead decompose the problem
into independent rank-1 subproblems trained in parallel and analyze each
separately, yielding a guarantee joint optimization cannot express:
_self-correction_, where the deflation mismatch in each rank-1 worker
provably vanishes over rounds, so individual rank-1 directions can be
certified correct rather than only their joint sum.

Key insight: parallel deflation with self-correction. In sequential
deflation, errors in early components are permanently frozen and
compound multiplicatively through the spectral gap ([Vandchali et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21)). We remove this dependency by training all rank-1
components _simultaneously_: each is assigned to a dedicated worker
that, at every communication round, rebuilds its deflation target from
the _latest_ predecessor estimates, so early mistakes are progressively
corrected rather than locked in. We call this property _self-correction_
and prove that the deflation mismatch
$`\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\|_{F}`$ vanishes as
$`\ell`$ grows
([Proposition 2](#Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 2 (Self-Correction). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
in contrast to sequential deflation ([Vandchali et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21); [Liao et al., 2024](#bib.bib16)) where the mismatch
is fixed and generically nonzero.

The bilinear setting differs from symmetric eigendecomposition ([Liao et
al., 2025](#bib.bib17)): the asymmetric structure
$`\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$ calls for Wedin perturbation
theory ([Wedin, 1972](#bib.bib22)) and a data-dependent recovery scale.
Two further mechanisms make parallel deflation practical for LoRA:
_advance learning_ (private warm-up of dormant components against
improving targets) and _per-module dynamic rank discovery_
(importance-driven growth under a global budget).

Related work.

_Deflation-based methods._ Hotelling’s deflation ([Hotelling,
1933](#bib.bib11)) is the classical approach for extracting principal
components; [Mackey (2008)](#bib.bib18) extended it to sparse PCA. Error
propagation in inexact sequential deflation was analyzed by [Liao et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib16) and [Vandchali et al. (2025)](#bib.bib21). The
model-parallel perspective was introduced by EigenGame ([Gemp et al.,
2021](#bib.bib9); [Gemp et al., 2022](#bib.bib10)), which casts
top-$`K`$ PCA as a multi-player game. [Liao et al. (2025)](#bib.bib17)
gave the first provable parallel deflation algorithm for PCA; we develop
parallel deflation for the bilinear regression setting, where the
asymmetric structure $`\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$ requires
Wedin-type perturbation analysis and the self-correction guarantee is
tied to a data-dependent recovery scale. ReLoRA ([Lialin et al.,
2024](#bib.bib32)) trains a sequence of low-rank updates that sum to a
high-rank result, which is sequential deflation under a different name;
our approach differs by running all components in parallel with provable
self-correction.

_Low-rank optimization._
Problem ([1](#S2.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
has been attacked via nuclear norm minimization ([Candès and Recht,
2009](#bib.bib8); [Recht et al., 2010](#bib.bib19)), projected gradient
descent ([Jain et al., 2010](#bib.bib13); [Kyrillidis and Cevher,
2014](#bib.bib14)), and factored methods ([Burer and Monteiro,
2003](#bib.bib7); [Tu et al., 2016b](#bib.bib20); [Kyrillidis et al.,
2018](#bib.bib15); [Park et al., 2017](#bib.bib6); [Bhojanapalli et al.,
2016](#bib.bib1); [Park et al., 2018](#bib.bib2); [Kim et al.,
2023](#bib.bib3); [Geyer et al., 2020](#bib.bib5); [Hsieh et al.,
2018](#bib.bib4)). These optimize the full low-rank matrix jointly but
do not decompose into independent rank-1 subproblems. [Kim et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib49) show that fixed-rank LoRA training converges to a
low-rank global minimum or diverges detectably, but this addresses the
fixed-rank setting without rank adaptation.

_Adaptive-rank methods with convergence theory._ dEBORA ([Zangrando et
al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) formulates rank selection as bilevel
optimization: an upper-level Frank-Wolfe on an $`\ell_{1}`$-constrained
simplex selects singular-value scaling factors while a lower-level
solver trains the basis matrices. dEBORA proves
$`\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})`$ convergence of the upper level and, notably,
finite-time rank identification (the algorithm provably discovers which
singular values are zero). As a joint optimization method, dEBORA does
not decompose into rank-1 subproblems and therefore does not face the
deflation error propagation problem; its convergence guarantee applies
to the upper-level simplex variable, not to the bilinear
$`\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{\top}`$ factorization itself.
ARD-LoRA ([Shinwari and Usama, 2025](#bib.bib48)) introduces learnable
per-head scaling factors with $`\mathcal{O}(1/T)`$ convergence to a
stationary point via a standard descent lemma; the guarantee is generic
smooth optimization and does not exploit the bilinear structure. Neither
decomposes into rank-1 subproblems nor exhibits self-correction
([Proposition 2](#Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 2 (Self-Correction). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).

## 2 Background and Problem Setup

Low-Rank Regression and LoRA. We study the linear model
$`\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}+\mathcal{E}`$, where
$`\mathbf{W}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}`$ has rank $`r^{\star}`$,
$`\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}`$ collects input features,
$`\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}`$ is the response, and
$`\mathcal{E}`$ is noise ($`\mathcal{E}=\mathbf{0}`$ in the noiseless
case; $`\mathcal{E}_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\varepsilon^{2})`$ in the
noisy case). The goal is to solve

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\min_{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}^{2}.
``` |  | (1) |

This directly models LoRA: for a pretrained layer with weight
$`\mathbf{W}_{0}`$, taking $`\mathbf{X}`$ as the input activations and
$`\mathbf{Y}=\Delta\mathbf{W}\cdot\mathbf{X}`$ reduces LoRA training
to ([1](#S2.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
with
$`\mathbf{W}^{\star}=\Delta\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{\top}`$.

Sequential vs. Parallel Deflation. *Sequential deflation* extracts
components one at a time: set $`\mathbf{Y}_{1}=\mathbf{Y}`$, compute
$`(\mathbf{a}_{k},\mathbf{b}_{k})=\texttt{Rank1}(\mathbf{Y}_{k},\mathbf{X},T_{k})`$
with budget $`T_{k}`$, then
$`\mathbf{Y}_{k+1}=\mathbf{Y}_{k}-\mathbf{b}_{k}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$.
Two defects: wall-clock time $`\sum_{k}T_{k}`$, and frozen errors that
compound multiplicatively ([Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)).
*Parallel deflation* assigns each component to its own worker and
indexes rounds by $`\ell=1,\ldots,L`$. At round $`\ell`$, worker $`k`$
holds $`(\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell},\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell})`$ and uses the latest
predecessor estimates to recompute
$`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}:=\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$.
The starred quantities
$`(\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star})`$ denote the *ideal*
components of the clean target
([Section 2](#S2 "2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
[Table 1](#S2.T1 "In 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
contrasts the three regimes: the parallel target $`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}`$
improves over rounds rather than being fixed.

| Method | Deflation target for component $`k`$ | Property |
|----|----|----|
| Exact Sequential | $`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}=\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}`$ | Fixed, ideal |
| Inexact Sequential | $`\mathbf{Y}_{k}=\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime}}\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime}}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$ | Fixed, approximate |
| Parallel (Ours) | $`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}=\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$ | Improves over rounds |

Table 1: Deflation targets for component $`k`$ under three deflation
regimes. Exact sequential ([Hotelling, 1933](#bib.bib11); [Mackey,
2008](#bib.bib18)) uses ideal predecessor outputs (unavailable in
practice, since they require ground-truth singular vectors); inexact
sequential ([Liao et al., 2024](#bib.bib16); [Vandchali et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21)) freezes approximate predecessors; parallel deflation
refreshes the target every round with the latest predecessor estimates,
so the target itself converges as $`\ell\to\infty`$.

Ideal Rank-1 Fit and Error Decomposition. Fix $`k\in[r]`$ and round
$`\ell`$. The *ideal rank-1 fit* of $`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}`$ is its best
rank-1 Frobenius approximation,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell},\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell})\in\argmin_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F},
``` |  | (2) |

satisfying
$`\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\sigma_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}`$
when the top triplet is unique. Define the *clean* deflation target
$`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}=\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}`$
(exact sequential, top row of
[Table 1](#S2.T1 "In 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"));
its best rank-1 fit defines the *ideal $`k`$-th component*
$`(\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star})`$ with
$`\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}=\sigma_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top}`$
via Eckart–Young–Mirsky ([Eckart and Young, 1936](#bib.bib46); [Mirsky,
1960](#bib.bib47)). Following the numerical-vs.-mismatch split of ([Liao
et al., 2024](#bib.bib16); [Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21); [Liao
et al., 2025](#bib.bib17)), we decompose worker $`k`$’s error at round
$`\ell`$ into the *numerical error*, *deflation mismatch*, and *total
error*:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle D_{k,\ell}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\left\|\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell}\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F},\quad\quad\text{(Numerical error)}`$ |  | (3) |
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{k,\ell}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\left\|\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F},\quad~~\quad\text{(Deflation mismatch)}`$ |  | (4) |
|  | $`\displaystyle G_{k,\ell}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\left\|\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell}\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}.\quad\quad~~~\text{(Total error)}`$ |  | (5) |

The triangle inequality gives $`G_{k,\ell}\leq D_{k,\ell}+B_{k,\ell}`$:
$`D_{k,\ell}`$ vanishes as the subroutine iterates, $`B_{k,\ell}`$
shrinks as predecessors improve, and our proof shows both vanish as
$`\ell\to\infty`$.

### 2.1 Assumptions

###### Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction).

For each component $`k`$, there exists a *contraction factor*
$`\mathcal{F}_{k}\in(0,1)`$ such that the rank-1 subroutine,
warm-started from $`(\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell-1},\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell-1})`$,
satisfies for all $`\ell\geq k`$:
$`\left\|\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell}\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}\leq\mathcal{F}_{k}\left\|\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell-1}\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell-1}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}`$,
where $`(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell},\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell})`$ is the
ideal rank-1 fit defined
in ([2](#S2.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).

###### Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap).

$`\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}`$ has distinct singular values
$`\sigma_{1}^{\star}>\sigma_{2}^{\star}>\cdots>\sigma_{r}^{\star}>0`$
with gaps
$`\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}:=\min\{\min_{j>k}|\sigma_{k}^{\star}-\sigma_{j}^{\star}|,\sigma_{k}^{\star}\}>0`$.
We normalize $`\sigma_{1}^{\star}=1`$.

###### Assumption 3 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery via Projection).

Parameter $`Q`$ in
[Algorithm 1](#alg1 "In 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
satisfies $`Q\geq 2\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$.

The projection in
[12](#alg1.l12 "In Algorithm 1 ‣ 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
together with the assumption above implies the per-component magnitude
bound $`G_{k,\ell}\leq\mathcal{R}_{k}:=Q+\sigma_{k}^{\star}`$ used
throughout the proofs
([Lemma 7](#Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 7 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
in
[Appendix D](#A4 "Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).

All three assumptions are standard:
[Assumption 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
is the rank-1 subroutine regularity condition ([Liao et al.,
2025](#bib.bib17)), holding for ALS ($`\mathcal{F}_{k}`$ controlled by
the local spectral ratio) and factored GD at small step size ([Jain et
al., 2013](#bib.bib24); [Zheng and Lafferty, 2015](#bib.bib26);
[Kyrillidis et al., 2018](#bib.bib15); [Park et al., 2017](#bib.bib6);
[Bhojanapalli et al., 2016](#bib.bib1); [Park et al., 2018](#bib.bib2);
[Kim et al., 2023](#bib.bib3); [Geyer et al., 2020](#bib.bib5); [Hsieh
et al., 2018](#bib.bib4)) (pseudocode in
[Appendix A](#A1 "Appendix A Rank-1 Subroutines ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"));
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
is the spectral-gap condition for deflation ([Liao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib16); [Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21); [Liao et al.,
2025](#bib.bib17)) ($`\sigma_{1}^{\star}=1`$ is a rescaling);
[Assumption 3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery via Projection). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")

and the resulting magnitude bound

is in the same spirit as bounded-iterate constructions in nonconvex
factor recovery ([Tu et al., 2016a](#bib.bib25); [Bhojanapalli et al.,
2016](#bib.bib1); [Park et al., 2018](#bib.bib2); [Ge et al.,

only needs to hold for $`\ell\geq s_{k}`$ (the warm-up round in

via

Algorithm 1 Parallel Rank-1 Deflation for Low-Rank Regression

Base Algorithm: Parallel Rank-1 Deflation.
[Algorithm 1](#alg1 "In 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
parallelizes inexact sequential deflation ([Liao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib16); [Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21); [Liao et al.,
2025](#bib.bib17)) for the bilinear model; we assume one worker per
component for exposition (when $`P<r`$, components are sharded
round-robin via a single all_gather per round). Three mechanisms drive
convergence: *staggered activation* (worker $`k`$ begins at $`\ell=k`$),
*warm-starting* from $`(\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell-1},\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell-1})`$,
and *deflation with current estimates*. The projection on
[12](#alg1.l12 "In Algorithm 1 ‣ 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
caps each rank-1 estimate’s Frobenius norm at
$`Q\geq\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$, making the bounded-recovery bound
[Lemma 7](#Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 7 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
automatic without affecting fixed points (the ideal estimate has norm
$`\sigma_{k}^{\star}\leq Q`$). The projection in
[12](#alg1.l12 "In Algorithm 1 ‣ 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
prevents the recovered component
$`\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell}\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}`$ from blowing
up while the deflated target $`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}`$ has not yet
tightened around $`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}`$ enough for the rank-1
subroutine to begin contracting. In practice we find that taking
$`Q=\infty`$ (i.e., skipping the projection) suffices. Two extensions,
*advance learning* and *dynamic rank discovery*, follow.

Advance Learning. In
[Algorithm 1](#alg1 "In 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
worker $`k`$ is idle for rounds $`1,\ldots,k{-}1`$. Inspired by
IncreLoRA’s reserve components ([Zhang et al., 2023a](#bib.bib29)), we
use this idle time: before activation, worker $`k`$ privately runs the
rank-1 subroutine against its current deflation target without
broadcasting, accumulating a good initialization. Unlike IncreLoRA’s
reserves, the warming component trains against the *correct* target
$`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}`$, which itself improves over rounds via
self-correction.

Theoretically, advance learning tightens the convergence constant: the
worst-case leading factor $`3\mathcal{R}_{k}`$ in
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
is replaced by $`\mathcal{R}_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\,s_{k}-1}+2)`$ when
pre-activation contraction yields $`D_{k,s_{k}-1}\ll\mathcal{R}_{k}`$.

Dynamic Rank Discovery. Attention and FFN sub-layers need different
capacity, and the right allocation varies with depth and task ([Zhang et
al., 2023b](#bib.bib28); [Zhang et al., 2023a](#bib.bib29); [Ding et
al., 2023](#bib.bib31)), motivating per-module rank assignment. AdaPaD
([Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
maintains a per-module active rank $`r_{i}`$ (initially $`1`$, capped at
$`r_{\max}`$), a shared budget $`B`$, and one private *reserve* per
module via advance learning. Every $`\Delta`$ mini-batches (one *global
round*, after a one-round warm-up), the top-$`h`$ eligible modules
($`r_{i}<r_{\max}`$) promote their reserve to a committed component and
start advance learning on a new reserve; growth stops when
$`\sum_{i}r_{i}\geq B`$.

Importance scoring. We adopt the importance-uncertainty product from
IncreLoRA ([Zhang et al., 2023a](#bib.bib29)): a module deserves
capacity if its parameters are both large in magnitude and still
receiving large gradient updates. With $`\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}`$ the
committed $`(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})`$ entries of module $`i`$, the raw
importance at step $`t`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S_{i}^{(t)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}\|_{2}}}\sum_{p\in\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}}|p|\cdot|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{L}|,
``` |  | (6) |

normalized for comparability across module sizes. Two EMAs
($`\beta_{1}{=}\beta_{2}{=}0.85`$) track signal and volatility, and the
allocation score is their product $`\bar{S}_{i}^{(t)}U_{i}^{(t)}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{S}_{i}^{(t)}=\beta_{1}\bar{S}_{i}^{(t-1)}+(1{-}\beta_{1})S_{i}^{(t)},\qquad U_{i}^{(t)}=\beta_{2}U_{i}^{(t-1)}+(1{-}\beta_{2})|S_{i}^{(t)}-\bar{S}_{i}^{(t)}|.
``` |  | (7) |

1: Model with $`M`$ adapter modules, max rank $`r_{\max}`$, budget

$`Q\geq\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$ (inherited by per-component updates as in

$`i=1,\ldots,M`$

5:   Cache $`\Delta`$ mini-batches
$`\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{\Delta}\}`$

Self-correction

9:      Forward with snapshot prefix $`[0,k)`$ + current component $`k`$

10:      Backward; update $`(\mathbf{a}_{i,k},\mathbf{b}_{i,k})`$

11:    end parfor

12:   end for

This per-module allocation is analogous to IncreLoRA’s incremental

committed components are snapshotted every $`\Delta`$ mini-batches, and

## 4 Theoretical Guarantees

Scope. Our analysis is formulated for the bilinear regression model
$`\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}+\mathcal{E}`$
([Equation 1](#S2.E1 "In 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
the exact structure of a single LoRA adapter layer; assumptions hold for
ALS and factored GD ([Jain et al., 2013](#bib.bib24); [Zheng and
Lafferty, 2015](#bib.bib26)). We treat each layer’s subproblem in
isolation and do not model the end-to-end nonlinear loss, as in
dEBORA ([Zangrando et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) and ARD-LoRA ([Shinwari
and Usama, 2025](#bib.bib48)); the gap to nonlinear practice is bridged
empirically in
[Section 5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 GLUE Classification ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
with NTK ([Jacot et al., 2018](#bib.bib41); [Arora et al.,
2019](#bib.bib42)) or local-linearization ([Lee et al.,
2019](#bib.bib43); [Malladi et al., 2023](#bib.bib44)) extensions
deferred
([Section 6](#S6 "6 Discussion and Conclusion ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
Proofs are in
[Appendix D](#A4 "Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery");
recall $`G_{k,\ell}\leq D_{k,\ell}+B_{k,\ell}`$ from
[Section 2](#S2 "2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").

Main Convergence Result. After a component-dependent warm-up, every
component’s total error decays nearly-linearly (exponential decay
modulated by a mild linear prefactor).

###### Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation).

Under
[Assumptions 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
and [3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery via Projection). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
where $`\mathcal{F}_{k}\in(0,1)`$ is the per-component contraction
factor of
[Assumption 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
and $`\mathcal{R}_{k}>0`$ is the magnitude bound of
[Lemma 7](#Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 7 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
define effective rates $`m_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}`$ and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
m_{k}=\max\Bigl\{\mathcal{F}_{k},\frac{1}{k}+\frac{k-1}{k}m_{k-1}\Bigr\}\quad\text{for }k\geq 2,
``` |  | (8) |

and starting rounds $`s_{1}=1`$ with $`s_{k+1}`$ chosen per-predecessor
(i.e., as a maximum over $`k^{\prime}\leq k`$) so that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
s_{k+1}\;=\;\max_{k^{\prime}\leq k}\,s_{k^{\prime}}\;+\;\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(\dfrac{\log\bigl(k\,C_{k+1}\mathcal{R}_{k^{\prime}}/\mathcal{T}_{k+1}^{\star}\bigr)}{1-m_{k}}\right)\;+\;\dfrac{(k+1)\,m_{k}}{1-m_{k}}
``` |  | (9) |

(full Lambert-$`W`$ form and proof in
[Section D.2](#A4.SS2 "D.2 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),

``` math

``` |  | (10) |

Self-Correction. The defining advantage of parallel over sequential
deflation: errors are transient, not permanent.

###### Proposition 2 (Self-Correction).

Under the conditions of
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
for all $`\ell\geq s_{k}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}\leq 3\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{R}_{k^{\prime}}(\ell-s_{k^{\prime}}+1)m_{k^{\prime}}^{\ell-s_{k^{\prime}}}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0.
``` |  | (11) |

In sequential deflation,
$`\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}`$ is fixed
after step $`k`$ and generically nonzero.

The proof bounds
$`\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}\leq\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}G_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}`$
via
[Lemma 9](#Thmtheorem9 "Lemma 9 (Deflated Target Mismatch). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
and applies
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
to force each $`G_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}\to 0`$
([Appendix D](#A4 "Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
[Table 2](#S4.T2 "In 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
summarizes the differences; see
[Figure 3](#A6.F3 "In F.1 Synthetic Validation ‣ Appendix F Theory-Validation Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").

| Property | Sequential ([Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)) | Parallel (ours) |
|----|----|----|
| Wall-clock time | $`\sum_{k=1}^{r}T_{k}`$ | $`LT`$ (up to $`r\times`$ faster) |
| Deflation mismatch | Fixed, generally nonzero | Vanishes as $`\ell\to\infty`$ |
| Error propagation | Accumulates and persists | Self-corrects over rounds |
| Compute allocation | Front-loaded compute essential | Uniform per-component budget suffices |
| Estimation error | Fixed algorithmic term | Vanishes as $`L\to\infty`$ |

Table 2: Sequential vs. parallel rank-1 deflation. “Front-loaded
compute” means a disproportionate share of iterations $`T_{k}`$ must be
spent on the first few components, since their errors propagate to all
later ones; under parallel deflation a uniform per-component budget
suffices because predecessor errors are corrected over rounds.

Estimation Bounds. The self-correction advantage propagates to weight
estimation.

###### Theorem 3 (Noiseless estimation).

If $`\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}`$ with
$`\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{W}^{\star})=r^{\star}`$ and
$`\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})>0`$, then after $`L`$ rounds:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\star}-\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mathbf{b}_{k,L}\mathbf{a}_{k,L}^{\top}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})}\Bigl(\sum_{k=r+1}^{r^{\star}}\sigma_{k}^{\star}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}G_{k,L}\Bigr),
``` |  | (12) |

where $`\sigma_{k}^{\star}`$ denotes the $`k`$-th singular value of
$`\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}`$.

The second term vanishes as $`L\to\infty`$ by
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery");
in the sequential baseline ([Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)) the
corresponding term is permanently positive.

###### Theorem 4 (Noisy estimation).

Under $`\mathcal{E}_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\varepsilon^{2})`$ with
$`\varepsilon\leq\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{T}_{\min}^{\star}/(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{\log(1/\gamma)}))`$,
with probability $`\geq 1-\gamma`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\star}-\textstyle\sum_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k,L}\mathbf{a}_{k,L}^{\top}\right\|_{F}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\kappa(\mathbf{X})\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_{k>r}\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{W}^{\star})+\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{X})^{-1}\sum_{k}G_{k,L}\Bigr)}}_{\text{algorithmic (vanishes)}}`$ |  | (13) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\underbrace{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{n\log(1/\gamma)}}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})}\Bigl(r+\sqrt{\frac{r}{\mathcal{T}_{\min}^{\star}}}\Bigr)\Bigr)}}_{\text{statistical (irreducible)}}.`$ |  |  |

The algorithmic term vanishes via $`\sum_{k}G_{k,L}\to 0`$
([Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"));
the statistical floor matches the Gaussian-perturbation bound for
deflation-based recovery ([Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21), Thm. 3),
so parallelization adds no new noise penalty (the sequential analogue’s
algorithmic term stays positive; here it decays in $`L`$; floor matches
[Figure 4](#A6.F4 "In F.1 Synthetic Validation ‣ Appendix F Theory-Validation Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
Advance learning tightens the leading constant $`3\mathcal{R}_{k}`$
in ([10](#S4.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
via $`s_{k}-1`$ rounds of private refinement.

## 6 Discussion and Conclusion

AdaPaD addresses two LoRA design questions: parallel training keeps
early rank-1 components from corrupting later ones, and per-module
dynamic rank discovery makes the rank distribution an output of
importance scores rather than a fixed input. We prove nearly-linear
convergence with a self-correcting deflation mismatch and an estimation
bound that splits into a vanishing algorithmic term plus an irreducible
statistical floor. Empirically, AdaPaD achieves the highest GLUE average
against four adaptive-rank baselines
([Section 5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 GLUE Classification ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
produces Qwen3-0.6B SQuAD/SQuAD v2 adapters $`30.7\%`$ smaller than
fixed-rank LoRA at competitive F1/EM
([Section 5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2 Parameter Efficiency on Reading Comprehension ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
and component-parallel sharding delivers up to $`2.66{\times}`$ speedup
on $`4{\times}`$H200
([Section 5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3 Multi-GPU Scaling on Qwen3-0.6B ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).

Limitations and future work. The theory covers the bilinear
model ([1](#S2.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"));
extension to nonlinear LoRA losses is empirical, bounds degrade as
$`\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}\to 0`$, and synchronous
$`\mathcal{O}(r^{2}(d+m))`$ communication can offset the speedup at very
large $`r`$. Validation at $`7`$B+ scale, NTK ([Jacot et al.,
2018](#bib.bib41); [Arora et al., 2019](#bib.bib42)) or
local-linearization ([Lee et al., 2019](#bib.bib43); [Malladi et al.,
2023](#bib.bib44)) extensions, asynchronous communication, and richer
importance scores are future work.

## Appendix B Background Perturbation Results

###### Theorem 5 (Weyl’s Inequality \[[Weyl, 1912](#bib.bib23)\]).

For $`\mathbf{M},\boldsymbol{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}`$, define
$`\tilde{\mathbf{M}}=\mathbf{M}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}`$. Then
$`|\tilde{\sigma}_{i}-\sigma_{i}|\leq\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\|_{2}`$ for
all $`i`$.

###### Theorem 6 (Wedin’s Theorem \[[Wedin, 1972](#bib.bib22)\]).

Let $`\mathbf{M}`$ and
$`\tilde{\mathbf{M}}=\mathbf{M}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}`$ have top singular
triplets $`(\sigma_{1},\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1})`$ and
$`(\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{1},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{1})`$.
If
$`\delta:=\min\{\min_{j\neq 1}|\sigma_{1}-\tilde{\sigma}_{j}|,\sigma_{1}\}>0`$,
then
$`\sin^{2}\angle(\mathbf{u}_{1},\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{1})+\sin^{2}\angle(\mathbf{v}_{1},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{1})\leq(\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{1}\|_{2}^{2})/\delta^{2}`$.

|  | $`\displaystyle G_{k,\ell}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\left\|\mathbf{b}_{k,\ell}\mathbf{a}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}.\quad\quad~~~\text{(Total error)}`$ |  | (16) |

where the ideal fit
$`(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell},\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell})`$ is defined
in ([2](#S2.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"));
and $`(\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star})`$ denotes the

###### Lemma 7 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery).

Under

and the same bound transfers to $`D_{k,\ell}`$ and $`B_{k,\ell}`$.

###### Proof.

By the projection in

###### Lemma 10 (Deflation Mismatch Bound).

Under
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
define $`C_{k}:=3\sigma_{k}^{\star}/\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}+1`$. If
$`\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\|_{2}<\frac{1}{2}\min_{j>k}|\sigma_{k}^{\star}-\sigma_{j}^{\star}|`$,
then
$`B_{k,\ell}\leq C_{k}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}`$.

###### Proof.

The deflation mismatch $`B_{k,\ell}`$ measures the gap between the best
rank-1 fit of the *actual* target $`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}`$ and the ideal
$`k`$-th component of the *clean* target $`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}`$. We
bound this gap by decomposing it into a singular-vector error and a
singular-value error, then control each via the two perturbation results
in
[Appendix B](#A2 "Appendix B Background Perturbation Results ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"):
Wedin’s theorem for the singular vectors and Weyl’s inequality for the
singular values.

*Step 1 (Rank-1 SVD expressions).* By the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem,
ideal sequential deflation removes exactly the first $`k-1`$ singular
components of $`\mathbf{Y}`$, so
$`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}=\sum_{j\geq k}\sigma_{j}^{\star}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{\star\top}`$
has leading singular triplet
$`(\sigma_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star})`$
with gap $`\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}`$ \[[Vandchali et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21), Lemma 1\]. The hypothesis
$`\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\|_{2}<\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}`$
together with
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
(distinct singular values of $`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}`$) ensures that
$`\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}`$ has a unique top singular triplet
$`(\sigma_{1_{k},\ell},\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell},\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell})`$,
so the ideal
fit ([2](#S2.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
is well-defined. By definition of the ideal fit and the ideal component,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\mathbf{b}}_{k,\ell}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\sigma_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top},\qquad\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}=\sigma_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top}.
``` |  | (18) |

*Step 2 (Decomposition into singular-vector and singular-value errors).*
Using ([18](#A4.E18 "Equation 18 ‣ Proof. ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
and the definition of $`B_{k,\ell}`$
in ([4](#S2.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
we add and subtract
$`\sigma_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}`$
and apply the triangle inequality:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{k,\ell}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left\|\sigma_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}-\sigma_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top}\right\|_{F}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left\|(\sigma_{1_{k},\ell}-\sigma_{k}^{\star})\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}+\sigma_{k}^{\star}(\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}-\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top})\right\|_{F}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{|\sigma_{k}^{\star}-\sigma_{1_{k},\ell}|}_{\text{singular-value error}}+\sigma_{k}^{\star}\underbrace{\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top}-\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}\right\|_{F}}_{\text{singular-vector error}},`$ |  | (19) |

using
$`\left\|\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}\right\|_{F}=1`$.
The decomposition is natural because the actual and ideal objects
in ([18](#A4.E18 "Equation 18 ‣ Proof. ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
are each a scalar times a unit-Frobenius rank-1 term, so the discrepancy
decomposes cleanly into a scale mismatch and a direction mismatch.

*Step 3 (Singular-value error via Weyl).* By
[Theorem 5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Weyl’s Inequality [ , ]). ‣ Appendix B Background Perturbation Results ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
$`|\sigma_{k}^{\star}-\sigma_{1_{k},\ell}|\leq\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F}`$.

*Step 4 (Singular-vector error via Wedin).* Insert
$`\pm\,\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}`$ and apply
the triangle inequality:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top}-\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha+\sin^{2}\beta},
``` |  | (20) |

where
$`\alpha:=\angle(\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell})`$,
$`\beta:=\angle(\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell})`$, and
we used
$`\left\|\mathbf{u}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\sin\angle(\mathbf{u},\tilde{\mathbf{u}})`$
for unit vectors. To apply Wedin’s theorem
([Theorem 6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6 (Wedin’s Theorem [ , ]). ‣ Appendix B Background Perturbation Results ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
we first verify the gap condition: by Weyl’s inequality,
$`|\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell})-\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star})|\leq\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{2}`$
for every $`j`$, which combined with the hypothesis
$`\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{2}<\tfrac{1}{2}\min_{j>k}|\sigma_{k}^{\star}-\sigma_{j}^{\star}|`$
yields
$`\delta_{k}:=\min\{\min_{j\geq 2}|\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell})-\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell})|,\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell})\}\geq\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}/2>0`$.
[Theorem 6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6 (Wedin’s Theorem [ , ]). ‣ Appendix B Background Perturbation Results ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
then gives
$`\sin^{2}\alpha+\sin^{2}\beta\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F}^{2}/(\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star})^{2}`$.
Plugging back
into ([20](#A4.E20 "Equation 20 ‣ Proof. ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
and using $`2\sqrt{2}<3`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\star\top}-\mathbf{u}_{1_{k},\ell}\mathbf{v}_{1_{k},\ell}^{\top}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{3}{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F}.
``` |  | (21) |

*Step 5 (Combine).* Substituting Steps 3
and ([21](#A4.E21 "Equation 21 ‣ Proof. ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
into ([19](#A4.E19 "Equation 19 ‣ Proof. ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
B_{k,\ell}\leq\frac{3\sigma_{k}^{\star}}{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F}=C_{k}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}-\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}\right\|_{F},\quad C_{k}:=\tfrac{3\sigma_{k}^{\star}}{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}}+1.
``` |  | (22) |

∎

### D.2 Proof of Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")

For the reader’s convenience, we restate the theorem before giving the
proof.

[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
(Restated). *Under
[Assumptions 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
and [3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery via Projection). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
define $`m_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}`$ and
$`m_{k}=\max\{\mathcal{F}_{k},\;\tfrac{1}{k}+\tfrac{k-1}{k}m_{k-1}\}`$
for $`k\geq 2`$, and let $`s_{1}=1`$ with $`s_{k+1}`$ chosen
via ([32](#A4.E32 "Equation 32 ‣ Deriving () from and . ‣ D.2 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
Then for all $`k\in[r]`$ and all $`\ell\geq s_{k}-1`$,
$`G_{k,\ell}\leq 3\mathcal{R}_{k}(\ell-s_{k}+2)\,m_{k}^{\ell-s_{k}+1}.`$*

###### Proof.

We proceed by strong induction on the component index $`k`$. We first
establish a coupled *triple recursion* on the numerical error, deflation
mismatch, and total error; then construct upper-bounding *surrogate
sequences* that admit closed-form decay; finally, verify the *five
warm-up conditions* on $`s_{k}`$ that make the surrogates valid. To
start, let $`s_{k}`$ satisfy the property that
$`\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}\leq\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$.

#### Base case ($`k=1`$).

$`\mathbf{Y}_{1,\ell}=\mathbf{Y}`$ for all $`\ell`$, so the first
deflation target is exact and $`B_{1,\ell}=0`$. Then
$`G_{1,\ell}=D_{1,\ell}\leq\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\ell}D_{1,0}\leq\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\ell}`$
by
[Assumption 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
and
[Lemma 7](#Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 7 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").
Since $`m_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}`$ and $`s_{1}=1`$,
$`G_{1,\ell}\leq\mathcal{R}_{1}m_{1}^{\ell}\leq 3\mathcal{R}_{1}(\ell+1)m_{1}^{\ell}`$,
which
satisfies ([10](#S4.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
The tighter factor $`1`$ for the base case is loosened to $`3`$ once
deflation enters at $`k\geq 2`$, because the surrogate initialization
$`\hat{G}_{k,s_{k}-1}=D_{k,s_{k}-1}+\hat{B}_{k,s_{k}-1}+\hat{B}_{k,s_{k}-2}\leq 3\mathcal{R}_{k}`$
via
[Lemma 7](#Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 7 (Bounded Rank-1 Recovery). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").

#### Inductive step: triple recursion.

Fix $`k\geq 2`$ and
assume ([10](#S4.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
holds for every $`k^{\prime}<k`$. We collect the three error recursions
used below.

*(i) Numerical error.*
[Lemma 8](#Thmtheorem8 "Lemma 8 (Numerical Error Recurrence). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
gives, for $`\ell\geq s_{k}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
D_{k,\ell}\leq\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigl(D_{k,\ell-1}+B_{k,\ell}+B_{k,\ell-1}\bigr).
``` |  | (23) |

*(ii) Deflation mismatch.* Combining
[Lemma 9](#Thmtheorem9 "Lemma 9 (Deflated Target Mismatch). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
with
[Lemma 10](#Thmtheorem10 "Lemma 10 (Deflation Mismatch Bound). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
B_{k,\ell}\leq C_{k}\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}\leq C_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}G_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}.
``` |  | (24) |

The key structural observation is that $`B_{k,\ell}`$ depends only on
the *earlier* components’ total errors $`G_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}`$ with
$`k^{\prime}<k`$: predecessor convergence automatically forces
$`B_{k,\ell}\to 0`$.

*(iii) Total error.*
Unrolling ([23](#A4.E23 "Equation 23 ‣ Inductive step: triple recursion. ‣ D.2 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
from round $`s_{k}-1`$ up to round $`\ell`$ and using
$`G_{k,\ell}\leq D_{k,\ell}+B_{k,\ell}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{k,\ell}\leq\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\ell-s_{k}+1}D_{k,s_{k}-1}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=s_{k}-1}^{\ell-1}\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\ell-\ell^{\prime}}\bigl(B_{k,\ell^{\prime}}+B_{k,\ell^{\prime}-1}\bigr)+B_{k,\ell}.
``` |  | (25) |

The three

#### Surrogate sequences.

Because the right-hand sides

condition in

|  |

|  |

``` |  | (27) |

with initialization

### D.3 Auxiliary Lemmas

###### Lemma 12 (Lambert-$`W`$ Bound).

Let $`m\in(0,1)`$ and $`g(x)=m^{x}(x+1)`$.

1.  (i)
    If $`\epsilon\geq-(em\log m)^{-1}`$, then $`g(x)\leq\epsilon`$ for
    all $`x\geq 0`$.
2.  (ii)
    If $`\epsilon<-(em\log m)^{-1}`$, then $`g(x)\leq\epsilon`$ whenever
    $`x\geq\tfrac{1}{\log m}W_{-1}(\epsilon m\log m)-1`$.

Moreover, the wrapper $`\hat{W}(a):=\max\{1,-W_{-1}(-a)\}`$ satisfies
$`\hat{W}(a)=\mathcal{O}(\max\{1,\log(1/a)\})`$, and admits the explicit
upper bound $`\hat{W}(a)\leq\log(1/a)+\log\log(1/a)+1`$ for
$`a\in(0,1/e)`$.

###### Proof.

Differentiating $`g(x)=m^{x}(x+1)`$ gives
$`g^{\prime}(x)=m^{x}\bigl(1+(x+1)\log m\bigr)`$, so $`g`$ attains its
maximum at $`x^{\star}=(\log(1/m))^{-1}-1`$ with value
$`g(x^{\star})=-(em\log m)^{-1}`$.

*Case (i).* If $`\epsilon\geq g(x^{\star})`$, then
$`g(x)\leq g(x^{\star})\leq\epsilon`$ for every $`x\geq 0`$, and any
non-negative $`x`$ satisfies the bound.

*Case (ii).* Suppose $`\epsilon<g(x^{\star})`$. On
$`(x^{\star},\infty)`$, $`g`$ is strictly decreasing, so the equation
$`g(x)=\epsilon`$ has a unique solution $`x_{\epsilon}>x^{\star}`$.
Taking logarithms and substituting $`y=(x+1)\log m`$ transforms
$`g(x)=\epsilon`$ into $`ye^{y}=\epsilon m\log m`$, which is solved by
$`y=W_{-1}(\epsilon m\log m)`$ on the decreasing branch (since
$`\epsilon m\log m\in(-1/e,0)`$). Hence
$`x_{\epsilon}=\tfrac{1}{\log m}W_{-1}(\epsilon m\log m)-1`$, and every
$`x\geq x_{\epsilon}`$ satisfies $`g(x)\leq\epsilon`$.

*Asymptotic statement.* By the standard expansion
$`-W_{-1}(-a)=\log(1/a)+\log\log(1/a)+o(1)`$ as $`a\to 0^{+}`$, we get
$`\hat{W}(a)=\mathcal{O}(\max\{1,\log(1/a)\})`$. The explicit
non-asymptotic bound $`\hat{W}(a)\leq\log(1/a)+\log\log(1/a)+1`$ for
$`a\in(0,1/e)`$ follows from the standard Lambert-$`W`$ inequality
$`-W_{-1}(-e^{-u-1})\leq u+\log u+1`$ for $`u\geq 1`$ (substitute
$`u=\log(1/a)-1`$). ∎

###### Lemma 13 (Geometric Re-anchoring of $`\hat{B}`$).

``` math

``` |  | (40) |

which is the post-warm-up branch
of ([38](#A4.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ Lemma 14 (Surrogate Sequence Domination). ‣ D.3 Auxiliary Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
The $`\min`$-ceiling enforces
$`\hat{B}_{k+1,\hat{s}_{k+1}}\leq\mathcal{R}_{k+1}`$, which we use when

This establishes $`\hat{B}_{k+1,\ell}\geq B_{k+1,\ell}`$ for every
$`\ell\geq 0`$.

We now bound $`G_{k+1,\ell}`$, starting with $`\ell\geq s_{k+1}`$.

from round $`s_{k+1}-1`$ up to round $`\ell`$ and

``` |  | (41) |

where we re-indexed the sum to start at $`s_{k+1}-1`$ (the dropped

$`\hat{B}_{k+1,\cdot}`$ from $`\hat{s}_{k+1}`$ to $`s_{k+1}-1`$ via
[Lemma 13](#Thmtheorem13 "Lemma 13 (Geometric Re-anchoring of 𝐵̂). ‣ D.3 Auxiliary Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").

### D.4 Proof of Proposition [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Proposition 2 (Self-Correction). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")

###### Proof.

By
[Lemma 9](#Thmtheorem9 "Lemma 9 (Deflated Target Mismatch). ‣ D.1 Supporting Lemmas ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"):
$`\left\|\mathbf{Y}_{k,\ell}-\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}\right\|_{F}\leq\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}G_{k^{\prime},\ell-1}`$.
Substituting
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"):
each term is
$`3\mathcal{R}_{k^{\prime}}(\ell-s_{k^{\prime}}+1)\,m_{k^{\prime}}^{\ell-s_{k^{\prime}}}\to 0`$
since $`m_{k^{\prime}}<1`$. ∎

### D.5 Proof of Theorem [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3 (Noiseless estimation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")

###### Proof.

Since $`\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})>0`$, for any $`\mathbf{W}`$ we have
$`\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\leq\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})^{-1}\|\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X}\|_{F}`$.
Applying this with
$`\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}-\sum_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k,L}\mathbf{a}_{k,L}^{\top}`$
and using the triangle inequality:
$`\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}-\sum_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k,L}\mathbf{a}_{k,L}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\|\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star}\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star\top}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{b}_{k,L}\mathbf{a}_{k,L}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right\|_{F}=\sum_{k=r+1}^{r^{\star}}\sigma_{k}^{\star}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}G_{k,L}`$,
where the first equality uses Eckart-Young-Mirsky: the best rank-$`r`$
approximation of $`\mathbf{Y}`$ in Frobenius norm leaves residual
$`\sum_{k>r}\sigma_{k}^{\star}`$. Dividing by
$`\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})`$ yields the stated bound. ∎

### D.6 Proof of Theorem [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (Noisy estimation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")

###### Proof.

Decompose into $`T_{1}`$ (truncation, bounded by Eckart-Young-Mirsky),
$`T_{2}`$ (noise perturbation, bounded by Weyl/Wedin and Gaussian
concentration following \[[Vandchali et al., 2025](#bib.bib21),
Lemma C.1\]), and $`T_{3}`$ (algorithmic error $`\sum_{k}G_{k,L}`$). The
algorithmic term inherits self-correction; the statistical term matches
the sequential baseline. Dividing by $`\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})`$
yields the stated bound.

The key observation is that parallelization does not affect the noise
term $`T_{2}`$. The noise $`\mathcal{E}`$ enters through the data model
$`\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}+\mathcal{E}`$, which is
identical for both sequential and parallel algorithms. The $`T_{2}`$
bound depends only on the spectral perturbation
$`\|\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{W}^{\star}\mathbf{X}\|_{2}=\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}`$,
controlled by standard Gaussian concentration (e.g.,
$`\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon(\sqrt{m}+\sqrt{n})`$ with high
probability). This perturbation is a property of the data, not the
algorithm, so the parallel and sequential statistical terms are the
same. The only difference is in $`T_{3}`$: for the parallel algorithm,
$`\sum_{k}G_{k,L}\to 0`$ by
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
while for the sequential baseline, the corresponding sum involves
permanently fixed numerical errors. ∎

### D.7 Verification that $`m_{k}<1`$

We show by induction on $`k`$ that the effective rate $`m_{k}`$ defined
in ([8](#S4.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
satisfies $`m_{k}\in(0,1)`$ for every $`k\in[r]`$.

*Base case ($`k=1`$).* By definition, $`m_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}`$, and
[Assumption 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
requires $`\mathcal{F}_{k}\in(0,1)`$ for every $`k`$. Hence
$`m_{1}\in(0,1)`$.

*Inductive step ($`k\geq 2`$).* Assume $`m_{k-1}\in(0,1)`$. The
auxiliary quantity $`\gamma_{k-1}=\tfrac{1}{k}+\tfrac{k-1}{k}m_{k-1}`$
is a convex combination of $`1`$ and $`m_{k-1}`$, so
$`\gamma_{k-1}\in(m_{k-1},\,1)\subset(0,1)`$ whenever $`m_{k-1}<1`$. By
[Assumption 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
$`\mathcal{F}_{k}\in(0,1)`$. Therefore
$`m_{k}=\max\{\mathcal{F}_{k},\,\gamma_{k-1}\}\in(0,1)`$, completing the
induction.

## Appendix E Game-Theoretic Interpretation

###### Proposition 15 (Nash equilibrium).

Under
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
define player $`k`$’s utility as
$`\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\mid\{(\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime}})\}_{k^{\prime}<k})=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{Y}-\sum_{k^{\prime}<k}\mathbf{b}_{k^{\prime}}\mathbf{a}_{k^{\prime}}^{\top}\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\|_{F}^{2}`$.
Then $`\{(\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\star},\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\star})\}_{k=1}^{r}`$
is the unique strict Nash equilibrium up to sign.

###### Proof.

By induction: given optimal predecessors, player $`k`$ maximizes utility
by finding the best rank-1 approximation of $`\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\star}`$,
which is unique under the strict spectral gap
([Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
∎

### F.2 Theoretical-Bound Validation

This experiment tests the quantitative form of
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"):
each worker’s total error $`G_{k,\ell}`$ (defined
in ([5](#S2.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")))
decays geometrically once the warm-up round $`s_{k}`$ from
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
is reached, and the empirical per-worker contraction rate matches the
recurrence ([8](#S4.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
for $`m_{k}`$. We fix $`m=50`$, $`d=80`$, $`n=200`$, $`r^{\star}=5`$ on
the exponential spectrum and run
[Algorithm 1](#alg1 "In 3 AdaPaD: The Algorithm ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
with $`T=10`$ ALS iterations per round and $`L=40`$ communication
rounds. For every seed and every worker $`k`$ we record the empirical
trajectory $`G_{k,\ell}`$ and fit
$`G_{k,\ell}\approx\widehat{C}_{k}\,\widehat{m}_{k}^{\,\ell}`$ by
least-squares regression on $`\log G_{k,\ell}`$, restricted to rounds at
which $`G_{k,\ell}`$ has fallen below half of its value at worker
activation. Here $`m_{k}`$ denotes the per-worker contraction factor of
the
recurrence ([8](#S4.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
and $`\mathcal{F}_{k}`$ denotes the per-component contraction factor of
the rank-1 subroutine from
[Assumption 1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Rank-1 Subroutine Contraction). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").
The empirical warm-up $`\widehat{s}_{k}`$ is taken as the first round
after which $`G_{k,\ell}`$ decays monotonically over the next five
rounds, and $`\widehat{m}_{k}`$ is the fitted slope; we use
$`\widehat{s}_{k}`$ and $`\widehat{m}_{k}`$ as the empirical estimates
of the theoretical $`s_{k}`$ and $`m_{k}`$.

[Figure 5](#A6.F5 "In F.2 Theoretical-Bound Validation ‣ Appendix F Theory-Validation Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
compares these estimates to the theoretical predictions. Panel (A)
overlays the fitted exponentials on the empirical $`G_{k,\ell}`$ curves
and shows that every worker enters geometric decay after a brief
warm-up. Panel (B) plots $`\widehat{s}_{k}`$ together with the
staggered-activation prediction $`s_{k}=k`$ and the Lambert-$`W`$ upper
bound
from ([32](#A4.E32 "Equation 32 ‣ Deriving () from and . ‣ D.2 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")):
the observed warm-up tracks $`s_{k}=k`$, while the Lambert-$`W`$ bound
is conservative on this well-separated spectrum, as expected of a
worst-case bound (for visual clarity, the displayed curve uses the
looser global-$`m_{k}`$ form
of ([32](#A4.E32 "Equation 32 ‣ Deriving () from and . ‣ D.2 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix D Complete Proofs ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
while the proof states a tighter per-predecessor maximum over
$`k^{\prime}\leq k`$; both are valid upper bounds on $`s_{k}`$).
Panel (C) places the fitted $`\widehat{m}_{k}`$ next to the recurrence
$`m_{k}=\max\{\mathcal{F}_{k},1/k+(k-1)m_{k-1}/k\}`$
from ([8](#S4.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")),
and the two agree within seed-to-seed noise. Together the three panels
confirm that the convergence rate predicted by
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
is attained empirically and that the fitted contraction rate
$`\widehat{m}_{k}`$ matches the
recurrence ([8](#S4.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
for $`m_{k}`$ within seed-to-seed variability.

### F.3 Spectral-Gap Sensitivity

The third experiment probes
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
directly by sweeping the spectral gap and tracking how convergence
degrades as it closes. We use a linear profile
$`\sigma_{k}^{\star}=1-(k-1)g`$ for $`k=1,\ldots,r^{\star}`$, clamped at
a floor of $`0.01`$ so the problem stays well-posed when $`g`$ is small.
Here $`g`$ is the spectral-gap step parameter for this experiment,
distinct from the growth interval $`\Delta`$ used in the main text
([Table 8](#A7.T8 "In Per-task hyperparameters. ‣ G.1 GLUE Training Details ‣ Appendix G Additional Experimental Details ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
The remaining settings match
[Section F.2](#A6.SS2 "F.2 Theoretical-Bound Validation ‣ Appendix F Theory-Validation Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"):
$`m=50`$, $`d=80`$, $`n=200`$, $`r^{\star}=5`$, $`T=10`$, $`L=30`$. On
this profile the per-worker gap from
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"),
$`\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}=\min\bigl(\min_{j>k}|\sigma_{k}^{\star}-\sigma_{j}^{\star}|,\sigma_{k}^{\star}\bigr)`$,
equals $`\min(g,\sigma_{k}^{\star})`$ for $`k<r^{\star}`$, so over the
swept range the ratio $`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$ directly controls
$`\min_{k}\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}/\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$. We sweep
$`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}\in\{0.01,0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5\}`$.

[Figure 6](#A6.F6 "In F.3 Spectral-Gap Sensitivity ‣ Appendix F Theory-Validation Experiments ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
reports the outcome. Panel (a) shows that all five gap settings converge
and that the per-round rate degrades smoothly as the gap shrinks: at
$`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}=0.5`$ the reconstruction error reaches its floor
within roughly five communication rounds $`\ell`$, while at
$`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}=0.01`$ it takes about twenty-five rounds.
Panel (b) plots the across-seed median of the final-round error against
the gap ratio on a log scale; even at $`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}=0.01`$ the
error drops below $`10^{-4}`$, so the algorithm degrades gracefully
rather than failing as the gap closes. A positive spectral gap is
therefore sufficient for convergence on this profile, and shrinking the
gap inflates the warm-up period $`s_{k}`$
([Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Parallel Rank-1 Deflation). ‣ 4 Theoretical Guarantees ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery"))
smoothly rather than triggering a sharp phase transition, consistent
with the role $`\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\star}`$ plays in
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery").

Figure 6: Spectral-gap sensitivity (validates
[Assumption 2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap). ‣ 2.1 Assumptions ‣ 2 Background and Problem Setup ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")).
(a) Convergence curves for five gap ratios $`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}`$;
smaller gaps require more rounds but still converge. (b) Final
reconstruction error vs. gap ratio decays to a floor below $`10^{-4}`$
even at $`g/\sigma_{1}^{\star}=0.01`$.

#### Orthogonal regularization.

We optionally include the orthogonal regularization term from
IncreLoRA \[[Zhang et al., 2023a](#bib.bib29)\],
$`\lambda_{\text{orth}}\bigl(\|\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}\|_{F}+\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\top}-\mathbf{I}\|_{F}\bigr)/(2n_{\text{layers}})`$,
which pushes each module’s component vectors toward an orthonormal
frame. We adopt it on GLUE with the per-task values in
[Table 8](#A7.T8 "In Per-task hyperparameters. ‣ G.1 GLUE Training Details ‣ Appendix G Additional Experimental Details ‣ AdaPaD: Adaptive Parallel Deflation forPEFT with Self-Correcting Rank Discovery")
and disable it on SQuAD.

#### Baseline reproduction.

We reproduce IncreLoRA as the primary comparison baseline because it is
the closest method to AdaPaD: both grow rank incrementally from rank-1
components via importance-based allocation with EMA scoring. We use the
authors’ official loralib (SVDLinear + RankAllocator) and their
published hyperparameters, ported to our software environment
(Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.6, Transformers 5.2, CUDA 12.9). For the
remaining baselines (AdaLoRA, SoRA, dEBORA) we cite published results
from their respective papers \[[Zhang et al., 2023b](#bib.bib28), [Ding
et al., 2023](#bib.bib31), [Zangrando et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)\].

### G.2 SQuAD/SQuAD v2 Training Details

#### Model and tokenizer.

We fine-tune Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B \[[Qwen Team, 2025](#bib.bib55)\] with the
standard AutoModelForQuestionAnswering span-prediction head. The
backbone has 28 transformer blocks with hidden size $`1024`$, FFN dim
$`3072`$, and grouped-query attention with $`16`$ query heads and $`8`$
KV heads. Inputs are tokenized at sequence length $`384`$ with
doc-stride $`128`$, following the HuggingFace canonical preprocessing
for both SQuAD v1.1 \[[Rajpurkar et al., 2016](#bib.bib39)\] and
SQuAD v2 \[[Rajpurkar et al., 2018](#bib.bib45)\].

#### Adapter configuration.

Both AdaPaD and the fixed-rank LoRA \[[Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib12)\]
baseline adapt all seven projection matrices in every transformer block:
$`W_{q}`$, $`W_{k}`$, $`W_{v}`$, $`W_{o}`$, $`W_{\text{gate}}`$,
$`W_{\text{up}}`$, $`W_{\text{down}}`$, for a total of
$`7\times 28{=}196`$ adapted modules. AdaPaD’s rank-1 components
initialise both $`\mathbf{a}_{k}`$ and $`\mathbf{b}_{k}`$ vectors from
$`\mathcal{N}(0,0.02)`$. We set $`r_{\max}{=}8`$ and allocate the
remaining budget by EMA importance scoring with the top-$`10`$ modules
per growth round.
````
