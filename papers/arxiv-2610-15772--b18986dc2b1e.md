###### Abstract

Recent focus in agentic reinforcement learning (RL) seeks to improve
credit assignment in long-horizon tasks. One promising direction
combines reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) and
on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), integrating trajectory-level
advantages with dense token-level supervision. This line of work often
conditions a teacher model on privileged context, such as agentic
skills, to score tokens generated by the student policy. Despite their
progress, they largely overlook ❶ the on-policy evolution of agentic
skills and ❷ the reliability of teacher supervision. Towards this end,
we introduce Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization (RSIPO), a
self-evolving agentic RL framework that enables agentic skills and the
policy model to co-evolve throughout the training process. Specifically,
RSIPO enables the policy itself to extract skills from completed
trajectories and explicitly optimizes its skill-generation capability
through trajectory replay. It further employs empirical Bayes shrinkage
to dynamically calibrate the student’s trust in teacher scores,
accounting for the fact that skill-enhanced teacher guidance can
sometimes be less reliable. Experiments with Qwen-family models on
embodied interaction and search-based agentic tasks demonstrate that
RSIPO outperforms all the compared baseline approaches. With Qwen2.5-7B
as the base model, RSIPO achieves an average success rate of 98.6% on
ALFWorld, yielding absolute improvements of 2.5% and 17.4% over the
second-best baseline and standard GRPO, respectively. Code is available
at [https://github.com/zfkarl/RSIPO](https://github.com/zfkarl/RSIPO).

## 1 Introduction

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) [Zhang et al. (2025a)](#bib.bib1);
[Zhang et al. (2025b)](#bib.bib2) has emerged as a key component of
large language model (LLM) post-training, substantially improving model
performance on long-horizon multi-turn tasks. Unlike conventional RL for
static and single-turn scenarios [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib3), agentic
RL aims to train models through interactions with dynamic environments,
where they iteratively reason, invoke tools, and incorporate
observations across multi-turn conversations [Lu et al.
(2026b)](#bib.bib4); [Singh et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5). This requires
learning sequential decision-making policies that reliably guide agents
toward successful task completion.

![Refer to caption](2610.15772v1/intro_paradigms.png)

Figure 1: Comparison of three paradigms of agentic RL. (a) RLVR-based
methods assign equal trajectory-level advantages to all response tokens.
(b) OPSD-based methods provide token-level supervision from a
self-teacher. (c) Hybrid methods combine the two branches, typically
using agentic skills as the teacher’s privileged context.

As shown in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"),
recent agentic RL approaches have explored reinforcement learning with
verifiable rewards (RLVR), on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), and
hybrid training frameworks that combine the two. Among them, RLVR-based
methods provide effective outcome-based trajectory-level advantages,
whereas OPSD-based methods offer dense and fine-grained token-level
supervision. Hybrid methods seek to combine these complementary signals,
typically coupling an RLVR branch with an OPSD branch for joint
optimization. In the OPSD branch, a teacher model conditioned on
privileged context scores student-generated tokens, and the student is
trained to reduce the token-level log-probability gap between the two
models. For example, SDAR [Lu et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib6) uses agentic
skills retrieved from an external skill library as privileged context
for the teacher model, whereas OPID [Yang et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib7) and
SEED [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib8) explore generating such skills with
external or internal analyzers.

Despite their progress in advancing agentic RL, existing hybrid
approaches face two key limitations. First, agentic skills are either
retrieved from a static external library or produced by a skill
generator trained off-policy in advance. Consequently, while the policy
improves at task execution during training, skill generation is not
explicitly optimized on-policy to evolve alongside it. Following the
experimental setup of SEED [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib8), we find that
only 0.25% of the trajectories in the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data
used to train the skill generator are successful, mainly because the
base model follows the required action format in just 11.0% of its
steps, whereas the policy’s rollout success rate rapidly exceeds 80%
during subsequent RL training
(Figure [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
(a)). As a result, 86.8% of the skills in the SFT data concern action
format or syntax, even though 99.7% of the policy’s actions are already
valid in the first 10 RL steps. The trajectories that the skill
generator must summarize thus shift from almost exclusively failures to
predominantly successes, creating a distribution shift that its initial
off-policy training does not explicitly address. Second, the OPSD
objective encourages the student to align with a skill-enhanced teacher,
implicitly assuming that the teacher consistently provides more reliable
guidance than the student’s own decisions. However, access to skills
does not guarantee better performance. As shown in
Figure [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
(b), the skill-enhanced teacher underperforms the student on 5.5%–22.4%
of tasks across different levels of student competence. Its advantage is
most pronounced on tasks that the student solves in one to three of four
rollouts. By contrast, for tasks with zero or four successful student
rollouts, the proportions of tasks on which the teacher outperforms and
underperforms the student differ only slightly (16.0% vs. 13.6% and 6.9%
vs. 5.5%, respectively).
Figure [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
(c) further shows that, during the first 15 RL training steps, the
teacher underperforms the student on more tasks than it outperforms the
student (22.9% vs. 15.0%) and continues to underperform on 4%–11% of
tasks thereafter. Therefore, uniformly trusting teacher guidance exposes
the student policy to unreliable supervision.

Figure 2: Preliminary analysis with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on ALFWorld. (a)
Success rate of the skill generator rollouts on SFT data and of the
policy rollouts during RL training. (b) Share of training tasks on which
the skill-enhanced teacher succeeds more or less often than the student,
grouped by the student’s successes in four separate rollouts on the
task. (c) The same shares over RL training, averaged over blocks of 15
steps.

These observations motivate Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization
(RSIPO), a self-evolving agentic RL framework that jointly improves
skill generation and task execution while calibrating the student’s
trust in teacher supervision. Building on the RLVR and OPSD branches,
RSIPO uses the student policy to rewrite skills and replays half of its
rollouts with these skills, measuring the resulting gains relative to
the remaining half, which serves as a control group. Each replay gain is
centered by subtracting the mean gain for the same task type, and the
resulting reward is assigned to the generated skill tokens. This
explicitly optimizes the policy’s skill-generation capability on-policy
without requiring auxiliary judge models or additional evaluation data.
Beyond skill evolution, RSIPO dynamically adjusts the student’s reliance
on the teacher in the OPSD branch. Specifically, it applies empirical
Bayes shrinkage to the replay gains to obtain robust and task-dependent
trust coefficients that weight the OPSD objective. These coefficients
increase the student’s reliance on reliable teacher guidance, downweight
unreliable supervision, and can disable distillation entirely on tasks
where the teacher is particularly unreliable.

We conduct extensive experiments across multiple base models on ALFWorld
[Shridhar et al. (2021)](#bib.bib12) for embodied interaction and seven
datasets for search-based agentic tasks. The results show that RSIPO
substantially outperforms a wide range of baselines, including
RLVR-based, OPSD-based, and hybrid agentic RL methods. On ALFWorld,
RSIPO achieves average task success rates of 95.7% and 98.6% with
Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B [Yang et al. (2024)](#bib.bib30),
respectively, surpassing the strongest prior method, SEED [Wu et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib8), by 3.9% and 2.5%. The performance improvements over
the standard GRPO+OPSD baseline are even larger, reaching 14.5% and
18.2%, respectively.

To summarize, our main contributions of this paper are threefold:

- ❶
  Co-Evolving Perspective. We identify limitations in skill generation
  and teacher supervision in existing agentic RL methods and propose a
  co-evolving perspective that couples on-policy skill improvement with
  adaptive trust in the teacher.
- ❷
  Self-Evolving Framework. We introduce RSIPO, a self-evolving framework
  that uses skill-induced performance gains measured through trajectory
  replay to drive skill-policy co-evolution and applies empirical Bayes
  shrinkage to calibrate the student’s trust in teacher supervision.
- ❸
  Empirical Validation. Extensive experiments across multiple models and
  datasets demonstrate that RSIPO outperforms existing agentic RL
  methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance on ALFWorld and
  search-based agentic tasks.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Agentic Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has demonstrated
substantial gains in single-turn reasoning. Critic-free methods such as
GRPO [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib3) and DAPO [Yu et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib11) estimate advantages from group-normalized outcome
rewards, eliminating the need for the learned value network used in
PPO [Schulman et al. (2017)](#bib.bib9). In agentic settings, policies
interact with embodied [Shridhar et al. (2021)](#bib.bib12),
shopping [Yao et al. (2022)](#bib.bib13), or search [Jin et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib14) environments over multiple turns. Attributing a
single terminal reward to a long sequence of decisions poses a credit
assignment challenge, motivating multi-turn training frameworks [Wang et
al. (2025)](#bib.bib15); [Zhang et al. (2025b)](#bib.bib2) and
step-level advantage estimation [Feng et al. (2025)](#bib.bib16). A
complementary line of work equips agents with reusable natural-language
experience, including reflections, insights, and skill libraries [Shinn
et al. (2023)](#bib.bib17); [Zhao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib18); [Wang et
al. (2023)](#bib.bib19), with recent methods incorporating such skills
into agentic RL training [Xia et al. (2026)](#bib.bib20); [Lu et al.
(2026b)](#bib.bib4). These approaches, however, typically rely on fixed
skill libraries, frozen external generators, or skill-generation
objectives that do not explicitly account for the utility of generated
skills in task execution.

### 2.2 On-Policy Self-Distillation

![Refer to caption](2610.15772v1/RSIPO_method_main.png)

Figure 3: Overview of the proposed RSIPO framework. Replay gains
measured by the execution check reward on-policy skill generation and
calibrate the student’s trust in the skill-enhanced teacher.

In on-policy distillation (OPD), a teacher model scores sequences
sampled from the student policy, allowing the student to learn from its
own generation distribution rather than a fixed corpus [Agarwal et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib21); [Gu et al. (2024)](#bib.bib22). On-policy
self-distillation (OPSD) uses the same model as the teacher by
conditioning it on privileged training-time context unavailable to the
student, such as worked solutions or feedback on the student’s
attempts [Zhao et al. (2026)](#bib.bib23); [Hübotter et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib24). Hybrid approaches combine this supervision with
RLVR, often using agentic skills as privileged context for the
teacher [Wang et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib25). For example, SDAR retrieves
skills from an external library [Lu et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib6), OPID
uses an external analyzer to extract skills from completed
episodes [Yang et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib7), and SEED trains the policy
itself to generate skills through supervised fine-tuning [Wu et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib8). Other methods modify how the teacher–student
log-probability gap contributes to optimization, for example by
aggregating it at the action-step level or annealing the distillation
signal over training [Zhang et al. (2026)](#bib.bib26); [Wang et al.
(2026b)](#bib.bib27); [Tan et al. (2026)](#bib.bib28).

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Preliminary and Notation

We consider an agent that solves a task $`x`$ by interacting with an
environment for at most $`T_{\max}`$ turns. At turn $`t`$, the policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ receives a context $`c_{t}`$ that contains the task
instruction, a window of recent observations and actions, and the
current observation. Then it generates a response
$`y_{t}=(y_{t,1},\dots,y_{t,|y_{t}|})`$ consisting of reasoning and
action tokens. The environment executes the action and returns the next
observation. An episode produces a trajectory
$`\tau=(c_{1},y_{1},\dots,c_{T},y_{T})`$ with $`T\leq T_{\max}`$ and a
verifiable outcome reward $`R(\tau)\in\{0,1\}`$ that indicates task
execution success.

#### RLVR Branch.

For each task, $`G`$ trajectories $`\{\tau^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{G}`$ are
sampled from the current policy. Following GRPO [Shao et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib10), trajectory $`\tau^{(i)}`$ receives the
group-normalized advantage
$`A^{(i)}=\bigl(R(\tau^{(i)})-\mu_{R}\bigr)/\sigma_{R}`$, where
$`\mu_{R}`$ and $`\sigma_{R}`$ are the mean and standard deviation of
the rewards in the group, and this advantage is shared by all tokens of
$`\tau^{(i)}`$. The policy is optimized with the clipped surrogate
objective $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}}`$ [Schulman et al.
(2017)](#bib.bib9) and a KL penalty $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}`$ toward a
reference policy:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{RLVR}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}}+\lambda_{\text{KL}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}.
``` |  | (1) |

#### OPSD Branch.

Following SEED [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib8), the policy itself
summarizes each completed trajectory into an agentic skill $`s`$,
prompted with an analysis template that presents the trajectory and asks
for an episode summary and a reusable skill. The skill-enhanced teacher
is the same policy with the skill inserted into its context,
$`\pi_{T}(\cdot\mid c_{t})=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid c_{t}\oplus s)`$. For
each token generated by the student, the two log-probabilities define a
gated distillation term:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\ell_{t,j}`$ | $`\displaystyle=g_{t,j}\bigl(\log\pi_{T}(y_{t,j})-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t,j})\bigr),`$ |  | (2) |
|  | $`\displaystyle g_{t,j}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sigma\bigl(\beta\bigl(\log\pi_{T}(y_{t,j})-\operatorname{sg}[\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t,j})]\bigr)\bigr),`$ |  |  |

where the conditioning on $`c_{t}`$ and $`y_{t,<j}`$ is omitted,
$`\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]`$ stops gradients, and the teacher
log-probabilities are treated as constants. The gate $`g_{t,j}`$ focuses
distillation on tokens for which the teacher is more confident than the
student. Let $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{OPSD}}`$ denote the average of
$`\ell_{t,j}`$ over all response tokens, the hybrid objective is:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{hybrid}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{RLVR}}+\lambda_{\text{OPSD}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{OPSD}}.
``` |  | (3) |

Following SEED [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib8), the policy is initialized
by supervised fine-tuning on skills that an external LLM writes for the
base model’s own trajectories, so that it can generate skills before RL
begins. No skill is provided at test time.

### 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO

RSIPO keeps both RLVR and OPSD branches of the hybrid agentic RL
framework and adds an execution check that measures how much a
self-generated skill actually improves the policy’s execution for every
training task. The measured gains are used twice: ❶ they reward the
policy model for writing helpful skills, so that skill generation
co-evolves with task execution, and ❷ they calibrate the student’s trust
in the skill-enhanced teacher.
Figure [3](#S2.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 2.2 On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
illustrates the overall RSIPO framework.

#### Execution Check via Trajectory Replay.

Before any outcome is inspected, the $`G`$ rollouts of task $`x`$ are
split uniformly at random into an evidence set $`\mathcal{E}_{x}`$ and a
control set $`\mathcal{C}_{x}`$ of equal size, so that
$`\mathcal{C}_{x}`$ is an unbiased sample of the student’s current
performance. RSIPO picks one trajectory from $`\mathcal{E}_{x}`$,
preferring a successful one when available, and lets the policy rewrite
a skill $`s_{x}`$ from it with the analysis template. If $`s_{x}`$ is
valid, the skill-enhanced teacher
$`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\cdot\oplus s_{x})`$ is replayed $`K`$ times
from exactly the same initial state of the task. Each episode is scored
by the utility:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u(\tau)=\mathbb{I}\bigl[R(\tau)=1\bigr]\Bigl(1-\alpha\,\frac{T(\tau)}{T_{\max}}\Bigr),
``` |  | (4) |

where $`T(\tau)`$ is the number of turns and $`\alpha`$ favors faster
successes. The replay gain compares the $`K`$ replays
$`\{\tilde{\tau}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}`$ with the control rollouts:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta_{x}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}u(\tilde{\tau}_{k})-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{x}|}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{x}}u(\tau).
``` |  | (5) |

Since both the replay and control groups contain only a few episodes,
$`\Delta_{x}`$ could be noisy. We approximate its sampling variance by
$`v_{x}=\sum_{a\in\{\text{rep},\,\text{ctl}\}}\tilde{u}_{a}(1-\tilde{u}_{a})/n_{a}`$,
where $`n_{a}`$ is the number of episodes in group $`a`$ and
$`\tilde{u}_{a}=(n_{a}\bar{u}_{a}+1)/(n_{a}+2)`$ is its Laplace-smoothed
mean utility.

#### Type-Level Empirical Bayes Shrinkage.

To obtain reliable estimates from such noisy measurements, RSIPO pools
the gains of tasks of the same type $`c`$, such as a task category in
ALFWorld or a source dataset in search-based tasks, across training
steps. For each type present in the current batch, it updates the moving
averages as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{m}_{c}\leftarrow(1-\eta)\,\mathbf{m}_{c}+\eta\,\bigl(\overline{\Delta},\ \overline{\Delta^{2}},\ \overline{v},\ 1\bigr),
``` |  | (6) |

where the averages are taken over the tasks of type $`c`$ in the batch
and the last entry corrects the bias of the zero initialization. Writing
$`\mathbf{m}_{c}=(m_{c}^{(1)},m_{c}^{(2)},m_{c}^{(v)},m_{c}^{(0)})`$,
the type mean $`\bar{\Delta}_{c}=m_{c}^{(1)}/m_{c}^{(0)}`$ and the
between-task variance
$`\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{c}=\max\bigl(0,\,m_{c}^{(2)}/m_{c}^{(0)}-\bar{\Delta}_{c}^{2}-m_{c}^{(v)}/m_{c}^{(0)}\bigr)`$,
i.e., the total variance minus the noise variance, specify a Gaussian
prior over the true gains of type $`c`$. Following empirical Bayes
estimation [Efron and Morris (1975)](#bib.bib29), the posterior mean of
the gain of task $`x`$ shrinks its own estimate toward the type mean
according to its reliability:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{g}_{x}=w_{x}\Delta_{x}+(1-w_{x})\bar{\Delta}_{c},\quad w_{x}=\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{c}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{c}+v_{x}},
``` |  | (7) |

with $`c=c(x)`$. Gains that are noisy or belong to types without genuine
variation across tasks are pulled toward the type mean, whereas reliable
and distinctive gains are largely preserved. When a task has no valid
replay, the corresponding replay gain falls back to the prior mean
$`\hat{g}_{x}=\bar{\Delta}_{c}`$.

#### On-Policy Skill Evolution.

RSIPO uses the execution check to train skill generation directly. Each
task contributes one sample, whose input is the analysis prompt
$`q_{x}`$ of the selected evidence trajectory and whose output
$`y^{(x)}`$ is the rewritten analysis containing $`s_{x}`$. The reward
is defined as the replay gain centered by the type mean:

|     |                                       |     |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       r_{x}=\Delta_{x}-\bar{\Delta}_{c(x)},
       ```                                    |     | (8) |

where $`r_{x}`$ is positive only if $`s_{x}`$ yields a larger replay
gain than the policy’s skills achieve on average for tasks of the same
type, and negative otherwise. In contrast to the trust coefficient
below, $`r_{x}`$ is not shrunk, because a policy-gradient signal should
be unbiased and shrinkage would suppress all rewards whenever the
between-task variance cannot be separated from noise. An invalid skill
receives the penalty $`r_{x}=-\rho`$, and a task whose replay does not
reproduce the initial state receives $`r_{x}=0`$.¹¹ 1 For search-based
tasks, invalid skills are not penalized ($`\rho=0`$), and a small reward
of $`0.05`$ is assigned to the format tokens of valid outputs to keep
the JSON structure stable. The skill-generation loss is a
reward-weighted log-likelihood over the tokens of the skill:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\ell}_{x}=\frac{\sum_{j}m_{x,j}\log\pi_{\theta}\bigl(y^{(x)}_{j}\mid q_{x},y^{(x)}_{<j}\bigr)}{\max\bigl(1,\sum_{j}m_{x,j}\bigr)},
``` |  | (9) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{skill}}=-\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}r_{x}\,\bar{\ell}_{x},
``` |  | (10) |

where $`X`$ is the task batch and $`m_{x,j}=1`$ marks the tokens inside
the skill string of a valid output, or all tokens of an invalid one.
Because the skills are written by the current policy from its own
trajectories and rewarded by their effect on its own execution, skill
generation is optimized on-policy and evolves together with task
execution.

#### Trust-Calibrated Self-Distillation.

The shrunk gain $`\hat{g}_{x}`$ estimates how much the skill-enhanced
teacher outperforms the student on task $`x`$. RSIPO maps it to a trust
coefficient:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\omega_{x}=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(1+\kappa\,\hat{g}_{x},\ 0,\ \omega_{\max}\bigr),
``` |  | (11) |

which weights the OPSD terms of all rollouts:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{OPSD}}(\bm{\omega})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\sum_{t,j}\omega_{x(i)}\,\ell^{(i)}_{t,j},
``` |  | (12) |

where $`\ell^{(i)}_{t,j}`$ is the term in
Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "In OPSD Branch. ‣ 3.1 Preliminary and Notation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"))
for the $`j`$-th token of turn $`t`$ in rollout $`i`$, $`x(i)`$ is the
rollout task, and $`N`$ is the number of response tokens. Setting
$`\omega_{x}=1`$ for all tasks recovers the original OPSD loss. With
$`\kappa=2`$ and $`\omega_{\max}=2`$, distillation is disabled when
$`\hat{g}_{x}\leq-0.5`$ and doubled when $`\hat{g}_{x}\geq 0.5`$. We use
$`\hat{g}_{x}`$ rather than $`\Delta_{x}`$ because a noisy per-task
estimate would directly distort the loss weight.

#### Overall Objective.

RSIPO optimizes all components in a single update:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}={}\mathcal{L}_{\text{RLVR}}+\lambda_{\text{OPSD}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{OPSD}}(\bm{\omega})+\mathcal{L}_{\text{skill}}.`$ |  | (13) |

The gradient of $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{skill}}`$ is accumulated with the
last PPO mini-batch so that both share one optimizer step, and the type
statistics are committed only after the update succeeds.

## 4 Experiment

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

#### Benchmarks.

We evaluate the proposed RSIPO framework and baseline approaches on
embodied interaction tasks using ALFWorld [Shridhar et al.
(2021)](#bib.bib12) and search-based agentic tasks. Following
Search-R1 [Jin et al. (2025)](#bib.bib14), we employ three single-hop
datasets, NQ [Kwiatkowski et al. (2019)](#bib.bib34), TriviaQA [Joshi et
al. (2017)](#bib.bib35), and PopQA [Mallen et al. (2023)](#bib.bib36),
and four multi-hop datasets, HotpotQA [Yang et al. (2018)](#bib.bib37),
2WikiMultiHopQA [Ho et al. (2020)](#bib.bib38), MuSiQue [Trivedi et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib39), and Bamboogle [Press et al. (2023)](#bib.bib40) for
search-based tasks. We report success rates on ALFWorld and exact-match
accuracy on search-based tasks.

#### Baselines.

We compare RSIPO with 13 baselines, including training-free methods like
Vanilla and Skill-Prompt\*, and methods from the three paradigms in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"),
namely the RLVR-based GRPO [Shao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib10), Skill-GRPO,
and Skill-GRPO\*, the OPSD-based OPSD [Zhao et al. (2026)](#bib.bib23),
and the hybrid GRPO+OPSD, Skill-SD [Wang et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib25),
RLSD [Yang et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib32), SDAR [Lu et al.
(2026a)](#bib.bib6), StepOPSD [Zhang et al. (2026)](#bib.bib26),
OPID [Yang et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib7), and SEED [Wu et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib8). Skill-Prompt\* adds skills from the SkillRL
library [Xia et al. (2026)](#bib.bib20) to the prompt at test time,
while Skill-GRPO uses them during training and Skill-GRPO\* also keeps
them at test time. All other methods, including RSIPO, act without
skills at test time.

#### Implementation Details.

We employ various Qwen-family models, including Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct,
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [Yang et al. (2024)](#bib.bib30), and
Qwen3-1.7B [Yang et al. (2025)](#bib.bib31) as base models. Following
SEED [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib8), we first fine-tune each model to
generate agentic skills using trajectory-skill pairs that are annotated
by GLM-5.2 [Z.ai (2026)](#bib.bib33) on training data. RSIPO then runs
for 150 steps with $`G=8`$ rollouts per task and 16 tasks per step on
ALFWorld or 128 on search-based tasks, using a learning rate of
$`10^{-6}`$, and a KL coefficient of 0.01 on ALFWorld and 0.001 on
search-based tasks. We keep the OPSD setting with
$`\lambda_{\text{OPSD}}=0.01`$ and $`\beta=5`$. For RSIPO, the evidence
and control sets each contain half of the rollouts, and we set $`K=4`$,
$`\alpha=0.5`$, $`\eta=0.1`$, and $`\rho=0.2`$. Each run uses 8
$`\times`$ NVIDIA H20 GPUs.

#### Embodied Interaction.

On ALFWorld, RSIPO achieves the highest average success rate across all
three backbones, reaching 95.7%, 98.6%, and 95.0% with Qwen2.5-3B,
Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen3-1.7B, respectively. These results correspond to
gains of 3.9%, 2.5%, and 3.0% over the strongest baseline, SEED, and
20.7%, 17.4%, and 48.9% over standard GRPO. Adding OPSD to GRPO does not
consistently improve performance, as GRPO+OPSD underperforms GRPO with
both Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-1.7B. This finding is consistent with our
preliminary analysis in
Figure [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"),
which suggests that uniformly trusting teacher guidance can hinder
student learning. In contrast, RSIPO outperforms GRPO+OPSD by 14.5%,
18.2%, and 63.0% with Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen3-1.7B,
respectively.

#### Search-Based Agentic Tasks.

RSIPO attains the highest average accuracy with Qwen2.5-7B and
Qwen3-1.7B, at 50.8% and 42.6%, and ranks second with Qwen2.5-3B, only
0.4% below SEED. It improves over GRPO by 8.9%, 8.8%, and 1.8% on the
three backbones. The margins are smaller than on ALFWorld, likely
because answering these questions relies more on retrieved evidence than
on procedural skills, so the skill-enhanced teacher provides less
additional guidance, as reflected by trust values close to one in Figure
4 (a).

#### Learning without Test-Time Skills.

Despite using no agentic skills at test time for inference, RSIPO
consistently outperforms baselines that receive skills during
evaluation, including Skill-Prompt\* and Skill-GRPO\*, across both
benchmarks and all evaluated backbones. With Qwen2.5-7B, RSIPO surpasses
Skill-GRPO\* by 10.3% on ALFWorld and 3.3% on search-based agentic
tasks. These results suggest that trust-calibrated self-distillation
helps the policy internalize knowledge from agentic skills generated
during training, rather than relying on explicit skill guidance at
inference.

#### Training Dynamics.

Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.3 Further Analysis ‣ 4 Experiment ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
(a) shows the training curves of RSIPO with Qwen2.5-3B. The training
success rate rises from 63% to over 90% on ALFWorld and from 32% to 53%
on search-based tasks, while the OPSD loss is roughly halved on
search-based tasks and stays stable on ALFWorld. The dynamic trust
tracks the reliability of the teacher. On ALFWorld, it exceeds 1.1
within 20 steps as the skill-enhanced teacher increasingly outperforms
the control rollouts. On search-based tasks, where the teacher offers
little extra benefit, it stays close to one, showing that even in such
unfavorable cases the calibrated distillation degrades gracefully to
approximately standard OPSD.

#### Ablation Study.

Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.3 Further Analysis ‣ 4 Experiment ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
(b) reports a cumulative ablation on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-3B. OPSD
alone improves the base model only from 21.9% to 28.1%, whereas GRPO
reaches 75.0%, and combining the two yields 81.2%. With SFT
initialization, the hybrid pipeline reaches 86.4%. On top of it, dynamic
trust and skill evolution bring gains of 3.6% and 5.6%, respectively,
and the full RSIPO reaches 95.7%. Removing either component from RSIPO
costs 3.7% to 5.7%, showing that the two components are complementary.

#### Visualization of On-Policy Skill Evolution.

Figure [5](#S4.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 4.3 Further Analysis ‣ 4 Experiment ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
traces the self-generated skills on ALFWorld using three non-exclusive
keyword-based categories: Procedure (numbered steps), Format (action
syntax), and Recovery (handling “Nothing happens”). Early skills
inherited from SFT often fall into Format or Recovery, with a negative
average replay gain. Procedure skills then exceed 94% in the last 45
steps, and the replay gain turns positive. For example, the step-0 skill
only advises checking the syntax, whereas the step-150 skill specifies a
systematic search-and-place procedure.

## 5 Conclusion

In this work, we presented RSIPO, a self-evolving agentic RL framework
that enables agentic skills and the policy to co-evolve throughout
training. RSIPO evaluates the utility of self-generated skills by
comparing skill-enhanced teacher replays with control rollouts. The
resulting replay gains reward the policy for generating useful skills
and calibrate the student’s trust in teacher supervision. Experiments on
embodied and search-based tasks demonstrate RSIPO’s advantages over
existing agentic RL methods. Future work will extend RSIPO to larger
models and a broader range of agentic tasks, including coding and
co-working scenarios.

## Limitations

Despite its effectiveness, RSIPO has two main limitations. First,
although trajectory replay requires neither additional evaluation data
nor auxiliary evaluation models, the extra rollouts increase training
costs. This introduces a trade-off between training effectiveness and
computational efficiency. Second, RSIPO relies on environments that can
be reset to a specified state, such as simulators and search
environments backed by offline retrieval corpora. Extending RSIPO to
non-resettable settings, such as interactions with live websites,
remains a challenge, which we leave as future work.

## Appendix A Introduction to Baselines

We compare the proposed RSIPO with a variety of baseline approaches,
grouped by how they use outcome rewards and teacher supervision. Methods
marked with \* have access to skills at test time, whereas all other
methods, including RSIPO, are evaluated without any skill in their
context.

#### Training-free Methods.

Vanilla evaluates the instruction-tuned base model without any
post-training. Skill-Prompt\* keeps the parameters of Vanilla fixed and
inserts task-relevant skills retrieved from the SkillRL library [Xia et
al. (2026)](#bib.bib20) into the prompt at test time, which measures the
benefit of skills as pure in-context guidance.

#### RLVR-based Methods.

GRPO [Shao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib10) normalizes the outcome rewards
within a group of rollouts and assigns the resulting advantage to every
token of a trajectory, without token-level supervision. Skill-GRPO
optimizes the same objective but places retrieved agentic skills in the
policy prompt during rollouts and updates, and removes them at test time
to examine whether the skill-guided behavior is retained. Skill-GRPO\*
shares the training procedure of Skill-GRPO but keeps the skills at test
time for evaluation.

#### OPSD-based Methods.

OPSD [Zhao et al. (2026)](#bib.bib23) uses the same model as student and
teacher. The teacher additionally conditions on training-only privileged
context and provides token-level targets on the student’s own samples,
while no privileged context is used at test time.

#### Hybrid Methods.

GRPO+OPSD adds the OPSD loss to the GRPO objective with a fixed weight.
Skill-SD [Wang et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib25) conditions only the teacher
on retrieved skills and transfers the resulting behavior to the student
through an importance-weighted distillation loss. RLSD [Yang et al.
(2026a)](#bib.bib32) lets the outcome advantage determine the direction
of each token update and uses the teacher–student log-probability gap
only to rescale its magnitude, with a contribution that decays during
training. SDAR [Lu et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib6) augments GRPO with an
auxiliary distillation term, where a sigmoid gate on the detached
teacher–student gap emphasizes the tokens endorsed by a
skill-conditioned teacher. StepOPSD [Zhang et al. (2026)](#bib.bib26)
operates at the level of action steps, where a teacher that observes a
successful trajectory from the same group reweights the step-level
advantages according to its log-probability gap with the student.
OPID [Yang et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib7) uses an external LLM analyzer to
summarize each on-policy trajectory into an episode-level skill and
skills for its critical steps, routes the latter to the corresponding
steps, and turns the resulting log-probability shift into a token-level
advantage. SEED [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib8) first fine-tunes the
policy on skills that an external LLM writes for its trajectories, and
then lets the policy itself summarize its rollouts into skills during RL
training, distilling the skill-conditioned teacher.

## Appendix C Training Algorithm of RSIPO

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix C Training Algorithm of RSIPO ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
summarizes one training step of RSIPO. The policy first samples $`G`$
rollouts for each task and writes a skill for each of them, which yields
the RLVR loss in
Eq. ([1](#S3.E1 "In RLVR Branch. ‣ 3.1 Preliminary and Notation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"))
and the OPSD terms in
Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "In OPSD Branch. ‣ 3.1 Preliminary and Notation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")).
RSIPO then runs the execution check for every task. It splits the
rollouts into an evidence set and a control set, rewrites a skill from
an evidence trajectory, and replays the skill-enhanced teacher $`K`$
times from the same initial state to measure the replay gain
$`\Delta_{x}`$ in
Eq. ([5](#S3.E5 "In Execution Check via Trajectory Replay. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")).
The gains of all tasks update the type-level statistics in
Eq. ([6](#S3.E6 "In Type-Level Empirical Bayes Shrinkage. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")),
from which RSIPO computes the shrunk gains $`\hat{g}_{x}`$, the skill
rewards $`r_{x}`$, and the trust coefficients $`\omega_{x}`$. Finally,
the policy is updated with the overall objective in
Eq. ([13](#S3.E13 "In Overall Objective. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")),
so that skill generation and task execution are optimized in the same
step.

0:  policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$, task batch $`X`$, type statistics
$`\{\mathbf{m}_{c}\}`$

1:  Sample $`G`$ rollouts per task; the policy writes a skill for each
rollout

2:  Compute $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{RLVR}}`$
(Eq. ([1](#S3.E1 "In RLVR Branch. ‣ 3.1 Preliminary and Notation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))
and the OPSD terms $`\ell_{t,j}`$
(Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "In OPSD Branch. ‣ 3.1 Preliminary and Notation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))

3:  for each task $`x\in X`$ do

4:   Randomly split its rollouts into $`\mathcal{E}_{x}`$ and
$`\mathcal{C}_{x}`$

5:   Rewrite $`s_{x}`$ from a trajectory in $`\mathcal{E}_{x}`$,
preferring a successful one

6:   if $`s_{x}`$ is valid then

7:    Replay $`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\cdot\oplus s_{x})`$ $`K`$ times
from the initial state of $`x`$

8:    Compute $`\Delta_{x}`$
(Eq. ([5](#S3.E5 "In Execution Check via Trajectory Replay. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))
and its noise variance $`v_{x}`$

9:   end if

10:  end for

11:  Update $`\{\mathbf{m}_{c}\}`$
(Eq. ([6](#S3.E6 "In Type-Level Empirical Bayes Shrinkage. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))
and compute $`\hat{g}_{x}`$
(Eq. ([7](#S3.E7 "In Type-Level Empirical Bayes Shrinkage. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))

12:  Compute $`r_{x}`$
(Eq. ([8](#S3.E8 "In On-Policy Skill Evolution. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))
and $`\omega_{x}`$
(Eq. ([11](#S3.E11 "In Trust-Calibrated Self-Distillation. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))

13:  Update $`\pi_{\theta}`$ with $`\mathcal{L}`$
(Eq. ([13](#S3.E13 "In Overall Objective. ‣ 3.2 The Proposed RSIPO ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")))

## Appendix F Case Study

Figures [9](#A5.F9 "Figure 9 ‣ Appendix E Prompts ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
and [10](#A5.F10 "Figure 10 ‣ Appendix E Prompts ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution")
show the execution check on a helpful skill for each benchmark, both
taken from late training steps of Qwen2.5-7B. In both figures, ✓ and ✗
mark successful and failed episodes together with their numbers of
turns, and ★ marks the selected evidence rollout. The trajectory
excerpts list the actions at selected turns $`t`$, each followed by the
next observation on ALFWorld or by the titles of the retrieved documents
on search-based tasks.

#### ALFWorld.

In
Figure [9](#A5.F9 "Figure 9 ‣ Appendix E Prompts ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"),
the student often fails to put two soapbottles into a cabinet: only two
of the eight rollouts succeed, and none of the four control rollouts
ever picks up the second soapbottle. As the excerpts show, the student
takes the first soapbottle out of the cabinet again after placing it. In
the selected evidence rollout, it finds the second soapbottle in the
garbage can at turn 19 while still holding the first, and its attempts
to pick up the second have no effect until it puts the first back at
turn 40. From this 45-turn success, the policy writes a skill that lists
garbage cans among the places to search and asks the agent to verify
that each pickup succeeds. With this skill, all four replays succeed
within 18 turns while all control rollouts fail, giving
$`\Delta_{x}=0.878`$ and $`r_{x}=0.811`$.

#### Search-Based Tasks.

In
Figure [10](#A5.F10 "Figure 10 ‣ Appendix E Prompts ‣ RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution"),
the question asks for the death date of the mother of Infanta Antónia of
Portugal. The six failed rollouts search for the death date directly,
retrieve another infanta, Maria Antonia, and answer the death date of
her mother. The selected evidence rollout instead searches for the
mother first, identifies Maria II, and then searches for her death date.
The policy turns this rollout into a general strategy that contains
neither a name nor the answer: identify the relative first, then search
for the requested attribute. With this skill, all four replays answer
correctly, whereas only one of the four control rollouts does, giving
$`\Delta_{x}=0.469`$ and $`r_{x}=0.459`$.
````
