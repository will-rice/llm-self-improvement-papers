###### Abstract

Verifiable rewards improve language models through reliable task-level
feedback, but methods based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
apply a sequence-level advantage uniformly across all tokens. This
coarse credit assignment reinforces or penalizes entire responses
without identifying which local decisions to preserve, reinforce, or
revise. Conversely, evidence-conditioned self-distillation provides
denser token-level supervision, yet teacher imitation can transfer
stylistic artifacts and miscalibrated confidence that destabilize
training when misaligned with task success. We introduce VERPO, which
converts evidence-conditioned guidance into reward-aligned token-level
credit assignment while retaining the outcome objective. VERPO
decomposes teacher guidance into an evidence-free reference term and
signed, evidence-induced corrections at each token. A stopped controller
combines selective acceptance, token-wise localization, and cost-aware
scaling by balancing alignment with the local GRPO update direction
against Fisher movement cost. Furthermore, we introduce Fisher Evidence
Contrast (FEC), which attenuates nuisance shifts along an estimated
evidence-presence direction through a regularized projection. Across
five scientific reasoning and tool-use tasks, VERPO prevents
optimization collapse and consistently achieves the highest multi-task
average across model backbones, yielding marked improvements
particularly on smaller models over strong baselines. Qualitative
diagnostics confirm that token acceptance selectively targets reasoning
bottlenecks consistent with local reward alignment and Fisher movement
cost. Code is available at
[GitHub](https://github.com/hamsterjiang23/VERPO).

## 1 Introduction

Outcome-based policy optimization with verifiable rewards has become a
central approach for improving the reasoning behavior of language
models ([DeepSeek-AI, 2025](#bib.bib2); [Yu et al., 2025](#bib.bib3)).
In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), task success
can be checked automatically, allowing policy updates to be grounded in
reliable outcome-level feedback. Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) derives group-relative
advantages from multiple on-policy rollouts and uses them to reinforce
or suppress generated responses. In
Fig. [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")(a),
GRPO ([2024](#bib.bib1)) resembles trial-and-error exploration: the
reward is reliable, but it is returned only after a full response is
completed. Consequently, the same sequence-level advantage is applied
uniformly across response tokens, leaving the model without explicit
information about which local decisions should be preserved, reinforced,
or revised ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)).

Evidence-conditioned self-distillation offers a natural way to make this
feedback denser ([Zhao et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7); [Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)). A self-teacher can replay sampled trajectories with
privileged evidence, such as training-only feedback, reference
information, or external signals, and then provide token-level guidance
to a student that will not receive such evidence at deployment time. In
Fig. [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")(b),
evidence can point to local correction opportunities along a rollout
trajectory rather than waiting until the final answer is judged.
However, we observe that relatively little work has examined whether an
evidence-induced teacher change is actually a reward-aligned correction.
A teacher conditioned on privileged evidence can induce shifts not only
task-relevant decisions, but also formatting, verbosity, confidence, or
reasoning style. Naively imitating such a teacher can therefore transfer
evidence-induced artifacts and destabilize learning when these changes
are not aligned with verifiable task success ([Armandpour et al.,
2026](#bib.bib13); [Harne et al., 2026](#bib.bib36)).

Table [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
summarizes this gap. GRPO ([2024](#bib.bib1)) preserves a direct reward
objective, but lacks dense token-level guidance and cannot decide which
local decisions should receive credit or correction.

Self-distillation methods such as SDPO ([Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)) provide dense token guidance, but do not by themselves
anchor each local teacher preference to verifiable reward improvement.
What is missing is not merely another weighted combination of an RL loss
and a distillation loss. Rather, the central problem is how to convert
evidence-conditioned guidance into reward-aligned token-level credit for
outcome-based policy optimization, while accounting for the cost of
changing the policy. This requires deciding which evidence-induced
corrections should be accepted, where along the response they should
act, and how strongly they should be scaled. This leads us to ask: _How
can evidence-conditioned guidance be converted into reward-aligned
token-level credit while accounting for the cost of changing the
policy?_

Bearing this in mind, we introduce VERPO, a Verified
Evidence-Regularized Policy Optimization framework. VERPO treats
evidence as a proposal for policy correction rather than as a
replacement for the outcome objective ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)).
It decomposes teacher guidance into two components: an evidence-free
reference term that preserves the behavior available without privileged
evidence, and a signed evidence-induced correction that proposes how the
policy should move at each token. This separation allows the method to
distinguish reference preservation from evidence-driven change, instead
of collapsing both into a single imitation target.

To this end, VERPO uses a stopped token-wise ZPD controller, named after
the zone of proximal development ([Vygotsky, 1978](#bib.bib35)). This
concept distinguishes independent capabilities from those attainable
with guidance. Analogously, the controller scales corrections by their
alignment with the local GRPO ([2024](#bib.bib1)) update direction and
Fisher movement cost. Guidance is strongest for reward-aligned
corrections with modest policy movement, and weakest for costly or
misaligned corrections. In
Table [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
VERPO frames reward-aligned token-level credit assignment through three
missing mechanisms: 1) selective acceptance, 2) token-wise localization,
and 3) cost-aware scaling of evidence-induced corrections. To construct
evidence directions, VERPO supports multiple teacher comparisons. The
Fix construction compares an evidence-conditioned teacher with an
evidence-free teacher, while the contrastive construction compares
teacher distributions under correct and incorrect evidence. To reduce
nuisance shifts caused by the mere presence of privileged evidence, we
introduce Fisher Evidence Contrast (FEC), which projects out the
corresponding evidence-presence component. These correction directions
can be used through two complementary update paths: they can weight the
evidence-distillation loss, modulate the token-level policy advantage,
or enter both paths through the same stopped controller. In summary, our
contributions are threefold:

- •
  We formulate evidence-conditioned self-distillation for RLVR as a
  reward-aligned token-level credit assignment problem. This formulation
  separates evidence-free reference preservation from signed
  evidence-induced policy correction, clarifying why dense teacher
  guidance should be treated as a local correction proposal rather than
  a direct imitation target.
- •
  We present VERPO, which uses a stopped token-wise ZPD controller to
  accept and scale each correction according to local reward alignment
  and Fisher movement cost. It further supports multiple
  evidence-direction constructions, including Fix, contrastive evidence
  correction, and Fisher Evidence Contrast, which removes nuisance
  shifts caused by evidence presence.
- •
  We conduct extensive experiments on a five-task suite spanning
  scientific reasoning and tool use ([Feng et al., 2024](#bib.bib32);
  [Tang et al., 2023](#bib.bib33)) across three model backbones.
  Diagnostic analyses further reveal selective, task-dependent
  correction behavior, showing that evidence is most useful when it is
  locally reward-aligned and incurs a small Fisher movement cost.

## 2 Related Work

#### Outcome Rewards and Token-level Supervision.

GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) learns from verifiable outcomes
using sequence-level advantages shared across tokens. Process
supervision, including Let’s Verify Step by Step ([Lightman et al.,
2024](#bib.bib28)) and Math-Shepherd ([Wang et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)),
supplies finer-grained targets through step verification ([Setlur et
al., 2025](#bib.bib30)). Math-Shepherd ([Wang et al., 2024](#bib.bib29))
constructs step labels automatically, reducing dependence on human
annotations while retaining an explicit process supervision stage. This
distinction separates the source of supervision from the granularity at
which it is applied: a reliable final outcome does not itself identify
which intermediate decisions deserve correction ([Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)). VERPO retains outcome-based optimization and uses
Teacher replay to propose local corrections without a learned process
verifier.

#### Self-distillation and Reward Alignment.

On-policy distillation ([Agarwal et al., 2023](#bib.bib5); [Gu et al.,
2023](#bib.bib6)) trains on student-generated trajectories with teacher
feedback, addressing the mismatch between fixed training sequences and
the student’s own generations. Privileged self-distillation instead
obtains an informative Teacher view by enriching the context available
during replay ([Lu et al., 2026](#bib.bib11)). OPSD ([Zhao et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib7)) and SDPO ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)) obtain
dense supervision from self-teachers conditioned on privileged solutions
or feedback. SRPO ([Li et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)) routes samples between
reward optimization and self-distillation, while RLSD ([Yang et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib10)) modulates distillation with reward-directed signals.
These mechanisms motivate selective guidance, but do not determine
token-wise acceptance by jointly measuring local reward alignment and
policy movement cost. VERPO uses a stopped ZPD weight to make this
decision for each eligible token. It keeps the evidence-free reference
term active when an evidence correction is rejected, separating
preservation of the base behavior from acceptance of a proposed change.

#### Contrastive and Selective Privileged Supervision.

Contrastive methods separate task evidence from contextual shifts.
RLCSD ([Pan et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)) contrasts correct and incorrect
hints to suppress style drift. CEPO ([Heakl et al., 2026](#bib.bib19))
uses correct-versus-wrong evidence for token credit, while OCSD ([Yang
et al., 2026b](#bib.bib20)) contrasts matched replay views to discount
shared scaffold effects. Selective methods instead restrict where
supervision applies: EDGE-OPD ([Lazaridis et al., 2026](#bib.bib14))
masks tokens by positive evidence, and ROSD ([Zhao et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib15)) localizes correction to erroneous spans.
Distillation studies identify reference-trajectory bias in privileged
Teachers ([Harne et al., 2026](#bib.bib36)) and weaker gradient
alignment on correct than incorrect rollouts ([Armandpour et al.,
2026](#bib.bib13)). SA-OPD ([Jiang et al., 2026](#bib.bib17)) filters
signals that combine weak input grounding with extreme divergence. These
approaches address evidence relevance, correction location, and signal
reliability. VERPO additionally separates evidence-free reference
restoration from signed correction, uses FEC to attenuate a measured
evidence-presence component under the Fisher metric, and accepts
token-wise corrections according to local reward alignment and movement
cost.

provides the extended comparison.

## 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization

### 3.1 Overview of VERPO

VERPO separates signed Teacher corrections from token-wise acceptance.
ZPD scales each correction by local reward alignment and Fisher movement
cost, while an evidence-free reference anchors the Actor’s behavior. We
use forward-KL geometry, denoted FKL.
Appendix [E.3](#A5.SS3 "E.3 Reverse-KL Counterpart ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
derives the reverse-KL counterpart, denoted RKL.

summarizes four training stages:

- •
  Stage 1: Rollout Sampling and Outcome Signal. The Actor samples
  response groups, and verifier rewards yield the GRPO ([Shao et al.,
  2024](#bib.bib1)) advantage $`\widehat{A}_{i}`$.
- •
  Stage 2: Token-Level Evidence Correction. Teacher replay under

- •
  Stage 3: ZPD Weight Estimation. Local alignment and Fisher cost
  determine each eligible token’s stopped weight $`w_{i,t}^{*}`$,

  Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").

### 3.2 FKL Evidence Direction and OPSD Decomposition

We separate reference restoration from evidence correction using base
and enriched Teacher endpoints $`q_{b},q_{e}`$, defined in
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").
The Actor $`p_{i,t}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{i},y_{i,<t})`$ uses the
original prompt and sampled prefix. For one Teacher comparison, define
$`\Delta=q_{e}-q_{b}`$ and $`q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=q_{b}+w\Delta`$ for
$`0\leq w\leq 1`$. Here $`\Delta`$ specifies the signed evidence
correction, while $`w`$ controls how far the Teacher target moves from
$`q_{b}`$ toward $`q_{e}`$. The path preserves normalization and
nonnegativity. Holding the Teacher endpoints and $`w`$ fixed, and
assuming positive Actor probability on the Teacher support, we expand
the forward KL as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathrm{KL}(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}\|p)=\mathrm{KL}(q_{b}\|p)-w\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta(v)\log p(v)+C(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}},q_{b}),
``` |  | (1) |

The first term restores $`q_{b}`$, the second contributes $`w\Delta`$ to
the negative logit gradient, and $`C`$ has no Actor gradient.
Appendix [E.2](#A5.SS2 "E.2 Exact Forward-KL Decomposition ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
derives both in
Equations ([E.5](#A5.E5 "In E.2 Exact Forward-KL Decomposition ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
and ([E.6](#A5.E6 "In E.2 Exact Forward-KL Decomposition ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")).
We therefore restore an independent evidence-free reference
$`q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ref}}`$ through

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t}a_{i,t}\mathrm{KL}(q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ref}}\|p_{i,t}),
``` |  | (2) |

Here $`a_{i,t}`$ selects valid response tokens and $`N`$ counts them.
This loss remains active independently of evidence mask $`m_{i,t}`$ and
stopped acceptance $`w_{i,t}`$, which control the correction. Thus,
rejecting an evidence-induced change does not disable reference
restoration. The evidence loss is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPSD}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(w)=-\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{evi}}}\sum_{i,t}m_{i,t}w_{i,t}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{i,t}(v)\log p_{i,t}(v).
``` |  | (3) |

$`Z_{\mathrm{evi}}`$ counts eligible tokens with a floor of one, and
zero weights remove corrections. These losses use the full vocabulary.
Appendix [H.4](#A8.SS4 "H.4 Method-Specific Hyperparameters ‣ Appendix H Experimental Details ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
details finite-support computation.

### 3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller

To derive acceptance, let $`\theta_{\mathrm{base}}`$ denote a
hypothetical Actor state after GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) and
reference updates. Training instead uses a joint loss, with
$`\bar{\theta}`$ denoting the EMA Teacher. For logits $`z_{i,t}`$ and
Jacobian $`G_{i,t}=\partial z_{i,t}/\partial\theta`$, the FKL direction
$`u_{i,t}=\Delta_{i,t}`$ induces $`d_{i,t}=G_{i,t}^{\top}u_{i,t}`$. At
step size $`\eta>0`$, ideal acceptance is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
w_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ideal}}=\arg\max_{0\leq w\leq 1}J_{\mathrm{RL}}\!\left(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+\eta wd_{i,t}\right).
``` |  | (4) |

For twice-differentiable $`J_{\mathrm{RL}}`$, a second-order expansion
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+\eta wd_{i,t})-J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})\approx\eta w\,\nabla J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})^{\top}d_{i,t}+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2}w^{2}\,d_{i,t}^{\top}\nabla^{2}J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})d_{i,t}.
``` |  | (5) |

The linear term measures reward alignment, but the quadratic term
penalizes movement only under negative directional curvature. We use
local alignment and nonnegative Fisher cost proxies instead. With
sampled-token one-hot vector $`e_{y_{i,t}}`$, $`[x]_{+}=\max(x,0)`$, and
$`F(p)=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}`$, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{h_{i,t}=\left[\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t})^{\top}u_{i,t}\right]_{+},\qquad c_{i,t}=u_{i,t}^{\top}F(p_{i,t})u_{i,t}.}
``` |  | (6) |

#### Selective Acceptance.

$`h_{i,t}`$ measures positive alignment with the unclipped local
GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) score direction, not verified
improvement. For nonzero $`\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t})`$ and
$`u_{i,t}`$, an angle below $`90^{\circ}`$ gives a positive inner
product and hence $`h_{i,t}>0`$, as illustrated in
Figure [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").
Fisher cost still controls the acceptance magnitude.
Equation ([7](#S3.E7 "In Cost-aware Scaling. ‣ 3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
assigns zero weight to nonpositive alignment.

#### Token-wise Localization.

Despite the shared advantage $`\widehat{A}_{i}`$, prefix-dependent
$`p_{i,t}`$ and $`u_{i,t}`$ give token-specific weights. The effective
weight $`m_{i,t}w_{i,t}^{*}`$ combines evidence eligibility with local
acceptance, independently of restoration.

#### Cost-aware Scaling.

A small logit step $`\delta u_{i,t}`$ incurs second-order KL movement
$`\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}c_{i,t}`$.
Appendix [E.6](#A5.SS6 "E.6 Benefit and Local-KL Cost Proxies ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
derives this Fisher approximation. We combine movement cost with a
positive floor as
$`\Lambda_{i,t}=\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c_{i,t}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}>0`$,
where $`\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}\geq 0`$ scales cost and
$`\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}>0`$ keeps the denominator positive. We then
maximize the surrogate
$`f_{i,t}(w)=h_{i,t}\log w+\Lambda_{i,t}\log(1-w)`$ over $`0<w<1`$. For
positive benefit, its first term discourages zero acceptance, while the
second penalizes full acceptance. This surrogate balances the local
proxies without asserting an exact reward improvement. For
$`h_{i,t}>0`$, strict concavity and
$`f^{\prime}_{i,t}(w)=h_{i,t}/w-\Lambda_{i,t}/(1-w)=0`$ give a unique
maximizer, extended to zero at $`h_{i,t}=0`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{w_{i,t}^{*}=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\frac{h_{i,t}}{h_{i,t}+\Lambda_{i,t}}\right]=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\frac{h_{i,t}}{h_{i,t}+\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c_{i,t}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}}\right].}
``` |  | (7) |

The stopped weight lies in $`[0,1)`$, increases with benefit at fixed
cost, and decreases with cost when both benefit and
$`\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}`$ are positive. Otherwise it is nonincreasing.
Stop-gradient blocks controller backpropagation.
Appendix [E.7](#A5.SS7 "E.7 Surrogate Acceptance Optimization and Properties ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
proves these properties and the zero-benefit convention.

### 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast

Figure 4: Evidence-presence shifts motivate FEC. (a) divergence under
correct, incorrect, and shuffled evidence; (b) task-contrast versus
evidence-presence displacement norms.

Fisher Evidence Contrast (FEC) subtracts the task contrast’s regularized
Fisher projection onto a measured evidence-presence direction.
Figure [4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
motivates this separation: incorrect and shuffled evidence also shift
the Teacher, although this shift need not be entirely task-irrelevant.

#### Evidence Contexts.

The frozen Teacher branches $`q^{0}`$, $`q^{+}`$, and $`q^{-}`$ score
the same sampled prefix with no evidence, the ground-truth answer, and a
wrong answer, respectively.

#### Endpoint Comparisons.

Fix ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)) uses
$`\Delta^{\mathrm{Fix}}=q^{+}-q^{0}`$. Contrastive correction, denoted
CTR, follows RLCSD ([Pan et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)) with
$`\Delta^{\mathrm{CTR}}=q^{+}-q^{-}`$.

#### FEC Refinement.

To attenuate the contrast along the measured evidence-presence
direction, we average the correct and incorrect Teacher branches and
compare this midpoint with the evidence-free branch. We then measure the
task contrast’s component along this direction under the Fisher metric
with a positive stabilizer $`\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}>0`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta^{\mathrm{nuis}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(q^{+}+q^{-}\right)-q^{0},\qquad\alpha^{\mathrm{FEC}}=\frac{(\Delta^{\mathrm{CTR}})^{\top}F(p)\Delta^{\mathrm{nuis}}}{(\Delta^{\mathrm{nuis}})^{\top}F(p)\Delta^{\mathrm{nuis}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}}.
``` |  | (8) |

The coefficient normalizes the Fisher inner product by the regularized
squared length of the presence proxy. Subtracting this component gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta^{\mathrm{FEC}}=\Delta^{\mathrm{CTR}}-\alpha^{\mathrm{FEC}}\Delta^{\mathrm{nuis}}.
``` |  | (9) |

Regularization attenuates the residual’s Fisher inner product with the
nuisance proxy without guaranteeing exact orthogonality. We stop
gradients through all of $`\Delta^{\mathrm{FEC}}`$, including its
Actor-dependent coefficient, and use it in the evidence loss and
controller.
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ FEC Refinement. ‣ 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
summarizes the variants alongside a geometric illustration of the
endpoint comparisons and FEC refinement.

### 3.5 Two Actor-Update Paths

The stopped controller supports two paths, evaluated separately. Path 1
modulates the GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) advantage, while
Path 2 scales the signed correction. For Path 1, the masked weight
$`\widetilde{w}_{i,t}=m_{i,t}w_{i,t}^{*}`$ combines evidence eligibility
with controller acceptance at each sampled token. For
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\geq 0`$, the modulated advantage is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{A}^{\prime}_{i,t}=\widehat{A}_{i}[1+\chi_{\mathrm{adv}}\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\operatorname{sg}(\widetilde{w}_{i,t})].
``` |  | (10) |

The positive stopped multiplier preserves the advantage sign and
clipping decision, equals one for zero or ineligible acceptance, and
contributes no gradient. With switches
$`\chi_{\mathrm{OPSD}},\chi_{\mathrm{adv}}\in\{0,1\}`$, the joint
objective is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\widehat{A}^{\prime})+\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}+\chi_{\mathrm{OPSD}}\lambda_{\mathrm{evi}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPSD}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(w^{*}).}
``` |  | (11) |

The outcome loss supplies the task signal, while
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ and $`\lambda_{\mathrm{evi}}`$ scale
reference restoration and accepted correction. Path 1 uses
$`(\chi_{\mathrm{adv}},\chi_{\mathrm{OPSD}})=(1,0)`$ and Path 2 uses
$`(0,1)`$.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

We use instruction-tuned Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B ([Yang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib34)), and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct ([Meta, 2024](#bib.bib37))
as backbones. Following the evaluation setup of SDPO ([Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)), we consider five benchmarks: Chemistry, Physics,
Biology, Materials, and Tool Use. The four science benchmarks use the
reasoning subsets of SciKnowEval ([Feng et al., 2024](#bib.bib32)) to
evaluate undergraduate-level scientific reasoning, while Tool Use uses
ToolAlpaca ([Tang et al., 2023](#bib.bib33)) to evaluate tool-call
generation given a user request and tool specifications. We compare
against GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)), which learns from
group-relative outcome rewards; SDPO ([Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)), which obtains dense token supervision from a
feedback-conditioned teacher; SRPO ([Li et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)), which
routes successful samples to the GRPO objective and unsuccessful samples
to the SDPO objective; RLSD ([Yang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib10)), which
uses verifier rewards to modulate self-distillation; and RLCSD ([Pan et
al., 2026](#bib.bib18)), which contrasts teachers conditioned on correct
and incorrect hints. We evaluate two VERPO variants: VERPO-LW applies
the ZPD weights to the evidence loss, while VERPO-AM uses them to
modulate the GRPO advantages.
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
reports each task’s best-checkpoint avg@16 and their unweighted mean as
Average. The test split also supplies the logged validation scores used
for selection.
Appendix [H](#A8 "Appendix H Experimental Details ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
clarifies this protocol and its limitations.

## 5 Conclusion

In this work, we introduce VERPO, a framework combining verifiable
outcome rewards with dense evidence-conditioned supervision without
assuming equal reliability of Teacher proposals. VERPO separates
evidence-free reference restoration from signed evidence-induced
correction, uses FEC to remove the measured evidence-presence component,
and applies a token-wise ZPD controller to selectively accept
corrections based on alignment with the local GRPO direction and
associated Fisher movement costs. Across five benchmarks and three model
backbones, VERPO achieves the best average performance within each model
block. Ablation studies show that stability depends on the Teacher
update rule, evidence construction, and KL geometry, while fine-grained
analysis finds accepted corrections concentrated mainly on intermediate
reasoning rather than final-answer tokens.

## Appendix A Extended Related Work

This appendix expands the discussion in
Section [2](#S2 "2 Related Work ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
covering outcome-based credit assignment, self-distillation, and
selective privileged supervision in greater detail.

#### RLVR and Fine-grained Credit Assignment.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) optimizes language
models using automatically checkable outcome rewards. Critic-free
methods such as GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) replace
traditional actor–critic value estimates with group-relative advantages
computed from multiple on-policy responses. DeepSeek-R1 ([DeepSeek-AI,
2025](#bib.bib2)) and DAPO ([Yu et al., 2025](#bib.bib3)) use related
group-relative updates for scalable RLVR. These sequence-level
advantages are shared across response tokens, obscuring which local
decisions caused the outcome. Process supervision obtains finer-grained
signals through human-annotated step verification in Let’s Verify Step
by Step ([Lightman et al., 2024](#bib.bib28)), automatically constructed
labels in Math-Shepherd ([Wang et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)), and
outcome-derived progress estimates in Rewarding Progress ([Setlur et
al., 2025](#bib.bib30)). Such approaches require human annotations or
automatically generated process targets together with a learned
verifier, whose local preferences need not match the verifiable
sequence-level objective. VERPO retains the outcome-derived GRPO
advantage as the task-level anchor and uses privileged Teacher replay to
propose token-level corrections, accepting them only when they align
with the local reward direction at justified Fisher movement cost.

#### On-policy Distillation and Self-distillation.

Knowledge distillation transfers a teacher’s predictive distribution to
a student ([Hinton et al., 2015](#bib.bib4)). On-policy distillation
reduces off-policy distribution shift by supervising student-generated
trajectories with an external teacher ([Agarwal et al.,
2023](#bib.bib5)). MiniLLM develops a related distribution-matching
formulation for language-model distillation ([Gu et al.,
2023](#bib.bib6)). OPSD removes the external teacher by conditioning the
same model on privileged solutions ([Zhao et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7)),
and SDPO constructs a feedback-conditioned self-teacher for dense
token-level supervision ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)). SRPO
anchors self-distillation to verifiable outcomes through sample-level
routing ([Li et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). RLSD modulates the magnitude of
self-distillation with a reward-directed signal ([Yang et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib10)), and EviSD applies analogous reward-anchored
modulation to evidence-conditioned search actions ([Xie et al.,
2026](#bib.bib12)). RLCSD uses contrastive privileged contexts to shape
its self-distillation signal  ([Pan et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)). These
methods do not jointly separate evidence-free reference retention from
signed evidence-induced change, and they do not decide whether an
individual correction is worth applying at its local policy cost. VERPO
addresses this control gap with a stopped token-wise ZPD controller that
accepts and scales each correction according to its alignment with the
local GRPO direction and its Fisher movement cost.

#### Selective and Contrastive Privileged Supervision.

Empirical studies show that privileged supervision is not uniformly
reliable across rollouts or tokens ([Armandpour et al.,
2026](#bib.bib13); [Harne et al., 2026](#bib.bib36); [Lazaridis et al.,
2026](#bib.bib14); [Zhao et al., 2026b](#bib.bib15); [Kaur et al.,
2026](#bib.bib16); [Jiang et al., 2026](#bib.bib17)). Teacher guidance
is better aligned on incorrect than on already-correct
trajectories ([Armandpour et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)), while privileged
conditioning can bias the Teacher toward a particular reference
trajectory rather than toward correctness in general ([Harne et al.,
2026](#bib.bib36)), induce behavior unrelated to the intended
correction ([Lazaridis et al., 2026](#bib.bib14)), overwrite valid
reasoning prefixes ([Zhao et al., 2026b](#bib.bib15)), suppress
verification and backtracking in thinking models ([Kaur et al.,
2026](#bib.bib16)), or amplify weakly input-grounded signals ([Jiang et
al., 2026](#bib.bib17)). RLCSD addresses privilege-induced style drift
by contrasting self-teachers conditioned on correct and incorrect
hints ([Pan et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)). CEPO similarly exploits
correct-wrong evidence for contrastive token credit ([Heakl et al.,
2026](#bib.bib19)), while OCSD uses structurally matched privileged and
ablated replay views to remove replay-scaffold effects in agentic
RL ([Yang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib20)). These contrastive constructions
reduce shared effects between privileged branches, but a Teacher
difference can still contain a component caused by the mere presence of
privileged evidence. Such a component can leak evidence-specific style,
confidence, or formatting into the Student without improving task
success. VERPO’s Fisher Evidence Contrast (FEC) estimates this
evidence-presence direction and projects it out of the task contrast
under the local Fisher metric, reducing privileged-context leakage
before the resulting correction is applied.

## Appendix D Preliminaries

To specify the quantities used by
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ Appendix C Algorithm ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
this appendix defines rollout normalization, the outcome objective,
frozen Teacher replay, and local KL geometry. These conventions support
the loss decompositions and controller approximations in
Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").

### D.1 Autoregressive Policy

To compare Actor and Teacher predictions at the same positions, we index
both distributions by the sampled response prefix. For each prompt, the
frozen behavior Actor $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ samples a group of
responses. Index $`i`$ ranges over responses, and $`\mathcal{G}(i)`$
denotes the responses generated for the same prompt as response $`i`$.
The current Actor factorizes as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{\theta}(y_{i}\mid x_{i})=\prod_{t=1}^{T_{i}}p_{i,t}(y_{i,t}),\qquad p_{i,t}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{i},y_{i,<t}).
``` |  | (D.1) |

Each factor scores one observed token rather than a new Teacher
completion. When padded tensors are used, $`a_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}`$ is the
valid-response-token mask. Analytic sums over $`t\leq T_{i}`$ are
equivalently restricted to $`a_{i,t}=1`$. The binary evidence mask
satisfies $`m_{i,t}\leq a_{i,t}`$, and the normalizers are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
N=\sum_{i,t}a_{i,t}=\sum_{i}T_{i},\qquad Z_{\mathrm{evi}}=\max\!\left(1,\sum_{i,t}a_{i,t}m_{i,t}\right).
``` |  | (D.2) |

We assume a batch contains at least one valid response token, so
$`N>0`$. The reference and outcome losses average over these tokens. The
evidence loss averages over eligible tokens without renormalizing by
acceptance weight. If none are eligible, its numerator vanishes and the
floor keeps its normalizer defined.

To measure reward alignment, we use the unclipped local GRPO ([Shao et
al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) score direction. For Actor logits $`z_{i,t}`$ and
sampled-token one-hot vector $`e_{y_{i,t}}`$, this direction is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{RL}}:=\widehat{A}_{i}\nabla_{z_{i,t}}\log p_{i,t}(y_{i,t})=\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t}).
``` |  | (D.3) |

This vector converts the sequence advantage into a token-local
comparison direction for ZPD. It is not the gradient of the full clipped
training loss, which also depends on importance ratios and clipping.

### D.2 RLVR and Group-Relative Policy Optimization

To obtain the outcome signal used in
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Overview of VERPO ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
we evaluate each sampled response with a scalar verifier reward
$`R_{i}`$. Our GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) variant centers
this reward within its prompt group without dividing by the within-group
standard deviation:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{A}_{i}=R_{i}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}(i)|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}(i)}R_{j}.
``` |  | (D.4) |

The advantage measures performance relative to responses to the same
prompt and is broadcast across the response tokens. A group with
identical rewards has zero advantage for every response.

To account for the difference between the current and behavior policies,
define the sampled-token importance ratio as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\rho_{i,t}(\theta)=\frac{p_{i,t}(y_{i,t})}{p_{i,t}^{\mathrm{old}}(y_{i,t})}
``` |  |

The denominator remains fixed during the Actor update. With asymmetric
clipping radii $`\epsilon_{\mathrm{low}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}}>0`$,
the outcome loss is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t}a_{i,t}\min\!\left\{\rho_{i,t}(\theta)\widehat{A}_{i},\,\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\rho_{i,t}(\theta),1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{low}},1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}}\right)\widehat{A}_{i}\right\}.
``` |  | (D.5) |

The minimum limits the incentive to move a sampled-token ratio farther
in the advantage-favored direction after crossing its clipping
threshold. The valid-token mask excludes padding, and $`N`$ fixes the
averaging scale.
Section [3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Two Actor-Update Paths ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
changes the token advantage in this loss while retaining the same ratio
and clipping rule.

### D.3 Feedback-Conditioned Teacher Replay

To construct evidence comparisons without changing the sampled
trajectory, we replay its prefixes under different Teacher contexts.
SDPO ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)) and SRPO ([Li et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)) also use feedback-conditioned replay for dense
supervision. VERPO represents each context condition $`c`$ through the
stopped replay operator

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{i,t}^{(c)}=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}\!\left(\cdot\mid\operatorname{reprompt}(x_{i},c),y_{i,<t}\right)\right].
``` |  | (D.6) |

The reprompt changes the Teacher’s evidence context, while the Actor
prefix $`y_{i,<t}`$ stays fixed. Teacher forcing therefore scores the
original trajectory without generating a replacement completion. The
stop-gradient operator prevents this supervision branch from receiving
Actor-loss gradients.

The Teacher parameters $`\bar{\theta}`$ are an
exponential-moving-average copy of the Actor across optimizer steps,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\theta}\leftarrow\gamma\bar{\theta}+(1-\gamma)\theta,\qquad 0<\gamma<1,
``` |  | (D.7) |

Here $`\gamma`$ retains the previous Teacher parameters and $`1-\gamma`$
weights the newly updated Actor. The Teacher changes between optimizer
steps but remains frozen within each Actor update. Its parameters
$`\bar{\theta}`$ are distinct from the hypothetical Actor state
$`\theta_{\mathrm{base}}`$ used only in the controller derivation.

### D.4 Divergence and Local Information Geometry

To distinguish the two loss geometries, we fix the argument order of the
KL divergence ([Kullback and Leibler, 1951](#bib.bib21)). For positive,
normalized distributions over the vocabulary, our conventions are

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}q(v)\log\frac{q(v)}{p(v)},`$ |  | (D.8) |
|  | $`\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{FKL}}(q,p)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{KL}(q\|p),\qquad D_{\mathrm{RKL}}(p,q)=\mathrm{KL}(p\|q).`$ |  |  |

FKL averages the log-density ratio under the Teacher, whereas RKL
averages it under the Actor. We require positive support wherever these
logarithms are evaluated. Finite-logit softmax distributions satisfy
this assumption in exact arithmetic. The finite-support implementation
is a separate approximation described in
Appendix [H.4](#A8.SS4 "H.4 Method-Specific Hyperparameters ‣ Appendix H Experimental Details ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").

Let $`z_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}`$ be the Actor logits and
$`p_{i,t}=\operatorname{softmax}(z_{i,t})`$. For vocabulary items
$`u,v\in\mathcal{V}`$, the softmax score identity is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial\log p_{i,t}(u)}{\partial z_{i,t}(v)}=\bm{1}[u=v]-p_{i,t}(v).
``` |  | (D.9) |

The Jacobian of the probabilities $`p_{i,t}`$ with respect to the
logits, not the Jacobian of their logarithms, gives the categorical
Fisher matrix. For an arbitrary vocabulary vector $`b`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(p_{i,t})=\operatorname{diag}(p_{i,t})-p_{i,t}p_{i,t}^{\top},\qquad F(p_{i,t})\bm{1}=0,\qquad b^{\top}F(p_{i,t})b=\operatorname{Var}_{V\sim p_{i,t}}[b(V)]\geq 0.
``` |  | (D.10) |

Thus constant shifts of a log-density contrast do not change its induced
logit direction. A small logit displacement
$`z_{i,t}\mapsto z_{i,t}+\epsilon b`$ induces the local policy movement

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_{i,t}\|\operatorname{softmax}(z_{i,t}+\epsilon b)\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}b^{\top}F(p_{i,t})b+O(\epsilon^{3}).
``` |  | (D.11) |

For fixed finite logits and direction $`b`$, the quadratic term measures
movement of the Actor distribution to second order as $`\epsilon`$ tends
to zero. It supplies the controller’s nonnegative movement proxy, not a
guarantee of reward improvement for a finite optimizer step.

## Appendix E Theoretical Analysis

This appendix provides the complete derivations behind the signed
evidence directions and token-wise controller in
Section [3](#S3 "3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
using the RLVR, Teacher-replay, and local-geometric conventions
collected in
Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Preliminaries ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").
We first establish exact loss identities, then distinguish the local
approximations and chosen surrogate used to construct the controller.
Teacher distributions, evidence masks, acceptance weights, and
constructed FKL directions or RKL contrasts are stopped during Actor
differentiation. This includes the Actor-dependent FEC projection
coefficients.

### E.1 Teacher Paths

To vary evidence strength while preserving a distribution, we connect
two Teacher endpoints at the same rollout prefix $`y_{<t}`$. Only the
evidence condition changes. We assume normalized distributions on a
finite vocabulary and positive Actor probability on the Teacher support.
For FKL, normalized endpoints imply $`\bm{1}^{\top}\Delta_{t}=0`$ for
$`\Delta_{t}=q_{e,t}-q_{b,t}`$. The arithmetic path is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{w,t}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=(1-w)q_{b,t}+wq_{e,t}=q_{b,t}+w\Delta_{t},\qquad 0\leq w\leq 1.
``` |  | (E.1) |

The weight interpolates from the base endpoint to the enriched endpoint.
Convex mixing preserves normalization and nonnegativity for
$`0\leq w\leq 1`$, while $`\Delta_{t}`$ records a signed probability
displacement.

For RKL, assume positive support for both endpoints and define

|     |                                             |     |       |
|-----|---------------------------------------------|-----|-------|
|     |
       ``` math
       r_{t}(v)=\log\frac{q_{e,t}(v)}{q_{b,t}(v)}.
       ```                                          |     | (E.2) |

This contrast measures relative density rather than an additive
probability change. To interpolate in these log-density coordinates,
define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{w,t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}(v)=\frac{q_{b,t}(v)^{1-w}q_{e,t}(v)^{w}}{Z_{t}(w)}=\frac{q_{b,t}(v)e^{wr_{t}(v)}}{Z_{t}(w)},\quad Z_{t}(w)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}q_{b,t}(u)e^{wr_{t}(u)}.
``` |  | (E.3) |

The partition function $`Z_{t}(w)`$ normalizes the exponentially tilted
base. Both endpoints are recovered at $`w=0`$ and $`w=1`$. Unlike the
arithmetic path, the intermediate distributions mix log densities before
normalization.

### E.2 Exact Forward-KL Decomposition

To recover the separation in
Equation ([1](#S3.E1 "In 3.2 FKL Evidence Direction and OPSD Decomposition ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")),
we expand the FKL objective with the Teacher endpoints and $`w`$ held
fixed. We suppress the token index and assume positive Actor probability
wherever the Teacher assigns mass. The loss for this single prefix is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FKL}}(\theta;w)=\mathrm{KL}(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}\|p_{\theta}).
``` |  | (E.4) |

Only its cross-entropy term depends on the Actor. Substituting
$`q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=q_{b}+w\Delta`$ into that term gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FKL}}(\theta;w)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(v)\log q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(v)\log p_{\theta}(v)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{KL}(q_{b}\|p_{\theta})-w\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta(v)\log p_{\theta}(v)+C(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}},q_{b}),`$ |  |

where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}},q_{b})=\sum_{v}q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(v)\log q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(v)-\sum_{v}q_{b}(v)\log q_{b}(v).
``` |  |

Thus the exact decomposition is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\mathrm{KL}(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}\|p)=\mathrm{KL}(q_{b}\|p)-w\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta(v)\log p(v)+C(q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}},q_{b}).}
``` |  | (E.5) |

The first term restores the base distribution, and the second isolates
the signed evidence correction. The remainder $`C`$ contains only
Teacher entropy terms and has zero Actor gradient under the stated
stop-gradient convention. This is an exact identity, without a
small-step approximation.

To verify the correction sign, differentiate with respect to the logits
of $`p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)`$ using

|     |                                                           |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \frac{\partial\log p(u)}{\partial z(v)}=\bm{1}[u=v]-p(v).
       ```                                                        |     |

Therefore

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FKL}}}{\partial z(v)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(u)\bigl(\bm{1}[u=v]-p(v)\bigr)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=p(v)-q_{w}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(v),`$ |  |

and the exact logit descent direction decomposes as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{-\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FKL}}=\underbrace{q_{b}-p}_{\text{base restoration}}+\underbrace{w\Delta}_{\text{evidence correction}}.}
``` |  | (E.6) |

The base contribution points from the Actor toward $`q_{b}`$, while
$`w\Delta`$ adds the accepted Teacher displacement. Retaining only this
second channel gives the signed evidence loss derived from OPSD ([Zhao
et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7)):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}(w)=-w\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta(v)\log p(v),\qquad-\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=w\Delta.
``` |  | (E.7) |

The zero-sum property of $`\Delta`$ cancels the softmax normalization
term in the gradient. A separate reference loss can therefore use
$`q^{\mathrm{ref}}`$ even when the evidence comparison starts at a
different base $`q_{b}`$.

### E.3 Reverse-KL Counterpart

To obtain the counterpart of the FKL separation, we substitute the
geometric Teacher path into RKL. Assume positive Actor and Teacher
distributions on the vocabulary, with $`r`$ and $`w`$ stopped. The
logarithm of the normalized path separates into a base density, a
weighted contrast, and a scalar normalizer:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RKL}}(\theta;w)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{KL}(p_{\theta}\|q_{w}^{\mathrm{RKL}})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}p(v)\left[\log p(v)-\log q_{b}(v)-wr(v)+\log Z(w)\right]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{KL}(p\|q_{b})-w\mathbb{E}_{v\sim p}[r(v)]+\log Z(w).`$ |  |

Hence

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\mathrm{KL}(p\|q_{w}^{\mathrm{RKL}})=\mathrm{KL}(p\|q_{b})-w\mathbb{E}_{v\sim p}[r(v)]+\log Z(w).}
``` |  | (E.8) |

The base term retains the original Teacher anchor. The contrast term
changes the Actor’s expected log-density preference, and $`\log Z(w)`$
completes the loss identity without contributing an Actor gradient.

To identify the resulting logit direction, apply the probability
Jacobian $`F(p)`$ to the derivative of RKL. For a fixed positive Teacher
$`q`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)`$ | $`\displaystyle=F(p)\bigl(\log p-\log q+\bm{1}\bigr)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=F(p)(\log p-\log q),`$ |  |

because $`F(p)\bm{1}=0`$. Substituting
$`\log q_{w}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=\log q_{b}+wr-\log Z(w)`$ yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{-\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RKL}}=\underbrace{F(p)(\log q_{b}-\log p)}_{\text{base restoration}}+\underbrace{w\xi}_{\text{evidence correction}},\qquad\xi=F(p)r.}
``` |  | (E.9) |

Here $`\xi`$ denotes the RKL evidence direction, written as $`u`$ in the
unified controller. Unlike the FKL displacement, it depends on the Actor
through $`F(p)`$. Writing $`\bar{r}=\mathbb{E}_{v\sim p}[r(v)]`$ exposes
this dependence:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\xi(v)=p(v)\bigl(r(v)-\bar{r}\bigr),\qquad\bm{1}^{\top}\xi=0.
``` |  | (E.10) |

The Fisher map centers the log contrast under the Actor and weights each
coordinate by its current probability. Thus $`\xi`$ sums to zero but is
not the probability displacement $`q_{e}-q_{b}`$. Its signed loss is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\mathrm{RKL}}(w)=\log Z(w)-w\mathbb{E}_{v\sim p}[r(v)],\qquad-\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=wF(p)r.
``` |  | (E.11) |

Differentiation acts through the expectation under $`p`$, not through
the stopped contrast. The resulting $`F(p)r`$ supplies the direction
used by the RKL controller and the complete gradient in
Appendix [E.9](#A5.SS9 "E.9 Complete Objective and Exact Logit Gradients ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").

### E.4 Fixed, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast

To instantiate the evidence channel in
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
we specify the endpoints and the FEC residual in both geometries. All
branches replay the same prefix. The RKL constructions require positive
Teacher support, and every projection is computed at the current Actor
distribution before its coefficient is stopped.

#### Fixed Evidence.

The Fixed construction uses the evidence-conditioned replay of
SDPO ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)) to define the endpoints
$`q_{b}=q^{0}`$ and $`q_{e}=q^{+}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{Fix}}=q_{t}^{+}-q_{t}^{0},\qquad r_{t}^{\mathrm{Fix}}=\log q_{t}^{+}-\log q_{t}^{0},\qquad u_{t}^{\mathrm{Fix},\mathrm{RKL}}=F(p_{t})r_{t}^{\mathrm{Fix}}.
``` |  | (E.12) |

This comparison includes both the change associated with correct
evidence and the change associated with presenting evidence at all. The
observed reward hacking for this tested direction is reported with
Table [3(a)](#S4.T3.st1 "In Table 3 ‣ Component Ablations. ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
not implied by this identity.

#### Contrastive Evidence.

The endpoint comparison follows RLCSD ([Pan et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)).
For $`K\geq 1`$ incorrect-evidence Teachers, define
$`q_{t}^{-}=K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}q_{t}^{-,k}`$. The construction uses
$`q_{b}=q^{-}`$ and $`q_{e}=q^{+}`$, so

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{CTR}}=q_{t}^{+}-q_{t}^{-},\qquad r_{t}^{\mathrm{CTR}}=\log q_{t}^{+}-\log q_{t}^{-},\qquad u_{t}^{\mathrm{CTR},\mathrm{RKL}}=F(p_{t})r_{t}^{\mathrm{CTR}}.
``` |  | (E.13) |

The incorrect branches are averaged in probability space before taking a
logarithm for RKL. Subtraction cancels additive shifts shared by the two
endpoints, but it retains context effects that differ between them.

#### Forward-KL FEC.

For normalized Teacher branches and a fixed Actor Fisher matrix, define
the task contrast and evidence-presence proxy. The target claim concerns
the residual Fisher inner product, without assuming that the proxy
contains only task-irrelevant information. In probability coordinates,
the directions are

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{task}}=q_{t}^{+}-q_{t}^{-},\qquad\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(q_{t}^{+}+q_{t}^{-})-q_{t}^{0}.
``` |  |

Both vectors sum to zero. The task vector equals
$`\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{CTR}}`$ in the main text, while the midpoint
comparison measures evidence presence. For the Fisher inner product
$`\langle a,b\rangle_{F(p_{t})}=a^{\top}F(p_{t})b`$, the projection
coefficient and residual are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=\frac{\langle\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{task}},\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}\rangle_{F(p_{t})}}{\langle\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}},\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}\rangle_{F(p_{t})}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}},\qquad\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}=\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{task}}-\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{FKL}}\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}.}
``` |  | (E.14) |

The coefficient $`\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{FKL}}`$ is the FKL coefficient
denoted $`\alpha^{\mathrm{FEC}}`$ in
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").
It subtracts a component along the measured nuisance direction. To
determine what regularization leaves behind, substitute this coefficient
into the residual inner product:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\langle\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}},\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}\right\rangle_{F(p_{t})}=\frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}}{\langle\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}},\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}\rangle_{F(p_{t})}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}}\left\langle\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{task}},\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}\right\rangle_{F(p_{t})}.
``` |  | (E.15) |

For $`\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}>0`$, the remaining inner product equals
the original one times a factor in $`(0,1]`$. Exact orthogonality
follows in the unregularized case only when the denominator is nonzero.
The residual still sums to zero, but adding it to a base distribution
need not preserve nonnegativity. We therefore use the stopped residual
directly in the signed loss:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FEC},t}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=-w_{t}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(v)\log p_{t}(v),\qquad-\nabla_{z_{t}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FEC},t}^{\mathrm{FKL}}=w_{t}\Delta_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}.
``` |  | (E.16) |

The stated gradient requires stopping the entire residual, including its
Actor-dependent coefficient. It recovers the direction in
Equation ([9](#S3.E9 "In FEC Refinement. ‣ 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
without claiming a valid arithmetic Teacher endpoint for FEC.

#### Reverse-KL FEC.

To apply the same projection idea in RKL geometry, first measure
evidence presence using positive Teacher branches and the normalized
geometric midpoint

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t}^{\mathrm{pres},\mathrm{RKL}}(v)=\frac{\sqrt{q_{t}^{+}(v)q_{t}^{-}(v)}}{\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\sqrt{q_{t}^{+}(u)q_{t}^{-}(u)}}
``` |  |

to measure evidence presence in log-density coordinates. Define

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle r_{t}^{\mathrm{task}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log q_{t}^{+}-\log q_{t}^{-},`$ | $`\displaystyle u_{t}^{\mathrm{task}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=F(p_{t})r_{t}^{\mathrm{task}},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle r_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log q_{t}^{\mathrm{pres},\mathrm{RKL}}-\log q_{t}^{0},`$ | $`\displaystyle u_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=F(p_{t})r_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}.`$ |  |

These log contrasts are mapped into logit directions before projection.
The midpoint normalization does not change $`u_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}`$
because $`F(p_{t})\bm{1}=0`$. Projecting in the same Fisher inner
product gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=\frac{(u_{t}^{\mathrm{task}})^{\top}F(p_{t})u_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}}{(u_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}})^{\top}F(p_{t})u_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{proj}}},\quad r_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}=r_{t}^{\mathrm{task}}-\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}r_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}},\quad u_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}=F(p_{t})r_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}.}
``` |  | (E.17) |

Linearity of $`F(p_{t})`$ gives
$`u_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}=u_{t}^{\mathrm{task}}-\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}u_{t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}`$.
Its residual Fisher inner product has the same ridge-dependent form as
Equation ([E.15](#A5.E15 "In Forward-KL FEC. ‣ E.4 Fixed, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")),
with $`u`$ replacing $`\Delta`$. We stop the constructed contrast
$`r_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}`$, including $`\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}`$,
before Actor differentiation. Using $`q_{t}^{-}`$ as the base then
defines

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{w,t}^{\mathrm{RKL},\mathrm{FEC}}(v)=\frac{q_{t}^{-}(v)e^{w_{t}r_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(v)}}{Z_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(w_{t})},\qquad Z_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(w)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}q_{t}^{-}(u)e^{wr_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(u)}.
``` |  | (E.18) |

Exponential tilting preserves a positive normalized distribution for
finite contrasts. Its endpoint at $`w=1`$ need not equal $`q_{t}^{+}`$
after projection. The corresponding signed correction is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FEC},t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=\log Z_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(w_{t})-w_{t}\mathbb{E}_{v\sim p_{t}}[r_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}(v)],\qquad-\nabla_{z_{t}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FEC},t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=w_{t}u_{t}^{\mathrm{FEC}}.
``` |  | (E.19) |

The Fisher map in this gradient comes from differentiating the Actor
expectation. No gradient flows through the projection construction. For
controller evaluation, the resulting direction and acceptance weight are
treated as fixed inputs to the Actor update.

### E.5 Ideal Token-Wise Controller

To connect acceptance to local reward improvement, we analyze one fixed
evidence direction at a time. Let
$`G_{i,t}=\partial z_{i,t}/\partial\theta`$ and
$`d_{i,t}=G_{i,t}^{\top}u_{i,t}`$ be its parameter-space descent
direction. The hypothetical Actor state $`\theta_{\mathrm{base}}`$
absorbs the outcome and reference updates for this local analysis. It
differs from the EMA Teacher state $`\bar{\theta}`$ and does not require
separate optimizer steps. At a fixed local step size $`\eta>0`$,
applying the correction with strength $`w`$ produces

|     |                                                     |     |        |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|--------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \theta^{+}(w)=\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+\eta wd_{i,t}.
       ```                                                  |     | (E.20) |

This defines a one-dimensional family of candidate Actor states rather
than an additional training loop. The ideal weight selects the state
with the largest RL objective:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{w_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ideal}}=\arg\max_{0\leq w\leq 1}J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+\eta wd_{i,t}).}
``` |  | (E.21) |

Evaluating this objective for each token is not the deployed controller.
To expose its local benefit and cost, assume that $`J_{\mathrm{RL}}`$
has locally bounded third derivatives along the fixed direction. Taylor
expansion for a sufficiently small step gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+\eta wd_{i,t})-J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\eta w\nabla J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})^{\top}d_{i,t}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2}w^{2}d_{i,t}^{\top}\nabla^{2}J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})d_{i,t}+O((\eta w)^{3}).`$ |  |

Define the directional benefit and curvature cost

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
B_{i,t}=\nabla J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})^{\top}d_{i,t},\qquad C_{i,t}=-d_{i,t}^{\top}\nabla^{2}J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})d_{i,t}.
``` |  | (E.22) |

The sign of $`B_{i,t}`$ determines first-order improvement, while
positive $`C_{i,t}`$ denotes negative reward curvature along the
direction. Under this additional condition, the quadratic model is
$`\eta wB_{i,t}-\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2}w^{2}C_{i,t}`$. Its constrained
optimum is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{w_{i,t}^{\mathrm{quad}}=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{[B_{i,t}]_{+}}{\eta C_{i,t}},0,1\right).}
``` |  | (E.23) |

This maximizes the truncated quadratic, not the original RL objective.
When $`C_{i,t}\leq 0`$, the diminishing-return argument does not apply.
The practical controller therefore replaces both quantities with
computable proxies and uses a separately chosen bounded surrogate.

### E.6 Benefit and Local-KL Cost Proxies

To avoid computing the true reward gradient and Hessian, we construct
the proxies in
Equation ([6](#S3.E6 "In 3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")).
At sampled token $`y_{i,t}`$, the unclipped local GRPO ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib1)) score gives the estimator

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla_{\theta}J_{i,t}\approx G_{i,t}^{\top}\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t}).
``` |  |

This is the parameter-space pullback of the local logit direction in
Equation ([D.3](#A4.E3 "In D.1 Autoregressive Policy ‣ Appendix D Preliminaries ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")).
It supplies a comparison direction without replacing the clipped
training objective. Substituting $`d_{i,t}=G_{i,t}^{\top}u_{i,t}`$ into
the exact directional benefit gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle\approx\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t})^{\top}G_{i,t}G_{i,t}^{\top}u_{i,t}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\approx\kappa_{i,t}\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t})^{\top}u_{i,t},\qquad\kappa_{i,t}>0,`$ |  |

where the second line uses the local isotropic approximation
$`G_{i,t}G_{i,t}^{\top}\approx\kappa_{i,t}I`$. Absorbing the unknown
positive scale and rejecting negative alignment yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{h_{i,t}=\left[\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t})^{\top}u_{i,t}\right]_{+}.}
``` |  | (E.24) |

The isotropic approximation discards anisotropy in the logit Jacobian
and the score estimator discards interactions with other tokens.
Consequently, $`h_{i,t}`$ measures local alignment rather than verified
reward improvement. Its positive part rejects nonpositive alignment
under this proxy.

The reward Hessian is generally indefinite, so $`C_{i,t}>0`$ need not
hold for the exact quadratic. Replace the negative Hessian along this
update by a positive-semidefinite Fisher surrogate, using the local
information geometry underlying natural-gradient methods ([Amari,
1998](#bib.bib22)):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
-\nabla_{\theta}^{2}J_{\mathrm{RL}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}})\approx\beta_{\mathrm{curv}}F_{\theta},\qquad F_{\theta}=G_{i,t}^{\top}F(p_{i,t})G_{i,t},\qquad\beta_{\mathrm{curv}}>0.
``` |  | (E.25) |

The positive scale $`\beta_{\mathrm{curv}}`$ belongs only to this
curvature approximation and differs from the EMA decay $`\gamma`$. This
replacement is a modeling choice, not an identity for the reward
Hessian. Reusing the isotropic approximation gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle C_{i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle\approx\beta_{\mathrm{curv}}d_{i,t}^{\top}F_{\theta}d_{i,t}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\beta_{\mathrm{curv}}(G_{i,t}d_{i,t})^{\top}F(p_{i,t})(G_{i,t}d_{i,t})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\approx\beta_{\mathrm{curv}}\kappa_{i,t}^{2}u_{i,t}^{\top}F(p_{i,t})u_{i,t}.`$ |  |

This quadratic form is the leading policy movement by
Equation ([D.11](#A4.E11 "In D.4 Divergence and Local Information Geometry ‣ Appendix D Preliminaries ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
with $`b=u_{i,t}`$. Define the Fisher movement term and absorb the
unknown positive curvature scale into the active cost coefficient:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{c_{i,t}=u_{i,t}^{\top}F(p_{i,t})u_{i,t},\qquad u_{i,t}^{\top}F(p_{i,t})u_{i,t}=\operatorname{Var}_{V\sim p_{i,t}}[u_{i,t}(V)]\geq 0.}
``` |  | (E.26) |

The variance identity establishes nonnegativity without assumptions on
the reward Hessian. For a small logit step $`\delta u_{i,t}`$, the
leading KL movement is $`\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}c_{i,t}`$. The
coefficient $`\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}`$ controls this proxy’s influence
in the deployed weight, without estimating the omitted curvature scale.

### E.7 Surrogate Acceptance Optimization and Properties

To turn the two proxies into acceptance, we use the surrogate introduced
in
Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization").
Suppress token indices and assume finite $`h\geq 0`$, $`c\geq 0`$,
$`\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}\geq 0`$, and $`\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}>0`$.
With $`\Lambda=\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}`$,
define

|     |                                                 |     |        |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------|-----|--------|
|     |
       ``` math
       f(w)=h\log w+\Lambda\log(1-w),\qquad w\in(0,1).
       ```                                              |     | (E.27) |

For positive benefit, the first term penalizes zero acceptance and the
second penalizes full acceptance. This choice retains a
benefit-versus-cost tradeoff but is not derived as an exact reward
objective. For $`h>0`$,
$`f^{\prime\prime}(w)=-h/w^{2}-\Lambda/(1-w)^{2}<0`$, so $`f`$ is
strictly concave. The first-order condition is

|     |                                    |     |
|-----|------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \frac{h}{w}-\frac{\Lambda}{1-w}=0,
       ```                                 |     |

whose unique global maximizer is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{w^{\star}=\frac{h}{h+\Lambda}=\frac{h}{h+\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}}\in[0,1).}
``` |  | (E.28) |

For $`h=0`$, the function reduces to $`\Lambda\log(1-w)`$ and has no
maximizer in the open interval. Its supremum occurs as $`w`$ tends to
zero, so we extend the solution continuously to $`w^{\star}=0`$. The
deployed controller sets $`w_{i,t}=\operatorname{sg}[w^{\star}_{i,t}]`$,
using $`w_{i,t}^{*}`$ for the same stopped weight in the main text.
These bounds require the default positive-part benefit. They do not
apply to an ablation that inserts an unclamped signed benefit into the
same fraction.

For $`h>0`$, the acceptance odds provide another interpretation of this
solution:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{w}{1-w}=\frac{h}{\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}},\qquad\operatorname{logit}(w)=\log h-\log(\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}).
``` |  | (E.29) |

The log-odds expression requires positive benefit, whereas the rational
rule also covers the zero-benefit boundary. To verify monotonicity
before stopping the weight, differentiate the rational expression:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial w^{\star}}{\partial h}=\frac{\Lambda}{(h+\Lambda)^{2}}>0,\qquad\frac{\partial w^{\star}}{\partial c}=-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}h}{(h+\Lambda)^{2}}\leq 0,\qquad\frac{\partial w^{\star}}{\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}}=-\frac{hc}{(h+\Lambda)^{2}}\leq 0.
``` |  |

Cost strictly reduces acceptance when $`h>0`$ and
$`\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}>0`$. At $`h=0`$, the controller rejects the
direction regardless of cost. The positive floor keeps acceptance below
one for finite inputs. PPO ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib31)) clips a
policy probability ratio, whereas ZPD computes a stopped scale from
evidence alignment and movement cost. Neither these monotonicity
properties nor the local proxies guarantee a reward increase. Reference
restoration remains ungated in both update paths.

### E.8 Multiplicative Advantage Modulation

To verify Path 1 in
Section [3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Two Actor-Update Paths ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
we insert acceptance into the outcome advantage instead of adding an
evidence gradient. With the path enabled, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{w}_{i,t}=m_{i,t}w_{i,t},\qquad s_{i,t}=1+\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\,\operatorname{sg}\!\left(\widetilde{w}_{i,t}\right),\qquad\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\geq 0,
``` |  | (E.30) |

and define the token-level advantage override

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\widehat{A}^{\prime}_{i,t}=s_{i,t}\widehat{A}_{i}=\widehat{A}_{i}\left[1+\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\,\operatorname{sg}\!\left(\widetilde{w}_{i,t}\right)\right].}
``` |  | (E.31) |

Here $`\widehat{A}_{i}`$ is the original group-relative sequence
advantage, repeated over valid response tokens, while $`s_{i,t}`$ is
token dependent. The stop-gradient prevents the current loss from
differentiating through the controller to change this scale. The scale
can still change when it is recomputed at a later Actor state.

To check its interaction with clipping, write the token-level loss
inherited from PPO ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib31)) using the same
importance ratio and asymmetric radii as
Equation ([D.5](#A4.E5 "In D.2 RLVR and Group-Relative Policy Optimization ‣ Appendix D Preliminaries ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell^{\mathrm{PPO}}_{i,t}(A)=-\min\!\left(\rho_{i,t}(\theta)A,\operatorname{clip}(\rho_{i,t}(\theta),1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{low}},1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}})A\right).
``` |  | (E.32) |

For the default nonnegative controller, $`s_{i,t}>0`$ is fixed during
the update, so positive scaling factors out of both branches:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell^{\mathrm{PPO}}_{i,t}(\widehat{A}^{\prime}_{i,t})=s_{i,t}\ell^{\mathrm{PPO}}_{i,t}(\widehat{A}_{i}),\qquad\nabla_{\theta}\ell^{\mathrm{PPO}}_{i,t}(\widehat{A}^{\prime}_{i,t})=s_{i,t}\nabla_{\theta}\ell^{\mathrm{PPO}}_{i,t}(\widehat{A}_{i}).
``` |  | (E.33) |

Thus positive scaling preserves the advantage sign and the selected
clipping branch, while scaling its gradient wherever the loss is
differentiable. At a clipping boundary, the same relation holds for a
consistently chosen subgradient. Ineligible tokens and zero acceptance
both give $`s_{i,t}=1`$. The mask therefore limits modulation without
changing the reference loss.

### E.9 Complete Objective and Exact Logit Gradients

To assemble the training update, we combine the two channels with the
normalizers in
Equation ([D.2](#A4.E2 "In D.1 Autoregressive Policy ‣ Appendix D Preliminaries ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")).
Assume $`N>0`$, binary $`m_{i,t}\leq a_{i,t}`$, and stopped Teacher
distributions, constructed directions, masks, and acceptance weights.
Here $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}`$ denotes the signed evidence loss
written as $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPSD}}`$ in the main text. The FKL
terms are

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t}a_{i,t}\mathrm{KL}(q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ref}}\|p_{i,t}),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{evi}}}\sum_{i,t}m_{i,t}w_{i,t}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{i,t}(v)\log p_{i,t}(v).`$ |  | (E.34) |

The reference channel averages over valid response tokens, while the
evidence channel averages over the eligible subset. Because
$`m_{i,t}\leq a_{i,t}`$, an additional factor $`a_{i,t}`$ in the
evidence numerator would be redundant. For RKL, assume positive support
and use the selected stopped log contrast $`r_{i,t}`$, including the
residual contrast when FEC is enabled:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{RKL}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t}a_{i,t}\mathrm{KL}(p_{i,t}\|q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ref}}),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\mathrm{RKL}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{evi}}}\sum_{i,t}m_{i,t}\left[\log Z_{i,t}(w_{i,t})-w_{i,t}\mathbb{E}_{v\sim p_{i,t}}[r_{i,t}(v)]\right].`$ |  | (E.35) |

The RKL partition term preserves the loss identity but contributes no
Actor gradient. The reference Teacher remains evidence-free even when
the selected contrast uses $`q^{-}`$ as its base. For binary path
switches $`\chi_{\mathrm{OPSD}},\chi_{\mathrm{adv}}`$, define
$`\widetilde{w}_{i,t}=m_{i,t}w_{i,t}`$ and
$`\widehat{A}^{\prime}_{i,t}=\widehat{A}_{i}[1+\chi_{\mathrm{adv}}\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\operatorname{sg}(\widetilde{w}_{i,t})]`$.
Combining these terms with GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)) gives
Equation ([11](#S3.E11 "In 3.5 Two Actor-Update Paths ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
under FKL and its geometry-matched RKL counterpart. Enabling only
$`\chi_{\mathrm{adv}}`$ gives Path 1, enabling only
$`\chi_{\mathrm{OPSD}}`$ gives Path 2, and enabling both gives the joint
setting. Write $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VERPO}}`$ for the complete loss
denoted $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}`$ in the main text.
Differentiating the three terms separately gives the following
token-logit descent directions, with the outcome loss evaluated using
the stopped modulated advantage. For compactness, write
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\star}=\chi_{\mathrm{OPSD}}\lambda_{\mathrm{evi}}`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle-\nabla_{z_{i,t}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VERPO}}^{\mathrm{FKL}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\nabla_{z_{i,t}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\widehat{A}^{\prime})+\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}a_{i,t}}{N}(q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ref}}-p_{i,t})+\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\star}}{Z_{\mathrm{evi}}}m_{i,t}w_{i,t}\Delta_{i,t},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle-\nabla_{z_{i,t}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VERPO}}^{\mathrm{RKL}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\nabla_{z_{i,t}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\widehat{A}^{\prime})+\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}a_{i,t}}{N}F(p_{i,t})(\log q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{ref}}-\log p_{i,t})+\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{evi}}^{\star}}{Z_{\mathrm{evi}}}m_{i,t}w_{i,t}F(p_{i,t})r_{i,t}.`$ |  | (E.36) |

These exact logit gradients recover the main-text FKL update and its RKL
counterpart under the stated stop-gradient and support assumptions. The
reference term contains neither acceptance weights nor path switches.
ZPD scales the outcome gradient through the advantage in Path 1 and the
signed evidence direction directly in Path 2. The two routes therefore
share acceptance without sharing a gradient channel.

### F.5 Probability Mass Retention under Top-K Truncation

To assess the vocabulary approximation in
Appendix [H.4](#A8.SS4 "H.4 Method-Specific Hyperparameters ‣ Appendix H Experimental Details ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
we examine the first 200 steps of five Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct ([Meta,

#### Support Construction and Measurement.

The correction support $`S`$ combines the top 128 candidates from each
of $`q^{+}`$, $`q^{-}`$, and $`q^{0}`$ with the sampled token and
removes duplicates. Thus $`K=128`$ specifies candidates per branch, not
the final support size. The reference channel uses a separate support
$`S_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ formed from its own top 128 candidates and the
sampled token. All selected probabilities retain the full softmax
normalization, without renormalizing the selected coordinates or adding
a tail bucket. For a distribution $`q`$, retained mass on $`S`$ is
$`M=\sum_{v\in S}q(v)`$.
Figure [10](#A6.F10 "Figure 10 ‣ Support Construction and Measurement. ‣ F.5 Probability Mass Retention under Top-K Truncation ‣ Appendix F Additional Results ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
reports the logged token means of $`1-M`$. The positive Teacher,
negative Teacher, and Actor share $`S`$, whereas the reference mass uses
$`S_{\mathrm{ref}}`$.

All numerical summaries use unsmoothed observations. Each logged mass

We aggregate steps using their logged token counts, rather than

#### Exact Truncated Gradient.

To identify what truncation changes, consider one token with
$`p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)`$ at unit temperature. Freeze $`S`$, the
signed direction $`\Delta`$ on its selected coordinates, and the
nonnegative weight $`w`$ during differentiation. Define the zero-padded
vector $`\Delta_{S}(v)=\mathbf{1}[v\in S]\Delta(v)`$ and the truncated
loss $`L_{S}=-w\sum_{v\in S}\Delta(v)\log p(v)`$. The softmax derivative
$`\partial\log p(v)/\partial z_{j}=\mathbf{1}[v=j]-p(j)`$ gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
s_{S}=\sum_{v\in S}\Delta(v),\qquad-\nabla_{z}L_{S}=w\bigl(\Delta_{S}-s_{S}p\bigr).
``` |  | (F.1) |

The extra term $`-ws_{S}p`$ comes from the live full-vocabulary
normalizer. It acts on selected and omitted logits, ensuring that the
logit update sums to zero even when the selected direction does not.
Since $`\|p\|_{1}=1`$ and $`w\geq 0`$, its absolute size satisfies

|     |                                                          |     |       |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|-------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \bigl\|-\nabla_{z}L_{S}-w\Delta_{S}\bigr\|_{1}=w|s_{S}|.
       ```                                                       |     | (F.2) |

Thus the simple direction $`w\Delta_{S}`$ recovers the negative loss
gradient when $`s_{S}=0`$, including a full-vocabulary difference of
normalized endpoints. A small-vocabulary finite-difference check
verifies both identities for nonzero mass defect, zero mass defect, full
support, and zero weight, with maximum coordinate error below
$`3.2\times 10^{-12}`$.

### G.1 ZPD Effective Weight Coverage

To measure the breadth of acceptance,
$`\mathrm{WeightEffectiveCoverage}`$ counts diagnostic tokens whose
stopped weight exceeds a fixed monitoring threshold:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{WeightEffectiveCoverage}=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{route}}\right|}\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{route}}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[w_{i,t}>\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}\right],\qquad\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}=10^{-3}.
``` |  | (G.1) |

The denominator counts routed diagnostic tokens, not every token in the
batch. Coverage therefore measures acceptance conditional on this set
and does not measure how often the routing mask selects tokens. The
threshold is used only for monitoring and does not enter the controller.
This metric differs from the strict nonzero fraction
$`\mathbf{1}[w_{i,t}>0]`$, which would count arbitrarily small numerical
weights as active.

### G.2 Mean Signed Benefit and Fisher Cost

To distinguish favorable and conflicting directions before rejection, we
retain signed alignment in the benefit diagnostic.
Equation ([6](#S3.E6 "In 3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
applies a positive part to this value for controller use. To expose
rejected as well as accepted directions, the logged benefit diagnostic
retains the value before this positive-part operation:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
b_{i,t}=\widehat{A}_{i}(e_{y_{i,t}}-p_{i,t})^{\top}u_{i,t},\qquad h_{i,t}=[b_{i,t}]_{+}.
``` |  | (G.2) |

The one-hot score direction compares the evidence move with the sampled
token’s outcome signal. Only $`h_{i,t}`$ enters the default acceptance
rule. The logged metric $`\mathrm{BenefitMean}`$, shown in the second
panel of
Figure [6](#S4.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ Training Dynamics. ‣ 4.4 Further Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"),
is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{BenefitMean}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{route}}|}\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{route}}}b_{i,t}.
``` |  | (G.3) |

It is a raw signed mean, not the mean of $`h_{i,t}`$ and not a
conditional mean over positive-benefit tokens. A negative value means
that the signed evidence directions oppose the local GRPO ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib1)) score direction on average. The default controller
assigns zero weight to each token with nonpositive benefit, but a
negative mean does not imply that every token is rejected. It also does
not measure the reward change after an optimizer step.

The logged metric $`\mathrm{FisherCostMean}`$, shown in the third panel,
is the mean nonnegative Fisher movement term from
Equation ([6](#S3.E6 "In 3.3 Token-Wise ZPD Controller ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{FisherCostMean}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{route}}|}\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{route}}}c_{i,t},\qquad c_{i,t}=u_{i,t}^{\top}F(p_{i,t})u_{i,t}\geq 0.
``` |  | (G.4) |

This nonnegative statistic measures local distribution movement, not
observed reward loss. It is the unscaled dynamic term in the controller
denominator. The full movement penalty is
$`\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}}c_{i,t}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cost}}`$, so the
plotted quantity excludes both the scale and the positive floor.

### G.3 FEC Residual Nuisance Fisher Covariance

To check the aggregate residual left by the projection,
$`\mathrm{FECResidualCov}`$ averages the signed Fisher covariance
between the retained FEC residual in
Equation ([9](#S3.E9 "In FEC Refinement. ‣ 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
and the measured nuisance direction in
Equation ([8](#S3.E8 "In FEC Refinement. ‣ 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{FECResidualCov}=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{FEC}}\right|}\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{FEC}}}\left\langle\Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{FEC}},\Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{nuis}}\right\rangle_{F(p_{i,t})}.
``` |  | (G.5) |

Here $`\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{FEC}}`$ contains the valid response-token
positions on which the FEC branches and projection diagnostics are
available.
Equation ([E.15](#A5.E15 "In Forward-KL FEC. ‣ E.4 Fixed, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ Appendix E Theoretical Analysis ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization"))
gives the remaining ridge-dependent covariance for each token under the
coefficient in
Equation ([8](#S3.E8 "In FEC Refinement. ‣ 3.4 Fix, Contrastive, and Fisher Evidence Contrast ‣ 3 VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")).

A small mean signed covariance does not establish token-wise
orthogonality. Positive and negative residuals can cancel in the
average, and small vector norms also produce small covariance without a
small angle.
Figure [6](#S4.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ Training Dynamics. ‣ 4.4 Further Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ VERPO: Verified Evidence Regularized Policy Optimization")
therefore provides an aggregate numerical check of the projection rather
than a bound on every residual. Neither the sign nor the magnitude
establishes a causal contribution to task accuracy.
````
