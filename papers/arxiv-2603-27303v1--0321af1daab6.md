###### Abstract

Protein scientific discovery is bottlenecked by the manual orchestration
of information and algorithms, while general agents are insufficient in
complex domain projects. VenusFactory2 provides an autonomous framework
that shifts from static tool usage to dynamic workflow synthesis via a
self-evolving multi-agent infrastructure to address protein-related
demands. It outperforms a set of well-known agents on the VenusAgentEval
benchmark, and autonomously organizes the discovery and optimization of
proteins from a single natural language prompt.

Scientific discovery is propelled by the synergistic co-evolution of
tools and intelligence [Asai et al. (2026)](#bib.bib1), while protein
serves as the primary substrate for this technological evolution within
life sciences [Gao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib2). In this context, the
integration of deep learning into protein science has yielded
substantial improvements in specific tasks, such as structure prediction
[Jumper et al. (2021)](#bib.bib3); [Abramson et al. (2024)](#bib.bib4),
functional annotation [Yu et al. (2023)](#bib.bib5); [Wang et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib6), and directed evolution [Yang et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib7); [Tan et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8). While these models
have achieved unprecedented accuracy, they are typically deployed as
isolated, static command-line interfaces with incompatible data formats
[Su et al. (2025)](#bib.bib9). This disconnection between conceptual
biological intent and low-level programmatic execution forces
researchers to dedicate disproportionate cognitive resources to
low-level pipeline orchestration and software dependency resolution,
thereby decoupling algorithmic intelligence from high-level biological
intuition.

Recent efforts such as Jupyter notebooks [Mirdita et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib10); [Su et al. (2025)](#bib.bib9) or graphical user
interfaces [Abramson et al. (2024)](#bib.bib4); [Van Kempen et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib11) have simplified the execution of individual tools;
however, they primarily simplify the execution of individual tools
rather than resolving the fundamental challenge of knowledge-driven
orchestration
(Fig. [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")a).
Protein engineering campaigns are rarely isolated algorithmic tasks;
rather, they necessitate the seamless synthesis of domain
knowledge—mined from vast literature—with heterogeneous computational
predictions (e.g., using AlphaFold prediction for ESM designed
mutations) [Swanson et al. (2025)](#bib.bib12). In existing frameworks,
bridging the gap between conceptual biological reasoning and technical
execution demands extensive manual intervention [Jiang et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib13); [Seo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib14). Researchers are
burdened not only with data wrangling but also with the cognitive load
of contextually aligning algorithmic outputs with biological hypotheses.
Consequently, scalability is constrained not by computational power, but
by the temporal overhead and cognitive burden of orchestrating these
disjointed workflows [M. Bran et al. (2024)](#bib.bib15); [Ding et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib16).

To address these limitations, we introduce VenusFactory2, an autonomous
framework that elevates the interaction paradigm from rigid tool
execution to collaborative scientific reasoning specifically tailored
for protein engineering and discovery. VenusFactory2 mimics a digital
research laboratory
(Fig. [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")b)
populated by specialized agents acting as Principal Investigator,
Machine Learning Specialist, Computational Biologist, and Scientific
Critic [Swanson et al. (2025)](#bib.bib12). By simulating the
collaboration in real human research teams, the system does not merely
execute pipelines but proactively translates abstract biological
objectives into rigorous experimental plans. Crucially,
VenusFactory2 achieves self-evolving capability expansion: it detects
methodological gaps and autonomously synthesizes—then deploys—executable
computational tools to resolve unforeseen challenges, enabling
open-ended discovery without manual intervention.

As illustrated in
Figure [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")c,
we simplify the research workflow of VenusFactory2 into a four-phase
protocol orchestrated by role-specific agents. (1) Objective: The
process initiates with the human researcher specifying high-level
biological objectives and optional dataset constraints. (2) Research:
The Principal Investigator agent conducts a feasibility analysis by
cross-referencing external literature with internal tool capabilities,
engaging in iterative dialogue to deeply research the experimental
design. (3) Implementation: This phase is synergized by the Machine
Learning Specialist and Computational Biologist. The former focuses on
code review, execution, and new computational tools development, while
the latter selects appropriate utilities to check and assemble the
analysis pipeline. (4) Summary: Finally, the Scientific Critic agent
aggregates outputs, execution logs, and reasoning traces to audit
biological validity and generate the final report.

To systematically assess procedural planning and reasoning capabilities
within the domains of protein discovery and directed evolution, we
established VenusAgentEval
(Fig. [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")d),
an expert-reviewed benchmark comprising $`148`$ validated instances
stratified into Question-, Task-, and Project-level complexities.
Formally, each evaluation task is defined as a tuple
$`T=(Q,P,\mathcal{C})`$, where the query $`Q`$ is paired with a
multi-modal prompt context $`P`$—encompassing amino acid sequences,
local file paths, and database identifiers—and a set of ground-truth
constraints $`\mathcal{C}`$ for objective scoring.

Comparative evaluation on the stratified VenusAgentEval benchmark
reveals distinct failure modes in existing paradigms
(Fig. [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")d).
VenusFactory2 demonstrates superior robustness across all complexity
tiers, achieving a weighted Project-level score of 78.8%. In sharp
contrast, generalist models such as DeepSeek-V3.1, while competitive in
atomic reasoning tasks (Task-level: 67.2%), suffer a catastrophic
performance collapse in long-horizon workflows (plummeting to 18.2% at
Project-level). This trajectory indicates that the implicit
Chain-of-Thought capabilities of general-purpose LLMs degrade rapidly
under the accumulation of biological context. Conversely,
domain-specific baselines like SciToolAgent [Ding et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib16) and ProtAgent [Ghafarollahi and Buehler
(2024)](#bib.bib17) exhibit architectural rigidity, failing
significantly in open-ended retrieval ($`<25\%`$ Question-level) due to
hard-coded pipelines. VenusFactory2 overcomes both the contextual
fragility of generalists and the procedural rigidity of specialists,
employing its explicit role-playing architecture to maintain logical
consistency across multi-step discovery campaigns.

To address the incremental demands of protein engineering, the execution
capability of VenusFactory2 integrates over 100 high-level computational
utilities
(Fig. [2](#S0.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")a),
stratified into four functional quadrants (Methods). The workflow is
anchored by the Research module, which implements retrieval-augmented
generation across major academic and biological databases [Sayers et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib18); [Consortium (2025)](#bib.bib19) to ground
experimental hypotheses. For candidate identification, the Protein
Discovery quadrant orchestrates structural or embedding alignment
algorithms with biophysical filters [Van Kempen et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib11); [Wu et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20), while the Directed
Evolution quadrant employs generative mutation models to optimize
functional properties under epistatic constraints [Su et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib21); [Tan et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8). Complementing these
pre-defined capabilities, the Auto-ML Infrastructure serves as an
adaptive engine [Tan et al. (2025)](#bib.bib22), enabling the runtime
encapsulation of user-specific datasets into deployable tools when
standard methods are insufficient.

The operational logic of VenusFactory2 initiates with the Research
module
(Fig. [2](#S0.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")b),
where the Principal Investigator agent translates high-level biological
objectives into structured execution protocols. Upon receiving project
constraints, the agent decomposes the objective into specific retrieval
sub-tasks, executing a hierarchical search strategy across web content,
academic literature, and patent databases to ground the scientific
hypothesis. To guarantee experimental feasibility, the module implements
a constraint verification loop: the agent engages in structured dialogue
to resolve ambiguities and performs a resource feasibility analysis by
cross-referencing retrieved methodologies against the system’s internal
tool library. This process culminates in a formalized research
plan—comprising current status, methodological workflow, and tool
invocation sequences—that serves as a deterministic blueprint to
minimize hallucinatory deviations during downstream execution.

The framework overcomes the limitations of static libraries by enabling
open-ended adaptability through autonomous tool synthesis
(Fig. [2](#S0.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")c).
When the Research module identifies a missing function, exemplified by a
requirement for allergenicity prediction, it aggregates training data
from external repositories, user uploads, or conversation history. The
agents then synthesize reproducible scripts to fine-tune foundation
models using parameter-efficient methods applied to architectural
templates (Methods). This workflow culminates in the automatic
encapsulation of the new model as a persistent and shareable Model
Context Protocol utility. Consequently, the system dynamically expands
its operational scope to address specific and evolving research
requirements.

To validate the utility for protein discovery, we deployed
VenusFactory2 on a targeted polyethylene terephthalate hydrolase
retrieval campaign
(Fig. [2](#S0.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")d).
Leveraging the VenusMine [Wu et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20), the system maps
natural language functional descriptors including thermostable PETase
directly to protein sequence space via high-dimensional embedding
retrieval. In cases where template sequences are absent, the Research
module initiates a literature-based search to establish a seed profile.
The subsequent workflow executes a hierarchical filtration protocol
wherein candidates are mined from metagenomic databases and subjected to
multiparametric profiling by the Computational Biologist agent. This
agent dynamically invokes predictive assays to screen for
physicochemical constraints including melting temperature ($`T_{m}`$)
and pH stability. In a blind validation benchmark,
VenusFactory2 successfully recapitulated the identification of KbPETase
from a vast space of unannotated candidates. This confirms the system’s
ability to execute precision retrieval by synthesizing semantic intent
with biophysical property filtering.

For complex optimization tasks, we evaluated VenusFactory2 in a directed
evolution scenario targeting a VHH antibody
(Fig. [2](#S0.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")e).
The objective involved enhancing binding affinity through combinatorial
mutagenesis. The system orchestrated a multi-stage pipeline, integrating
AlphaFold2 [Jumper et al. (2021)](#bib.bib3) for structural resolution
and VenusREM [Tan et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8) for zero-shot mutational
scanning. Crucially, the system demonstrated adaptive reasoning to
bridge a methodological gap: while the initial tool output was limited
to single-point fitness scores, the project required multi-site
predictions. Identifying this discrepancy, the Machine Learning
Specialist agent autonomously synthesized and executed a Ridge
Regression [Hsu et al. (2022)](#bib.bib23) algorithm, utilizing
single-point priors to model combinatorial epistatic effects. The
resulting top-ranked variants, including double and quadruple mutants,
exhibited high correlation with ground-truth wet-lab data. This case
validates the system’s ability to expand beyond static tool definitions
by dynamically constructing computational logic for specific biological
objectives.

Collectively, VenusFactory2 advances the transition from static tool
execution to intent-centric protein engineering. By resolving the
cognitive complexity of workflow orchestration and enabling runtime
capability expansion, the framework empowers experimentalists to deploy
sophisticated computational strategies without requiring specialized
programming expertise. This work establishes a foundation for
self-evolving scientific agents capable of driving autonomous and
open-ended discovery in biomedicine.

### Methods

#### Research Tool Ecosystem

Federated Web Intelligence. To synthesize multi-modal scientific
information, the agent employs a federated search interface that
interrogates four distinct information domains. (1) Academic Literature:
We integrate programmatic access to
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), [Web of
Science](https://www.webofscience.com/), and [Semantic
Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/) to retrieve peer-reviewed
articles, complemented by [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/) and
[bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/) for preprints, ensuring coverage of
the latest algorithmic advances. (2) Technical Repositories: To bridge
theoretical concepts with implementation, the system queries
[GitHub](https://github.com/) for codebases, and [Hugging
Face](https://huggingface.co/) and [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/) for
benchmarks and datasets. (3) Intellectual Property: Industrial relevance
is assessed by mining the [Google Patents Public
Datasets](https://console.cloud.google.com/marketplace/product/google_patents_public_datasets/google-patents-public-data)
via [Google BigQuery](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery), allowing the
agent to verify novelty and freedom-to-operate. (4) Open-Web Context:
For unstructured content (e.g., industry news, documentation), the
system leverages the [Tavily](https://tavily.com/) and
[DuckDuckGo](https://duckduckgo.com/) Search API, optimized for
LLM-centric retrieval, to capture high-precision contextual
intelligence.

Database Interrogation Engine. To ensure comprehensive coverage of the
protein universe, VenusFactory2 integrates a high-throughput data
collection engine spanning nine canonical repositories across sequence,
structure, enzymology, and functional networks. (1) Sequence and
Function: [UniProt](https://www.uniprot.org/) [Consortium
(2025)](#bib.bib19) serves as the central repository for amino acid
sequences and curated annotations ($`>250`$ million entries). This is
supplemented by [NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) [Sayers et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib18) databases (GenBank, RefSeq) to capture non-canonical
entries. Additionally, [InterPro](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/)
\[[1](#biba.bib1)\] is leveraged to assign functional signatures,
classifying proteins into families and domains ($`\sim 41,000`$
entries). (2) Structural Data: [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/)
\[[2](#biba.bib2)\] provides over $`200,000`$ experimentally determined
3D structures. For targets lacking experimental data, the system
retrieves predicted structures from the [AlphaFold
Database](https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/) \[[3](#biba.bib3)\] via UniProt
ID mapping. (3) Biochemical and Interaction Networks: To contextualize
proteins within complex biological systems, the engine queries
[BRENDA](https://www.brenda-enzymes.org/) \[[4](#biba.bib4)\] for
comprehensive enzyme kinetics and functional parameters. Protein-ligand
binding affinities and small-molecule bioactivity are retrieved via
[ChEMBL](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/) \[[5](#biba.bib5)\].
Furthermore, [KEGG](https://www.genome.jp/kegg/) \[[6](#biba.bib6)\] is
integrated to map targets onto higher-order metabolic and signaling
pathways, while the [STRING](https://string-db.org/) database
\[[7](#biba.bib7)\] provides probabilistic profiling of protein-protein
interactions. Technically, the module implements an asynchronous,
multithreaded architecture to maximize data acquisition throughput (see
Supplementary
Table [1](#S1 "1 Tools ‣ Self-evolving AI agents for protein discovery and directed evolution")).

#### Directed Evolution Tools

Mutational Scanning. For directed evolution prediction, we deployed a
comprehensive suite of protein language models (PLMs), prioritizing
architectures validated through wet-lab experiments \[[8](#biba.bib8),
[9](#biba.bib9)\] or superior benchmark performance \[[10](#biba.bib10),
[11](#biba.bib11)\]. To capture sequence-based evolutionary patterns,
the system integrates the evolutionary scale modeling suite, including
ESM2 \[[12](#biba.bib12)\], ESM1v \[[13](#biba.bib13)\], and ESM1b
\[[14](#biba.bib14)\]. For structure-conditioned predictions, we
deployed hybrid architectures to leverage geometric constraints:
VenusREM [Tan et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8) integrates homologous sequences
with structural information. ProSST \[[15](#biba.bib15)\] utilizes
discrete attention mechanisms. ProtSSN \[[16](#biba.bib16)\], a
sequence-structure serial network. SaProt [Su et al. (2023)](#bib.bib21)
employs Foldseek-derived [Van Kempen et al. (2024)](#bib.bib11) token
encoding. The inverse folding model ESM-IF \[[17](#biba.bib17)\] and
MIF-ST \[[18](#biba.bib18)\], combining graph neural networks.
Furthermore, for “Low-N” scenarios involving iterative wet-lab feedback,
we implemented a sample-efficient Ridge Regression protocol [Hsu et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib23) which is implemented by VenusFactory2 itself. This
supervised module augments one-hot amino acid encoding with probability
density features derived from unsupervised PLMs, demonstrating superior
generalization in data-scarce regimes.

Functional Sites Detection. To constrain the mutational search space and
prevent the disruption of critical biological functions, we developed
the VenusX \[[19](#biba.bib19)\] module, an ensemble of residue-level
predictors designed to identify and mask essential sites. Built upon
PLMs, these models were trained on datasets partitioned at a strict
$`90\%`$ sequence identity threshold to minimize data leakage. The suite

curated InterPro entries \[[1](#biba.bib1)\], supporting binary

Homology Mining Engine. To enable comprehensive protein discovery, we

synergizing sequence and structural representations through multi-round
iterative searches, VenusMine facilitates the identification of remote
homologs beyond the reach of local alignment, a capability validated by
the discovery of high-performance PET hydrolases.

Biophysical Filtration Suite. To facilitate the rigorous down-selection
of protein candidates, we established a comprehensive filtration suite
within the VenusFactory2 platform, synergizing deep learning with
classical biophysics. Leveraging diverse PLMs as foundational encoders,
we integrated an ensemble of predictive modules to model critical
operational constraints. These include predictors for manufacturability
(binary solubility status \[[25](#biba.bib25), [26](#biba.bib26)\],
quantitative solubility score \[[27](#biba.bib27)\], membrane

\[[31](#biba.bib31)\]), and safety profile (immunogenicity

system deploys established bioinformatics utilities to calculate
fundamental physicochemical descriptors, including solvent accessible
surface area, relative rolvent accessibility, and secondary structure
composition based on Biopython \[[36](#biba.bib36)\]. (see Supplementary
Table ).

#### Auto Machine Learning

Template-based Model Adaptation. To adapt foundation PLMs for
specialized downstream tasks, VenusFactory2 implements a rigorous
optimization framework that bridges general-purpose representations with
specific scientific objectives. This infrastructure enables a continuum
of tasks—from continuous property regression to discrete
classification—governed by strict configuration schemas. By
encapsulating model specifications within static .config definitions
(Appendix Table ), the system constrains the generative action space,
thereby ensuring the syntactic validity and executability of
autonomously synthesized code.

To balance computational efficiency with feature alignment, the agent
supports a spectrum of training strategies, ranging from full-parameter
updates to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) \[[37](#biba.bib37)\].
The PEFT infrastructure is integrated into VenusFactory2 to support the
LoRA family, optimizing resource consumption without compromising
expressivity. Specifically, the system supports: (1) standard LoRA
\[[38](#biba.bib38)\] and weight-decomposed LoRA (DoRA)
\[[39](#biba.bib39)\] for maximizing learning capacity; (2) adaptive
budgeting via AdaLoRA \[[40](#biba.bib40)\] for sparsity control; and
(3) quantization-aware QLoRA \[[41](#biba.bib41)\] and IA3
\[[42](#biba.bib42)\] for deployment in resource-constrained
environments. For structure-dependent tasks, the agent additionally
incorporates the SES-Adapter \[[31](#biba.bib31)\], which employs
cross-attention to explicitly fuse sequence representations with
structural embeddings derived from Foldseek [Van Kempen et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib11) or DSSP \[[43](#biba.bib43)\].

For the projection phase, the architecture bifurcates its processing
logic based on the granularity of the biological objective. For
protein-level tasks (e.g., stability or solubility prediction), the
system aggregates the raw residue representations
$`H\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}`$ into a global latent embedding to predict
the target property $`\hat{y}`$:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\hat{y}=\mathcal{F}_{\text{task}}\left(\Psi_{\text{agg}}(H)\right),\quad\text{where }\Psi_{\text{agg}}\in\{\text{MeanPool},\text{LightAttn},\text{Attn1D}\}
``` |  | (1) |

Here, $`\Psi_{\text{agg}}`$ denotes the dynamically selected aggregation
operator—ranging from baseline mean pooling to complex attention-based
convolutions—and $`\mathcal{F}_{\text{task}}`$ represents the decoding
Multi-Layer Perceptron. Conversely, for residue-level tasks (e.g.,
binding site identification), the system bypasses temporal pooling to
maintain topological resolution, mapping embeddings directly to
sequence-aligned labels $`\hat{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times K}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{Y}=\text{Softmax}\left(HW_{\text{token}}+b_{\text{token}}\right)
``` |  | (2) |

where $`W_{\text{token}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}`$ and
$`b_{\text{token}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}`$ are the learnable parameters of
the token-wise projection layer, and $`K`$ represents the number of
functional classes.

De novo Code Generation. Complementing the template-based
infrastructure, the framework supports a generative modeling paradigm
for scenarios necessitating bespoke architectures or lightweight tabular
learning. Diverging from the rigid config-driven adaptation of PLMs,
this module capitalizes on the inherent code generation proficiencies of
the underlying LLM to synthesize fully autonomous, self-contained
executable scripts. Upon analyzing the user’s data topology and
input-output context, the agent dynamically authors and persists
Python-based modeling pipelines as discrete files. This mechanism allows
for the de novo construction of diverse architectures—from classical
regressors (e.g., XGBoost, Random Forest) to custom neural
networks—ensuring execution logic is strictly tailored to immediate
scientific constraints without the architectural bias of pre-trained
backbones.

#### Construction of VenusAgentEval

Benchmark Rationale and Positioning. To systematically evaluate the
specialized capabilities of VenusFactory2 in protein engineering, we
established VenusAgentEval. Distinct from general-domain reasoning
benchmarks \[[50](#biba.bib50), [51](#biba.bib51)\] and broad scientific
tool-use frameworks [Ding et al. (2025)](#bib.bib16), VenusAgentEval
stands as the first vertical agentic benchmark dedicated to protein
science, specifically targeting the sub-domains of protein discovery and
directed evolution.

Hierarchical Construction Strategy. Addressing the challenge of
synthesizing tasks with both scientific depth and quantifiable metrics,
we adopted a hierarchical three-stage construction strategy comprising
multi-agent generation, adversarial dialectical verification, and expert
adjudication.

Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Generation. To mitigate single-model knowledge
bias \[[52](#biba.bib52)\], we assembled a consortium of four advanced
LLMs (GPT-4 \[[53](#biba.bib53)\], Claude-3.7 \[[54](#biba.bib54)\],
Gemini-2.5-Pro \[[55](#biba.bib55)\], and DeepSeek-R1
\[[56](#biba.bib56)\]). Configured with a unified prompt simulating a
senior computational biologist, this ensemble engaged in a parallel
role-playing protocol. Over 100 interaction rounds, the system generated
a preliminary corpus of 400 candidate questions, leveraging distinct
reasoning architectures to enhance stylistic diversity and domain
coverage.

Adversarial Dialectical Verification. To enforce logical consistency and
biological plausibility, candidates were subjected to an automated
adversarial debate mechanism modeled after the academic peer-review
process \[[57](#biba.bib57)\]. For each question $`q`$, a “Review
Committee” of non-generating agents independently evaluated scientific
significance and self-consistency, prompting the “Author” agent to
iteratively refine the content. Upon revision, the committee assigned a
quantitative quality score $`S_{m}(q)\in[0,5]`$, with the final
retention metric $`S_{final}(q)`$ calculated as:

|     |                                    |     |     |
|-----|------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{final}(q)=\sum_{m\in C}S_{m}(q)
       ```                                 |     | (3) |

where $`C`$ denotes the set of reviewing agents. This dialectical
optimization effectively filtered out formally plausible yet
biologically shallow instances, selecting the top 200 high-scoring
questions.

Expert Adjudication and Stratification. In the final validation stage,
the verified dataset underwent a rigorous single-blind review by three
independent biology Ph.D. experts. This human-in-the-loop mechanism
\[[58](#biba.bib58)\] discarded entries with factual errors and
stratified surviving questions by cognitive load into Question-level
(atomic retrieval), Task-level (multi-step workflow), and Project-level
(long-horizon planning). Following a majority-vote selection rule, the
final VenusAgentEval dataset comprises 148 curated instances (58
Question-level, 60 Task-level, and 30 Project-level), establishing a
benchmark of high scientific rigor (detailed in Supplementary Tables -).

#### Agents Evaluation Strategy

Pointwise Reasoning for Pairwise Evaluation. To address the stochastic
nature of biological tool invocation and the limitations of standard
LLM-based evaluation—specifically calibration errors in pointwise
scoring and verbosity bias in pairwise comparisons—we implemented a
decoupled evaluation architecture termed Pointwise Reasoning for
Pairwise Evaluation. This framework separates evidence extraction from
final adjudication to ensure objectivity. In the analytic phase, an
Agent Analyst (based on GPT-4o-mini) decomposes response pairs based on
strict criteria: scientific validity, evidence sufficiency, and logical
consistency. Crucially, this agent produces structured qualitative
critiques rather than numerical scores, isolating factual accuracy from
stylistic presentation. Subsequently, an independent judge instance
synthesizes these evidence-based audits to determine a winner, thereby
grounding the adjudication in verified content rather than surface-level
fluency.

Rank-Weighted Scoring Protocol. To quantify performance across the model
cohort, we employed a scoring metric derived from exhaustive pairwise
interactions. For a given query, models are ranked according to their
total victory frequency. To penalize mediocrity while rewarding
consistent superiority, we apply a linear rank-weighting scheme. Let
$`N`$ denote the total number of competing models, $`W_{m}`$ the total
win count for model $`m`$, and $`r_{m}`$ the specific rank of the model
derived from $`W_{m}`$ (where $`r=1`$ indicates the highest win count).
The final normalized capability score $`S_{m}`$ is calculated as:

|     |                                        |     |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{m}=\frac{W_{m}\times(N-r_{m}+1)}{N}
       ```                                     |     | (4) |

The reported performance metric represents the mean $`S_{m}`$ across the
test corpus, providing a robust indicator of relative scientific
reasoning capability that accounts for both win rate and comparative
standing.

To identify the optimal reasoning engine for VenusFactory2, we evaluated

superfluous structural retrieval for sequence-based predictions.

Scientific Response Assessment. This dimension evaluates the output of
the Scientific Critic, prioritizing (1) Correctness, gauging the
accuracy of reasoning-based task completion; (2) Causal Completeness,
measuring the integrity of inference chains; (3) Hallucination Control,
scrutinizing outputs for fabricated biological entities; and (4)
Constraint Adherence, ensuring strict compliance with prompt
instructions.

#### System Architecture and Agent Orchestration

Overview. VenusFactory2 operates as a closed-loop multi-agent system
designed to automate protein engineering workflows. The architecture
transforms abstract natural language queries into executable
bioinformatics pipelines through a centralized orchestration mechanism.
The workflow follows a sequential logic: (1) task decomposition and
planning, (2) code generation and tool execution, (3) autonomous error
correction, and (4) result synthesis and reporting. This process is
collaboratively executed by four specialized agents: the Principal
Investigator, the Machine Learning Specialist, the Computational
Biologist, and the Scientific Critic. The specific implementation and
functional responsibilities of each agent are detailed below.

Principal Investigator. The Principal Investigator serves as the central
planner, responsible for interpreting research intent and architectural
task decomposition. Upon receiving a user query, this agent performs
three critical functions: (1) Intent Analysis: It parses the input to
extract key entities, such as target sequences or database identifiers,
and determines the necessary analysis paradigms. (2) Feasibility
Assessment: By cross-referencing the internal tool library, it
constructs a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of analysis steps, defining
input-output dependencies and execution priorities. (3) Context
Management: To support iterative optimization, the agent implements a
two-level memory mechanism. At the interaction level, it retains
conversational context to resolve ambiguities in multi-turn dialogue. At
the execution level, it serializes and stores intermediate results and
parameter configurations, allowing the system to bypass redundant
computations in subsequent steps.

Machine Learning Specialist. The Machine Learning Specialist functions
as the algorithmic engine, tasked with the lifecycle management of
predictive models. Its core responsibilities include: (1) Dynamic Code
Generation: It translates the PI’s abstract requirements into executable
Python scripts, implementing architectures ranging from lightweight
regression models for multi-site variant scoring to fine-tuning large
protein language models on domain-specific datasets. (2) Automated
Debugging: The agent monitors runtime logs in real-time. Upon detecting
exceptions or convergence failures, it triggers a self-correction loop
to diagnose error sources, adjust hyperparameters, or revise data
preprocessing logic without human intervention. (3) Tool Registration:
Post-training, the agent encapsulates model weights and inference
interfaces into standardized tools, registering them into the system
library for immediate invocation by other agents.

Computational Biologist. The Computational Biologist acts as the
interface for biological data acquisition and standard bioinformatics
tool execution. Guided by the central plan, this agent executes the
following tasks: (1) Multimodal Data Retrieval: It queries external
repositories—such as sequence databases, structural archives, and
literature indices—to curate training data or reference sets. (2) Tool
Orchestration: It manages the execution of standard bioinformatics
utilities, including sequence alignment, structural folding, and
functional annotation tools, ensuring proper parameterization and
resource allocation. (3) Data Standardization: To address format
inconsistencies common in biological data, the agent implements adaptive
parsing logic, converting heterogeneous outputs into unified data
structures compatible with downstream machine learning modules.

Scientific Critic. The Scientific Critic serves as the quality assurance
and synthesis module. Its primary function is to transform raw
computational outputs into coherent scientific insights. This involves:
(1) Logical Verification: During execution, the agent audits
intermediate results against the initial research objectives, flagging
inconsistencies, missing data, or biological implausibility for
re-evaluation. (2) Result Integration: It aggregates disparate data
streams—including model metrics, structural files, and functional
predictions—into a consolidated analysis record. (3) Narrative
Reconstruction: Finally, the agent synthesizes a structured report that
summarizes methodology, interprets key findings with supporting
evidence, and proposes data-driven directions for subsequent
experimental validation.

#### Prompt Engineering and Formalized Role Definition

To ensure deterministic execution within the stochastic multi-agent
environment, we formalized the prompt engineering framework as a
structured mapping function $`\Phi`$, which projects natural language
directives into constrained agentic behaviors. This framework is
governed by a set of role-specific templates
$`\mathcal{T}=\{T_{PI},T_{ML},T_{Bio},T_{SC}\}`$, designed to enforce
strict output schemas, operational traceability, and evidence-based
reasoning. The planning phase is mathematically modeled as a
decomposition function driven by the Principal Investigator
($`\mathcal{A}_{PI}`$). Given a user query $`U`$, the agent generates a
sequential execution plan $`\mathcal{P}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{A}_{PI}(U\mid T_{PI})=\{(s_{t},\tau_{t},\theta_{t})\}_{t=1}^{N}
``` |  | (5) |

where $`s_{t}`$ denotes the natural language description of the sub-task
at step $`t`$, $`\tau_{t}`$ represents the specific tool identifier
mapped from the system library, and $`\theta_{t}`$ constitutes the
validated parameter set derived from the user’s intent. This mapping
enforces a strict dependency structure where $`\theta_{t}`$ may
reference outputs $`o_{k<t}`$ from preceding steps. Subsequently, the
execution phase delegates these tasks to the domain-specific executors,
the Machine Learning Specialist ($`\mathcal{A}_{ML}`$) and the
Computational Biologist ($`\mathcal{A}_{Bio}`$). The state transition of
the workflow is defined by the iterative update of the execution history
$`\mathcal{H}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{H}_{t-1}\cup\{(o_{t},\lambda_{t})\}\quad\text{where}\quad(o_{t},\lambda_{t})=\text{Exec}_{\tau_{t}}(\theta_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1},T_{exec})
``` |  | (6) |

Here, $`o_{t}`$ represents the structured output artifacts (e.g., model
weights, sequences, or PDB files), and $`\lambda_{t}`$ denotes the
execution trace logs required for reproducibility. Finally, the
synthesis phase is governed by the Scientific Critic
($`\mathcal{A}_{SC}`$), which aggregates the cumulative history
$`\mathcal{H}_{N}`$ into a coherent scientific narrative $`R`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R=\mathcal{A}_{SC}(\mathcal{H}_{N}\mid T_{SC},\mathcal{C}_{ver})
``` |  | (7) |

subject to verification constraints $`\mathcal{C}_{ver}`$ that strictly
bound generated claims to the evidence provided in $`\mathcal{H}_{N}`$.
Full definitions of these prompt templates and constraint schemas are
detailed in Supplementary Section .

#### Autonomous Tool Creation and Self-Evolving Workflows

Developing task-specific predictive tools in protein engineering
traditionally demands profound cross-disciplinary expertise.
Specifically, fine-tuning modern protein language models presents
significant operational bottlenecks due to the necessity of navigating
high-dimensional hyperparameter spaces, configuring training pipelines,
and managing heterogeneous datasets. Consequently, optimal predictive
performance is frequently inaccessible to researchers lacking
specialized algorithmic knowledge.

To overcome these barriers, VenusFactory2 features a closed-loop,
self-evolving workflow that empowers its multi-agent system to
autonomously orchestrate the end-to-end model development lifecycle.
Upon encountering requests beyond the scope of its existing tool
library, the system avoids execution failure by dynamically
standardizing input data, selecting appropriate foundational
architectures, and formulating optimized training scripts. By
abstracting away these engineering complexities,
VenusFactory2 transforms static tool libraries into a dynamic
repository, consistently yielding customized predictors and packaging
them as standard Model Context Protocol (MCP) services for future reuse.

To empirically validate this workflow, we conducted a case study in de
novo allergenicity predictor synthesis. The campaign was initiated with
the following prompt:

> “Please evaluate the allergenicity potential for these modified food
> enzymes and the protein sequence is in the uploaded file.”

Upon receiving the request, the Principal Investigator identified the
intent as protein allergenicity analysis. After cross-referencing the
tool library, it recognized a methodological gap: existing modules
handled general functional annotation but lacked a dedicated
allergenicity predictor. To resolve this, the agent autonomously decided
to synthesize a novel tool and generated a complete execution plan:

> Step 1: Search papers related to allergenicity prediction.
> Step 2: Use web search to find datasets for allergen / non-allergen
> prediction.
> Step 3: Split the downloaded training data into an 8:2 training and
> validation set.
> Step 4: Generate the training configuration file for the ESM2-8M model
> with LoRA.
> Step 5: Train the model using the generated configuration and report
> performance.
> Step 6: Package the trained checkpoint as a new MCP service and
> register it into the VenusFactory2 tool registry.
> Step 7: Use the newly minted MCP tool to predict the sequences in the
> provided FASTA file.

Following this blueprint, the Computational Biologist and Machine
Learning Specialist collaborated to execute the plan. They began with
keyword searches across PubMed and arXiv, utilizing the summarized
findings to guide dataset retrieval from open platforms such as Hugging
Face. Upon processing the dataset into the requisite 8:2 split, the
Machine Learning Specialist selected the ESM2-8M architecture and
autonomously fine-tuned it via LoRA for two epochs, monitoring
convergence metrics without human intervention.

Once training concluded, VenusFactory2 reported an accuracy of 0.92 and
an AUROC of 0.96 on the independent test set. The agent subsequently
packaged the model checkpoint, inference schemas, and metadata into a
new MCP tool, registering it within the platform library. Finally,
VenusFactory2 deployed this synthesized tool on the user’s uploaded
FASTA sequence, while the Scientific Critic translated the technical
metrics into an accessible final report. Through this process, a
transient experimental requirement was transformed into a permanent,
discoverable computational asset.

#### Adaptive Directed Evolution of a VHH Antibody

Protein directed evolution constitutes a formidable challenge in protein
engineering, historically bottlenecked by the exponential growth of the
mutational landscape. Even screening single-point mutations demands
rigorous evaluation of structural constraints, functional effects, and
experimental feasibility. To demonstrate how VenusFactory2 supports
iterative combinatorial optimization, we deployed the system in a
directed evolution campaign aimed at enhancing the binding affinity of a
VHH antibody.

The process was initiated via a natural-language directive specifying
the wild-type target:

> “I have a specific VHH antibody with the protein sequence
> MQVQLVESGGGLAQAGGSLRLSCAVSGMPEFARAMGWFRQAPGKERELLAAIEGIGATTYYADSVKGRFTISRDDAANTVLLQMNSLKPDDTAVYYCAAAFSVTIPTRARHWVDWGPGTLVTVSSDDDDKSGGGGSHHHHHH.
> The current goal is to improve the binding affinity of this antibody.”

Upon receiving this request, the Principal Investigator interpreted the
objective as a structure-guided affinity maturation task and formulated
the following execution protocol:

> Step 1: Predict the three-dimensional conformation of the provided VHH
> sequence using AlphaFold2 to establish a structural baseline.
> Step 2: Perform structural homology mining across comprehensive
> databases using FoldSeek to curate an evolutionary profile.
> Step 3: Execute a zero-shot, in silico saturated single-point
> mutational scan utilizing the VenusREM architecture.

Orchestrating this blueprint, the Computational Biologist first resolved
the structure via AlphaFold2 [Jumper et al. (2021)](#bib.bib3) and
retrieved structural homologs from databases including AFDB and PDB via
FoldSeek [Van Kempen et al. (2024)](#bib.bib11). These multimodal
inputs—sequence, predicted structure, and homology alignments—were
autonomously routed to VenusREM [Tan et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8). The
Scientific Critic then analyzed the resulting zero-shot fitness
landscape, isolating high-potential single-point mutation sites to guide
the first round of wet-lab validation.

Following the acquisition of first-round experimental fitness data, the
human researcher uploaded the results to the platform to initiate the
combinatorial optimization phase:

> “For this VHH, I have completed single-point mutation experiments. The
> experimental data are in the uploaded folder. Please recommend
> higher-order mutation sites based on these results.”

Recognizing that standard zero-shot tools lack the capacity to natively
model complex epistatic interactions from user-provided empirical data,
the Principal Investigator autonomously pivoted the computational
strategy to bridge this methodological gap:

> Step 1: Dynamically synthesize and train a regularized Ridge
> Regression model using the uploaded empirical data to predict
> combinatorial epistatic effects.
> Step 2: Batch-evaluate higher-order mutants and rank the
> top-performing combinatorial candidates.

Tasked with this objective, the Machine Learning Specialist autonomously
parsed the uploaded wet-lab data, applied one-hot encoding to the mutant
sequences, and trained a regularized Ridge Regression model [Hsu et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib23). This supervised architecture effectively mapped
single-point empirical priors to epistatic contributions, mitigating
overfitting. Leveraging this autonomously generated model, the
Scientific Critic executed high-throughput batch inference across the
combinatorial sequence space, ranking and recommending the
top-performing double, triple, and quadruple mutants.

Retrospective analysis against ground-truth wet-lab binding data
confirmed that the agent-synthesized regression model achieved
significantly higher experimental correlation for multi-point variants
than a naive linear summation of single-point effects. This campaign
underscores VenusFactory2’s capacity to autonomously recognize
methodological limitations and dynamically synthesize bespoke
algorithmic solutions, functioning as an adaptive computational
co-scientist for iterative biological optimization.

#### End-to-End Protein Discovery and Precision Mining

Navigating billion-scale metagenomic spaces to discover novel,
industrially relevant enzymes traditionally requires assembling
disjointed bioinformatics pipelines and manually configuring complex
filtration thresholds. To enable comprehensive discovery,
VenusFactory2 integrates a massive, deduplicated sequence
repository—spanning databases like GOPC \[[20](#biba.bib20)\], UniProt
[Consortium (2025)](#bib.bib19), MGnify \[[21](#biba.bib21)\], and OMG
\[[22](#biba.bib22)\]—with an agent-orchestrated screening
infrastructure powered by VenusMine [Wu et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20).
Rather than relying on static filters, VenusFactory2 empowers its agents
to dynamically assemble structure-aware search and biophysical screening
protocols directly from natural-language objectives.

To validate this end-to-end capability, we conducted a blind retrieval
campaign for polyethylene terephthalate (PET) hydrolases. The discovery
process was initiated with a purely functional description rather than a
traditional template sequence:

> “Please search the metagenomic databases to discover a novel PET
> hydrolase that exhibits significantly higher thermostability and
> catalytic activity than the standard IsPETase.”

Upon receiving this prompt, the Principal Investigator recognized the
intent as a de novo enzyme discovery task requiring both homology mining
and stringent biophysical filtration. It subsequently formulated a
comprehensive execution strategy:

> Step 1: Search for scientific literature on PETase to provide an
> overview and background information.
> Step 2: Retrieve the protein sequence and information for the
> well-studied PETase from Ideonella sakaiensis using its UniProt ID .
> Step 3: Download the predicted protein structure for PETase from the
> AlphaFold database using its UniProt ID.
> Step 4: Conduct enzyme mining using VenusMine.
> Step 5: Screen candidates for thermostability.
> Step 6: Screen candidates for solubility.
> Step 7: Rank the surviving candidates and compile the final enzyme
> discovery report.

Following this blueprint, the agents autonomously executed the
multi-stage pipeline. The Research module first identified a reliable
seed PETase from the literature. Transitioning to sequence space
expansion, the Computational Biologist employed FoldSeek [Van Kempen
et al. (2024)](#bib.bib11) for structural alignment against the
AlphaFold Database, followed by a cascade expansion into the
million-scale database using MMseqs2 \[[24](#biba.bib24)\] to construct
a non-redundant library. To distill this vast pool, the system leveraged
ProstT5 \[[61](#biba.bib61)\]. Operating in sequence-to-structure
translation mode, it encoded candidates into high-dimensional
embeddings, allowing the agent to cluster sequences based on conserved
catalytic core architectures while discarding structurally divergent
hits.

Crucially, during the in silico screening phase, the Computational
Biologist departed from static retrieval protocols. Interpreting the
user’s explicit requirement for high thermostability and activity, it
dynamically selected predictive assays from the Biophysical Filtration
Suite, deploying fine-tuned ESM-2 \[[12](#biba.bib12)\] for melting
temperature ($`T_{m}`$), ProtSolM \[[26](#biba.bib26)\] for solubility,
and AlphaFold2 [Jumper et al. (2021)](#bib.bib3) to verify structural
fidelity.

Through this agent-mediated orchestration, VenusFactory2 successfully
narrowed the vast candidate space down to KbPETase (APET-14) [Wu et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib20), a novel enzyme from Kibdelosporangium banguiense.
The discovered enzyme exhibited exceptional experimental metrics: a
melting temperature ($`T_{m}`$) of 80.1°C, exceeding IsPETase
\[[62](#biba.bib62)\] by 32°C; a catalytic activity at 50°C that is 97x
that of IsPETase and 5.7x that of the thermophilic benchmark LCC; and a
catalytic efficiency ($`k_{\text{cat}}/K_{m}`$) 1.87x and 1.3x higher
than those of the engineered FastPETase \[[63](#biba.bib63)\] and LCC,
respectively. This successful recapitulation demonstrates how
VenusFactory2 translates a declarative natural language prompt into the
precision retrieval of high-performance enzymes.
````
