###### Abstract

A major challenge for world models in multi-agent systems is to
understand interdependent agent dynamics, predict interactive
multi-agent trajectories, and plan over long horizons with collective
awareness, without centralized supervision or explicit communication. In
this paper, MetaMind, a general and cognitive world model for
multi-agent systems that leverages a novel meta-theory of mind
(Meta-ToM) framework, is proposed. Through MetaMind, each agent learns
not only to predict and plan over its own beliefs, but also to inversely
reason goals and beliefs from its own behavior trajectories. This
self-reflective, bidirectional inference loop enables each agent to
learn a metacognitive ability in a self-supervised manner. Then,
MetaMind is shown to generalize the metacognitive ability from
first-person to third-person through analogical reasoning. Thus, in
multi-agent systems, each agent with MetaMind can actively reason about
goals and beliefs of other agents from limited, observable behavior
trajectories in a zero-shot manner, and then adapt to emergent
collective intention without an explicit communication mechanism.
Extended simulation results on diverse multi-agent tasks demonstrate
that MetaMind can achieve superior task performance and outperform
baselines in few-shot multi-agent generalization.

## 1 Introduction

World models endow intelligent agents with the human-like ability to
jointly understand environment dynamics, predict future trajectories,
and plan actions over an extended horizon ([Ding et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2); [Hafner et al., 2025](#bib.bib1); [Prasanna et al.,
2024](#bib.bib42); [Wang et al., 2025c](#bib.bib45)). Particularly, by
abstracting low-level, high-dimensional sensory data into compact state
representations in a latent space, world models enable sample-efficient
imagination and long-horizon reasoning through internal simulation
rather than direct interactions with the environment ([Vafa et al.,
2024](#bib.bib39); [Sun et al., 2024](#bib.bib41)). These properties
facilitate generalization ability, counterfactual reasoning, and
long-term planning ([Yang et al., 2025](#bib.bib40); [Wang et al.,
2025d](#bib.bib35)). Recent advances have demonstrated that world models
can capture the underlying dynamics of complex domains, including visual
control, open-world exploration, and physical reasoning by only learning
from images or videos ([Assran et al., 2025](#bib.bib4); [Samsami et
al., 2024](#bib.bib12); [Wang et al., 2025c](#bib.bib45)). These
advances enable a transition from reactive policies to model-based
planning, thus providing a foundation for more general-purpose
intelligence.

Despite the past success in single-agent systems, existing world models
([Zhang et al., 2025c](#bib.bib6); [Zhang et al., 2024b](#bib.bib17);
[Nomura et al., 2025](#bib.bib16)) cannot support strategic planning in
multi-agent systems due to non-stationary dynamics with adaptive
behaviors, heterogeneous goals, and partial observations. Existing
multi-agent world models can be divided into two categories. The first
one is centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) models
([Zhang et al., 2024b](#bib.bib17)) that rely on centralized
representation aggregation to capture inter-agent dependencies during
training. However, such a global context is unavailable under
decentralized execution, which makes it hard to reuse the learned world
model as an efficient test-time planner for long-horizon imagination.
The second one is communication-based decentralized models ([Nomura et
al., 2025](#bib.bib16)) that reduce partial observability via message
exchange. However, messages are not directly available in counterfactual
imagination and must be simulated, which increases inference cost and
can exacerbate rollout drift. Hence, to enable world models for joint
understanding, prediction, and long-horizon planning in multi-agent
systems, we must overcome key challenges:

- •
  _Understanding_: In multi-agent environments, the evolution of
  observations is shaped by _coupled_ interactions among multiple
  decision-makers, where each agent simultaneously acts on and reacts to
  the others. The same observation change can be consistent with
  different multi-agent explanations, thus making it fundamentally
  ambiguous to determine causal effects of each agent. Moreover, agents
  may have distinct goals, observe asymmetric information and follow
  heterogeneous policies ([Zhang et al., 2024a](#bib.bib20)), thus
  producing non-stationary, interdependent behavior that violates the
  independence assumptions commonly used in standard world models.
  Hence, a major challenge is to understand _agent-indexed, factorized_
  beliefs that disentangle the latent goals and beliefs of each
  participant while preserving their interaction structures. Such
  beliefs must attribute causal responsibility across agents through
  inverse inference rather than compressing everything in sensory data.
  In short, understanding requires structured, transferable abstractions
  that separate “who acts,” “with which goal,” and “under which case,”
  rather than a single-perspective dynamics model.
- •
  _Prediction_: Prediction in multi-agent systems is interactive, where
  each agent’s future states are conditioned not only on the
  environment, but also on behavior of others under heterogeneous
  policies. This can lead to closed-loop distribution shift,
  long-horizon error compounding, and severe causal confounding when
  modeling trajectories from the perspective of a single agent. Such
  effects cannot be reliably handled by standard supervised dynamics
  predictors developed for single-agent world models. Hence, reliable
  prediction by world models in multi-agent systems requires cognitive
  inference over latent goals and beliefs, and modeling updates of other
  agents over multiple steps.
- •
  _Planning_: Planning is fundamentally strategic over joint action and
  belief spaces of multiple adaptive decision-makers. Rather than
  optimizing under a fixed environment, an agent must anticipate how
  others will interpret its behavior and adjust their responses for
  optimal planning. This suggests higher-order reasoning about goals and
  beliefs, e.g., “I know that you know that I know”, and more
  importantly, for maintaining and updating _collective beliefs_ over
  latent goals, private beliefs, and shared environment state as the
  interaction unfolds. Hence, it is challenging to achieve online
  long-horizon planning.

To address these challenges, the world model at any given agent must
treat other agents not as part of an unstructured background dynamics,
but as _intentional entities_, whose behavior is driven by goals and
beliefs, and therefore admits causal, explainable principles as shown in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")(a).
The theory-of-mind (ToM) provides this perspective by explicitly
inferring others’ beliefs and goals, thus forming a foundation for joint
understanding, prediction, and planning in multi-agent systems ([Mao et
al., 2024](#bib.bib30)). However, existing ToM-based approaches ([Shum
et al., 2019](#bib.bib34); [Westby and Riedl, 2023](#bib.bib51); [Li et
al., 2024](#bib.bib31)) typically rely on centralized or supervised
training with specific partners and tasks, and learn a fixed perspective
and structure of multi-agent interactions, which limits the
generalization and cannot capture goal-conditioned causal mechanisms.

The main contribution of this paper is MetaMind, a general, cognitive
world model that learns from an ego agent’s experience and generalizes
to multi-agent systems in a zero-shot manner. At the core of MetaMind,
we propose a self-supervised _Meta-ToM_ framework that leverages only
observable behavioral trajectories, without relying on explicit
communication. Meta-ToM enables MetaMind to: (a) perform self-reflection
by inversely inferring beliefs and goals to ensure
inference-consistency, and turn passive dynamics modeling into
goal-directed causal modeling for disentangled understanding, (b)
generalize the inverse inference ability to unseen agents through
analogical reasoning, which we name “_metacognition ability_”, for more
reliable, explicit multi-agent trajectory predictions, and (c) learn
collective beliefs for long-term, adaptive planning. These features
provide a decentralized learning approach without symbolic communication
systems or centralized context. Extended simulations across SMAC tasks
show that MetaMind achieves  54% higher win rate than exsiting
decentralized multi-agent world models under limited training steps, and
a 2.4-fold improvement in few-shot generalization than exsiting
centralized multi-agent world models.

## 2 Related Works

### 2.1 World Models in Multi-Agent Systems

Recent advances in multi-agent systems have increasingly explored the
use of world models to improve sample efficiency, generalization, and
planning. Some works, such as COMBO ([Zhang et al., 2024a](#bib.bib20))
and MABL ([Venugopal et al., 2023](#bib.bib5)), introduce compositional
or hierarchical structures to separate shared dynamics from
agent-specific behavior for better representation learning. DIMA ([Loo
et al., 2025](#bib.bib13)) and DCT ([Zhang et al., 2024b](#bib.bib17))
adopt generative modeling frameworks, including diffusion processes and
transformer architectures, to capture the complex, high-dimensional
interactions among agents. GAWM ([Shi et al., 2025b](#bib.bib10))
proposes centralized attention modules to address observation
inconsistencies in partially observable environments, while GRAD ([Liu
et al., 2024](#bib.bib19)) incorporates symbolic reasoning layers to
enhance decision interpretability. DCWMC ([Nomura et al.,
2025](#bib.bib16)) infers multi-agent states with a shared symbolic
communication system. However, these approaches often rely on
centralized supervision, task-specific structures, or passive inference,
and lack generalization and scalability, thus, they cannot enable joint
understanding, prediction, and planning as a human-like intelligence in
scalable and heterogeneous multi-agent systems.

### 2.2 Theory of Mind

ToM ([Baker et al., 2011](#bib.bib22); [Rabinowitz et al.,
2018](#bib.bib21); [Jara-Ettinger, 2019](#bib.bib44)) endows agents with
the ability to infer others’ latent mental states, including goals and
beliefs. Early works ([Oguntola et al., 2023](#bib.bib36); [Zhang et
al., 2025d](#bib.bib24)) framed ToM as supervised behavior prediction or
inverse planning over latent rewards and policies. Building on this
foundation, ToM approaches have been applied in inverse reinforcement
learning (IRL) [Ng et al. (2000)](#bib.bib50); [Wu et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib28); [Gelpí et al. (2025)](#bib.bib43), intent-aware
multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) ([Qi and Zhu,
2018](#bib.bib37); [Wang et al., 2022](#bib.bib25)), and human reward
learning ([Tian et al., 2021](#bib.bib33)), to enhance representation
learning and improve coordination by integrating inferred goals or
agent-level cognition. To increase generality and reduce reliance on
prior knowledge, AutoToM ([Zhang et al., 2025d](#bib.bib24)) and
MUMA-ToM ([Shi et al., 2025a](#bib.bib29)) adopt self-supervised and
multimodal inference strategies with robustness and adaptability across
agents. Hypothetical minds ([Cross et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) and
LLM-ToM ([Li et al., 2023](#bib.bib32)) use large language models for
explicit hypothesis generation and mental state attribution in complex
social reasoning. However, most of these works require supervised or
centralized multi-agent training and are sensitive to strategic behavior
under partial observability ([Rabinowitz et al., 2018](#bib.bib21)).
They also need priors in the multi-agent structure, e.g., the number,
roles, and topology of agents, thus, they cannot zero-shot generalize to
new partners, group sizes, or interaction graphs ([Zhang et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib52)). Moreovber, to the best of our knowledge, explicit
ToM-style inference has not yet been used in world models for
model-based planning.

## 3 Preliminaries

### 3.1 Temporal Difference-Based World Model

While the proposed Meta-ToM framework can apply to a broad range of
world models, we use single-agent TD-MPC2, i.e., a temporal-difference
(TD)-based learning approach with model predictive control (MPC), as a
concrete framework to construct MetaMind for multi-agent systems. Given
observation $`\bm{o}_{\mathrm{t}}`$ of the agent in single-agent
settings, the TD-based world model ([Hansen et al., 2024](#bib.bib3)) is
given by

|     |     |                                                                                                                         |     |     |
| --- | --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\text{Belief Encoder: }\quad\bm{b}_{t}=\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}(\bm{o}_{t},\bm{g})`$                        |     | (1) |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\text{Forward Dynamics: }\quad\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g})`$ |     |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\text{Reward Predictor: }\quad\tilde{r}_{t}=\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g})`$        |     |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\text{Terminal Value: }\quad\tilde{q}_{t}=\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi}(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g})`$          |     |     |

where $`\bm{g}`$ is a learnable goal vector, $`\bm{b}_{t}`$ is
goal-oriented belief by $`\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}`$, $`\bm{a}_{t}`$ is an
action from MPC, $`\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t}`$ is the predicted belief by
$`\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}`$, $`\tilde{r}_{t}`$ is the predicted reward by
$`\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}`$ at step $`t`$, and $`\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi}`$
estimates the discounted return with the parameter $`\varphi`$. We
define a multi-step prediction loss:

|     |                                           |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |     |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(\varphi)`$ | $`\displaystyle\!=\!\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\varsigma^{t}\big(\underbrace{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}\left(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}\right)\!-\!\operatorname{sg}(\mathcal{B}_{\varphi}(\bm{o}_{t+1},\bm{g}))\right\|^{2}}_{\text{Imagination Loss}}`$ |     |     |
|     |                                           | $`\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{\Im(\tilde{r}_{t},r_{t})+\Im(\tilde{q}_{t},q_{t})}_{\text{Reward and Value Loss}}\big)\Big],`$                                                                                                                                   |     | (2) |

where $`\varsigma\in(0,1)`$ is the time weighting,
$`\tau=\{\bm{o}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},r_{t},\bm{o}_{t+1}\}`$ is a sampled
actual trajectory, $`\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)`$ is the stop-gradient
operation, and
$`q_{t}=r_{t}+\gamma Q_{\varpi}(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1},\pi_{\varphi}(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1},\bm{g}))`$
is the TD target at step $`t`$ with a factor $`\gamma\in(0,1]`$ and
multi-Q exponential moving average (EMA) with parameters $`\varpi`$. To
ensure scale-invariant learning and stable multi-goal learning, TD-MPC2
formulates the reward and value prediction as discrete regression in
log-transformed space, where $`\Im(\cdot)`$ is soft cross-entropy (More
details can be seen in Appendix
[A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Multi-Q Network with EMA ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
and
[A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 Discrete Regression Objective in Log-Space. ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")).

### 3.2 Model Predictive Control

We perform MPC in the differentiable belief space induced by
$`\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}`$ as the planner of the world model. At each
step, we optimize a Gaussian proposal $`\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})`$
over candidate action sequences
$`\bm{a}_{t:t+\bar{H}}=\{\bm{a}_{t},\bm{a}_{t+1},\cdots,\bm{a}_{t+\bar{H}}\}`$
to maximize the expected cumulative return over horizon $`\bar{H}`$:

|     |                                                                          |                                                                                                                                                                 |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\left\{\mu^{*},\sigma^{*}\right\}=\arg\max_{\mu,\sigma}`$ | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{a}_{t:t+\bar{H}}}\left[\sum_{h=0}^{\bar{H}-1}\gamma^{h}\mathcal{R}\left(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+h},\bm{a}_{t+h},\bm{g}\right)\right.`$ |     |     |
|     |                                                                          | $`\displaystyle\left.+\gamma^{\bar{H}}\mathcal{Q}\left(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+\bar{H}},\bm{a}_{t+\bar{H}},\bm{g}\right)\right].`$                                    |     | (3) |

After iterative optimization, the first action $`\bm{a}_{t}`$ is
executed in the environment, while subsequent steps are warm-started by
using $`\pi_{\varphi}(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{g})`$ as initial mean $`\mu_{0}`$.

## 4 Proposed MetaMind with Meta-ToM

As shown in Figure
[1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"),
MetaMind comprises two components: a goal-conditioned world model
component for forward prediction and planning, and a Meta-ToM component
for inverse inference. In this section, we will present the design of a
novel, self-supervised Meta-ToM component for world models in
multi-agent systems.

#### Inverse Inference.

In multi-agent systems, an agent cannot rely solely on its own
observations, as they are partial and their evolution is causally
coupled through multi-agent interactions, while other agents’ goals and
beliefs remain hidden. To understand and learn the psychological
principles with causal structures underlying behavior, as shown in
Figure
[1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")(a),
we can perform inverse inference of latent mental states, including both
beliefs and goals, from externally observable behavioral trajectories.
Let $`\Psi_{\theta}`$ be the inverse goal predictor and
$`\Omega_{\theta}`$ be the inverse belief predictor with parameters
$`\theta`$. Then, the estimated goal and belief can be, respectively,
obtained by
$`\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}=\Psi_{\theta}(\bm{a}_{<t},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t-1})`$ and
$`\hat{\bm{b}}_{t}=\Omega_{\theta}(\bm{a}_{t},\hat{\bm{g}}_{k})`$. We
optimize $`\Psi_{\theta}`$ and $`\Omega_{\theta}`$ by minimizing the
loss of consistency of inverse reasoning:

|     |                                                   |                                                                                                                    |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{T}\|\Psi_{\theta}(\bm{a}_{<k},\hat{\bm{g}}_{k-1})-\bm{g}\|^{2}`$ |     | (4) |
|     |                                                   | $`\displaystyle+\|\Omega_{\theta}(\bm{a}_{k},\mathrm{sg}(\hat{\bm{g}}_{k}))-\bm{b}_{t}\|^{2}\Big],`$               |     |     |

where $`\mathrm{sg}(\cdot)`$ is the stop-gradient operator. As
illustrated in Figure
[1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")(b),
the inverse inference models $`\Psi_{\theta}`$ and $`\Omega_{\theta}`$
ground Meta-ToM by understanding potential mental states from its own
behavioral trajectories, i.e., thinking “Why and when I did these
actions?” in a self-supervised manner.

#### Self-Reflection.

Since inverse inference from behavior is generally non-identifiable,
i.e., multiple latent explanations for the same behavior, which can lead
to ambiguous mental states. Hence, we introduce an explicit
self-reflection by cycle-consistency to make the inverse inference
behaviorally identifiable. Particularly, the agent verifies whether its
predicted beliefs and goals can regenerate original behavioral
trajectories, i.e., “whether I will take the same action at the inferred
belief and goal.”, as illustrated in Figure
[1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")(b).
Given a behavioral trajectory $`\bm{a}_{0:T}`$, the latent mental states
$`\hat{\bm{b}}_{t},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}`$ from the inverse models are passed
to the forward policy model $`\pi_{\varphi}`$ and reconstruct actions
with
$`\hat{\bm{a}}_{t}=\pi_{\varphi}(\hat{\bm{b}}_{t},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t})`$.
Hence, the belief-action cycle-consistency loss by minimizing the
discrepancy between the predicted actions $`\hat{\bm{a}}_{t}`$ and
original actions $`\bm{a}_{0:T}`$ is given by:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ref}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{T}\left\|\pi_{\varphi}(\hat{\bm{b}}_{T-k},\hat{\bm{g}}_{T-k})-\bm{a}_{T-k}\right\|^{2}\Big].
``` |  | (5) |

The cycle consistency objective
([5](#S4.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ Self-Reflection. ‣ 4 Proposed MetaMind with Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))
ensures that inferred mental states are not only plausible but also
behaviorally sufficient. This reflective loop plays a dual role that
serves both as a self-improvement signal for $`\Psi_{\theta}`$ and
$`\Omega_{\theta}`$, and as a diagnostic tool for identifying mismatches
among beliefs, goals, and actions, thus stabilizing inverse reasoning.

#### Analogical Inference.

Unlike the internal, inaccessible beliefs and goals of each agent,
actions are universally recognized, externally observable and
protocol-agnostic across heterogeneous agents ([Fudenberg and Levine,
1998](#bib.bib56); [Baker et al., 2009](#bib.bib49); [Baker et al.,
2017](#bib.bib57)). Hence, the Meta-ToM framework endows the agent with
an analogical inference ability, which zero-shot generalizes the
cognitive ability from first-person reasoning to third-person inference,
i.e., from thinking “Why and when did I perform these actions” to
thinking “Why and when they did these actions”. Particularly, the
learned inverse models $`(\Psi^{i}_{\theta},\Omega^{i}_{\theta})`$ of
agent $`i`$ from self-behavioral trajectories can be used for mental
state estimation of other agents. Given an observable action trajectory
$`\{\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)}\}`$ of agent $`j`$, agent $`i`$ can actively
estimate the goals and beliefs of agent $`j`$, respectively, by
analogical inference:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)}:=\Psi^{i}_{\theta}\left(\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t-1}^{(i,j)}\right),\hat{\bm{b}}_{t}^{(i,j)}:=\Omega^{i}_{\theta}\left(\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)}\right).
``` |  | (6) |

In this way, the Meta-ToM enables explicit multi-agent modeling. Based
on estimated $`\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)}`$ and
$`\hat{\bm{b}}_{t}^{(i,j)}`$ from Meta-ToM, agent $`i`$ predicts beliefs
and actions of agent $`j`$ with the forward models, respectively, by
$`\tilde{\bm{b}}^{(i,j)}_{t+1}=\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}(\hat{\bm{b}}_{t}^{(i,j)},\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)})`$
and
$`\tilde{\bm{a}}^{(i,j)}_{t+1}=\pi_{\varphi}(\hat{\bm{b}}^{(i,j)}_{t},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)})`$
for reliable imagination rollouts without centralized information in
multi-agent systems.

#### Self-Supervised Collective Beliefs.

In multi-agent environments, an ego agent must plan under uncertainty
about others’ latent beliefs and goals. We therefore construct a
*collective belief* that aggregates inferred mental states in a
permutation-invariant and goal-conditioned manner, producing a compact
interaction-aware latent representation. This representation summarizes
*who is around, what they likely intend, and how they may respond*, thus
enabling scalable long-horizon planning. Let
$`\mathcal{E}_{\varrho}^{i}(\cdot)`$ be a belief fusion module of agent
$`i`$ for collective beliefs with parameters $`\varrho`$. Given the
actual belief $`\bm{b}^{i}_{t+1}`$ of agent $`i`$, we optimize
$`\mathcal{E}_{\varrho}(\cdot)`$ by a self-supervised objective:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{col}}(\varrho)=\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\varrho}^{i}(\{\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}^{(i,j)},\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)}\})-\operatorname{sg}(\bm{b}^{i}_{t+1})\big\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  | (7) |

The loss function in

provides a more accurate imagination of multi-agent interactions and
enables emergent group awareness, thus enabling long-term planning
without explicit communication or prior information. Moreover, any
permutation-invariant module for belief fusion. Here, we apply the
transformer-based aggregation approach ([Zhang et al.,
2024b](#bib.bib17)), as shown in Appendix

## 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM

In this section, we prove *goal identification*, i.e., distinct goals
induce distinguishable behavioral trajectories, for the effectiveness of
the proposed Meta-ToM. In particular, goal identification guarantees
that minimizing the behavioral residual leads to accurate estimations of
goals. Moreover, the representation-equivalence class is the invariant
shared across agents. While the set of internal representations may
vary, the set of possible tasks $`\mathcal{T}`$ is fixed. Formally, let
$`\Gamma`$ be the group of representation transformations mapping
heterogeneous encodings $`\bm{g}^{j}`$ to the same task
$`\kappa\in\mathcal{T}`$. Goal identification is therefore proven at the
task-equivalence level: for any two distinct tasks $`\kappa_{1}`$,
$`\kappa_{2}`$ with encodings $`\bm{g}_{1}`$, $`\bm{g}_{2}`$,
respectively, the induced behavioral distributions must be
distinguishable, i.e.,
$`p(\bm{a}_{t:t+H}\mid\bm{g}_{1})\;\neq\;p(\bm{a}_{t:t+H}\mid\bm{g}_{2}),`$
while encodings within the same equivalence class $`[\bm{g}]`$ are
treated as indistinguishable, and identification guarantees task-level
intentions rather than agent-specific internal representations. We omit
the subscript parameters $`\theta`$, $`\varphi`$, and the agent index
$`i`$ for brevity in the next discussions.

###### Definition 1 (Behavioral TD residual).

Given an observation horizon $`H\geq 2`$ and a behavioral trajectory
$`\bm{a}_{0:H-1}=(\bm{a}_{0},\ldots,\bm{a}_{H-1})`$. For any candidate
goal $`\bm{g}\in\mathcal{G}`$, we have
$`\bm{b}_{t}(\bm{g}):=\Omega(\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g})`$,
$`\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}(\bm{g}):=\mathcal{D}\big(\bm{b}_{t}(\bm{g}),\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}\big)`$,
and
$`\tilde{\bm{a}}_{t+1}(\bm{g}):=\pi\big(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}(\bm{g}),\bm{g}\big)`$.
Equivalently, let
$`F_{\bm{g}}(\bm{a}):=\pi\!\big(\mathcal{D}(\Omega(\bm{a},\bm{g}),\bm{a},\bm{g}),\,\bm{g}\big)`$,
and then $`\tilde{\bm{a}}_{t+1}(\bm{g})=F_{\bm{g}}(\bm{a}_{t})`$. The
one-step TD residual and the stacked long-horizon residual are,
respectively, defined by

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\phi_{t}(\bm{g}):=\bm{a}_{t+1}-\tilde{\bm{a}}_{t+1}(\bm{g})=\bm{a}_{t+1}-F_{\bm{g}}(\bm{a}_{t}),`$ |  | (8) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}):=\big[\phi_{0}(\bm{g});\ \phi_{1}(\bm{g});\ \ldots;\ \phi_{H-2}(\bm{g})\big].`$ |  |  |

We use $`d_{H}(\bm{g}):=\|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g})\|_{2}`$ as the behavioral
cognition loss for goal identification. Since $`\mathcal{B}`$,
$`\Omega`$ and $`\pi`$ are continuously differentiable,
$`\Phi_{H}(\bm{g})`$ is differentiable in goal $`\bm{g}`$.

###### Definition 2 (Residual margin for discrete goal identification).

Let $`\mathcal{C}=\{\bm{g}^{1},\ldots,\bm{g}^{K}\}\subset\mathcal{G}`$
be a discrete codebook that records learned goal representations
$`\{\bm{g}^{k}\}`$, and let the true goal be
$`\bm{g}^{*}=\bm{g}^{k^{*}}\in\mathcal{C}`$. Given $`\Phi_{H}(\bm{g})`$
and $`d_{H}(\bm{g}):=\|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g})\|_{2}`$, the residual margin of
$`\mathcal{C}`$ at horizon $`H`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})\ :=\ \min_{k\neq k^{*}}\ \big(\|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})-\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\|_{2}\big).
``` |  | (9) |

###### Theorem 1 (Goal identification).

Let observable behavior trajectory $`\bm{a}^{j}_{0:H-1}`$ be generated
by $`\pi^{j}`$ and $`\mathcal{D}^{j}`$ under the true goal
$`\bm{g}^{*}`$. Assume $`\pi(\bm{b},\bm{g})`$ is $`\xi_{\pi}`$-Lipschitz
in $`\bm{b}`$ and $`\mathcal{D}(\cdot,\bm{a}^{j},\bm{g})`$ is
$`\xi_{\mathcal{D}}`$-Lipschitz in $`\bm{b}`$. The per-step mismatches
are given by
$`\Delta\bm{b}^{j}_{t}:=\Omega(\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*})-\bm{b}_{t}^{j}`$,
$`\Delta\mathcal{D}_{t}(\bm{b}):=\mathcal{D}(\bm{b},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*})-\mathcal{D}^{j}(\bm{b},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*})`$,
and
$`\Delta\pi_{t+1}:=\pi^{j}(\bm{b}_{t+1}^{j},\bm{g}^{*})-\pi(\bm{b}_{t+1}^{j},\bm{g}^{*})`$.
The behavioral-mismatch energy is:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{\mathrm{act}}:=\left(\sum_{t=0}^{H-2}\Big(\,\|\Delta\pi_{t+1}\|+\xi_{\pi}\|\varepsilon_{t}\|\Big)^{2}\right)^{1/2},
``` |  | (10) |

where
$`\varepsilon_{t}:=\|\Delta\mathcal{D}_{t}(\bm{b}_{t}^{j})\|+\xi_{\mathcal{D}}\|\Delta\bm{b}^{j}_{t}\|`$.
If $`\delta_{\mathrm{act}}<\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})/2`$,
then the nearest-neighbor
$`\hat{k}=\arg\min_{k}\|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})\|_{2}`$ returns
$`\hat{\bm{g}}=\bm{g}^{\hat{k}}=\bm{g}^{*}`$.

###### Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification).

If there exist $`m`$ and $`\beta>0`$ such that for every window
$`[k,k+m-1]\subseteq[0,H-2]`$, discrete-time sliding-window persistence
of excitation is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{t=k}^{k+m-1}\Big(\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\bm{g}}(\bm{g}^{*})\Big)^{\!\top}\Big(\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\bm{g}}(\bm{g}^{*})\Big)\ \succeq\ \beta\,I_{d_{\bm{g}}}.
``` |  | (12) |

Then, with codebook separation $`\rho_{\min}>0`$, the sufficient horizon
size $`H`$ for robust goal identification is:

H\ \geq\ 1\;+\;m\Big(\big\lfloor L\big\rfloor+1\Big),\qquad L\ :=\ \Big(\frac{4\,\delta_{\mathrm{act}}}{\rho_{\min}\sqrt{\beta}}\Big)^{\!2}.
``` |  | (13) |

Theorem
[1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
converts Meta-ToM from a heuristic to a principled method by proving

[1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
introduces a behavioral-mismatch energy $`\delta_{\mathrm{act}}`$ and

$`\bar{\gamma}_{H}>2\,\delta_{\mathrm{act}}`$. It justifies the
self-reflection and cycle-consistency objective since they reduce
$`d_{H}(g^{*})`$ and enlarge the effective margin.

links smoothness and codebook separation to finite-sample separability,

$`\beta`$ reduce the required horizon.
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
shows *when inverse inference should succeed with finite trajectories*
and provides guidance for choosing the trajectory chunk length used by
Meta-ToM in practice. In a nutshell, Theorems
[1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")-[2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")

explain why inverse inference by Meta-ToM is effective, when zero-shot
analogical inference will succeed, and how to ensure closed-loop
reliability in multi-agent settings.

### 6.3 Impact of Imagination Horizon

Figure
[3](#S6.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 6.1 Experimental Settings ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
studies how the imagination horizon affects multi-agent performance,

next-step information and therefore is not affected by varying $`H`$.
From Figure 3, a longer imagination horizon generally leads to higher
win rates for MetaMind and MARIE, when $`H`$ increases from 5 to 15.
This improvement is significant for coordination-intensive tasks that
require anticipating delayed tactical outcomes, where short-horizon
rollouts tend to be short-sighted. When $`H`$ reaches 20, the benefit
becomes smaller, which is caused by compounding model errors and
increased uncertainty in long-horizon imagined trajectories. MetaMind
reaches the best win rates for $`H\geq 10`$ and shows a smaller
performance reduction at $`H{=}20`$ than MARIE on long-horizon
coordination tasks. For example, on 2c_vs_64zg, MetaMind achieves 43.0 %

performance when the horizon increases to $`H{=}20`$. Figure

suggests that the proposed meta-ToM and collective belief modeling can
stabilize planning under partial observability when the imagination
horizon becomes longer. Based on these results, all remaining
experiments use $`H{=}15`$ as the default horizon, which provides a
balance between lookahead and rollout reliability.

### 6.4 Multi-Agent Generalization

In Figure
[4](#S6.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 6.2 Task Performance ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"),
we evaluate multi-agent generalization with few environment interactions
under teammate distribution shift. In particular, for each SMAC map, we
first train a population of world models for agents. During testing, we
form evaluation teams by randomly replacing one agent in a converged
team with a world model drawn from this population, while keeping the
remaining agents fixed. This creates a distribution shift in observable
behaviors and coordination conventions without changing the
environmental dynamics. This protocol aims to measure (a) teammate
generalization, i.e., robustness to partner-policy changes; (b)
coordination robustness, i.e., the ability to maintain effective
collaboration when implicit coordination protocols differ; and (c)
compositional generalization, i.e., whether agents trained under
different runs can be composed into a functioning team. We propose a
few-shot learning approach for MetaMind to fine-tune inverse inference,
as given in Appendix
[A.4](#A1.SS4 "A.4 Few-Shot Learning for Inverse Belief Inference ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind").

As shown in Figure
[4](#S6.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 6.2 Task Performance ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"),
MetaMind exhibits stronger generalization under controlled
partner-policy replacement. It achieves an average median win rate of
62.7% across 8 SMAC maps, whereas MARIE reaches only 25.9%. Moreover,
MetaMind is robust in the lower tail, achieving a win rate of 59.4% at
the 25th percentile versus 23.0% by MARIE. Compared to MARIE, MetaMind
achieves an average 2.4-fold improvement in few-shot generalization.
This indicates MetaMind has far fewer catastrophic coordination failures
when implicit conventions shift. This is because: (a) analogical
inference maps observed actions to latent goal/belief, thereby reducing
reliance on a single co-adapted protocol, such as the emergent
communication systems in DCWMC; (b) self-reflection regularizes inverse
inference toward behaviorally identifiable explanations, thereby
improving transfer to unseen partner policies; and (c) collective-belief
aggregation absorbs local partner changes into an interaction-aware
belief. Moreover, it is observed that the replacement of teammate breaks
the sender-receiver codebook alignment of DCWMC, so messages become
uninterpretable, thereby causing the communication channel failures and
collapsed coordination performance.

### 6.5 Scalability

Figure [5](#S6.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 6.2 Task Performance ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
shows that MetaMind scales with both model capacity and task diversity.
As the parameter budget increases from 1M to 200M, the normalized win
rate improves from 0.21 to 0.55. It indicates that larger models
effectively translate additional capacity into better multi-task
coordination rather than being bottlenecked by negative transfer.
Moreover, the average inference cost of goal identification for MM
MetaMind is 12.3, compared to 5.3 for SM MetaMind. This indicates that
even under multi-map training, MetaMind can still accurately infer
others’ goals/beliefs and remain reliable across diverse maps with
coordination. Such robust intent identification provides a stable basis
for partner prediction and planning, which in turn supports strong
cooperative performance as model capacity grows.

## 7 Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed *MetaMind*, a generalized cognitive
world model with Meta-ToM for multi-agent systems, which learns from an
ego agent’s experience and generalizes to interactive environments in a
zero-shot manner. We have developed Meta-ToM as a self-supervised
inverse-inference paradigm that treats other agents as intentional
entities and actively reasons latent beliefs and goals from behavioral
trajectories. Meta-ToM combines self-reflection to learn
goal-conditioned causal mechanisms, enables analogical inference to
generalize mental-state estimation across agents, and constructs
permutation-invariant collective beliefs for long-horizon planning under
strategic adaptation. Moreover, we have provided theoretical guarantees
for goal identification from behaviors, thereby offering principled
conditions on separability and horizon length for robust inverse
inference. Extended simulations across SMAC tasks show that MetaMind
achieves  54% higher win rate than exsiting decentralized multi-agent
world models under limited training steps, and a 2.4-fold improvement in
few-shot generalization than exsiting centralized multi-agent world
models.

## Appendix A Implementation Details

### A.1 Multi-Q Network with EMA

The use of a multi-Q Network with an exponential moving average (EMA)
provides more conservative and stable value targets, which can
effectively reduce overestimation bias and variance during
temporal-difference learning ([Hansen et al., 2024](#bib.bib3)). We
maintain an ensemble of $`N_{Q}`$ parallel Q-functions
$`\{Q_{\varpi^{(i)}}\}_{i=1}^{N_{Q}}`$, each with parameters
$`\varpi^{(i)}`$ and EMA target copy $`\bar{\varpi}^{(i)}`$. The EMA
targets are updated with momentum $`\mu\in(0,1)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\varpi}^{(i)}\leftarrow\mu\,\bar{\varpi}^{(i)}+(1-\mu)\,\varpi^{(i)},\qquad i=1,\dots,N_{Q}.
``` |  | (14) |

At training step $`t`$, we form a conservative TD target by uniformly
sampling a subset $`\mathcal{M}_{t}\subset\{1,\dots,N_{Q}\}`$ with
$`|\mathcal{M}_{t}|=2`$, evaluating the EMA targets, and taking the
minimum:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t}=r_{t}+\lambda\,\gamma\,\min_{j\in\mathcal{M}_{t}}Q_{\bar{\varpi}^{(j)}}\!\left(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1},\;\pi_{\varpi}\!\left(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1},\bm{g}\right)\right),
``` |  | (15) |

where $`\lambda\in(0,1]`$ is a constant factor, $`\gamma\in(0,1)`$ is
the discount, and $`\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}`$ is the latent belief state
predicted from $`(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g})`$ by the model in the
main body. The policy prior is
$`\tilde{\bm{a}}_{t+1}=\pi_{\varpi}(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{g})`$. This
stochastic subsampling-and-minimization yields a conservative bootstrap
that mitigates overestimation while remaining computationally efficient.
By using the TD target $`q_{t}`$ from
([15](#A1.E15 "Equation 15 ‣ A.1 Multi-Q Network with EMA ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
the per-network value loss is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{Q}\!\left(\varpi^{(i)}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\zeta^{t}\;\Im\!\left(\mathbf{s}^{(i)}_{t},\;q_{t}\right)\right],\qquad i=1,\dots,N_{Q},
``` |  | (16) |

where $`\zeta\in(0,1]`$ is the temporal weighting factor,
$`\tau=\{\bm{o}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},r_{t},\bm{o}_{t+1}\}`$ is a sampled
trajectory, and $`\mathbf{s}^{(i)}_{t}`$ are the logits produced by the
$`i`$-th Q head at $`(\bm{b}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g})`$.

### A.2 Discrete Regression Objective in Log-Space.

Following the main body, both reward and value are trained via discrete
regression ([Hansen et al., 2024](#bib.bib3)) in a log-transformed space
using a soft cross-entropy $`\Im(\cdot)`$ to ensure scale-invariant,
multi-goal learning. Concretely, let the value head of the $`i`$-th
Q-network output logits $`\mathbf{s}^{(i)}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}`$ over
fixed log-space bins $`\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}`$. Let $`\sigma(\cdot)`$
denote the softmax and let $`\mathbf{w}(y)\in\Delta^{K-1}`$ be a soft
target distribution induced by the scalar $`y`$, i.e., by linear
interpolation between neighboring bins in log space. Then, the process
can be given by:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Im\!\left(\mathbf{s},y\right)=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{k}(y)\,\log\sigma(\mathbf{s})_{k},\quad\tilde{y}(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma(\mathbf{s})_{k}\,b_{k},`$ |  | (17) |

where $`\tilde{y}(\mathbf{s})`$ is the differentiable expectation used
for reporting or for auxiliary terms in the prediction loss
([2](#S3.Ex1 "In 3.1 Temporal Difference-Based World Model ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")).

### A.3 Aggregation Approaches for Collective Belief

We aggregate the unordered neighbor set by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
X^{(0)}=\{\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,j)}:\;j\in\mathcal{N}(i)\},\qquad\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,j)}=\big[\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}^{(i,j)};\,\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)}\big],
``` |  | (18) |

by using $`L`$ permutation-invariant self-attention layers followed by
an order-invariant pooling. For each element $`\bm{x}\in X^{(\ell)}`$
and self-attention layer $`\ell=0,\dots,L-1`$, linear projections are
defined by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Q_{\ell}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}W_{Q}^{(\ell)},\quad K_{\ell}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}W_{K}^{(\ell)},\quad V_{\ell}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}W_{V}^{(\ell)},
``` |  | (19) |

where $`W_{Q}^{(\ell)}`$, $`W_{K}^{(\ell)}`$, and $`W_{V}^{(\ell)}`$ are
parameters. The attention output for $`\bm{x}`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{Attn}_{\ell}(\bm{x},X^{(\ell)})=\sum_{\bm{y}\in X^{(\ell)}}\alpha_{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\,V_{\ell}(\bm{y}),\qquad\alpha_{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{\exp\!\big(Q_{\ell}(\bm{x})K_{\ell}(\bm{y})^{\!\top}/\sqrt{d_{k}}\big)}{\sum_{\bm{z}\in X^{(\ell)}}\exp\!\big(Q_{\ell}(\bm{x})K_{\ell}(\bm{z})^{\!\top}/\sqrt{d_{k}}\big)},
``` |  | (20) |

where $`d_{K}`$ represents the dimension of the key and query. Apply an
output projection and a pointwise two-layer forward update:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{\bm{x}}=\mathrm{Attn}_{\ell}(\bm{x},X^{(\ell)})\,W_{O}^{(\ell)},\qquad\bm{h}=\sigma\!\big(\tilde{\bm{x}}W_{1}^{(\ell)}+\bm{b}_{1}^{(\ell)}\big)W_{2}^{(\ell)}+\bm{b}_{2}^{(\ell)},
``` |  | (21) |

with weights $`W_{O}^{(\ell)}`$, $`W_{1}^{(\ell)}`$, $`W_{2}^{(\ell)}`$,
biases $`\bm{b}_{1}^{(\ell)}`$,$`\bm{b}_{2}^{(\ell)}`$, and nonlinearity
$`\sigma(\cdot)`$. Set

|     |                                                   |     |      |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       X^{(\ell+1)}=\{\bm{h}\,:\,\bm{x}\in X^{(\ell)}\}.
       ```                                                |     | (22) |

Since attention weights and summations range over the entire set, this
layer is permutation-invariant with respect to the ordering of
$`X^{(\ell)}`$. From the final set $`X^{(L)}`$, scalar scores and
pooling weights are computed, respectively, by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
s(\bm{x})=\bm{x}\bm{w}_{s}+c_{s},\qquad\beta_{ij}=\frac{\exp\!\big(s(\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,j)})\big)}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\exp\!\big(s(\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,k)})\big)},
``` |  | (23) |

where $`\bm{w}_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ and $`c_{s}\in\mathbb{R}`$. The
neighbor features are aggregated through a linear map

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u(\bm{x})=\sigma(\bm{x}W_{u}+\bm{b}_{u}),\qquad\bm{z}_{i}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\beta_{ij}\,u\!\big(\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,j)}\big),
``` |  | (24) |

with parameters $`W_{u}`$ and $`\bm{b}_{u}`$. Finally, the collective
belief is projected by the pooled vector:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{E}_{\varrho}^{i}\!\big(\{\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,j)}\}\big)=\sigma\!\Big(\,[\bm{x}_{t}^{(i,i)};\,\bm{z}_{i}]\,W_{o}+\bm{b}_{o}\Big),
``` |  | (25) |

where parameters $`W_{o}`$ and $`\bm{b}_{o}`$. By construction, the
aggregation
([25](#A1.E25 "Equation 25 ‣ A.3 Aggregation Approaches for Collective Belief ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))
is invariant to neighbor indices. All weights and biases above are part
of $`\varrho`$.

### A.4 Few-Shot Learning for Inverse Belief Inference

![Refer to caption](2603.00808v1/FewShot.png)

Figure 6: Few-shot learning for inverse belief inference by a
self-supervised method in multi-agent systems.

We propose a self-supervised, few-shot adaptation approach, with a
similar architecture to joint-embedding predictive architecture proposed
in ([Assran et al., 2025](#bib.bib4)), that personalizes the inverse
belief encoder of agent $`i`$ to any new partner $`j`$ by using only
short behavioral trajectories $`\{\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)}\}`$ and without
supervised labels or explicit communication, as shown in Figure
[6](#A1.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ A.4 Few-Shot Learning for Inverse Belief Inference ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind").
Particularly, the student inverse encoder $`\Omega_{\theta}^{i}`$ maps
the current action to a latent belief
$`\hat{\bm{b}}^{(i,j)}_{t}\;=\;\Omega_{\theta}^{i}\!\big(\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)},\bm{g}_{t}^{(i,j)}\big)`$,
as given in
([6](#S4.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Analogical Inference. ‣ 4 Proposed MetaMind with Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
which is fed into a frozen predictor $`\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}^{i}`$ to
generate a one-step belief prediction:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}^{(i,j)}\;=\;\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}^{i}\!\big(\hat{\bm{b}}^{(i,j)}_{t},\bm{a}^{(i,j)}_{t}\hat{\bm{g}}^{(i,j)}_{t}\big),\quad\text{with }\ \mathrm{sg}\left(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}^{i}\right).
``` |  | (26) |

Learning is driven by an action-space temporal consistency objective,
i.e., L2 regression to the next observed action:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv-belief}}^{(i,j)}\;=\;\sum_{t}\big\|\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}^{i}\!\big(\Omega_{\theta}^{i}(\bm{a}_{t}^{(i,j)},\hat{\bm{g}}^{(i,j)}_{t})\big)-\bar{\Omega}_{\theta}^{i}(\bm{a}_{t+1}^{(i,j)},\hat{\bm{g}}^{(i,j)}_{t+1})\big\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  | (27) |

where we maintain an EMA target encoder $`\bar{\Omega}^{i}`$ as the
teacher encoder to stabilize few-shot updates and prevent representation
drift as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\Omega}^{i}\;\leftarrow\;\tau\,\bar{\Omega}^{i}+(1-\tau)\,\Omega_{\theta}^{i},\quad\tau\in[0,1).
``` |  | (28) |

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Big\|\pi(\mathcal{D}(\bm{b}_{t}^{j}+\Delta\bm{b}^{j}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*}),\bm{g}^{*})-\pi(\mathcal{D}(\bm{b}_{t}^{j},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*}),\bm{g}^{*})\Big\|`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\xi_{\pi}\ \|\mathcal{D}(\bm{b}_{t}^{j}+\Delta\bm{b}^{j}_{t},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*})-\mathcal{D}(\bm{b}_{t}^{j},\bm{a}_{t},\bm{g}^{*})\|`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq\xi_{\pi}\xi_{\mathcal{D}}\ \|\Delta\bm{b}^{j}_{t}\|.`$ |  | (36) |

Take norms in
([34](#A3.E34 "Equation 34 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.1 Proof of Theorem : Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))
and apply the triangle inequality together with
([35](#A3.E35 "Equation 35 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.1 Proof of Theorem : Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))-([36](#A3.E36 "Equation 36 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.1 Proof of Theorem : Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\phi_{t}(\bm{g}^{*})\|\ \leq\ \underbrace{\|\Delta\pi_{t+1}\|}_{\text{policy mismatch}}+\,\xi_{\pi}\big(\underbrace{\|\Delta\mathcal{D}_{t}(\bm{b}_{t}^{j})\|}_{\text{dynamics mismatch}}+\,\xi_{\mathcal{D}}\underbrace{\|\Delta\bm{b}^{j}_{t}\|}_{\text{belief mismatch}}\big):=\alpha_{t}.
``` |  | (37) |

Therefore, we can obtain

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})=\sum_{t=0}^{H-2}\|\phi_{t}(\bm{g}^{*})\|^{2}\ \leq\ \sum_{t=0}^{H-2}\alpha_{t}^{2}\ =\ \delta_{\mathrm{act}}^{\,2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad d_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\ \leq\ \delta_{\mathrm{act}}.
``` |  | (38) |

Given the empirical residual gap
$`\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*}):=\min_{k\neq k^{*}}\ \|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})-\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\|_{2}`$
defined in
Definition [2](#Thmdefinition2 "Definition 2 (Residual margin for discrete goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"),
$`\gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})\ :=\ \min_{k\neq k^{*}}\ \|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})\|_{2}`$
and $`d_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\leq\delta_{\mathrm{act}}`$ given in
([38](#A3.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.1 Proof of Theorem : Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
for any $`k\neq k^{*}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})\ \geq\ \gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})-d_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\ \geq\ \gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})-\delta_{\mathrm{act}}.
``` |  | (39) |

Hence, under $`\delta_{\mathrm{act}}<\gamma/2`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})\ \geq\ \gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})-\delta_{\mathrm{act}}\ >\ \gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})/2\ >\ 0.
``` |  | (40) |

Therefore for every $`k\neq k^{*}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})\ \geq\ d_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})+\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})\ >\ d_{H}(\bm{g}^{*}),
``` |  | (41) |

thus, $`\bm{g}^{*}`$ is the unique minimizer of
$`k\mapsto d_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})`$, and the nearest-neighbor estimator
returns $`\hat{\bm{g}}=\bm{g}^{*}`$. Theorem
[1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
is proved. ∎

### C.2 Proof of Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (Local linear margin via Lipschitz Jacobian). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"): Local Linear Margin

###### Proof.

Fix $`k\neq k^{*}`$ and set $`\Delta_{k}:=\bm{g}^{k}-\bm{g}^{k^{*}}`$.
For each coordinate $`\Phi_{H}`$, by the fundamental theorem of
calculus,
$`\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})-\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}})=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}}+s\Delta_{k})^{\top}\Delta_{k}\,ds`$.
Stacking all coordinates yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})-\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}})=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}}+s\Delta_{k})\,ds\Big)\Delta_{k}=:J_{\rm avg}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}},\bm{g}^{k})\,\Delta_{k}.
``` |  | (42) |

Since $`\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}})=0`$, we have
$`\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})=J_{\rm avg}\Delta_{k}`$.

Assume $`J_{\Phi}`$ is $`L_{J}`$-Lipschitz near $`\bm{g}^{*}`$. Then

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\big\|J_{\rm avg}-J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\big\|`$ | $`\displaystyle=\Big\|\int_{0}^{1}\big(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}}+s\Delta_{k})-J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)\,ds\Big\|`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big\|J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{k^{*}}+s\Delta_{k})-J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\big\|\,ds`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}L_{J}s\|\Delta_{k}\|\,ds=\frac{L_{J}}{2}\,\|\Delta_{k}\|.`$ |  | (43) |

Applying the Weyl’s perturbation inequality, i.e., for any matrices
$`A,B`$, $`\sigma_{\min}(A+B)\geq\sigma_{\min}(A)-\|B\|`$, to
$`A=J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})`$ and $`B=J_{\rm avg}-J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})`$,
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma_{\min}(J_{\rm avg})\geq\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*}))-\|J_{\rm avg}-J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:JavgLip})}}{{\geq}}\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*}))-\frac{L_{J}}{2}\|\Delta_{k}\|.
``` |  | (44) |

If
$`\|\Delta_{k}\|\leq\phi_{\star}:=\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*}))/L_{J}`$,
then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma_{\min}(J_{\rm avg})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})).
``` |  | (45) |

By using $`\|Ax\|\geq\sigma_{\min}(A)\|x\|`$ and
([42](#A3.E42 "Equation 42 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Proof of Lemma : Local Linear Margin ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})=\|J_{\rm avg}\Delta_{k}\|\geq\sigma_{\min}(J_{\rm avg})\,\|\Delta_{k}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*}))\,\|\bm{g}^{k}-\bm{g}^{k^{*}}\|,
``` |  | (46) |

and
([46](#A3.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Proof of Lemma : Local Linear Margin ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))
follows by minimizing over $`k\neq k^{*}`$. Lemma
[1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (Local linear margin via Lipschitz Jacobian). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
is proved. ∎

### C.3 Proof of Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"): Sufficient Horizon Size for Goal Identification

###### Proof.

We have the persistence-of-excitation defined in
([12](#S5.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))
as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{t=k}^{k+m-1}\Big(\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\bm{g}}(\bm{g}^{*})\Big)^{\!\top}\Big(\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\bm{g}}(\bm{g}^{*})\Big)\ \succeq\ \beta\,I_{d_{g}},
``` |  | (47) |

at the true goal $`\bm{g}^{*}`$ and we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{t}(\bm{g}^{*}):=\frac{\partial\bm{\phi}_{t}}{\partial\bm{g}}(\bm{g}^{*}),\quad W_{H}(\bm{g}^{*}):=\sum_{t=0}^{H-2}G_{t}(\bm{g}^{*})^{\top}G_{t}(\bm{g}^{*}).
``` |  | (48) |

Partition the index set $`\{0,1,\ldots,H-2\}`$ into
$`q:=\big\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\big\rfloor`$ disjoint windows
$`\mathcal{I}_{i}=\{(i-1)m,\ldots,im-1\}`$ for $`i=1,\ldots,q`$. By
([12](#S5.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
for each window, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}G_{t}^{\top}G_{t}\succeq\beta I_{d_{\bm{g}}}.
``` |  | (49) |

Then, adding these positive semi-definite inequalities over
$`i=1,\ldots,q`$ obtains

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
W_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})=\sum_{t=0}^{H-2}G_{t}^{\top}G_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\ \sum_{t\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}G_{t}^{\top}G_{t}\succeq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta I_{d_{g}}=q\,\beta\,I_{d_{\bm{g}}}.
``` |  | (50) |

By the Rayleigh-Ritz characterization, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lambda_{\min}\!\big(W_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)=\min_{\|z\|=1}z^{\top}W_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})z\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{tag:1})}}{{\geq}}\ \min_{\|z\|=1}z^{\top}(q\beta I)z=q\beta=\Big\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\Big\rfloor\,\beta.
``` |  | (51) |

Let $`J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})`$ be the stacked Jacobian. By taking the
singular value decomposition $`J_{\Phi}=U\Sigma V^{\top}`$, then we have
$`W_{H}=J_{\Phi}^{\top}J_{\Phi}=V\Sigma^{2}V^{\top}`$, and every
eigenvalue of $`W_{H}`$ equals a squared singular value of $`J_{\Phi}`$.
Hence, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma_{\min}\!\big(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)=\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}\!\big(W_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)}.
``` |  | (52) |

By the local constructive margin inequality
([46](#A3.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Proof of Lemma : Local Linear Margin ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
for each $`k\neq k^{*}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{\min}\!\big(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)\,\|\bm{g}^{k}-\bm{g}^{k^{*}}\|.
``` |  | (53) |

Let $`\rho_{\min}:=\min_{k\neq\ell}\|\bm{g}^{k}-\bm{g}^{\ell}\|`$.
Taking the minimum over $`k\neq k^{*}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*}):=\min_{k\neq k^{*}}\|\Phi_{H}(\bm{g}^{k})\|\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{\min}\!\big(J_{\Phi}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)\,\rho_{\min}.
``` |  | (54) |

Now substitute
([52](#A3.E52 "Equation 52 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.3 Proof of Theorem : Sufficient Horizon Size for Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"))
into
([54](#A3.E54 "Equation 54 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.3 Proof of Theorem : Sufficient Horizon Size for Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
and then apply
([51](#A3.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.3 Proof of Theorem : Sufficient Horizon Size for Goal Identification ‣ Appendix C Proof ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\,\rho_{\min}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}\!\big(W_{H}(\bm{g}^{*})\big)}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\,\rho_{\min}\,\sqrt{\Big\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\Big\rfloor\,\beta}.
``` |  | (55) |

Hence, a sufficient condition is given by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{\mathrm{act}}\ <\ \frac{1}{4}\,\rho_{\min}\,\sqrt{\Big\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\Big\rfloor\,\beta}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Big\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\Big\rfloor\ >\ \Big(\frac{4\,\delta_{\mathrm{act}}}{\rho_{\min}\sqrt{\beta}}\Big)^{\!2}\;=\;T.
``` |  | (56) |

To ensure the strict inequality on the left, it is enough to require

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Big\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\Big\rfloor\ \geq\ \big\lfloor T\big\rfloor+1,
``` |  | (57) |

because $`\lfloor T\rfloor+1>T`$ for all real $`T`$. Since
$`\lfloor x\rfloor\geq n`$ iff $`x\geq n`$ for integer $`n`$, the above
is equivalent to

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{H-1}{m}\ \geq\ \big\lfloor T\big\rfloor+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H\ \geq\ 1\;+\;m\Big(\big\lfloor T\big\rfloor+1\Big).
``` |  | (58) |

Under this condition, we have
$`\delta_{\mathrm{act}}<\bar{\gamma}_{H}(\mathcal{C};\bm{g}^{*})/2`$, so
by
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind"),
the nearest-neighbor rule recovers the true goal. Corollary
[2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Sufficient horizon size for goal identification). ‣ 5 Goal Identification by Meta-ToM ‣ MetaMind: General and Cognitive World Models in Multi-Agent Systems by Meta-Theory of Mind")
is proved. ∎

###### Remark 2.

If $`T\notin\mathbb{N}`$, the simpler bound $`H\geq 1+m\lceil T\rceil`$
already ensures $`\lfloor\frac{H-1}{m}\rfloor\geq\lceil T\rceil>T`$.
When $`T\in\mathbb{N}`$, an extra window, replacing $`\lceil T\rceil`$
by $`T+1`$, is needed to guarantee strict inequality.

## Appendix E Further Discussion

### E.1 Effectiveness

The proposed MetaMind demonstrates effectiveness since:

#### Meta-ToM Mechanism.

By introducing a self-supervised Meta-ToM mechanism, each agent develops
a bidirectional inference loop between beliefs and actions, which turns
passive trajectory modeling into active, goal-oriented reasoning based
on causal behavioral principles.

#### Self-Reflection.

The self-reflection reduces the ambiguity inherent in inverse inference
and provides a principled means for bounding estimation error, as
formally established through the identification analysis.

#### Meta-Cognition.

By generalizing introspective inference from single-agent trajectories
to other agents by analogical reasoning, MetaMind exhibits a form of
meta-cognition that enables zero-shot adaptability in heterogeneous
multi-agent environments. A self-supervised approach can be used for
learning accurate inverse belief inference and learning collective
beliefs in multi-agent systems.

These elements enable MetaMind to support reliable prediction and
long-horizon planning without centralized supervised learning or
explicit communication.

### E.2 Limitations

Despite the promising results of the proposed MetaMind, several
limitations remain.

#### Limited Communication.

MetaMind depends on observable neighbor trajectories. When task-relevant
partners are partially or fully unobservable, e.g., occlusion,
misrecognition, sensing dropout, inverse and reflective inference become
underconstrained. This degrades belief-goal estimation
$`\big(\tilde{\bm{b}}_{t+1}^{(i,j)},\,\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{(i,j)}\big)`$
and cannot accurately form collective aggregation
$`\mathcal{E}_{\varrho}^{i}(\cdot)`$. Consequently, prediction and
planning reliability cannot be ensured under persistent partial
observability of the task-related partners.

#### Goals Difference.

The framework assumes a shared task structure across agents despite
heterogeneous policies and representations. In adversarial or weakly
cooperative settings, particularly with a large or evolving set of
unknown goals, this assumption may be violated. Such violations
compromise goal identifiability and limit zero-shot generalization
beyond the known goal codebook.

#### Practical Applications.

Current evaluations are simulation-centric. Real embodied multi-agent
deployments introduce additional uncertainties (sensor noise, delays,
non-ideal dynamics), resource constraints, safety requirements, and
nonstationary conditions. These factors may reduce the fidelity of
reflective inference and collective belief formation relative to
controlled benchmarks.

### E.3 Future Work

Several promising directions arise from these limitations:

#### Mixed-Communication Mechanism.

A feasible solution is to extend MetaMind with a *mixed-communication
mechanism* that flexibly combines implicit inference from trajectories
with lightweight explicit signals when behavioral observations are
insufficient. Specifically, (a) when neighbors are fully observable,
agents rely on Meta-ToM inference as before; (b) under occlusion,
recognition failure, or sparse observations, agents trigger minimal
communication channels, such as compressed latent codes or uncertainty
flags, to exchange partial beliefs without requiring full symbolic
protocols; and (c) attention-based gating can adaptively decide when and
with whom to communicate, balancing efficiency and robustness. This
hybrid design preserves MetaMind’s decentralized nature while extending
its applicability to partially observable multi-agent environments,
enabling reliable coordination even in the presence of hidden or
transient partners.

#### Robust Goal Modeling.

A key direction is to relax the shared-task assumption and pursue
open-set goal identification, where agents must infer and act under
previously unseen goals and a potentially large goal space. Concretely,
we aim to (a) learn compositional and disentangled goal representations
that support systematic generalization beyond the goal codebook, (b)
incorporate uncertainty-aware inference, e.g., calibrated posteriors or
confidence scores, to detect goal novelty and trigger few-shot
adaptation, and (c) develop meta-learning and continual discovery
procedures that grow the goal repertoire online while preserving past
competencies.

#### Practical Applications.

A promising direction is to validate MetaMind in real-world embodied
multi-agent systems, where uncertainty, partial observability, and
physical constraints are unavoidable. One feasible pathway is to first
deploy MetaMind in controlled robotic platforms, such as multi-robot
navigation or cooperative manipulation, where ground-truth sensing
allows systematic benchmarking. Next, incorporating domain adaptation
and sim-to-real transfer techniques can mitigate the gap between
simulated and physical environments, ensuring that the reflective
inference and goal identifiability mechanisms remain robust under sensor
noise and actuation delays. Finally, integration with human-AI
collaborative settings, such as assistive robotics or mixed-reality
coordination tasks, can test the scalability of MetaMind under
heterogeneous partners and real-time decision requirements. This
progressive pipeline provides a practical route to extend the framework
beyond simulation and toward embodied intelligence in open-world
applications.
````
