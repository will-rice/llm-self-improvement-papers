# Richness Is Not Evenness: Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training

Pranjal Srivastava Affiliation: University of Michigan

###### Abstract

Recursive self-training of language models, a setting studied in model
collapse, can reduce vocabulary while making frequent words, mostly
function words, less dominant. We fine-tune English GPT-2 on its own
output for nine generations, vary the training-data sampling
temperature, and measure every model with one fixed sampler. As training
temperature rises, Rényi entropy changes turn from loss to gain at
distinct temperatures: min-entropy first, then collision entropy,
Shannon entropy and observed richness. Between the outer crossings, word
types fall while the most-common word becomes less dominant. Untruncated
training at $`T=1`$ lies in this interval: at 13,000 words, generation 9
has 24% fewer types than generation 0 yet collision entropy is 0.91 bits
higher. The ordering persists with a finer grid, different measurement
samplers, a fixed word budget and five fresh training seeds. Min-entropy
again crosses before richness in four further settings (in Hindi only at
interpolated roots) and in 12 of 13 distinct measurements under eight
text representations. Next-token identities and a categorical simulation
isolate decoding and sampling effects; the neural crossing order remains
empirical. Fine-tuning on human text can produce the same split while
likelihood improves, and no single order’s sign reliably identifies
worse likelihood across the tested settings. We recommend reporting
entropy profiles at stated budgets with an independent quality measure.

## 1 Introduction

Training language models on synthetic text can erode quality and
diversity ([Shumailov et al., 2024](#bib.bib7); [Dohmatob et al.,
2024](#bib.bib9)). Single diversity scores measure different properties:
richness counts observed word types, whereas higher-order entropies
increasingly emphasize common words. Losing rare words while spreading
usage more evenly among those retained can therefore lower richness and
raise higher-order entropy. The reported direction depends on which
property is measured.

We fine-tune English GPT-2 on its own samples for nine generations,
varying the temperature and nucleus (top-$`p`$) threshold of the
_training sampler_. Every model is measured with one fixed sampler
($`T=1`$, top-$`p=0.95`$). As training temperature rises, min-entropy
$`H_{\infty}`$ turns from loss to gain first, near $`T=0.927`$, followed
by collision entropy $`H_{2}`$ ($`0.962`$), Shannon entropy $`H_{1}`$
($`0.997`$) and observed richness $`H_{0}`$ ($`1.035`$)
(Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Between the outer crossings, word types fall while the most-common word
becomes less dominant. Untruncated sampling at $`T=1`$ lies inside this
interval.

The outer ordering survives 12 of 13 distinct measurements under eight
representations and recurs under LoRA, in OLMo and in German and Hindi
GPT-2 (Hindi only by interpolation). We test the diagnostic value of
these changes: truncation lowers entropy relative to untruncated
training in ten settings, but no order’s sign reliably identifies worse
likelihood, and human adaptation can produce the same split while
likelihood improves.

Diversity profiles are standard ([Hill, 1973](#bib.bib21); [Tóthmérész,
1995](#bib.bib23)). [Drayson et al. (2025)](#bib.bib17) already observed
rising $`n`$-gram diversity with worsening perplexity in recursive GPT-2
under pure sampling ($`T=1`$, no truncation). Our contribution is the
measured ordering of sign changes across entropy orders, its sensitivity
to measurement choices, and tests of its diagnostic limits. Five fresh
training seeds replicate the primary ordering
(Appendix [G](#A7 "Appendix G Fresh-seed studies and family-held-out analysis ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Section [6.2](#S6.SS2 "6.2 Ordered zero crossings across training temperatures ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
presents the main crossing result; the later experiments test its
transfer and diagnostic limits, with detailed controls in the
appendices.

## 2 Background and related work

#### Diversity profiles.

Rényi entropy ([Rényi, 1961](#bib.bib2)), extended by limits at orders
zero and one, includes observed log-richness, Shannon entropy ([Shannon,
1948](#bib.bib3)) and collision entropy. Yule’s $`K`$ has been studied
as a constancy measure of text ([Tweedie and Baayen, 1998](#bib.bib5)).
Collision entropy is closely related and has also been studied in this
role ([Tanaka-Ishii and Aihara, 2015](#bib.bib4)). In ecology,
$`\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}`$ is Simpson’s concentration ([Simpson,
1949](#bib.bib20)) and $`2^{H_{\alpha}}`$ are Hill numbers ([Hill,
1973](#bib.bib21); [Jost, 2006](#bib.bib22)); reporting a profile over
orders, whose members weight rare and abundant species differently, is
long-standing practice ([Tóthmérész, 1995](#bib.bib23)). The Vendi Score
and its order-indexed extensions apply the same idea to the spectra of
similarity matrices ([Friedman and Dieng, 2023](#bib.bib27); [Pasarkar
and Dieng, 2024](#bib.bib28)), coverage-rarefied Hill–Shannon diversity
has been used to compare the claims made by language models with web
search ([Wright et al., 2026](#bib.bib58)), and higher-order Rényi
entropies of repeated sequences separate model text from natural
language ([Tanaka-Ishii, 2026](#bib.bib59)). [Zouhar et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib24) sweep Rényi orders to evaluate tokenizers, and
[France and Albinsson (2026)](#bib.bib25) find that entropy- and
concentration-based measures can rank human and model text differently.
Recommending a profile is therefore not new; our question is how the
_sign_ of each order’s change behaves under controlled recursion.

#### Generation and recursive training.

Distinct-$`n`$ ([Li et al., 2016](#bib.bib12)) and self-BLEU ([Zhu et
al., 2018](#bib.bib13)) measure output diversity, MAUVE ([Pillutla et
al., 2021](#bib.bib11)) measures distributional fit to reference text,
and diversity metrics differ in whether they capture form or content
([Tevet and Berant, 2021](#bib.bib32)). Recursive degradation has been
studied empirically and through scaling laws ([Shumailov et al.,
2024](#bib.bib7); [Dohmatob et al., 2024](#bib.bib9)), also in image
models ([Alemohammad et al., 2024](#bib.bib8)), including effects on
lexical and syntactic diversity ([Guo et al., 2024](#bib.bib10)) and
repetition ([Herel and Mikolov, 2024](#bib.bib38)); even without
recursion, synthetic training data over-concentrate $`n`$-gram features
([Zhu et al., 2025](#bib.bib36)). Curation, the share of fresh data, and
whether synthetic data replace or accumulate with real data all change
the outcome ([Ferbach et al., 2024](#bib.bib19); [Briesch et al.,
2023](#bib.bib34); [Gerstgrasser et al., 2024](#bib.bib55); [Kazdan et
al., 2025](#bib.bib37)). Next-token concentration has been used to track
collapse, with perplexity-based filtering as a remedy ([Gambetta et al.,
2026](#bib.bib31)), and the time a model takes to forget a symbol has
been analyzed theoretically ([Suresh et al., 2025](#bib.bib30)).
Definitions of collapse vary ([Schaeffer et al., 2026](#bib.bib18)). We
define degradation by a stated reference-likelihood cutoff and evaluate
diversity changes separately.

#### Closest prior results.

[Seddik et al. (2024)](#bib.bib45) derive the collision ratchet, with
its rate, for recursive maximum-likelihood training of categorical
models;
Proposition [7](#Thmproposition7 "Proposition 7 (Truncated categorical refitting is a diversity ratchet). ‣ D.9 Proposition (diversity ratchet) ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
(Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"))
extends the argument to upper-set truncation. [Dohmatob et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib9) model truncation as cutting the tail of the
distribution and low temperature as narrowing it, and [Marchi et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib56) prove that closed-loop retraining at a non-trivial
sampling temperature degenerates, either to a few outputs or to a
uniform “heat death”, unless enough fresh data enter each round. Under
untruncated, infinite-sample recursive tempering of a non-uniform
categorical law, all positive orders switch sign at $`T=1`$ and support
size stays fixed
(Propositions [4](#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4 (Tempering acts by dilation). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
and [5](#Thmproposition5 "Proposition 5 (Temperature sensitivity). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
The separate neural roots depart from this baseline, without isolating
the effects of sampling, autoregressive contexts, refitting or the fixed
truncated reader. [Drayson et al. (2025)](#bib.bib17) vary the decoder
in recursive GPT-2: under pure sampling ($`T=1`$, no truncation),
$`n`$-gram diversity rises while perplexity worsens; all five other
decoders lose $`n`$-gram diversity. Meanwhile, [Kovač et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib35) link lexical diversity to recursive distribution
shift. We measure the order of zero crossings across the Rényi spectrum,
test its sensitivity to budget and representation, and ask what it lets
a diagnostic infer.

#### Recent and concurrent work.

[Hu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib39) independently recommend diversity
profiles across orders and scales. [Skobelev et al. (2026)](#bib.bib70)
measure diversity by collision probability, bound the absolute
model–target collision gap by the square root of the KL divergence from
target to model, and show that supervised fine-tuning can leave a model
under- or over-dispersed. Their setting is fine-tuning toward a fixed
target at order 2; ours tracks the sign changes across Rényi orders
during recursive self-training. Other studies address diversity loss in
post-training ([Karouzos et al., 2026](#bib.bib40)), entropy-based early
warnings ([Zhang, 2026](#bib.bib41)), recursion in larger models
([Nakamura et al., 2026](#bib.bib42)), the loss of rare forms within
languages ([Vanmassenhove, 2026](#bib.bib43)), ecological diversity
measures for linguistic structure ([Gude et al., 2026](#bib.bib44)), and
the diversity of synthetic data sources ([Schaffelder and Gatt,
2026](#bib.bib46)). Concurrently, [Liu and Han (2026)](#bib.bib57) trace
rapid lexical collapse in vLLM to draws shared across requests and
replayed across generations. Changing seeds removes the rapid loss in
their controls, revising their earlier account. Our Transformers harness
keys streams by replicate, generation and phase, sharing them across
paired arms but not replaying an unchanged seed across generations. [Liu
(2026)](#bib.bib26) finds that self-training amplifies surface markers
while deeper syntactic structures decay, and that 100-word-window
type–token ratio rises. In our own English GPT-2 runs at $`T=1`$, the
seed-mean ratio over non-overlapping 100-word windows at common length
rises from $`0.746`$ to $`0.841`$ while the common-length type inventory
falls by 43%.

## 3 The measure

#### Setup.

Let $`p=(p_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{V}}`$ be a distribution over a vocabulary,
either a next-token softmax or the empirical word frequencies
$`\hat{p}_{v}=f_{v}/N`$ of a text. For finite $`\alpha\geq 0`$ with
$`\alpha\neq 1`$, the Rényi entropy is
$`H_{\alpha}(p)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log_{2}\sum_{i:p_{i}>0}p_{i}^{\alpha}`$.
Thus $`H_{0}=\log_{2}|\operatorname{supp}p|`$, and the limiting orders
are Shannon entropy $`H_{1}=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log_{2}p_{i}`$ and
min-entropy $`H_{\infty}=-\log_{2}\max_{i}p_{i}`$. Entropy does not
increase with the order, so $`H_{0}\geq H_{1}\geq H_{2}\geq H_{\infty}`$
(Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Collision entropy is $`H_{2}=-\log_{2}C`$, where
$`C(p)=\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}`$ is the probability that two independent draws
coincide.

#### Yule’s $`K`$.

In empirical-probability form ([Yule, 1944](#bib.bib1)),

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
K=10^{4}\,\frac{\sum_{v}f_{v}^{2}-N}{N^{2}}=10^{4}\Big(2^{-H_{2}(\hat{p})}-\tfrac{1}{N}\Big).
``` |  | (1) |

By
([1](#S3.E1 "In Yule’s 𝐾. ‣ 3 The measure ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
at fixed $`N`$ Yule’s $`K`$ is a strictly decreasing function of
$`H_{2}`$, so for texts of equal length a fall in $`K`$ and a rise in
$`H_{2}`$ are the same event. The $`1/N`$ offset can break this
correspondence between texts of different lengths, which is one reason
we compare texts at matched budgets. The offset $`10^{4}/N`$ vanishes as
$`N`$ grows, so for long texts
$`K\approx 10^{4}\cdot 2^{-H_{2}(\hat{p})}`$.

#### Measurement.

For a token string $`x_{1:N}`$ we compute
$`H_{2}(x_{1:N})=H_{2}(\hat{p})`$ from its observed counts, so no model
probabilities or logits are needed. Higher orders emphasize common
types, whereas $`H_{0}`$ only counts observed types. We use “evenness”
loosely for this sensitivity to concentration; $`H_{2}`$ also depends on
richness and is not normalized by the size of the support.

Order 2 has Simpson’s unbiased pair-count estimator ([Simpson,
1949](#bib.bib20)). Under i.i.d. sampling,
$`\widehat{C}_{\mathrm{u}}=\sum_{v}f_{v}(f_{v}-1)/[N(N-1)]`$ is unbiased
for the collision probability, and
$`K=10^{4}(N-1)\widehat{C}_{\mathrm{u}}/N`$. This does not make
$`-\log_{2}\widehat{C}_{\mathrm{u}}`$ an unbiased estimate of entropy.
Integer orders above two have analogous falling-factorial estimators,
fixed-sample counterparts of those analyzed under Poisson sampling by
[Acharya et al. (2017)](#bib.bib54)
(Appendix [D.2](#A4.SS2 "D.2 Unbiased estimators for integer-order power sums ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

## 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy

For standard temperature and nucleus controls ([Holtzman et al.,
2020](#bib.bib6)), the following facts are known. Majorization orders
tempered distributions ([Marshall et al., 2011](#bib.bib71)), implying
temperature monotonicity of positive-order Rényi entropies ([Mattei and
Loureiro, 2026](#bib.bib72)). [Nadeem et al. (2020)](#bib.bib73) give
the Shannon entropy-reduction counterparts for top-$`k`$, nucleus and
low-temperature sampling; [Skobelev et al. (2026, App. J)](#bib.bib70)
concurrently state order-2 temperature monotonicity and a support-size
cap. We restate the order-2 facts, with proofs in
Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"),
for the document-level analysis in
§[5](#S5 "5 From softmax to document: the 𝑉-bridge ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training").

###### Proposition 1 (Temperature monotonicity).

For a softmax $`p_{i}(T)\propto e^{z_{i}/T}`$ over finite, non-constant
logits, $`H_{2}(p_{T})`$ is strictly increasing in $`T`$ on
$`(0,\infty)`$, with $`H_{2}\to\log_{2}m`$ as $`T\to 0^{+}`$, where
$`m`$ is the number of tied maxima (so the limit is $`0`$ if the maximum
is unique), and $`H_{2}\to\log_{2}|\mathcal{V}|`$ as $`T\to\infty`$.

With $`\beta=1/T`$, $`I\sim p_{T}`$ and $`\mu=\mathbb{E}[z_{I}]`$,
$`\frac{d}{d\beta}\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}=2\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}(z_{i}-\mu)=2\operatorname{Cov}(p_{I},z_{I})>0`$,
because $`p_{i}`$ increases with $`z_{i}`$. Raising the temperature
therefore lowers the next-token collision probability.

###### Proposition 2 (Truncation strictly lowers $`H_{2}`$).

Let $`S`$ be an upper set, meaning
$`\min_{i\in S}p_{i}\geq\max_{j\notin S}p_{j}`$ (top-$`k`$ and top-$`p`$
selection both produce one), with retained mass
$`a=\sum_{i\in S}p_{i}\in(0,1)`$, and let $`q_{i}=p_{i}/a`$ on $`S`$.
Then $`H_{2}(q)<H_{2}(p)`$.

Conditioning on an upper set gives
$`\mathbb{E}[p_{I}\mid I\in S]\geq\mathbb{E}[p_{I}]`$
(Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Upper-set conditioning). ‣ D.5 Proposition and Lemma ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
and renormalizing then yields
$`C(q)=a^{-1}\mathbb{E}[p_{I}\mid I\in S]>C(p)`$. Keeping the top 20 of
100 equally likely tokens already costs $`\log_{2}5\approx 2.3`$ bits.

###### Proposition 3 (Fixed-support cap).

If sampling is restricted to a set $`S`$ with $`|S|=k`$, then for any
reshaping of the surviving mass (for example, any temperature applied
within $`S`$), $`H_{2}\leq\log_{2}k`$, with equality if and only if the
distribution is uniform on $`S`$.

The cap applies to a *fixed* retained set. Raising the temperature
before top-$`p`$ selection can enlarge that set, and the retained set
differs from context to context, so the cap by itself does not bound
document-level entropy independently of temperature.

## 5 From softmax to document: the $`V`$-bridge

Let $`p_{c_{t}}`$ be the conditional token distributions at $`N`$
contexts and $`\bar{p}=N^{-1}\sum_{t}p_{c_{t}}`$ their mean. We first
relate the collision entropy of this mean to the variation across
contexts, and then connect it to sampled frequencies.

###### Lemma 1 (Variance decomposition).

With mean per-token collision
$`\bar{C}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t}\sum_{v}p_{c_{t}}(v)^{2}`$ and
across-context dispersion
$`V=\sum_{v}\operatorname{Var}_{t}[p_{c_{t}}(v)]\geq 0`$, where
$`\operatorname{Var}_{t}`$ averages over the $`N`$ contexts,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C(\bar{p})=\bar{C}-V,\qquad H_{2}(\bar{p})=-\log_{2}(\bar{C}-V).
``` |  | (2) |

This is the additive Gini–Simpson partition ([Rao, 1982](#bib.bib67);
[Lande, 1996](#bib.bib68)) applied to conditional token distributions:
it separates within-context concentration from between-context
variation. Deterministic conditionals have zero per-token entropy, yet
their mean can spread its mass over many different argmaxes. It also
explains why the next-token results of
§[4](#S4 "4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
do not fix the direction of document-level change: a change of
temperature changes both $`\bar{C}`$ and $`V`$, and $`H_{2}(\bar{p})`$
rises exactly when $`\bar{C}-V`$ falls.

For a token process of fixed length $`N`$, let $`p_{c_{t}}`$ be the
*sampling law* given the history and $`\hat{p}_{N}`$ the empirical
frequencies. Each one-hot sampled token minus its sampling law is a
martingale difference, so
$`\mathbb{E}\|\hat{p}_{N}-\bar{p}_{N}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1/N`$
(Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
The bound compares empirical frequencies with the average sampling law
along the same random path; fixed-population convergence and
autoregressive entropy bias require separate arguments.

#### Fixed-context measurement.

On softmax probabilities of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct at 200 fixed 48-token
contexts from four Gutenberg books
(Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Figure values and experimental detail ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
$`T=0.1`$ gives $`\bar{C}=0.90`$ and $`V=0.88`$, so
$`H_{2}(\bar{p})=5.65`$ bits, far above the value
$`-\log_{2}\bar{C}=0.14`$ bits implied by the mean per-context
collision; computing $`V`$ directly reproduces
([2](#S5.E2 "In Lemma 1 (Variance decomposition). ‣ 5 From softmax to document: the 𝑉-bridge ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"))
to within $`1.1\times 10^{-8}`$ at every temperature.

## 6 Experiments

Word-level Rényi values apply NLTK word_tokenize ([Bird et al.,
2009](#bib.bib66)), version 3.9.1, to the original text, lowercase the
resulting tokens, and drop tokens that contain no letter or number;
punctuation attached to a word stays with it. The same English-trained
tokenizer is applied to German, Spanish and Hindi. Chinese uses Han
characters (U+4E00–U+9FFF). Next-token softmax values use each model’s
byte-pair encoding (BPE) vocabulary. We compare changes only within one
representation.

### 6.1 Setup

#### Protocol.

Each generation samples a training corpus from the current model and
fine-tunes it on that corpus. We vary training temperature $`T`$ and
top-$`p`$; measurements use one fixed *measurement sampler* ($`T=1`$,
top-$`p=0.95`$, top-$`k`$ disabled; the “probe” in the code), holding
the reader constant across trained models. We call top-$`p=0.80`$ at
$`T=1`$ the *truncated* arm and top-$`p=1`$ at $`T=1`$ the *untruncated*
arm. Random numbers are shared across arms: each draw is keyed by seed,
generation and phase, and all arms use the same pool of real-text
prompts. The LoRA ([Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib16)) arms of a replicate
start from one saved initial adapter, verified by a SHA-256 fingerprint
of its trainable tensors. PEFT’s zero-initialized $`B`$ matrices
([Mangrulkar et al., 2022](#bib.bib65)) yield identical initial outputs
even with different random $`A`$ matrices.

#### Budgets and seeds.

Richness depends strongly on the length of a text, so every comparison
states its budget. A *common-length* comparison truncates the
generation-0 and generation-9 texts of each seed to their shared length,
and a *fixed-budget* comparison uses the first 13,000 words of every
text. Most studies use three seeds; the primary English grid and its
refinements, the OLMo and Hindi grids, the Pythia study and the
checkpoint-branch intervention use five (Pythia’s first stage was later
extended to eight, and the independent-rerun comparison in
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
used ten). Held-out cross-perplexity is the exponential of mean
next-token negative log likelihood on real text disjoint from training.

#### Boundaries and sampling defaults.

Before training samples are concatenated, the harness removes the
padding-token ID. For GPT-2 this also removes the end-of-sequence (EOS)
token. OLMo-2 retains its distinct EOS, and Qwen2.5-Instruct retains its
primary EOS but removes its second stop ID, which equals padding. The
German and Hindi checkpoints declare an ordinary subword as their EOS,
so their continuations end only when that subword happens to be sampled
(Appendix [E.1](#A5.SS1 "E.1 Changing the representation ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Every sampling call in the training harness passes the temperature,
top-$`p`$ and top-$`k=0`$ explicitly and otherwise inherits the
checkpoint’s generation configuration. For Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct that
configuration sets a repetition penalty of 1.1, which therefore applied
to every Qwen-Instruct training corpus and measurement. No other sampled
checkpoint sets one;
§[6.6](#S6.SS6 "6.6 An exception traced to a sampler setting ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
tests its effect with a matched rerun.

#### Models and data.

The primary model under full fine-tuning (full-FT) is GPT-2 with 124M
parameters (listed as 117M in [Radford et al. 2019](#bib.bib14)).
Robustness settings use German ([dbmdz, 2023](#bib.bib62)) and Spanish
([flax-community, 2024](#bib.bib64)) GPT-2 checkpoints, gpt2-hindi
([surajp, 2023](#bib.bib63)), Wenzhong-GPT2-110M ([Zhang et al.,
2022](#bib.bib61)), Qwen2.5-0.5B base and Instruct ([Qwen Team et al.,
2025](#bib.bib15)), and SmolLM2-360M base and Instruct ([Ben Allal et
al., 2025](#bib.bib60)). Instruction-tuned checkpoints receive the same
raw literary prefixes, without chat templates. OLMo-2-1B ([Walsh et al.,
2025](#bib.bib29)), whose released checkpoint OLMo-2-0425-1B has 1.48B
parameters, extends the temperature grid in scale, and
Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct supplies next-token distributions on fixed contexts.
English training text is five books from NLTK’s Gutenberg selection
(Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Figure values and experimental detail ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
with Austen’s *Persuasion* held out, and
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Additional held-out works ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
evaluates three further held-out works. German, Spanish and Chinese use
Project Gutenberg literature and Hindi uses Wikisource. The genres are
not uniform: the Spanish training text includes travel essays and the
Chinese held-out text is a travelogue
(Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Figure values and experimental detail ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

### 6.2 Ordered zero crossings across training temperatures

#### Design.

We sweep the training sampler’s temperature and top-$`p`$ for English
GPT-2 under full fine-tuning. A coarse grid of 15 cells
(Table [1](#S6.T1 "Table 1 ‣ A fixed budget. ‣ 6.2 Ordered zero crossings across training temperatures ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"))
is refined near $`T=1`$ with five further temperatures at top-$`p=1`$
($`0.85`$, $`0.95`$, $`0.98`$, $`1.02`$ and $`1.05`$). Every cell has
five seeds and nine generations, 900 trained generation-steps in all;
corpus sizes and optimizer settings are in
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Figure values and experimental detail ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training").
The count analyses use a separate eight-batch dump from the measurement
sampler, while the logged $`H_{2}`$ series uses two batches.
Continuations are concatenated without a separator before counting;
inserting separators changes no conclusion
(Appendix [E.1](#A5.SS1 "E.1 Changing the representation ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
A grid with generation batch 32 serves as a protocol sensitivity check
(Appendix [E.2](#A5.SS2 "E.2 Densifying the crossing region ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

For each order $`\alpha`$ we define $`T^{\ast}(H_{\alpha})`$ as the
first temperature at which the seed-mean change from generation 0 to
generation 9 crosses zero from below, using linear interpolation between
sampled cells. Crossings need not be unique: at top-$`p=0.80`$, richness
turns upward near $`T=1.240`$ and back down near $`1.340`$.

#### Refined crossings.

On the top-$`p=1`$ axis every order falls at $`T=0.70`$ and rises at
$`T=1.25`$, but each turns at a different temperature:
$`T^{\ast}(H_{\infty})=0.927`$, $`T^{\ast}(H_{2})=0.962`$,
$`T^{\ast}(H_{1})=0.997`$ and $`T^{\ast}(H_{0})=1.035`$. At four
measured temperatures ($`0.95`$, $`0.98`$, $`1.00`$ and $`1.02`$)
richness falls while min-entropy rises; the interval between the outer
crossings is interpolated from these cells. We call
$`T^{\ast}(H_{\infty})<T^{\ast}(H_{0})`$ the *outer ordering* and the
complete sequence
$`T^{\ast}(H_{\infty})<T^{\ast}(H_{2})<T^{\ast}(H_{1})<T^{\ast}(H_{0})`$
the *full ordering*. Resampling the five seeds jointly across all cells
(4,000 bootstrap draws) gives 95% intervals of $`[0.917,0.950]`$,
$`[0.959,0.963]`$, $`[0.993,1.001]`$ and $`[1.030,1.039]`$, and every
draw keeps the full ordering. These intervals hold the grid fixed and so
leave out interpolation error, which is not negligible: refining the
grid moved the $`H_{2}`$ root from $`0.949`$ to $`0.961`$ and then to
$`0.962`$, and the first move fell outside the coarse-grid interval of
$`[0.941,0.956]`$. The third decimal therefore describes the interpolant
rather than the resolution of the experiment. On the coarse grid,
individual seeds follow the same ordering wherever all four roots are
bracketed: in 5 of 5, 4 of 4 and 5 of 5 seeds at top-$`p=1.0`$, $`0.8`$
and $`0.6`$. The two top-$`p=0.4`$ cells bracket no crossing. Across 18
sampled orders, all 4,000 common-length resamples preserve decreasing
crossing temperature with increasing order. Without interpolation, every
measured temperature has at most one change from negative to positive
along those orders, in each seed and in the mean
(Appendix [E.5](#A5.SS5 "E.5 Post-hoc checks of order, head concentration and measurement scale ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
This single-crossing statement concerns only the sampled orders; where
signs differ, neither generation uniformly dominates the other on the
Rényi/Hill profile ([Patil and Taillie, 1982](#bib.bib74); [Tóthmérész,
1995](#bib.bib23)). These analyses were not prespecified.

#### A fixed budget.

With common-length budgets, training at $`T=1`$ gives
$`\Delta H_{0}=-0.81`$ bits (43% fewer types) and $`\Delta H_{2}=+0.94`$
bits. Every grid corpus has more than 13,000 words, so the analysis can
be repeated at that fixed budget in every cell. At $`T=1`$ it gives
$`\Delta H_{0}=-0.400`$ bits (24% fewer types) and
$`\Delta H_{2}=+0.907`$ bits, and the roots become $`0.936`$, $`0.963`$,
$`0.990`$ and $`1.022`$. The same four cells show falling richness and
rising min-entropy, and all 4,000 resamples keep the ordering. The
budget changes the size of the richness loss and the width of the
interval. At tested prefixes of at most 1,000 words, however,
generation 9 has more types at $`T=1`$ in every seed; the split appears
in four of five seeds at 5,000 words and all five at tested budgets from
8,000 upward.

#### Word frequencies behind the split.

At common length and $`T=1`$, *the* falls from 4.9% to 1.8% of words.
The ten commonest generation-0 types, all function words, account for
104% of the collision-probability decrease; the rest offset it slightly.
Removing the 50 commonest types makes all four mean entropy changes
negative
(Appendix [E.5](#A5.SS5 "E.5 Post-hoc checks of order, head concentration and measurement scale ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
This rise describes reduced function-word concentration and could
accompany grammatical degradation, which unigram counts do not test;
reference likelihood worsens here.

#### Apparent recovery while quality worsens.

At top-$`p=0.60`$, raising the training temperature from $`1.00`$ to
$`1.18`$ moves $`\Delta H_{2}`$ toward zero, from $`-1.49`$ to $`-1.26`$
bits, while cross-perplexity worsens from $`11.6`$ to $`37.3`$ times its
generation-0 value; both changes occur in all five seeds. Richness stays
near $`-4.4`$ bits. A monitor watching $`H_{2}`$ alone would read this
as partial recovery. At top-$`p=0.80`$, raising the temperature from
$`1.00`$ to $`1.09`$ also moves $`\Delta H_{2}`$ toward zero, but there
cross-perplexity improves in four of five seeds.

#### Scope of the theory.

Tempering changes common and rare types at different rates
(Propositions [4](#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4 (Tempering acts by dilation). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
and [5](#Thmproposition5 "Proposition 5 (Temperature sensitivity). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
and i.i.d. resampling biases collision probability and observed support
in known ways
(Proposition [6](#Thmproposition6 "Proposition 6 (Collision bias and expected occupancy). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Higher-order plug-in entropy bias can have either sign. Predicting the
crossing order through nine neural refits under a fixed reader requires
more than these one-step population and sampling results. A categorical
simulation alternating tempering, 13,000 draws and refitting with
uniform smoothing of weight $`0.02`$, starting from our measured unigram
law, reproduces the outer ordering; the $`H_{\infty}`$ and $`H_{2}`$
roots remain in the same grid bracket, and the neural roots remain
empirical
(Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

### 6.3 Distinct-$`n`$, repetition and self-BLEU also cross

We rescore the 15 coarse cells with distinct-$`n`$, rep-4 and
self-BLEU-4, comparing generation 9 with generation 0 at each pair’s
common-length budget over five seeds

of $`n`$-gram positions, and rep-4 is one minus distinct-4. Self-BLEU-4
samples up to 40 newline-delimited segments of at least eight tokens as

top-$`p=1.0`$, $`0.8`$ and $`0.6`$. Self-BLEU crosses at $`1.0`$ and

### 6.6 An exception traced to a sampler setting

In Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct under LoRA, the truncated arm loses 59% of its
types ($`H_{0}`$ falls by $`1.30`$ bits) while $`H_{2}`$ rises by
$`0.47`$ bits and $`H_{\infty}`$ by $`1.62`$, and cross-perplexity is 22
times worse
(Tables [6](#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ Appendix A Supporting experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
and [7](#A1.T7 "Table 7 ‣ Appendix A Supporting experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
The text has fewer types but a less concentrated frequency distribution,
so rising collision entropy coexists here with both lost richness and
worse reference likelihood. SmolLM2-360M-Instruct, from a different
family, contracts normally at every order. On the Qwen checkpoint,
sampled with the repetition penalty discussed below, the effect holds
across truncation levels: at the fixed 13,000-word budget $`H_{2}`$
rises in every seed at every tested top-$`p`$ from $`0.60`$ to $`1.00`$,
though only by $`0.07`$ bits at $`0.60`$. Leakage of other scripts does
not explain it: non-ASCII words, mostly Chinese and Cyrillic, make up
$`0.6\%`$ of the first 13,000 words at generation 0 and $`0.5\%`$ on the
truncated arm at generation 9.

Removing the inherited repetition penalty eliminates this exception. The
checkpoint sets it to 1.1, inherited in every phase
(§[6.1](#S6.SS1 "6.1 Setup ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
down-weighting tokens already in the context. We reran both arms and the
checkpoint’s three other training samplers at penalty 1.0, matching
initial adapters, random streams, corpus and budgets
(Table [7](#A1.T7 "Table 7 ‣ Appendix A Supporting experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Without the penalty, generation-0 collision entropy is $`6.57`$ bits,
close to the base model’s, and the truncated arm contracts like every
other setting: at 13,000 words it loses 86% of its types, $`H_{0}`$,
$`H_{1}`$ and $`H_{2}`$ fall by $`2.84`$, $`2.67`$ and $`1.18`$ bits
($`H_{2}`$ in all three seeds), and $`H_{\infty}`$ falls by $`0.19`$ on
average. The untruncated arm shows the weaker split of the other
untruncated arms
(§[6.5](#S6.SS5 "6.5 Separation by the direction of change ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
losing 26% of its types while $`H_{2}`$ holds ($`+0.02`$) and
$`H_{\infty}`$ rises ($`+0.43`$). The top-$`p=0.60`$ and $`0.95`$ arms
now contract as well, with $`H_{2}`$ falling by $`1.53`$ and $`0.79`$
bits in every seed, while training at $`T=1.5`$ still raises it. The
other tables and detection pool retain the labeled penalized runs: they
show that another sampler setting can produce the same
richness–concentration disagreement.

### 6.7 What the profile can and cannot diagnose

We test profile movement, discrimination and threshold transfer. A run
is labeled degraded when held-out cross-perplexity reaches twice its
generation-0 value; this measures fit to one reference text.
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
gives protocols, controls and an intervention study.

#### Movement and sign.

Let $`M_{g}=\max(|\Delta H_{0}|,|\Delta H_{\infty}|)`$ at generation
$`g`$, and let $`D_{g}`$ indicate that the two changes have opposite
signs and both exceed $`0.20`$ bits in magnitude, a floor chosen to
reduce sensitivity to sampling noise. The development pool has 95
trajectories above the cutoff, the 75 coarse-grid runs and 20
Pythia-160m runs from the first stage of the frozen-detector study
(Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
together with six no-training controls and three runs adapted on human
text. The trajectory score $`\max_{g}M_{g}`$ reaches AUROC $`0.994`$,
but six of its nine negatives never trained, so this mainly shows that
it detects movement. The three alarm rules behave differently
(Table [13](#A2.T13 "Table 13 ‣ Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"),
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")):
magnitude alone fires on 92 of 95 above-cutoff runs, and adding the sign
clause catches all 95. Without its floor the clause also fires on every
unchanged model, and even with the floor it flags all three human-data
adaptations, whose profiles change by $`\Delta H_{0}=-0.636`$ and
$`\Delta H_{\infty}=+0.433`$ bits at 13,000 words while cross-perplexity
improves to $`0.60`$ times its starting value. These controls use
top-$`k=50`$; the alarm also flags all five human adaptations under the
primary protocol
(Appendix [G.2](#A7.SS2 "G.2 Matched controls under the primary measurement protocol ‣ Appendix G Fresh-seed studies and family-held-out analysis ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

#### Cross-setting classification.

On 660 corpora from 16 settings, we choose a feature pair by nested
leave-one-setting-out validation and score it on the held-out setting.
The mean AUROC over the 14 folds that contain both classes is $`0.897`$.
Selection picks $`(H_{2},\text{distinct-}2)`$ in 11 folds,
$`(H_{0},H_{2})`$ in two and $`(H_{0},H_{1})`$ in one. Evaluated as a
fixed pair in every fold, $`(H_{2},\text{distinct-}2)`$ has the highest
mean of the 15 pairs, $`0.963`$, against $`0.961`$ for $`(H_{0},H_{2})`$
and $`0.917`$ for $`(H_{0},H_{1})`$; differences this small do not
single out a best pair. A one-feature post-hoc baseline exceeds both
leading fixed pairs: unsigned richness change $`|\Delta H_{0}|`$ scores
$`0.970`$, while generation index alone, which uses no text, scores
$`0.908`$. The nested and fixed-pair numbers evaluate different
procedures, so their gap is not an estimate of selection bias. The worst
fold is English $`T=2`$, where nested selection chooses
$`(H_{0},H_{1})`$ and scores $`0.194`$.
Appendices [E.5](#A5.SS5 "E.5 Post-hoc checks of order, head concentration and measurement scale ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
and [G.3](#A7.SS3 "G.3 Holding out complete model families ‣ Appendix G Fresh-seed studies and family-held-out analysis ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
give the in-sample fit and family holdouts. Four settings are
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct runs sampled with the checkpoint’s repetition
penalty.

#### Pythia: one training regime, three decoders.

Pythia-160m ([Biderman et al., 2023](#bib.bib33)) is trained with five
seeds at each of three decoders under a single regime: pure recursion,
learning rate $`10^{-5}`$ and one epoch. The $`2\times`$ cutoff on
*Persuasion* splits the untruncated $`T=1`$ arm, with three seeds below
it ($`1.84`$, $`1.93`$ and $`1.94`$) and two above ($`2.07`$ and
$`2.09`$), and all ten runs at top-$`p=0.8`$ or $`T=2`$ (top-$`p=0.95`$)
exceed it. The exploratory endpoint score
$`\max(|\Delta H_{0}|,|\Delta H_{2}|)`$ has AUROC $`0.944`$, the same as
richness alone. That figure rests on 30 of 30 concordant comparisons
across decoders but only 4 of 6 within the $`T=1`$ arm (AUROC
$`0.667`$), so the pooled ranking mostly separates decoders rather than
quality within a decoder. One-sided scores stay near chance
($`0.472`$–$`0.528`$).
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
describes a preliminary frozen-detector test whose healthy runs used a
different training recipe.

#### Changing the reference work.

With models, scores and cutoff held fixed, three more books confirm that
the GPT-2 grid loses likelihood: all 100 endpoints exceed the
$`2\times`$ cutoff on every work, and at $`T=1`$ the mean ratios are
$`3.43`$–$`4.48`$, against $`3.83`$ on *Persuasion*. Pythia’s labels
move more. *Sense and Sensibility* places 11 of 15 runs above the
cutoff, while *Moby Dick*, *The Adventures of Buster Bear* and the
three-book aggregate place all 15 above it, which leaves no negative
class and makes AUROC undefined
(Table [19](#A3.T19 "Table 19 ‣ Appendix C Additional held-out works ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Even within English literature, the choice of reference text changes the
labels.

## 7 Conclusion

On the English GPT-2 training-temperature grid, min-entropy changes sign
first and richness last. Between these crossings, models have fewer word
types and a less dominant most-common word. At sufficient budgets,
$`T=1`$ lies inside this interval under all three readers. The full
ordering holds at interpolated roots in every bracketed setting (Hindi
has no measured split cell), but in only 9 of 13 distinct measurements
under eight representations; 12 keep the outer ordering. The interval
moves with the setting, and phrase-level spectra can reverse the order.

Report richness and higher orders at a stated budget and representation,
with independent quality measures. The same split occurs during
human-text fine-tuning while likelihood improves, so the signs of
entropy changes do not identify degradation or its cause. Validate
thresholds on healthy trajectories under the intended decoder and
reference text.

## Limitations

#### Theory and resolution.

The theory characterizes decoding and sampling effects; the neural
crossing order remains empirical. Most comparisons use three to five
seeds; coarse grids and linear interpolation limit precision, and
bootstrap intervals exclude interpolation error. The Hindi min-entropy
root is fragile, and the vocabulary-composition comparison of
Appendix [A.5](#A1.SS5 "A.5 Tokenizer composition and script drift ‣ Appendix A Supporting experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
is a heuristic based on three settings.

#### Quality and transfer.

Quality is measured only through reference likelihood and automatic
metrics, without human judgments. The other languages each use a single
held-out work, and we do not know which works the models saw in
pretraining. Setting-level folds share model families, Pythia’s negative
class depends on the reference, and the intervention results rest on
five seeds, so neither threshold transfer nor intervention timing is
validated.

#### Protocol and scale.

The recursive models have at most $`1.48`$B parameters and train on
literary corpora of uneven genre. Because most settings train for two
epochs per generation, the number of updates changes with corpus length
and with added real data. EOS handling differs by tokenizer, and the
GPT-2 EOS variant also halves the generation batch
(Appendix [E.1](#A5.SS1 "E.1 Changing the representation ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
The Qwen-Instruct runs in the main tables inherited a repetition
penalty, which only the reruns of
§[6.6](#S6.SS6 "6.6 An exception traced to a sampler setting ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
remove. Training on Apple MPS is not bitwise deterministic.

#### Measurement and data.

Word, character and model-token entropies are not comparable, a Han-only
unit can lose almost all coverage, and unigram measures miss
phrase-level loops. The released count multisets recompute Rényi and
$`n`$-gram metrics but not the 12,000-word detection features (released
as rows), self-BLEU, MAUVE or perplexity. Released endpoint, reader and
control texts support reanalysis. The counts cover every setting except
two whose text was not kept (the four-arm study and the temperature
sweep of
Appendix [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 Decoder and training temperature sweeps ‣ Appendix A Supporting experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
Several secondary runs lack exact revision or resume provenance
(Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Figure values and experimental detail ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"));
the endpoint MAUVE featurizer is also not fully recorded
(Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Detection experiments and interventions ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

## Broader Impact Statement

Treating a fall in diversity as evidence of poor quality can penalize
legitimate specialization or underrepresented varieties, so these
diagnostics should be used with explicit units, coverage checks and an
independent quality evaluation before they filter data or models.

### A.1 Predictive confidence and reference likelihood

We measure predictive perplexity as $`2^{\overline{H_{1}(q_{t})}}`$, the
exponentiated mean entropy of the truncated next-token law $`q_{t}`$
over held-out human contexts. It uses no generated samples and differs
both from model-scored sample perplexity and from word-level unigram
entropy. Cross-perplexity instead scores the observed reference tokens
under the untruncated model. On the top-$`p=0.80`$ arm, predictive
perplexity under the measurement sampler falls from $`26.3`$ to $`2.4`$
while cross-perplexity rises from $`50.3`$ to $`2{,}189`$: more
concentrated predictions accompany worse reference likelihood
(Figure [3](#A1.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ A.1 Predictive confidence and reference likelihood ‣ Appendix A Supporting experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).

Figure 3: English GPT-2, generation 0 to 9. On the truncated arms,
predictive perplexity $`2^{\overline{H_{1}(q_{t})}}`$ falls while
cross-perplexity on held-out real text rises. The first two panels
average entropy over human contexts, using the arm’s own sampler or the
measurement sampler; they do not score generated sequences. The

### A.2 Decoder and training temperature sweeps

Propositions [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Temperature monotonicity). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
and [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Truncation strictly lowers 𝐻_2). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
give opposing next-token effects, and
Proposition [3](#Thmproposition3 "Proposition 3 (Fixed-support cap). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
bounds entropy only while the retained set stays fixed, so it gives no
document-level bound when contexts have different nuclei. Holding the

#### Chinese script loss.

High-temperature Chinese training nearly eliminates Han script. Han
characters fall from 83.8% of all emitted characters (including
whitespace) at generation 0 to 82.1% at generation 1, then 4.8% at
generation 2, 0.7% at generation 3 and 0.05% by generation 9, an average
of 145 Han characters per sampled corpus spread over 36–125 distinct
types. The model emits fragmented Latin-script text instead
(“*Authorization490 didn entityraceiddler time Exploset Change
conclusionThus*”).

On the eight-batch dumps these Han characters give $`H_{2}=4.78`$ bits
($`4.47`$ on the logged two-batch sample). With at most 125 observed
types the value cannot exceed $`\log_{2}125\approx 7`$ bits and is not
comparable to a 13,000-character estimate, so we exclude this cell from
the entropy comparison and report script coverage instead: the
restricted unit discards almost all of the output while still returning
a finite score. Tokenizer-level cross-perplexity registers the damage
($`18.3\times`$).

### A.5 Tokenizer composition and script drift

simple diagnostic: decode each vocabulary entry and measure its
character composition. It is only a heuristic for finite-temperature
recursive training, which need not converge to a uniform law, and
byte-level tokenizers need care because decoding a token on its own can
differ from decoding it within a sequence.

#### Healthy controls and an independent quality measure.

Six no-training trajectories keep cross-perplexity at $`1.00\times`$. At
a fixed 13,000 words every change in $`H_{0}`$, $`H_{1}`$ and $`H_{2}`$
stays within $`0.15`$ bits, and the largest absolute min-entropy change
is $`0.240`$ bits. The unfloored sign rule fires on all six and the
floored rule on none. Three trajectories adapted on human text instead
improve cross-perplexity to $`0.60\times`$, with mean
$`\Delta H_{0}=-0.636`$ and $`\Delta H_{\infty}=+0.433`$ at the same
budget, and the floored rule fires on all three. These controls use
top-$`k=50`$, top-$`p=0.95`$ and $`T=1`$, prompts from the held-out
book, and smaller batches than the main grid, and all six no-training
chains share this one sampler. The seed fixes the prompts; the global
token-sampling seed was not recorded. Human adaptation trains on
sequential 128-token chunks of the second half of *Persuasion*, whose
first half is the evaluation reference, without gradient clipping and
with a different optimizer budget. Under this protocol, the floored
alarm can fire while reference likelihood improves; calibrating the main
grid requires matched prompts, samplers and optimizer budgets. An
exploratory MAUVE calculation falls from $`0.707`$ to $`0.079`$ at
$`T=1`$, top-$`p=1`$ (five seeds), and gives $`0.022`$ at $`T=0.70`$
(two seeds). The feature script mean-pools final-layer states and
defaults to GPT-2, 200 passages and 128 tokens per passage. Retained
features have width 768, inconsistent with GPT-2-large, but the
invocation, passage length limit and exact model revision were not
recorded. We therefore cannot identify the featurizer with certainty or
claim exact reproducibility of these MAUVE values. Two high-temperature
endpoints have insufficient passage coverage.

#### Frozen external detector.

A serialized detector and threshold were frozen before the Pythia
evaluation and recorded by SHA-256. Obtaining a healthy stratum required
trying pure recursion, then 25–75% real-data mixing (all nine runs
degraded), then a lower learning rate as well; the six healthy runs mix

## Appendix D Proofs

Throughout, $`C(p)=\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}`$ and $`H_{2}=-\log_{2}C`$;
logarithms are base 2 unless written $`\ln`$.

### D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration

These are the statements behind
§[6.2](#S6.SS2 "6.2 Ordered zero crossings across training temperatures ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training").
Fix a distribution $`q`$ on a finite alphabet with $`q_{i}>0`$, and
write

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\psi(\beta)\;=\;\ln\sum_{i}q_{i}^{\beta},\qquad\text{so}\qquad\psi(1)=0,\qquad H_{\alpha}(q)=\frac{\psi(\alpha)}{(1-\alpha)\ln 2}\ \text{ bits}.
``` |  |

$`\psi`$ is convex, being a log-sum-exp of the linear functions
$`\beta\mapsto\beta\ln q_{i}`$. Let
$`q^{(\beta)}_{i}=q_{i}^{\beta}/\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\beta}`$ denote the
escort distribution at $`\beta`$, and recall the standard identities
$`\psi^{\prime}(\beta)=\mathbb{E}_{q^{(\beta)}}[\ln q]`$ and
$`\psi^{\prime\prime}(\beta)=\operatorname{Var}_{q^{(\beta)}}(\ln q)\geq 0`$.

###### Proposition 4 (Tempering acts by dilation).

For every finite $`\alpha\neq 1`$ and $`\beta>0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
H_{\alpha}\!\left(q^{(\beta)}\right)\;=\;\frac{\psi(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\,\psi(\beta)}{(1-\alpha)\ln 2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{1}\!\left(q^{(\beta)}\right)\;=\;[\psi(\beta)-\beta\,\psi^{\prime}(\beta)]/\ln 2.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

$`\sum_{i}\big(q_{i}^{\beta}/\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\beta}\big)^{\alpha}=\big(\sum_{i}q_{i}^{\alpha\beta}\big)\big/\big(\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\beta}\big)^{\alpha}`$.
Taking $`\ln`$ gives $`\psi(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\psi(\beta)`$, and
dividing by $`(1-\alpha)\ln 2`$ gives the first claim; $`\beta=1`$
recovers $`H_{\alpha}(q)`$ since $`\psi(1)=0`$. For the second, apply
L’Hôpital’s rule in $`\alpha`$ at $`\alpha=1`$: the numerator’s
derivative is $`\beta\psi^{\prime}(\alpha\beta)-\psi(\beta)`$ and the
denominator’s is $`-\ln 2`$. At $`\beta=1`$ this is
$`-\psi^{\prime}(1)/\ln 2=-\sum_{i}q_{i}\log_{2}q_{i}`$, the Shannon
entropy, as required. ∎

###### Proposition 5 (Temperature sensitivity).

Let
$`G(\alpha)=-\partial_{\beta}H_{\alpha}(q^{(\beta)})\big|_{\beta=1}`$,
the rate at which sharpening reduces $`H_{\alpha}`$. Then
$`G(\alpha)\geq 0`$ for all $`\alpha\geq 0`$, with

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{\ln 2}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\alpha)-\psi^{\prime}(1)}{\alpha-1}=\frac{\alpha}{\ln 2}\int_{0}^{1}\psi^{\prime\prime}(1+s(\alpha-1))\,ds,
``` |  |

with $`G(1)=\operatorname{Var}_{q}(\ln q)/\ln 2`$ by continuity, and
$`G(0)=0`$; as $`\alpha\to\infty`$,
$`G(\alpha)\to H_{1}(q)-H_{\infty}(q)\geq 0`$.

###### Proof.

Differentiating
Proposition [4](#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4 (Tempering acts by dilation). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
gives
$`\partial_{\beta}H_{\alpha}=\alpha[\psi^{\prime}(\alpha\beta)-\psi^{\prime}(\beta)]/((1-\alpha)\ln 2)`$.
The divided difference of $`\psi^{\prime}`$ equals the displayed
integral. Since
$`\psi^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{Var}_{q^{(\beta)}}(\ln q)\geq 0`$,
$`G\geq 0`$. At order zero, tempering preserves the set of positive
probabilities, so population $`H_{0}`$ is constant. Finally,
$`\psi^{\prime}(\alpha)\to\ln\max_{i}q_{i}`$ as $`\alpha\to\infty`$;
substituting in the divided difference gives the stated limit. ∎

*Recursive tempering.* For non-uniform $`q`$, $`\psi^{\prime\prime}>0`$,
so the derivative above is negative at every $`\beta>0`$ for finite
$`\alpha>0`$, $`\alpha\neq 1`$. The limiting orders satisfy
$`\partial_{\beta}H_{1}=-\beta\psi^{\prime\prime}(\beta)/\ln 2<0`$ and
$`\partial_{\beta}H_{\infty}=[\psi^{\prime}(\beta)-\ln\max_{i}q_{i}]/\ln 2<0`$.
Since $`(q^{(\beta)})^{(\beta^{\prime})}=q^{(\beta\beta^{\prime})}`$,
$`g`$ untruncated infinite-sample steps at fixed temperature $`T`$ give
$`q^{(T^{-g})}`$ for every integer $`g\geq 1`$. Relative to $`q`$, every
positive-order entropy rises for $`T>1`$, falls for $`0<T<1`$ and is
unchanged at $`T=1`$, while $`H_{0}`$ stays fixed.

###### Proposition 6 (Collision bias and expected occupancy).

For $`N\geq 2`$ i.i.d. draws from $`q`$, with counts $`n_{i}`$,
empirical distribution $`\hat{q}`$ and $`\widehat{C}=C(\hat{q})`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\hat{C}=C+\frac{1-C}{N},\qquad\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\sum_{i}n_{i}(n_{i}-1)}{N(N-1)}\right]=C,\qquad C=\sum_{i}q_{i}^{2}.
``` |  |

Consequently
$`H_{2}(q)-\mathbb{E}H_{2}(\hat{q})\leq\log_{2}(1+(1-C)/(NC))`$ by
Jensen’s inequality, which leaves the sign of the deficit undetermined.
For the observed support $`S_{N}`$,
$`\mathbb{E}S_{N}=\sum_{i}[1-(1-q_{i})^{N}]`$ ([Hurlbert,
1971](#bib.bib49), Eq. 14).

For a triangular sequence $`q^{(N)}`$ on $`K_{N}\to\infty`$ types (an
alphabet size, unrelated to Yule’s $`K`$), suppose a fraction
$`\phi\in(0,1]`$ of the types satisfy $`c_{1}/N\leq q_{i}\leq c_{2}/N`$
with $`0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty`$, and the remaining types (if any)
satisfy $`\min_{i\,\mathrm{heavy}}Nq_{i}/\ln K_{N}\to\infty`$. Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\phi K_{N}e^{-c_{2}}(1+o(1))\leq\mathbb{E}(K_{N}-S_{N})\leq\phi K_{N}e^{-c_{1}}(1+o(1)).
``` |  |

When $`c_{1}=c_{2}=c`$,
$`\log_{2}K_{N}-\log_{2}\mathbb{E}S_{N}\to-\log_{2}(1-\phi e^{-c})`$.
The expected number of missing types is $`\Theta(K_{N})`$, whereas this
logarithmic deficit is $`\Theta(1)`$.

###### Proof.

Binomial marginals give
$`\mathbb{E}n_{i}^{2}=Nq_{i}(1-q_{i})+N^{2}q_{i}^{2}`$ and
$`\mathbb{E}[n_{i}(n_{i}-1)]=N(N-1)q_{i}^{2}`$, which prove both
collision identities. Convexity of $`-\log_{2}`$ gives the bound, and
summing occupancy indicators gives $`\mathbb{E}S_{N}`$. For light types,
$`(1-q_{i})^{N}=e^{-Nq_{i}}(1+O(Nq_{i}^{2}))`$ uniformly, so their
probabilities of being missing lie between $`e^{-c_{2}}`$ and
$`e^{-c_{1}}`$ up to a factor $`1+o(1)`$. Each heavy type is absent with
probability at most $`e^{-Nq_{i}}=K_{N}^{-\omega(1)}`$, so the heavy
types contribute $`o(1)`$ in total. Summing proves the bound, and with
$`c_{1}=c_{2}=c`$, $`\mathbb{E}S_{N}=K_{N}(1-\phi e^{-c}+o(1))`$, which
proves the limit. ∎

The logarithmic deficit above differs from the plug-in deficit
$`\log_{2}K_{N}-\mathbb{E}\log_{2}S_{N}`$, which is at least as large by
Jensen’s inequality. Under the proposition’s assumptions the difference
tends to zero. Writing $`\mu_{N}=\mathbb{E}S_{N}=\Theta(K_{N})`$,
occupancy indicators have nonpositive pairwise covariance, so
$`\operatorname{Var}S_{N}\leq\mu_{N}`$. Chebyshev’s inequality gives
$`\Pr(|S_{N}-\mu_{N}|>\mu_{N}/2)=O(K_{N}^{-1})`$. On the complementary
event, $`|\ln(S_{N}/\mu_{N})|\leq 2|S_{N}/\mu_{N}-1|`$ has expectation
$`O(K_{N}^{-1/2})`$; on the remaining event,
$`1\leq S_{N},\mu_{N}\leq K_{N}`$ bounds its contribution by
$`O(\ln K_{N}/K_{N})`$. Thus the two richness deficits have the same
limit when $`c_{1}=c_{2}`$.
Appendix [E.3](#A5.SS3 "E.3 Factorial checks of the occupancy and collision formulas ‣ Appendix E Representation and estimator controls ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
computes both. The lower mass bound is essential: with $`K_{N}=N^{2}`$
and $`q_{i}=N^{-2}`$, $`\mathbb{E}S_{N}=\Theta(N)`$ and the logarithmic
deficit grows as $`(1+o(1))\log_{2}N`$. Population support cannot be
recovered exactly from a finite sample without further assumptions about
unseen types ([Good, 1953](#bib.bib47); [Chao, 1984](#bib.bib50)). When
$`\phi<1`$, the heavy-type condition forces $`K_{N}\ln K_{N}/N\to 0`$,
so the total mass of light types is $`O(K_{N}/N)\to 0`$; the proposition
does not describe a tail of fixed positive mass in that case.

The logarithm introduces bias even when the collision-probability
estimator is unbiased. For $`q=(1/2,1/2)`$ and $`N=2`$, the plug-in
entropy deficit is $`0.5`$ bits while its Jensen bound is
$`\log_{2}(1.5)=0.585`$, and the U-statistic can be zero, which makes
its logarithm infinite. On a measured English generation-0 law (the
seed-0 baseline of the main grid, $`H_{2}=6.96`$ bits) at
$`N=13{,}000`$, the bound is $`0.0137`$ bits, the delta-method deficit
is $`0.0131`$, and a 4,000-draw Monte Carlo estimate is
$`0.0128\pm 0.0006`$ bits (standard error). The logarithm of the
U-statistic has a residual bias estimated at $`+0.0008\pm 0.0006`$ bits
(standard error). For this non-degenerate law, $`N`$ exceeds the support
size, so the U-statistic is positive and nonconstant; strict Jensen
inequality makes its entropy bias strictly positive, despite Monte Carlo
uncertainty. The richness deficit on the same law is $`1.69`$ bits.

#### Limits of a first-order crossing explanation.

Suppose each generation lost a fixed amount $`L_{\alpha}`$ of
order-$`\alpha`$ entropy through finite sampling and gained
$`G(\alpha)(1-1/T)`$ through tempering, the first-order expansion of
Proposition [5](#Thmproposition5 "Proposition 5 (Temperature sensitivity). ‣ D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training").
The balance $`1-1/T^{\ast}=L_{\alpha}/G(\alpha)`$ then puts any crossing
above one whenever both loss and sensitivity are positive, so it cannot
produce the measured roots below one for $`H_{1}`$, whose i.i.d. plug-in
loss is always positive, or for $`H_{2}`$, whose loss is positive for
large $`N`$
(Appendix [D.8](#A4.SS8 "D.8 Plug-in bias, with its sign ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")).
A root below one requires a negative loss, which can occur at higher
orders: writing $`\hat{H}_{\alpha}=H_{\alpha}(\hat{q})`$ for the plug-in
estimate, exact binomial averaging for $`q=(0.9,0.1)`$ and $`N=100`$
gives $`\mathbb{E}\hat{H}_{2}-H_{2}=-0.0007285`$,
$`\mathbb{E}\hat{H}_{3}-H_{3}=+0.0008191`$ and
$`\mathbb{E}\hat{H}_{\infty}-H_{\infty}=+0.0008076`$ bits, so orders 3
and $`\infty`$ gain entropy from sampling alone. Such a gain could place
$`T^{\ast}(H_{\infty})`$ below one, but endpoint sensitivities do not
establish a monotone ratio of loss to gain for the intermediate orders.
The neural result is therefore empirical, and the categorical simulation
provides one example of the same outer ordering.

#### A categorical simulation.

We simulate a categorical model that alternates tempering, 13,000 draws
and refitting for nine generations. It pads the alphabet to 50,257
types, mixes each refit with a uniform smoothing term of weight
$`0.02`$, and measures with 13,000 further draws from a unigram analogue
of the neural grid’s top-$`p=0.95`$ measurement sampler. Across 16
replicates the roots are $`0.993`$, $`0.994`$, $`1.005`$ and $`1.129`$
for orders $`\infty`$, 2, 1 and 0. The simulation reproduces the outer
ordering, with richness turning well after the other orders, but the
$`H_{\infty}`$ and $`H_{2}`$ roots ($`0.9934`$ and $`0.9937`$) lie in
the same grid bracket, $`[0.95,1.00]`$, so their order is not resolved.
It does not predict the neural roots, and without smoothing its richness
does not recover in the tested range. That need not hold in general,
since a fixed nucleus sampler can expose previously excluded types even
though the fitted support cannot grow (see the two-type example below).
In detail, the generation-0 law is the unsmoothed unigram law of the
English generation-0 text used above; each generation tempers the
current smoothed law, draws 13,000 tokens, and refits by maximum
likelihood before mixing in the uniform term; and the measurement
sampler applies top-$`p=0.95`$ to the current smoothed law. The 14
temperatures run from $`0.70`$ to $`2.00`$ ($`0.70`$, $`0.80`$,
$`0.90`$, $`0.95`$, $`1.00`$, $`1.02`$, $`1.05`$, $`1.10`$, $`1.20`$,
$`1.30`$, $`1.45`$, $`1.60`$, $`1.80`$ and $`2.00`$), and replicate
$`i`$ uses the same random seed at every temperature.

Population support and the support seen by a fixed sampler must also be
distinguished. Take $`q=(0.9999,0.0001)`$, untruncated training at
$`T=2`$, $`N=13{,}000`$ and unsmoothed categorical refitting. A fixed
top-$`p=0.95`$ sampler initially keeps only the first type. Tempering
can raise the second type’s mass until it enters the nucleus, so
observed $`H_{0}`$ can increase without adding any type outside the
original population support. A nine-generation simulation does so in all
100 tested seeds. Thus $`G(0)=0`$ does not imply that richness under a
fixed sampler cannot recover under maximum-likelihood refitting.

### D.2 Unbiased estimators for integer-order power sums

For integers $`N\geq m\geq 2`$, write $`(a)_{m}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-m+1)`$.
Under multinomial sampling, an unbiased estimator of
$`P_{m}(q)=\sum_{i}q_{i}^{m}`$ is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{P}_{m,\mathrm{u}}=\frac{\sum_{i}(n_{i})_{m}}{(N)_{m}},\qquad\mathbb{E}\widehat{P}_{m,\mathrm{u}}=P_{m}(q).
``` |  |

Indeed, $`(n_{i})_{m}`$ counts ordered tuples of $`m`$ distinct sample
positions that all contain type $`i`$. Each tuple has probability
$`q_{i}^{m}`$, so $`\mathbb{E}(n_{i})_{m}=(N)_{m}q_{i}^{m}`$, and
summing over types proves the identity. It is the fixed-sample
counterpart of the bias-corrected estimator of [Acharya et al.
(2017)](#bib.bib54): for Poisson sample size
$`M\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(N)`$, their numerator $`\sum_{i}(n_{i})_{m}`$ is
divided by $`N^{m}`$, the $`m`$th power of the expected sample size. At
order 2 the fixed-sample estimator above is the pair-count estimator.
Applying the nonlinear entropy transform
$`\log_{2}\widehat{P}_{m,\mathrm{u}}/(1-m)`$ requires a separate bias
analysis, and a zero estimate gives infinite entropy.

### D.3 Order monotonicity $`H_{0}\geq H_{1}\geq H_{2}\geq H_{\infty}`$

Let $`P_{\alpha}=\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}`$ and
$`\pi_{i}=p_{i}^{\alpha}/P_{\alpha}`$ (the escort distribution). In
bits,
$`\frac{dH_{\alpha}}{d\alpha}=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}\ln 2}\big[\ln P_{\alpha}+(1-\alpha)\tfrac{P_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{P_{\alpha}}\big]`$
with $`P_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}\ln p_{i}`$.
Substituting $`\ln P_{\alpha}=\alpha\ln p_{i}-\ln\pi_{i}`$ reduces the
bracket to
$`\sum_{i}\pi_{i}\ln(p_{i}/\pi_{i})=-\ln(2)\,\operatorname{KL}(\pi\|p)\leq 0`$.
The continuous limits cover orders $`1`$ and $`\infty`$, and
zero-probability entries are omitted from the support. Hence
$`H_{\alpha}`$ is non-increasing in $`\alpha`$. $`\square`$

### D.4 Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Temperature monotonicity). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training") (temperature monotonicity)

With $`p_{i}=e^{\beta z_{i}}/Z`$, $`Z=\sum_{j}e^{\beta z_{j}}`$ and
$`\mu=\sum_{j}p_{j}z_{j}`$, the quotient rule gives
$`\frac{dp_{i}}{d\beta}=p_{i}(z_{i}-\mu)`$, so
$`\frac{d}{d\beta}\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}=2\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}(z_{i}-\mu)=2\operatorname{Cov}(p_{I},z_{I})`$,
where $`I\sim p`$. Taking an independent copy $`I^{\prime}\sim p`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
2\operatorname{Cov}(p_{I},z_{I})=\mathbb{E}\big[(p_{I}-p_{I^{\prime}})(z_{I}-z_{I^{\prime}})\big].
``` |  |

Since $`p_{i}`$ is strictly increasing in $`z_{i}`$ for $`\beta>0`$, the
two factors share a sign, so the product is nonnegative pointwise and
its expectation is nonnegative, and strictly positive unless $`z`$ is
constant. Thus $`\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}`$ strictly increases in $`\beta`$ and
strictly decreases in $`T`$, and $`H_{2}`$ strictly increases in $`T`$.
The boundary limits follow by factoring out $`e^{\beta z_{\max}}`$ (so
$`\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}\to 1/m`$ and $`H_{2}\to\log_{2}m`$ as
$`\beta\to\infty`$) and from $`p_{i}\to 1/|\mathcal{V}|`$ as
$`\beta\to 0`$, which requires finite logits
($`H_{2}\to\log_{2}|\mathcal{V}|`$). $`\square`$

### D.5 Proposition [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Truncation strictly lowers 𝐻_2). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training") and Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Upper-set conditioning). ‣ D.5 Proposition and Lemma ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")

###### Lemma 2 (Upper-set conditioning).

With $`X=p_{I}`$, $`I\sim p`$, and $`S`$ an upper set of mass $`0<a<1`$,
$`\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\in S]\geq\mathbb{E}[X]`$; equality holds precisely
when every retained $`p_{i}`$ and every strictly positive discarded
$`p_{j}`$ share a common value.

*Proof.* Let $`A=\sum_{i\in S}p_{i}^{2}`$, $`a=\sum_{i\in S}p_{i}`$, and
let $`B,b`$ be the corresponding sums over the complement. Then
$`\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\in S]=A/a`$, $`\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\notin S]=B/b`$
and $`\mathbb{E}[X]=A+B`$. Because $`S`$ is an upper set,
$`A/a\geq\min_{i\in S}p_{i}\geq\max_{j\notin S}p_{j}\geq B/b`$. By total
expectation,
$`\mathbb{E}[X]=a\,\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\in S]+b\,\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\notin S]`$,
and subtracting gives
$`\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\in S]-\mathbb{E}[X]=b\big(\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\in S]-\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\notin S]\big)\geq 0`$.
Equality forces equality throughout this chain. Because the weights
$`p_{i}/a`$ are positive on $`S`$ and the weights $`p_{j}/b`$ are
positive exactly on the strictly positive discarded entries,
$`A/a=\min_{i\in S}p_{i}`$ makes all retained $`p_{i}`$ equal,
$`\max_{j\notin S}p_{j}=B/b`$ makes all strictly positive discarded
$`p_{j}`$ equal, and the middle link makes the two values coincide; the
converse is immediate. $`\square`$

*Proposition [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Truncation strictly lowers 𝐻_2). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training").*
With $`q_{i}=p_{i}/a`$ on $`S`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{i\in S}q_{i}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{i\in S}p_{i}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{a}\mathbb{E}[X\mid I\in S]\geq\tfrac{1}{a}\mathbb{E}[X]>\mathbb{E}[X]=\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}.
``` |  |

Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Upper-set conditioning). ‣ D.5 Proposition and Lemma ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
gives the first, weak inequality, and the factor $`1/a>1`$ makes the
second strict. Applying $`-\log_{2}(\cdot)`$ reverses the inequality, so
$`H_{2}(q)<H_{2}(p)`$. Without the upper-set condition the result fails:
keeping the two smaller types of $`p=(0.9,0.05,0.05)`$ raises $`H_{2}`$
from $`0.295`$ to $`1`$ bit. $`\square`$

### D.6 Proposition [3](#Thmproposition3 "Proposition 3 (Fixed-support cap). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training") (support cap)

For $`q`$ supported on $`S`$ with $`|S|=k`$, the Cauchy–Schwarz
inequality gives
$`1=(\sum_{i\in S}q_{i})^{2}\leq k\sum_{i\in S}q_{i}^{2}`$, so
$`\sum_{i\in S}q_{i}^{2}\geq 1/k`$ and $`H_{2}(q)\leq\log_{2}k`$, with
equality if and only if $`q`$ is uniform on $`S`$. No reshaping of mass
within $`S`$, at any temperature, can exceed $`\log_{2}k`$. $`\square`$

### D.7 Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (Variance decomposition). ‣ 5 From softmax to document: the 𝑉-bridge ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training") (the $`V`$-bridge)

For a uniformly chosen position $`t`$,
$`\operatorname{Var}_{t}[p_{c_{t}}(v)]=\overline{p_{c_{t}}(v)^{2}}-\bar{p}(v)^{2}`$.
Summing over $`v`$ gives $`C(\bar{p})=\bar{C}-V`$ exactly, with
$`V\geq 0`$. $`\square`$

Equivalently, $`1-C(\bar{p})=(1-\bar{C})+V`$: pooled Gini–Simpson
diversity is the sum of its mean within-context value and the
between-context variance ([Rao, 1982](#bib.bib67); [Lande,
1996](#bib.bib68)). This additive partition differs from a decomposition
into independent diversity components in the sense of [Jost
(2007)](#bib.bib69).

For the fixed-length path statement, let $`X_{t}`$ be the one-hot
sampled token, $`\mathcal{F}_{t-1}`$ the history up to position $`t-1`$,
and $`p_{t}=\mathbb{E}[X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]`$ its sampling law.
The differences $`X_{t}-p_{t}`$ are martingale differences, so their
cross inner products have zero expectation. Hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\left\|\hat{p}_{N}-\bar{p}_{N}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[1-\|p_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{1}{N}.
``` |  |

This requires the conditionals of the actual sampler, including any
truncation; softmaxes on unrelated human contexts do not qualify. The
bound alone gives neither an i.i.d. entropy-bias formula for the path
nor a fixed limiting distribution.

### D.8 Plug-in bias, with its sign

For $`N`$ i.i.d. draws, $`f\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(N,p)`$ with
$`\mathrm{Binomial}(N,p_{v})`$ marginals,
$`\mathbb{E}[\hat{p}_{v}^{2}]=p_{v}^{2}+p_{v}(1-p_{v})/N`$, so
$`\mathbb{E}[\widehat{C}]=C+(1-C)/N`$. For independent but non-identical
conditionals the same calculation gives the $`V`$-bridge form
$`\mathbb{E}\,C(\hat{p})=C(\bar{p})+(1-\bar{C})/N`$. For comparison,
[Miller (1955)](#bib.bib48) and [Paninski (2003)](#bib.bib51) study bias
in Shannon-entropy estimation. To determine the sign of the induced bias
in $`H_{2}`$, write $`P_{3}=\sum_{v}p_{v}^{3}`$. A delta-method
expansion of $`\hat{H}_{2}=-\log_{2}\widehat{C}`$, using
$`\operatorname{Var}\,\widehat{C}=\tfrac{4}{N}(P_{3}-C^{2})+O(N^{-2})`$,
gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\,\hat{H}_{2}-H_{2}=-\frac{C+C^{2}-2P_{3}}{N\,C^{2}\ln 2}+O(N^{-2}).
``` |  |

The remainder is controlled because
$`1/|\operatorname{supp}p|\leq\widehat{C}\leq 1`$, so $`-\log_{2}`$ and
its derivatives are bounded on the range of $`\widehat{C}`$, and the
moments about $`C`$, $`\mathbb{E}(\widehat{C}-C)^{j}`$ are $`O(N^{-2})`$
for $`j=3,4`$; a fourth-order Taylor expansion with Lagrange remainder
then gives the stated error, with constants that depend on $`p`$. The
numerator is strictly positive for non-degenerate $`p`$: from
$`P_{3}=\sum_{v}p_{v}\,p_{v}^{2}\leq p_{\max}C`$ and
$`C\geq p_{\max}^{2}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C+C^{2}-2P_{3}\ \geq\ C\big(1+C-2p_{\max}\big)\ \geq\ C\big(1-p_{\max}\big)^{2}\ >\ 0
``` |  |

whenever $`p_{\max}<1`$. Hence, for all sufficiently large $`N`$,
$`\mathbb{E}\,\hat{H}_{2}<H_{2}`$: the collision-rate bias dominates the
competing Jensen term, and the plug-in $`H_{2}`$ is biased *downward*,
which supports the asymptotic entropy form of the ratchet in
Proposition [7](#Thmproposition7 "Proposition 7 (Truncated categorical refitting is a diversity ratchet). ‣ D.9 Proposition (diversity ratchet) ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training").
$`\square`$

### D.9 Proposition [7](#Thmproposition7 "Proposition 7 (Truncated categorical refitting is a diversity ratchet). ‣ D.9 Proposition (diversity ratchet) ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training") (diversity ratchet)

###### Proposition 7 (Truncated categorical refitting is a diversity ratchet).

Idealize generation $`g{+}1`$ as the categorical maximum-likelihood
estimate (the empirical distribution) $`\hat{p}_{g}`$ of $`N`$ draws
from the top-$`p`$ truncation $`q_{g}`$ ($`a<1`$) of generation $`g`$’s
law $`p_{g}`$. Then
$`\mathbb{E}\big[2^{-H_{2}(\hat{p}_{g})}\mid p_{g}\big]>2^{-H_{2}(p_{g})}`$
for every $`N`$.

*Proof.* Truncation gives $`C(q_{g})>C(p_{g})`$
(Proposition [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Truncation strictly lowers 𝐻_2). ‣ 4 Effects of decoding on next-token entropy ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")),
and categorical refitting gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}[C(\hat{p}_{g})\mid q_{g}]=C(q_{g})+\frac{1-C(q_{g})}{N}\geq C(q_{g})>C(p_{g}).
``` |  |

This proves the collision inequality for every $`N`$. For the entropy
form, fix $`p_{g}`$ and its retained set. The expansion above gives
$`\mathbb{E}[H_{2}(\hat{p}_{g})\mid q_{g}]=H_{2}(q_{g})+O(N^{-1})`$, and
since $`H_{2}(q_{g})<H_{2}(p_{g})`$, the expected entropy also decreases
for sufficiently large $`N`$. The entropy conclusion is asymptotic,
whereas the collision inequality is exact. $`\square`$

Without truncation ($`a=1`$, $`q_{g}=p_{g}`$) the collision inequality
still holds, since
$`\mathbb{E}[C(\hat{p}_{g})\mid p_{g}]=C(p_{g})+(1-C(p_{g}))/N>C(p_{g})`$
for non-degenerate $`p_{g}`$, and the sign result of
Appendix [D.8](#A4.SS8 "D.8 Plug-in bias, with its sign ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
shows that expected $`H_{2}`$ also falls for large $`N`$, by
$`O(N^{-1})`$ rather than by the fixed amount that truncation removes.
The rise of the neural untruncated arms
(§[6.4](#S6.SS4 "6.4 Truncation lowers collision entropy relative to untruncated training ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training"))
lies outside this model.

### D.10 Entropy and reference cross-perplexity

For one categorical law, $`2^{H_{1}(p)}\geq 2^{H_{2}(p)}`$ by order
monotonicity. Cross-perplexity on a reference law $`r`$ is
$`2^{H(r,p)}`$, where $`H(r,p)=H(r)+\operatorname{KL}(r\|p)`$ in bits,
which is finite if and only if $`r\ll p`$ on a finite alphabet. The two
quantities can move in opposite directions: for uniform $`r`$ on $`K>1`$
types and $`p_{\epsilon}=(1-(K-1)\epsilon,\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon)`$,
as $`\epsilon\downarrow 0`$, $`H_{1}(p_{\epsilon})\to 0`$ while
$`H(r,p_{\epsilon})\to\infty`$.

### E.4 Finite-sample estimator checks

We compare the finite-sample estimators of
Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
on known distributions ([Chao and Jost, 2012](#bib.bib52); [Chao et al.,
2014](#bib.bib53), cf.).

#### Estimator comparisons.

A uniform law on 5,000 types, Zipf laws on 20,000 types ($`s=0.8`$,
$`1.0`$ and $`1.2`$) and measured endpoint laws are sampled at budgets
from $`N/8`$ to $`2N`$, with $`N=13{,}000`$. On the English generation-0
law of
Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Tempering, sampling and the scope of the crossing illustration ‣ Appendix D Proofs ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
at $`N=13{,}000`$, the mean pair-count collision estimate over 4,000
draws is $`8.0166\times 10^{-3}`$ against a true value of
$`8.0181\times 10^{-3}`$. The plug-in entropy deficit is
$`0.0128\pm 0.0006`$ bits (standard error), against the Jensen bound of
$`0.0137`$ and the delta-method approximation of $`0.0131`$, and the log
U-statistic retains $`+0.0008\pm 0.0006`$ bits (standard error); its
population bias is strictly positive by the Jensen argument above.
Observed richness instead has a $`1.69`$-bit deficit at 31% type
coverage, and no collision correction estimates unseen types.

Low type coverage alone does not determine the accuracy of the
delta-method correction. Its collision-rate shift relative to $`C`$ is
$`(1-C)/(NC)`$, so small $`NC`$ can make the expansion poor; higher
moments also enter the entropy correction. On the uniform law at
$`N=1{,}625`$ ($`NC=0.33`$, 28% type coverage), it predicts a
$`4.44`$-bit deficit against a measured $`2.03`$, an error of $`2.41`$
bits. The error falls to $`0.88`$ at $`NC=0.65`$ and $`0.09`$ at
$`NC=2.6`$. Conversely, on the Zipf law with $`s=1.2`$ at $`N=1{,}625`$,
the delta-method prediction matches the Monte Carlo deficit to within
its standard error (a difference of $`0.0004`$ bits against a standard
error of $`0.0015`$) at 2.4% type coverage ($`NC=94`$). Coverage and the
estimated collision scale $`N\,2^{-\widehat{H}_{2}}`$ should both be
reported; neither low coverage nor $`NC`$ alone is a universal accuracy
criterion.

#### The reported $`H_{0}`$ and $`H_{2}`$ changes are large relative to seed variability.

For each cell on the six-temperature top-$`p=1`$ axis, we divide the
mean common-length change from generation 0 to generation 9 by its
standard error across the five seeds, a one-sample $`t`$ statistic that
treats seeds as the independent units, since generations within a
trajectory are not independent. Observed changes sit $`10.5`$ to $`442`$
standard errors from zero for $`H_{2}`$ (median $`49`$) and $`11.3`$ to
$`89`$ for $`H_{0}`$ (median $`51`$). These ratios quantify variability
across training seeds, whereas same-law rarefaction resamples a fixed
distribution. Separate generation-0 sampling controls, compared at each
pair’s common length (about 70,000 words), give a largest movement of
$`0.061`$ bits at any of the four primary orders and a two-point profile
statistic below $`0.049`$. Calibrating the 13,000-word alarm rules
requires controls at that smaller budget. For comparison, the $`T=1`$
changes in
Table [1](#S6.T1 "Table 1 ‣ A fixed budget. ‣ 6.2 Ordered zero crossings across training temperatures ‣ 6 Experiments ‣ Richness Is Not Evenness:Ordered Rényi Crossings under Self-Training")
are $`-0.81`$ bits for $`H_{0}`$ and $`+0.94`$ for $`H_{2}`$. An
autoregressive bias correction remains a separate problem from these
sampling controls and the $`0.013`$-bit i.i.d. reference calculation.
````
