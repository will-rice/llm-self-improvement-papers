# Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomy for AI Safety Gates

Arsenios Scrivens

March 2026

###### Abstract

Can classifier-based safety gates maintain reliable oversight as AI
systems improve over hundreds of iterations? We provide comprehensive
empirical evidence that they cannot. On a self-improving neural
controller ($`d=240`$), eighteen classifier configurations — spanning
MLPs, SVMs, random forests, k-NN, Bayesian classifiers, and deep
networks achieving 100% training accuracy — all fail the _dual
conditions_ for safe self-improvement established in our companion
paper \[[12](#bib.bib12)\]. Theorem 1 of \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] proves
this impossibility for power-law risk schedules $`\delta_{n}=O(n^{-p})`$
with $`p>1`$, which subsume all practically relevant risk budgets; the
tight finite-horizon ceiling (\[[12](#bib.bib12)\] Theorem 5) provides a
universal bound showing classifier utility grows at most subpolynomially
under any summable schedule. Three safe RL gate paradigms (CPO,
Lyapunov, safety shielding) also fail under practical computational
budgets. The results extend to MuJoCo benchmarks (Reacher-v4, $`d=496`$;
Swimmer-v4, $`d=1{,}408`$; HalfCheetah-v4, $`d=1{,}824`$). Crucially, at
controlled distribution separations
$`\Delta_{s}\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0\}`$, all classifiers still fail —
including the NP-optimal test \[[10](#bib.bib10)\] and MLPs with 100%
training accuracy — demonstrating that the impossibility is structural,
not an artifact of low separation.

We then demonstrate that the impossibility is specific to
_classification_, not to safe self-improvement itself. A Lipschitz ball
verifier — a pure $`O(d)`$ norm check — achieves zero false accepts with
100% soundness across dimensions
$`d\in\{84,240,768,2{,}688,5{,}760,9{,}984,17{,}408\}`$ using provable
analytical Lipschitz bounds (unconditional $`\delta=0`$). _Ball
chaining_ demonstrates feasibility of unbounded parameter-space
traversal: on MuJoCo Reacher-v4, 10 chains yield $`+4.31\pm 0.08`$
reward improvement ($`n{=}5`$ seeds) with $`\delta=0`$ throughout; on
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (7.6B parameters) during LoRA fine-tuning, 42 chain
transitions traverse $`234\times`$ the single-ball radius with zero
detected safety violations across 200 steps (conditional $`\delta=0`$,
using estimated Lipschitz constants with a $`5\times`$ safety factor;
see
§[6.3](#S6.SS3 "6.3 Limitations ‣ 6 Discussion ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"),
limitation 2). This LLM-scale experiment validates the compositional
Lipschitz _mechanism_, not the adequacy of the safety oracle. An
expanded 50-prompt oracle covering five attack categories confirms the
mechanism is oracle-agnostic: the ball acceptance rate remains 75.5%
while the stricter oracle catches 20 safety violations invisible to the
simpler check, saving 7,550 prompt evaluations through ball
amortization. Compositional per-group verification enables safe radii up
to $`37\times`$ larger than full-network balls. An important
distinction: at $`d\leq 17{,}408`$, $`\delta=0`$ is _unconditional_
(proved via analytical Lipschitz bounds); at LLM scale, it is
_conditional_ on estimated Lipschitz constants being valid upper bounds
— a falsifiable engineering question, not a mathematical necessity
(§[6.3](#S6.SS3 "6.3 Limitations ‣ 6 Discussion ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates")).

## 1 Introduction

Modern AI safety deployments rely on classifier-based gates — red-team
evaluators and learned discriminators — to filter self-modification. But
do these approaches maintain reliable oversight as systems improve over
hundreds or thousands of iterations?

Consider any iteratively self-modifying system gated by a binary safety
classifier. Two requirements are natural: (1) the cumulative
false-accept rate should be bounded ($`\sum\delta_{n}<\infty`$),
guaranteeing a strictly positive probability of never passing an unsafe
mutation (Borel–Cantelli); and (2) the system should not stagnate — it
must accept infinitely many safe improvements
($`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$). These _dual conditions_ formalize the
engineering intuition that “you can’t test your way to safety over
unbounded iterations.” Our companion paper \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] proves
that under distribution overlap between safe and unsafe modifications,
no classifier can satisfy both conditions simultaneously. But does this
impossibility manifest in practice, or do real-world systems escape it
through favorable structure?

We answer definitively: _no classifier escapes._ Across 18
configurations, three safe RL baselines, three MuJoCo environments, and
controlled distribution separations up to $`\Delta_{s}=2.0`$, every
classification-based gate fails. Beyond confirming Theorem 1, this paper
measures empirical constants ($`\sigma^{*}\propto d^{-0.54}`$,
$`\Delta_{s}\in[0.059,0.091]`$, Hölder ratios $`\leq 0.58`$) and
validates the verification escape at LLM scale — delivering three
standalone contributions: (1) a systematic negative methodology across
18 classifier configurations, three safe RL baselines, and controlled
$`\Delta_{s}`$ up to 2.0; (2) a validated verification protocol
(Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 5.4 Ball Chaining: Unbounded Verified Self-Improvement ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"))
with empirically calibrated parameters across four orders of magnitude
in dimension; and (3) quantitative scaling laws that theory alone does
not predict.

### 1.2 Related Work

Self-improving AI safety. The alignment literature discusses recursive
self-improvement \[[6](#bib.bib6), [13](#bib.bib13)\] and concrete
safety challenges \[[3](#bib.bib3)\] but lacks formal impossibility
results for the safety–utility coupling. RLHF \[[11](#bib.bib11)\] uses
a learned reward model as a safety gate — when thresholded into a binary
accept/reject decision, this becomes a classifier-based approach subject
to the impossibility of Theorem 1 (see \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] §1 for the
analogy and its limits). Constitutional AI \[[4](#bib.bib4)\] partially
escapes by formalizing some rules as verifiable predicates.

Safe reinforcement learning. [García and Fernández \[7\]](#bib.bib7)
survey constrained MDPs; [Achiam et al. \[1\]](#bib.bib1) formalize
constrained policy optimization; [Berkenkamp et al. \[5\]](#bib.bib5)
provide Lyapunov-based guarantees. GP-based safe exploration methods —
SafeOpt \[[14](#bib.bib14)\] and StageOpt \[[15](#bib.bib15)\] — expand
a verified safe set iteratively using Gaussian process confidence
bounds. The key distinction is the guarantee type: GP-based methods
provide probabilistic safety and scale as $`O(n^{3})`$, while the
Lipschitz ball provides deterministic $`\delta=0`$ at $`O(d)`$ per step.

Formal verification of neural networks. [Katz et al. \[9\]](#bib.bib9),
[Wong and Kolter \[18\]](#bib.bib18), and [Weng et al.
\[17\]](#bib.bib17) verify neural networks via SMT solvers and convex
relaxations. Tools such as CROWN \[[19](#bib.bib19)\] and
$`\alpha`$-$`\beta`$-CROWN \[[16](#bib.bib16)\] provide state-of-the-art
_input-space_ verification. Our Lipschitz ball verifier solves a
complementary _parameter-space_ verification problem: given a parameter
region $`\mathcal{B}_{r}(\theta_{0})`$, we bound system behavior for all
$`\theta\in\mathcal{B}_{r}`$. A comprehensive treatment is
in \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] §1.2 and Appendix B.

### 2.1 Safe Self-Improvement Framework

A self-improving system maintains parameters $`\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$
and iteratively proposes mutations $`\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots`$ At
each step $`n`$, a safety oracle
$`S:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{\text{safe},\text{unsafe}\}`$ determines ground
truth. A safety gate
$`g_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}`$ filters
mutations cheaply, incurring false acceptance rate
$`\delta_{n}=P^{-}(g_{n}\text{ accepts})`$ and true positive rate
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=P^{+}(g_{n}\text{ accepts})`$.

The dual conditions for safe self-improvement require:

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}<\infty\quad\text{(bounded risk)}\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty\quad\text{(unbounded utility)}
``` |  |

### 2.2 Classification Impossibility (Theorem 1)

###### Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility; \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] Theorem 1).

Let $`P^{+},P^{-}`$ be distributions on $`\mathbb{R}^{k}`$ with
$`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ and $`D_{\alpha_{0}}(P^{+}\|P^{-})<\infty`$ for some
$`\alpha_{0}>p/(p-1)`$. Then for any sequence of binary classifiers with
$`\delta_{n}\leq c/n^{p}`$ for some $`c>0,p>1`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}<\infty\implies\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}<\infty
``` |  |

The per-step bound is
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ where
$`\beta=(\alpha-1)/\alpha<1`$. The result holds for *any* binary
classifier. Proof in
Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Self-Contained Proof: Classification Impossibility (Theorem ) ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates");
tightness analysis in \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] §3.

### 2.3 Verification Escape (Theorem 2)

###### Theorem 2 (Verification Escape; \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] Theorem 2).

There exists a verification-based gate achieving $`\delta_{n}=0`$ for
all $`n`$ and $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$.

Construction. Let $`\theta_{0}`$ be safe on a defined operating domain
$`\mathcal{D}`$ with margin $`m>0`$ and let $`L`$ be a Lipschitz
constant for the trajectory map. The ball verifier accepts $`\theta`$
iff $`\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|<r`$ where $`r=m/L`$.
See \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] §4 for the full proof.

## 3 Experimental Systems

### 3.1 LTC Controller

We use a Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) neural network \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] as
the self-improving controller:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}\odot x+\tanh(W_{\text{in}}\cdot\text{obs}+W_{\text{rec}}\cdot x+b)`$ |  | (1) |
|  | $`\displaystyle u`$ | $`\displaystyle=W_{\text{out}}\cdot\tanh(x)`$ |  | (2) |

Architecture: $`4\to 12\to 2`$. Parameters:
$`d=4\times 12+12^{2}+12+12\times 2+12=240`$.

### 3.2 Environments

2D point-mass. A robot navigates between randomly placed start/target
positions in $`[-10,10]^{2}`$ while avoiding 3 circular obstacles.
Physics: mass $`=1.0`$, damping $`=0.5`$, max force $`=5.0`$,
$`\Delta t=0.02`$ s. Safety: a controller is *unsafe* if the robot’s
center enters any obstacle radius.

MuJoCo Reacher-v4. 2-DOF planar arm ($`\text{obs}=11,\text{act}=2`$).
LTC architecture $`11\to 16\to 2`$, $`d=496`$. Safety threshold: mean
episode reward $`\geq-20`$.

MuJoCo Swimmer-v4. 3-link planar swimmer
($`\text{obs}=8,\text{act}=2`$). LTC architecture $`8\to 32\to 2`$,
$`d=1{,}408`$. Safety threshold: mean episode reward $`\geq 30`$.

MuJoCo HalfCheetah-v4. 6-DOF planar runner
($`\text{obs}=17,\text{act}=6`$). LTC architecture $`17\to 32\to 6`$,
$`d=1{,}824`$. Safety threshold at 70th percentile of random-controller
reward distribution.

### 3.3 Mutation Model

Gaussian perturbation
$`\Delta\theta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})`$, $`\sigma=0.01`$ (2D
system). Approximately 19% of mutations from a safe controller remain
safe.

### 3.4 Oracle

Ground-truth safety oracle evaluates a controller on $`N_{\text{traj}}`$
trajectories of $`N_{\text{steps}}`$ steps each with deterministic
seeds. 2D system: 15 trajectories $`\times`$ 200 steps, using $`M=100`$
fixed (start, target) pairs. MuJoCo: 10 episodes $`\times`$ 500 steps.

All experiments use fixed random seeds (np.random.seed(42)) and
deterministic oracles. Multi-seed validation ($`n{=}5`$ seeds) is
provided for all positive verification results
(§[5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Lipschitz Ball Construction (2D System) ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"),
§[5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3 Directed Safe Self-Improvement ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates")–[5.5](#S5.SS5 "5.5 Constrained Ball Chaining: Physical Safety on MuJoCo ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates")).

## 4 Classification Gates Fail

### 4.1 Task-Specific Classification Gates

We implement three classification-based safety gates of increasing
sophistication:

Gate A (Static MLP). A 2-layer MLP ($`240\to 32\to 1`$) trained on 200
labeled examples before the loop. Fixed throughout.

Gate B (Retrained MLP). Same architecture, retrained every 50 steps on
accumulated data.

Gate C (CBF-Adaptive). A hybrid gate combining a control barrier
function with an adaptive boundary estimator.

Results over $`N=500`$ self-improvement iterations:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| Gate | $`\delta`$ (mean) | Fitted $`p`$ | $`\sum\delta_{N}`$ | $`\prod(1{-}\delta_{n})`$ | TPR (mean) | Scenario |
| A (Static) | 0.37 | 0.26 | 183.6 | 0.0 | 0.42 | Constant $`\delta`$ |
| B (Retrained) | 0.02 | — | 13.5 | 0.0 | 0.01 | Vacuous gate |
| C (CBF-Adaptive) | 0.09 | 0.02 | 45.2 | 0.0 | 0.05 | Slow decay |

No gate achieves the dual conditions. Gate A maintains constant
$`\delta`$ ($`p=0.26<1`$). Gate B becomes vacuous (rejects everything
after step 42). Gate C decays too slowly ($`p\approx 0.02\ll 1`$).

### 4.2 Classifier Baselines: Universality of Impossibility

We evaluate five classifier families trained on 300 labeled examples:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| Classifier | Training Acc. | $`\sum\delta_{200}`$ | $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{200}`$ | Dual |
| Logistic Regression | 61.0% | 60.0 | 77.0 | FAIL |
| k-NN ($`k=5`$) | 72.3% | 41.0 | 52.0 | FAIL |
| Random Forest (50 trees) | 86.7% | 51.0 | 66.0 | FAIL |
| Linear SVM | 59.0% | 53.0 | 85.0 | FAIL |

All five fail. Even the Gaussian Bayes-optimal classifier fails —
confirming the impossibility stems from distribution overlap, not
classifier choice.

### 5.4 Ball Chaining: Unbounded Verified Self-Improvement

When the system exhausts the verified region, ball chaining overcomes
this by re-verifying at each chain transition.

1: Safe controller $`\theta_{0}`$, operating domain $`\mathcal{D}`$,
chains $`K`$, inner steps $`N`$

2: for $`k=0,1,\ldots,K-1`$ do

3:   Compute safety margin $`m_{k}`$ of $`\theta_{k}`$ on
$`\mathcal{D}`$

4:   Estimate Lipschitz constant $`L_{k}`$ at $`\theta_{k}`$; set
$`r_{k}=m_{k}/(2.5\cdot L_{k})`$

5:   for $`j=1,\ldots,N`$ do

6:    Estimate performance gradient $`\nabla J`$ via finite differences

7:    Propose $`\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\eta\cdot\nabla J/\|\nabla J\|`$

8:    if $`\|\theta^{\prime}-\theta_{k}\|<r_{k}`$ then accept ($`O(d)`$
norm check)

9:    end if

10:   end for

11:   Set $`\theta_{k+1}\leftarrow\arg\max_{j}J(\theta^{\prime}_{j})`$
among accepted proposals

12:   Re-verify: confirm $`\theta_{k+1}`$ is $`\mathcal{D}`$-safe with
positive margin

13: end for

14: return $`\theta_{K}`$, chain history

## 6 Discussion

### 6.1 What the Impossibility Means

Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility; [] Theorem 1). ‣ 2.2 Classification Impossibility (Theorem 1) ‣ 2 Background ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates")
establishes that any classifier-based safety gate faces a mathematical
ceiling that is a consequence of distribution overlap, not a limitation
of architecture or training. Our experiments confirm this: all 18
classifier configurations fail, including a deep MLP with 100% training
accuracy.

### 6.2 What the Verification Escape Means

The impossibility is inherent to *classification*, not to safe
self-improvement itself. The practical lesson: safety gates for
self-improving systems are better built on verification than
classification. Ball chaining
(§[5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4 Ball Chaining: Unbounded Verified Self-Improvement ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates")–[5.5](#S5.SS5 "5.5 Constrained Ball Chaining: Physical Safety on MuJoCo ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"))
demonstrates that verification-based gates enable *unbounded* safe
self-improvement.

### 6.3 Limitations

1.  1.
    Domain restriction. The ball verifier guarantees
    $`\mathcal{D}`$-safety, not universal safety.
2.  2.
    Lipschitz estimation gap. At $`d\leq 17{,}408`$, we provide
    analytical bounds (unconditional $`\delta=0`$). At LLM scale,
    Lipschitz constants are estimated via finite differences;
    $`\delta=0`$ is conditional on the estimate being correct.
    \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] Proposition 3 proves finite closed-form
    constants exist; computing them tractably at LLM scale is the key
    open problem.
3.  3.
    Mutation scale trade-off. Required $`\sigma^{*}`$ decreases as
    $`O(d^{-0.54})`$. Ball chaining recovers practical improvement
    rates: $`17.2\times`$ displacement on Reacher, $`234\times`$ on
    Qwen2.5-7B.
4.  4.
    Limited model scale. We test up to 7.6B parameters.
5.  5.
    Environment complexity. Standard testbeds ($`d=240`$–$`1{,}824`$).
    The variable-$`\Delta_{s}`$ experiment
    (§[4.6](#S4.SS6 "4.6 Variable Distribution Separation ‣ 4 Classification Gates Fail ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"))
    and LLM experiment
    (§[5.7](#S5.SS7 "5.7 LLM-Scale Mechanism Validation: LoRA Fine-Tuning of Qwen2.5-7B ‣ 5 Verification Escapes the Impossibility ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"))
    address this.
6.  6.
    LLM safety oracle scope. The 10-prompt oracle validates the
    mechanism, not production safety. The 50-prompt expansion confirms
    oracle-agnosticism.
7.  7.
    Margin shrinkage under chaining. Safety margin can decrease across
    successive chains; margin convergence to zero is expected when the
    optimizer pushes toward constraint boundaries.
8.  8.
    No adversarial mutation analysis.
9.  9.
    Non-stationarity. The verifier’s $`\delta=0`$ guarantee is per-ball;
    the classifier failure results do not depend on stationarity.

### 6.4 Relationship to Neural Network Verification Tools

Tools such as CROWN \[[19](#bib.bib19)\],
$`\alpha`$-$`\beta`$-CROWN \[[16](#bib.bib16)\], and
Reluplex \[[9](#bib.bib9)\] solve *input-space* verification. Our
Lipschitz ball solves *parameter-space* verification — a complementary
axis:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | Input-space (CROWN et al.) | Parameter-space (this paper) |
| Question | Is *this* network safe on all inputs? | Are all *nearby* networks safe? |
| Verified set | $`\{x:\|x-x_{0}\|\leq\varepsilon\}`$ | $`\{\theta:\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq r\}`$ |
| Use case | Certifying a deployed model | Gating self-modification |
| Cost | $`O(\text{poly}(d_{\text{net}}))`$ per property | $`O(d)`$ per mutation check |

### 6.5 When Our Results Do Not Apply

1.  1.
    Perfectly separable distributions ($`P^{+}\perp P^{-}`$).
2.  2.
    Discrete or quantized parameters.
3.  3.
    Large safety margins where no gate is needed.
4.  4.
    Short deployments with generous risk budgets.
5.  5.
    Engineered non-overlap via restricted modification spaces.

## 7 Conclusion

We provide comprehensive experimental validation of the
classification–verification dichotomy for AI self-improvement safety
(Theorems 1–2 of \[[12](#bib.bib12)\]):

- •
  Classification fails universally. Eighteen classifier configurations
  all fail the dual conditions, including a deep MLP with 100% training
  accuracy. Three safe RL paradigms fail under partial rollouts. All
  results extend to MuJoCo benchmarks ($`d`$ up to 1,824). At controlled

## Appendix A Experimental Details

### A.1 Feature Extraction for Classification Gates

The 13-dimensional feature vector $`\varphi(\theta)`$ consists of:
Frobenius norms of $`W_{\text{in}},W_{\text{rec}},W_{\text{out}}`$ (3
features); spectral radius of $`W_{\text{rec}}`$ (1); mean, std, min,
max of time constants $`\tau`$ (4); norm of bias $`b`$ (1); mean, std,
min, max of all parameters (4).

### A.2 Gate Training Details

Gates A/B: 2-layer MLP with 32 hidden units, ReLU, SGD (lr = 0.01, 50
epochs). Gate C: CBF margin = 0.3, 40 adversarial samples per
refinement.

### A.3 Classifier Baselines Details

Original baselines (300 samples). Logistic Regression, k-NN ($`k{=}5`$),
Random Forest (50 trees, depth 5), Linear SVM ($`C{=}1.0`$), Gaussian
Bayes (full covariance).

Enhanced baselines (1000 samples). Three feature maps (13-d handcrafted,
240-d raw, 253-d concatenated), deep 3-layer MLP (128-64-32, 300 epochs,
100% training accuracy).

### A.4 Safe RL Comparison Details

200 steps, $`d=240`$, margin $`m=0.3883`$, 50 scenarios. CPO:
5-trajectory, 100-step rollouts. Lyapunov: expansion rate 1.05. Shield:
rejects constraint violations. Ball: $`r\ll\sigma\sqrt{d}`$ at
$`\sigma=0.01`$.

### A.5 Scaling Analysis Details

For each $`n_{\text{hid}}\in\{6,12,24,48,72,96,128\}`$: controller
search over 5,000 initializations on $`M=50`$ scenarios. Lipschitz: 200
random + 20 axis-aligned perturbations, $`2.5\times`$ safety factor.
Analytical bounds via Gronwall inequality and trajectory-specific
forward sensitivity.

### A.6 Lipschitz Ball Experiment Details

Operating domain: 100 scenarios, seed 12345. Lipschitz: 300 samples.
Ball validation: 200 inside, 100 outside. Self-improvement: 500 steps,
$`\sigma=0.000330`$, $`r=0.007313`$.

### A.7 Directed Improvement Details

Domain: 50 scenarios. Gradient: 10 FDs, perturbation $`10^{-4}`$. Step
size: $`\sigma_{\text{step}}=r/(2\sqrt{d})\approx 0.0013`$. Projection
at $`0.95r`$.

## Appendix B Proofs

### B.1 Self-Contained Proof: Classification Impossibility (Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility; [] Theorem 1). ‣ 2.2 Classification Impossibility (Theorem 1) ‣ 2 Background ‣ Empirical Validation of the Classification–Verification Dichotomyfor AI Safety Gates"))

We reproduce the proof of \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] Theorem 1. Fix
$`\alpha\in(p/(p-1),\,\alpha_{0})`$ (valid since $`\alpha_{0}>p/(p-1)`$)
and set $`\beta=(\alpha-1)/\alpha\in(0,1)`$.

Step 1 (Per-step Hölder bound). Write
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\int_{A_{n}}dP^{+}=\int_{A_{n}}(dP^{+}/dP^{-})\,dP^{-}`$.
Apply Hölder’s inequality:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\underbrace{\left(\int_{A_{n}}\left(\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}dP^{-}\right)^{1/\alpha}}_{\leq\,C_{\alpha}}\cdot\;\underbrace{\left(\int_{A_{n}}dP^{-}\right)^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}}_{=\,\delta_{n}^{\beta}}
``` |  |

so $`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ where
$`C_{\alpha}=\exp((\alpha{-}1)D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})/\alpha)`$.

Step 2 (Summation). For $`\delta_{n}\leq c/n^{p}`$ with $`p>1`$, choose
$`\alpha`$ so that $`p\beta>1`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}c^{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-p\beta}<\infty\qed
``` |  |

### B.2 Lipschitz Ball Soundness Proof

If $`\theta\in B(\theta_{0},r)`$ with $`r=m/L`$:
$`\sup_{t}d(\text{traj}_{\theta}(t),\text{traj}_{\theta_{0}}(t))\leq L\cdot\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|<m`$.
Since $`\theta_{0}`$ has margin $`m`$, safety is preserved. ∎

### B.3 Analytical Lipschitz Bounds

Gronwall:
$`\|u(\theta+\Delta\theta)-u(\theta)\|\leq\|W_{\text{out}}\|\cdot\exp(\|W_{\text{rec}}\|\cdot T)\cdot T\cdot\|\Delta\theta\|`$.

Trajectory-specific: Forward sensitivity ODE
$`\dot{S}_{j}(t)=A(t)S_{j}(t)+e_{j}(t)`$. On 2D ($`d=240`$):
$`L_{\text{Gronwall}}=3{,}227`$ ($`378\times`$ conservative),
$`L_{\text{traj}}=112`$ ($`13\times`$ conservative). Both yield 100%
ball soundness.
````
