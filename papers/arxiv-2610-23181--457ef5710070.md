# Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning

Heming Zou^(1,2,\*)    Qi Wang¹    Yixiu Mao¹    Yun Qu¹   
Yuyi Guo^(1,2,\*)    Zeyuan Liu^(1,2,\*) Affiliation: Chenglin Li¹,
Weijie Liu², Saiyong Yang², Xiangyang Ji¹

###### Abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has become a scalable
post-training paradigm for large language models. However, training
agents over long-horizon interactions presents two challenges: using
only terminal rewards makes turn-level credit assignment sparse and
high-variance, while sequential mini-batch updates on a fixed rollout
batch introduce policy staleness as the current policy drifts from the
behavior policy. Existing approaches obtain denser credit via learned
critics, process reward models, or on-policy distillation, but they rely
on a value network, step-level annotations, or a stronger external
teacher that increase training complexity. Meanwhile, importance
sampling and clipping mitigate policy drift, but they do not specify a
finite target that stops sequential updates from overshooting. In this
work, we introduce TreeOPSD, a critic-free, tree-aware posterior
self-distillation framework for long-horizon agentic reinforcement
learning. Treating each interaction turn as a tree node, TreeOPSD
branches alternative continuations from shared prefixes and recursively
backs up terminal rewards into implicit turn-level credit. It then maps
each credit to a closed-form, bounded teacher distribution parameterized
by the likelihood ratio relative to the frozen behavior policy.
Minimizing the divergence between this teacher and the student policy
recovers the policy-gradient direction as the tangent at the behavior
policy while bounding updates toward a finite target across sequential
mini-batches. Empirically, TreeOPSD improves agentic capabilities across
several competitive benchmarks and model scales, especially for
long-horizon tasks, e.g., raising the average accuracy of
Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 by 4.97 points over GRPO on the Deep
Research setting.

## 1  Introduction

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib5); [Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib18)) has emerged as a
central post-training paradigm for large language models (LLMs), using
outcome rewards to incentivize reasoning or environment interactions on
complex problem-solving tasks. While reasoning-oriented RLVR typically
learns from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rollouts, agentic RL ([Yao et al.,
2022](#bib.bib4)) extends this paradigm to long-horizon, multi-turn
trajectories in which reasoning is interleaved with actions and
environment observations. As the interaction horizon grows, a single
terminal reward provides increasingly sparse and high-variance
supervision across intermediate turns: broadcasting one trajectory-level
advantage over the entire rollout over-credits unhelpful actions in
successful trajectories and penalizes useful steps in failed ones
([Arjona-Medina et al., 2019](#bib.bib8); [Li et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib42)). At the same time, updating the policy across
sequential mini-batches within each rollout batch induces policy
staleness as the current policy departs from the behavior policy ([Zheng
et al., 2025a](#bib.bib41)).

To address the credit assignment problem, actor–critic methods such as
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib6))
estimate token-level advantages with a learned critic, which is
difficult to train accurately and susceptible to value flattening ([Li
et al., 2026c](#bib.bib49)). Process reward models (PRMs) ([Lightman et
al., 2024](#bib.bib39)) provide step-level rewards but require process
annotations, and summing them as discounted returns is vulnerable to
reward hacking ([Cheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib44)). On-policy
distillation (OPD) ([Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib47); [Gu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib48)) provides dense token-level supervision on student
rollouts at the cost of querying a stronger external teacher. Extending
OPD, on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) ([Zhao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib45); [Kaur et al., 2026](#bib.bib50)) constructs a
self-teacher by conditioning the model on privileged context (e.g.,
reference solutions), which relies on task-specific heuristics and can
yield unstable supervision. These limitations leave open how to derive
dense intermediate credit directly from terminal verifier rewards.
Sampling independent trajectories confounds all turns along each
rollout, whereas multi-turn interaction exposes discrete turn-level
prefixes as natural branching points. Branching alternative
continuations from a shared prefix fixes the preceding history to
isolate how subsequent decisions affect terminal outcomes, forming a
rollout tree on which recursive backup converts terminal rewards into
implicit turn-level credit without auxiliary value or reward models ([Ji
et al., 2026](#bib.bib20); [Zou et al., 2026a](#bib.bib23)).

Sequential mini-batch updates on a fixed rollout batch create a separate
optimization challenge. While all rollouts in the batch are sampled from
a frozen behavior policy $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, each update
step moves the current policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ further away, increasing
policy staleness for subsequent mini-batches. Policy-gradient methods
compensate for this mismatch with importance-sampling ratios, and
stabilize training through clipping ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib6))
or ratio regularizers ([Zheng et al., 2025a](#bib.bib41)) as those
ratios depart from one. However, such controls only constrain the
importance ratio while keeping the collection-time credit fixed, so the
surrogate still specifies an ascent direction rather than a finite
destination ([Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)). We therefore seek an
objective that maps tree credit to a finite target and adapts its
gradient as $`\pi_{\theta}`$ evolves, halting at the target and
reversing sign after overshoot.

To this end, we introduce TreeOPSD, a critic-free posterior
self-distillation framework for long-horizon agentic RL. TreeOPSD first
maps backed-up turn credit into a frozen turn-level teacher distribution
relative to the behavior policy, and then minimizes the divergence
between this teacher and the current student policy across sequential
mini-batches. Geometrically, the tangent of this distillation objective
at the behavior policy recovers the exact tree policy-gradient
direction, while later mini-batches scale each update by the current
student–teacher gap rather than a fixed collection-time credit weight.
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
illustrates this decoupling of target construction and distribution
matching.

In summary, our main contributions are two-fold:

1.  1.  We derive closed-form, bounded turn-level target distributions via
        an affine map of backed-up credit relative to the frozen behavior
        policy. Because this affine map commutes with tree backup, matching
        the backed-up parent target is gradient-equivalent to aggregating
        descendant distillation losses.
2.  2.  We formulate a Forward-KL posterior distillation objective whose
        tangent at the behavior policy recovers the tree policy gradient,
        while its student–teacher residual vanishes at the target and
        reverses sign upon overshoot to stabilize sequential mini-batch
        optimization.

Empirically, TreeOPSD improves agentic capabilities across competitive
benchmarks and model scales, and it elicits longer-horizon agent
behavior with more interaction turns in Deep Research.

## 2  Preliminaries

### 2.1  Agentic reinforcement learning

We consider an LLM policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ interacting with an
environment in the ReAct setting ([Yao et al., 2022](#bib.bib4)). Given
a prompt $`x\sim\mathcal{D}`$, at each turn $`1\leq t\leq T`$ the policy
generates an $`L_{t}`$-token actor span
$`a_{t}=(a_{t,1},\ldots,a_{t,L_{t}})`$. For nonterminal turns ($`t<T`$),
$`a_{t}`$ comprises a thought and a tool action, after which the
environment transition $`P_{\mathrm{env}}`$ returns an observation
$`o_{t}`$ and updates the history as
$`\mathcal{H}_{t}:=(\mathcal{H}_{t-1},a_{t},o_{t})`$ from
$`\mathcal{H}_{0}:=x`$. At the terminal turn $`T`$, the policy emits the
final answer $`a_{T}`$, yielding
$`\mathcal{H}_{T}:=(\mathcal{H}_{T-1},a_{T})`$. The trajectory
distribution factorizes as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\mathcal{H}_{T}\mid x)=\underbrace{\pi_{\theta}(a_{T}\mid\mathcal{H}_{T-1})}_{\text{final answer}}\underbrace{\prod_{t=1}^{T-1}\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1})P_{\mathrm{env}}(o_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1},a_{t})}_{\text{actor--observation}},
``` |  | (1) |

where each turn likelihood decomposes autoregressively as
$`\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1})=\prod_{j=1}^{L_{t}}\pi_{\theta}(a_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1},a_{t,<j})`$.
Only policy-generated tokens contribute gradients, while observation
tokens from $`P_{\mathrm{env}}`$ enter $`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$ as fixed
context and are masked from the policy loss.

Given a reward function $`R(\mathcal{H}_{T})`$ and a frozen reference
policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$, the standard KL-regularized agentic RL
objective ([Ouyang et al., 2022](#bib.bib11); [Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib5)) with reference weight $`\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\geq 0`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\max_{\theta}\ \mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\sim\mathcal{D}\\
\mathcal{H}_{T}\sim\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\cdot\mid x)\end{subarray}}[R(\mathcal{H}_{T})]-\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\cdot\mid x)\|\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(\cdot\mid x)\right)\right].
``` |  | (2) |

Following recent advances ([Yu et al., 2026](#bib.bib10); [Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23); [Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)), we focus on
rule-based outcome rewards that are binary or sparse
($`R(\mathcal{H}_{T})\in[0,1]`$) without an explicit reference penalty
($`\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}=0`$), where intermediate turns $`t<T`$ receive no
direct supervision.

### 2.2  Initialize-then-expand rollout trees

To estimate intermediate turn credit from terminal rewards alone,
tree-based policy optimization branches alternative continuations from
shared prefixes via an initialize-then-expand sampling scheme ([Hou et
al., 2025](#bib.bib19); [Ji et al., 2026](#bib.bib20); [Dong et al.,
2026](#bib.bib21); [Zong et al., 2026](#bib.bib22); [Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23)). For each prompt $`x`$, the frozen behavior policy
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ first samples $`M`$ complete
trajectories $`\mathcal{H}_{m,T_{m}}`$, where $`T_{m}`$ is the
terminal-turn index of trajectory $`m`$. From each trajectory $`m`$, the
sampler uniformly draws a multiset
$`\mathcal{A}_{m}=\{t_{m,1},\ldots,t_{m,N}\}`$ of $`N`$ nonterminal
anchor indices $`1\leq t_{m,n}<T_{m}`$ (or $`t_{m,n}=0`$ if
$`T_{m}=1`$), and samples one fresh terminal suffix $`S_{m,n}`$ from
each prefix $`\mathcal{H}_{m,t_{m,n}}`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{m,T_{m}}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(\cdot\mid x),`$ |  | $`\displaystyle m=1,\ldots,M,`$ |  | (3) |
|  | $`\displaystyle S_{m,n}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{H}_{m,t_{m,n}}),`$ |  | $`\displaystyle t_{m,n}\in\mathcal{A}_{m},\quad n=1,\ldots,N.`$ |  |  |

Retaining the $`M`$ initial rollouts alongside their $`MN`$ branched
continuations yields $`K:=M(N+1)`$ terminal trajectories. Because a
uniformly branched continuation starts mid-trajectory, its expected
generation cost in tokens or tool calls is roughly half that of a full
rollout ([Ji et al., 2026](#bib.bib20); [Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23)), so an $`(M,N)`$ tree matches the expected rollout
budget of $`G`$ full trajectories in Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)) when $`M(1+N/2)\approx G`$.
Merging shared prefixes across the $`K`$ trajectories produces a rollout
tree with node set $`\mathcal{T}_{x}`$, where shared turns appear once
while independently sampled continuations form distinct nodes. Each node
$`i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}`$ represents an $`L_{i}`$-token actor span
$`a_{i}`$ conditioned on pre-turn history $`h_{i}`$, with token-level
context $`h_{i,j}:=(h_{i},a_{i,<j})`$ for $`1\leq j\leq L_{i}`$ and
sampled child set $`\mathcal{C}_{i}`$. Each terminal trajectory
$`k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}`$ ends at a leaf node $`i_{k}\in\mathcal{T}_{x}`$
and receives verifier reward $`R_{k}`$.

### 2.3  Tree-based turn credit

Following common practice in tree-based policy optimization ([Hou et
al., 2025](#bib.bib19); [Ji et al., 2026](#bib.bib20); [Dong et al.,
2026](#bib.bib21); [Zong et al., 2026](#bib.bib22); [Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23)), terminal outcomes on $`\mathcal{T}_{x}`$ are
propagated upward to assign credit to each turn. First, the $`K`$
terminal rewards for prompt $`x`$ are standardized into trajectory
advantages $`A_{k}`$, as in group-based RLVR:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{k}:=\frac{R_{k}-\operatorname{mean}(\{R_{k^{\prime}}\}_{k^{\prime}=1}^{K})}{\operatorname{std}(\{R_{k^{\prime}}\}_{k^{\prime}=1}^{K})},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K,
``` |  | (4) |

using the sample standard deviation (with $`A_{k}:=0`$ when all rewards
are equal). These leaf advantages are then recursively backed up through
$`\mathcal{T}_{x}`$ using behavior-policy likelihood weights:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\omega_{i,c}`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\frac{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_{c}\mid h_{c})}{\sum_{c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}}\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_{c^{\prime}}\mid h_{c^{\prime}})},`$ | $`\displaystyle V_{i}`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}A_{k},&i=i_{k},\\
\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{i}}\omega_{i,c}V_{c},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}`$ |  | (5) |

where $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_{c}\mid h_{c})`$ is the
behavior-policy likelihood of child span $`a_{c}`$ given the shared
post-observation history $`h_{c}=(h_{i},a_{i},o_{i})`$ of parent turn
$`i`$. Because $`V_{i}`$ already aggregates the outcomes of all branches
below turn $`i`$, each turn $`i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}`$ enters the gradient
once rather than being repeated across the $`K`$ trajectories. With
$`L_{x}:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}L_{i}`$ denoting the total number of
unique actor tokens in $`\mathcal{T}_{x}`$, the prompt-level tree
policy-gradient direction is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g_{x}^{\mathrm{tree}}:=\frac{1}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{i}}V_{i}\left.\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})\right|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}.
``` |  | (6) |

Eq. [6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
assigns the weight $`V_{i}/L_{x}`$ to every policy-generated token in
turn $`i`$, providing the exact tree policy-gradient ascent direction at
the behavior policy $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$.

## 3  TreeOPSD

While the tree policy gradient $`g_{x}^{\mathrm{tree}}`$ in
Eq. [6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
is exact at the on-policy point
$`\pi_{\theta}=\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, practical RLVR training
partitions each rollout batch into sequential mini-batches. Once earlier
mini-batches update $`\theta`$, the current policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$
drifts away from the behavior policy $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$
that generated the tree. Continuing to apply the collection-time credit
$`V_{i}`$ as a fixed linear weight ignores this off-policy drift and
specifies only an initial ascent direction rather than a finite
destination ([Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)). Conversely, multiplying
token gradients by raw importance ratios
$`\pi_{\theta}/\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ amplifies variance on long
turns ([Ionides, 2008](#bib.bib66); [Liu et al., 2018](#bib.bib68); [Liu
et al., 2020](#bib.bib69)), while ratio clipping truncates the update
without defining a target distribution where optimization should
converge ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib6); [Zheng et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib41); [Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)).

These limitations arise because linear policy-gradient surrogates
entangle credit assignment with policy updating instead of specifying an
explicit target distribution. Drawing on Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) and
variational policy optimization ([Beck and Teboulle, 2003](#bib.bib52);
[Abdolmaleki et al., 2018](#bib.bib51); [Vaswani et al.,
2022](#bib.bib53); [Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)), we decouple each
iteration into two steps: first solve a KL-regularized proximal
improvement problem around $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ to convert
backed-up turn credits into a frozen target distribution, and then
project the evolving policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ onto that target across
sequential mini-batches. This decoupling addresses both bottlenecks:
target construction maps backed-up credits into tree-consistent, bounded
targets at every depth, while distribution matching replaces
high-variance importance ratios with a convex projection whose gradient
vanishes once the student reaches the target. Writing $`Q`$ for a
candidate target distribution, $`\mathcal{J}(Q)`$ for a proximal
objective anchored at $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, $`P_{\theta}`$
for the current student distribution, and $`D(Q\|P_{\theta})`$ for a
statistical divergence, the two steps are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\underbrace{Q^{\star}=\arg\max_{Q}\mathcal{J}(Q)}_{\text{target construction}}\qquad\underbrace{\theta^{\prime}=\arg\min_{\theta}D\!\left(Q^{\star}\|P_{\theta}\right)}_{\text{distribution matching}}.
``` |  | (7) |

Target construction is executed once per rollout tree from
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, holding $`Q^{\star}`$ fixed across the
batch, whereas distribution matching re-evaluates
$`D(Q^{\star}\|P_{\theta})`$ at every mini-batch step as
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ evolves
(Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")).

Figure 2: TreeOPSD framework. The behavior policy
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ generates a shared-prefix rollout tree,
and terminal verifier rewards $`R_{k}`$ are backed up into turn credits
$`V_{i}`$. Each credit defines a frozen Bernoulli teacher
$`q_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}+V_{i}/\tau`$, and each bar shows one turn against
the behavior level $`\tfrac{1}{2}`$ (dashed). In each of the $`E`$
sequential mini-batches, the student posterior
$`p_{\theta,i}=\sigma(s_{\theta,i})`$ is recomputed under the current
policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$, and the spring-like ascent weight
$`\Gamma_{i}=\tau(q_{i}-p_{\theta,i})`$ pulls it toward $`q_{i}`$,
vanishing at the teacher and reversing sign after overshoot.

### 3.1  Behavior-relative turn distributions

As established in
Section [2.3](#S2.SS3 "2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"),
branching from shared prefixes naturally converts sparse terminal
outcomes into dense turn-level credit $`V_{i}`$ without an external
teacher or learned critic. Unlike token-level distillation where an
external teacher supplies full-vocabulary distributions at every token,
here the supervision signal is a single turn-level scalar $`V_{i}`$
attached to the entire realized turn span $`a_{i}`$ of length $`L_{i}`$.
To match this turn-level credit granularity, $`P_{\theta}`$ at node
$`i`$ must represent how strongly the complete turn $`a_{i}`$ is
reinforced or suppressed relative to the behavior policy
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ that sampled it, using only the logged
probabilities of the sampled tokens rather than vocabulary-wide behavior
logits ([Qi et al., 2026](#bib.bib70)).

Directly multiplying token likelihood ratios across a multi-token turn
compounds off-policy discrepancies exponentially with $`L_{i}`$ ([Liu et
al., 2018](#bib.bib68); [Liu et al., 2020](#bib.bib69)), causing severe
variance and length bias between short tool calls and long reasoning
turns. We therefore parameterize the turn-level policy shift by the
per-token average log-likelihood ratio ([Meng et al.,
2024](#bib.bib14)), or equivalently the geometric-mean likelihood ratio
([Zheng et al., 2025b](#bib.bib16)), which normalizes this shift by turn
length:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
s_{\theta,i}:=\frac{1}{L_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{i}}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})},\qquad r_{\theta,i}:=e^{s_{\theta,i}}=\left(\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{i}\mid h_{i})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_{i}\mid h_{i})}\right)^{1/L_{i}}.
``` |  | (8) |

At the on-policy point $`\pi_{\theta}=\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, the
log-ratio score is zero and $`r_{\theta,i}=1`$. As $`\pi_{\theta}`$
drifts during sequential updates, $`r_{\theta,i}`$ rises above one when
turn $`i`$ becomes more likely than under
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ and falls below one when it becomes less
likely. Because normalizing $`r_{\theta,i}`$ over the full continuation
space requires an intractable partition function, we follow
noise-contrastive and relative density-ratio estimation ([Yamada et al.,
2013](#bib.bib1); [Goodfellow et al., 2014](#bib.bib3); [Cranmer et al.,
2015](#bib.bib2); [Kobayashi, 2021](#bib.bib13); [Chen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib15)) and form a two-point conditional distribution between
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ and the behavior baseline
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ (whose ratio to itself is $`1`$).
Normalizing the pair $`(r_{\theta,i},1)`$ with
$`\sigma(s):=(1+e^{-s})^{-1}`$ yields the binary posterior

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\theta,i}:=\frac{r_{\theta,i}}{1+r_{\theta,i}}=\sigma(s_{\theta,i}),\qquad P_{\theta,i}:=\operatorname{Ber}(p_{\theta,i}).
``` |  | (9) |

Normalizing in likelihood-ratio space makes $`P_{\theta,i}`$ invariant
to the raw sequence probability of $`a_{i}`$ and eliminates any
partition function over unsampled continuations. This odds map
bijectively converts $`s_{\theta,i}`$ into a Bernoulli distribution
whose log-odds equal $`s_{\theta,i}`$, mapping the on-policy anchor
$`r_{\theta,i}=1`$ to $`p_{\theta,i}=1/2`$ and reflecting
$`p_{\theta,i}`$ symmetrically around $`1/2`$ when $`\pi_{\theta}`$ and
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ are swapped.

### 3.2  Teacher construction

Given the student posterior
$`P_{\theta,i}=\operatorname{Ber}(p_{\theta,i})`$, we construct a
turn-level teacher $`Q_{i}^{\star}=\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i})`$ in the
same Bernoulli family. Notice from
Eqs. [6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
and [8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ 3.1 Behavior-relative turn distributions ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
that the on-policy tree gradient at node $`i`$ is the gradient of the
linear surrogate $`V_{i}s_{\theta,i}`$ (up to the weight
$`L_{i}/L_{x}`$), while inverting the sigmoid map $`p=\sigma(s)`$ in
Eq. [9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.1 Behavior-relative turn distributions ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
expresses the log-ratio score of any candidate Bernoulli distribution
$`\operatorname{Ber}(q)`$ as its log-odds
$`s(q)=\sigma^{-1}(q)=\log\frac{q}{1-q}`$. Thus, in Bernoulli
coordinates, the linear policy-improvement term $`V_{i}s`$ takes the
exact form $`V_{i}\log\frac{q}{1-q}`$. Following PMD and KL-regularized
policy optimization ([Ouyang et al., 2022](#bib.bib11); [Abdolmaleki et
al., 2018](#bib.bib51); [Vaswani et al., 2022](#bib.bib53)), we
regularize this linear improvement with a KL trust-region penalty from
the on-policy behavior distribution $`\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)`$ (where
$`s=0`$) at temperature $`\tau>0`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{J}_{i}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(q)\right):=V_{i}\log\frac{q}{1-q}-\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(\tfrac{1}{2})\|\operatorname{Ber}(q)\right).
``` |  | (10) |

Choosing the forward-direction KL penalty from the behavior anchor
$`\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)`$ to the candidate teacher
$`\operatorname{Ber}(q)`$ is key to preserving tree credit across
depths. In log-odds coordinates, this KL penalty provides a smooth local
quadratic trust region around zero whose derivative is linear in $`q`$
and globally bounded in $`(-1/2,1/2)`$, so the concavity of
$`\mathcal{J}_{i}`$ yields a closed-form teacher probability $`q_{i}`$
that is affine in $`V_{i}`$, together with the target likelihood ratio
$`r_{i}^{\star}`$ (Proposition B.2 in
Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Calibrated affine targets on a fixed tree ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{i}:=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{V_{i}}{\tau},\qquad r_{i}^{\star}:=\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}=\frac{\tau+2V_{i}}{\tau-2V_{i}}.
``` |  | (11) |

Positive credit places the target above the behavior policy
($`q_{i}>1/2`$, $`r_{i}^{\star}>1`$), negative credit places it below
($`q_{i}<1/2`$, $`r_{i}^{\star}<1`$), and zero credit leaves the
behavior policy unchanged ($`q_{i}=1/2`$, $`r_{i}^{\star}=1`$).

To ensure that every turn target remains strictly valid and bounded
without ad-hoc clipping, we calibrate $`\tau`$ from the group size
$`K`$. By Samuelson’s inequality ([Samuelson, 1968](#bib.bib65)), the
standardized terminal advantages in
Eq. [4](#S2.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
are bounded by $`(K-1)/\sqrt{K}`$, and the convex tree backup in
Eq. [5](#S2.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
preserves this bound at every internal node. Setting
$`\tau=(K-1)/(\rho\sqrt{K})`$ for a target radius $`\rho\in(0,1/2)`$
confines every $`q_{i}`$ to $`[1/2-\rho,1/2+\rho]`$ and every target
ratio to the depth-independent interval

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{i}^{\star}\in\left[\frac{1-2\rho}{1+2\rho},\,\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\right].
``` |  | (12) |

Moreover, as proved in
Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Calibrated affine targets on a fixed tree ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
and validated in
Section [4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Pointwise credit and the posterior map ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"),
the affine teacher in
Eq. [11](#S3.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 3.2 Teacher construction ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
is the unique symmetric radius-calibrated map that commutes with convex
tree backup.

### 3.3  Distribution matching and off-policy analysis

With the teacher $`Q_{i}^{\star}=\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i})`$ frozen at
rollout, the second step in
Eq. [7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
projects the evolving student
$`P_{\theta,i}=\operatorname{Ber}(p_{\theta,i})`$ onto $`Q_{i}^{\star}`$
across sequential mini-batches. A principled choice of projection
divergence $`D`$ follows directly from the proximal objective in
Eq. [10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Teacher construction ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"):
by Bregman and Fenchel–Young duality ([Banerjee et al.,
2005](#bib.bib67); [Blondel et al., 2020](#bib.bib63)), the optimality
gap between $`Q_{i}^{\star}`$ and any student $`P_{\theta,i}`$ is
exactly the Forward KL divergence
$`\mathcal{J}_{i}(Q_{i}^{\star})-\mathcal{J}_{i}(P_{\theta,i})=\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{i}^{\star}\|P_{\theta,i})`$
([Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib47); [Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)).
Weighting each turn’s Forward KL by $`L_{i}/L_{x}`$ to match the token
normalization in
Eq. [6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
yields the TreeOPSD loss:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta):=\frac{\tau}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}L_{i}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(Q_{i}^{\star}\|P_{\theta,i}\right)=\frac{1}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}L_{i}\left[-V_{i}s_{\theta,i}+\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(\tfrac{1}{2})\|P_{\theta,i}\right)\right]+C_{x},
``` |  | (13) |

where $`C_{x}`$ is independent of $`\theta`$. The second expression in
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
shows why Forward-KL matching stabilizes off-policy updates: every step
that reduces the distillation loss $`\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta)`$
monotonically increases the tree-weighted proximal RL objective
(Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 On-policy tangent and off-policy drift analysis ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")),
and the loss combines linear credit ascent $`-V_{i}s_{\theta,i}`$ with a
convex restoring penalty around $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ that
activates only as $`\pi_{\theta}`$ drifts away from the behavior policy.

We now establish two structural theorems showing how
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
preserves exact turn-level credit across the tree
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"))
and corrects off-policy drift across sequential mini-batches
(Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")).

overshoot, whereas fixed credit keeps pushing in the initial direction.

###### Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation).

Let $`\mathbb{E}_{k\mid i}`$ denote the expectation over terminal leaves
$`i_{k}`$ below turn $`i`$ under the recursive backup weights in
Eq. [5](#S2.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").
For any student probability $`p_{\theta,i}\in(0,1)`$, the leaf-level
Forward KL losses decompose as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{k\mid i}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i_{k}})\|\operatorname{Ber}(p_{\theta,i})\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{k\mid i}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i_{k}})\|\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i})\right)\right]+D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i})\|\operatorname{Ber}(p_{\theta,i})\right).
``` |  | (14) |

Because the first term on the right-hand side is independent of
$`\theta`$, matching the single backed-up parent teacher
$`Q_{i}^{\star}=\operatorname{Ber}(q_{i})`$ yields the exact same
gradient on turn $`i`$ as aggregating the distillation losses of all
descendant leaves below $`i`$. Equivalently, the affine map
$`q_{i}=1/2+V_{i}/\tau`$ commutes with tree backup and coincides with
the Forward-KL barycenter ([Banerjee et al., 2005](#bib.bib67)) of its
child teachers:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{i}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{V_{i}}{\tau}=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{i}}\omega_{i,c}q_{c}=\arg\min_{q\in(0,1)}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{i}}\omega_{i,c}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(q_{c})\|\operatorname{Ber}(q)\right).
``` |  | (15) |

Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
guarantees lossless credit compression on the rollout tree: the parent
target $`q_{i}`$ is identical whether descendant credits are mapped
before backup or the backed-up credit $`V_{i}`$ is mapped afterward
(Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")(a)),
allowing
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
to score each unique turn once without repeating shared prefixes across
leaves. Proofs are given in
Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Calibrated affine targets on a fixed tree ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")–[B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2 Exact Forward-KL aggregation ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").

###### Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction).

For every token $`j`$ in turn $`i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}`$, differentiating
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
with respect to $`\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})`$ yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{x}}{\partial\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})}=\frac{\tau}{L_{x}}(p_{\theta,i}-q_{i})=-\frac{1}{L_{x}}\left[V_{i}-\tau\left(p_{\theta,i}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right].
``` |  | (16) |

Here the bracketed ascent weight $`\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i})`$ subtracts
an off-policy drift correction from the on-policy credit $`V_{i}`$.
Expressed in terms of the current and target likelihood ratios
$`r_{\theta,i}`$ and $`r_{i}^{\star}`$, it takes the self-normalized
form

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i}):=V_{i}-\tau\left(p_{\theta,i}-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\tau\frac{r_{i}^{\star}-r_{\theta,i}}{(1+r_{i}^{\star})(1+r_{\theta,i})}.
``` |  | (17) |

Across sequential mini-batch updates, the ascent gradient
$`-\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta)`$ satisfies three properties:

1.  (i)
    Exact on-policy tangent: At the behavior policy
    $`\pi_{\theta}=\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, we have
    $`r_{\theta,i}=1`$ and $`p_{\theta,i}=1/2`$, so the drift correction
    vanishes and
    $`-\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})=g_{x}^{\mathrm{tree}}`$
    exactly recovers
    Eq. [6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").
2.  (ii)
    Bounded additive drift cancellation: As $`\pi_{\theta}`$ drifts away
    from $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ on subsequent mini-batches, the
    additive correction $`-\tau(p_{\theta,i}-1/2)`$ counteracts the
    policy shift, remaining Lipschitz in $`s_{\theta,i}`$ and uniformly
    bounded for all $`r_{\theta,i}\in(0,\infty)`$ without the
    exponential variance of multiplicative importance ratios ([Ionides,
    2008](#bib.bib66); [Liu et al., 2018](#bib.bib68)).
3.  (iii)
    Finite-target equilibrium and sign reversal:
    $`\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i})`$ vanishes once $`r_{\theta,i}`$ reaches
    $`r_{i}^{\star}`$ and reverses sign if earlier mini-batches push the
    turn past $`r_{i}^{\star}`$, whereas fixed-credit updates keep
    $`V_{i}`$ unchanged regardless of drift.

Figures [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")(b)–(c)
illustrate these dynamics, and
Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 On-policy tangent and off-policy drift analysis ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
proves
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
along with scalar contraction and parameter-level drift bounds between
TreeOPSD and fixed-credit updates.

### 3.4  Practical optimization

Algorithm [1](#algorithm1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 3.4 Practical optimization ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
implements
Eq. [7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
in two stages per training iteration. We first freeze the behavior
policy $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, sample a batch $`\mathcal{B}`$
of $`B`$ prompts, and partition it into $`E`$ disjoint mini-batches
$`\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{e=1}^{E}\mathcal{B}_{e}`$, corresponding to $`E`$
sequential optimizer blocks. In the target-construction stage, we
generate the $`(M,N)`$ rollout tree $`\mathcal{T}_{x}`$, compute
backed-up credits $`V_{i}`$, and set the frozen teachers
$`q_{i}=1/2+V_{i}/\tau`$ for all $`x\in\mathcal{B}`$. In the
distribution-matching stage, each block $`e=1,\ldots,E`$ evaluates its
mini-batch $`\mathcal{B}_{e}`$ under the current policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$, recomputes the off-policy scores $`s_{\theta,i}`$ and
posteriors $`p_{\theta,i}`$, and backpropagates the token loss-gradient
weights
$`\tau(p_{\theta,i}-q_{i})/L_{x}=-\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i})/L_{x}`$ to
update $`\theta`$.

Input: Prompt dataset $`\mathcal{D}`$; initial policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$;
prompt batch size $`B`$; initial-rollout count $`M`$; branch count
$`N`$; optimizer-block count $`E`$; target radius $`\rho`$.

Output: Updated policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$.

foreach *training iteration* do

$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}\leftarrow\operatorname{stopgrad}(\pi_{\theta})`$;

partition into disjoint mini-batches
$`\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{e=1}^{E}\mathcal{B}_{e}`$;

   Set $`K=M(N+1)`$ and $`\tau=(K-1)/(\rho\sqrt{K})`$;

   foreach *prompt $`x\in\mathcal{B}`$* do

      Sample and retain $`M`$ complete rollouts
$`\mathcal{H}_{m,T_{m}}`$ from
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x)`$;

         Draw a multiset $`\mathcal{A}_{m}=\{t_{m,1},\ldots,t_{m,N}\}`$
of $`N`$ branch anchors from rollout $`m`$;

         foreach *$`t_{m,n}\in\mathcal{A}_{m}`$* do

            Sample one fresh terminal suffix $`S_{m,n}`$ from prefix
$`\mathcal{H}_{m,t_{m,n}}`$;

terminal trajectories;

$`V_{i}`$ by
Eq. [5](#S2.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning");

$`p_{\theta,i}=\sigma(s_{\theta,i})`$ under the current policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$;

$`\tau(p_{\theta,i}-q_{i})/L_{x}`$ to every actor token in turn $`i`$;

## 4  Experiments

### 4.1  Experimental setup

##### Datasets and models.

We evaluate TreeOPSD on two representative multi-turn settings.
(1) Multi-Hop QA: trains on the HotpotQA (Hotpot) training split ([Yang
et al., 2018](#bib.bib26)) and evaluates on its validation split,
2WikiMultiHopQA (2Wiki) ([Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib27)), MuSiQue
([Trivedi et al., 2022](#bib.bib28)), and Bamboogle ([Press et al.,
2023](#bib.bib29)) with a local E5 retrieval server ([Wang et al.,
2022](#bib.bib24)) built over a Wikipedia dump ([Karpukhin et al.,
2020](#bib.bib25)). (2) Deep Research: trains on the 6,102 unique
questions released with OpenResearcher ([Li et al., 2026d](#bib.bib34))
and evaluates on BrowseComp-Plus (BC+) ([Chen et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib30)) with closed-corpus retrieval, then tests open-web
transfer on BrowseComp (BC) ([Wei et al., 2025](#bib.bib31)), GAIA
([Mialon et al., 2024](#bib.bib32)), and xbench-DeepSearch (xbench)
([Chen et al., 2025a](#bib.bib33)). Main experiments use
Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 ([Yang et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib35)).

##### Baselines.

We compare TreeOPSD with four baselines. (1) ReAct ([Yao et al.,
2022](#bib.bib4)): evaluates the base model in the same multi-turn agent
and tool scaffold. (2) GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)): normalizes
terminal rewards over complete rollouts from each prompt. (3) GSPO
([Zheng et al., 2025b](#bib.bib16)): replaces token-level importance
ratios with a sequence-level ratio while retaining group-relative
terminal advantages. (4) TreePO: uses the same initialize-then-expand
trees, likelihood-weighted turn credits $`V_{i}`$, prompt normalization,
and optimizer blocks as TreeOPSD, but applies the fixed ascent
coefficient $`V_{i}/L_{x}`$ throughout optimization instead of matching
a finite posterior target. GRPO and GSPO use $`G=8`$ rollouts per
prompt, and both tree methods use $`M=4`$ initial rollouts and $`N=2`$
continuations per initial rollout to match that budget under
expectation.

##### Metrics.

We report exact match (EM) in both settings. “Avg” is the unweighted
mean over HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle for
Multi-Hop QA, and over BrowseComp-Plus, BrowseComp, GAIA, and
xbench-DeepSearch for Deep Research. We evaluate every five updates
through step 80 on Multi-Hop QA and step 70 on BrowseComp-Plus.
Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Experimental and implementation details ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
gives the data splits, rewards, training settings, and evaluation
protocol.

### 4.3  Pointwise credit and the posterior map

##### Pointwise and listwise credit.

Listwise updates center credit inside the sampled group, which removes
the value shared by every child. TreeOPSD uses that child backup
$`V_{c}`$ as pointwise turn credit, so two useful children can both rise
relative to the behavior policy.
Figure [5](#S4.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 4.2 Main results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")

Panels (a) and (b) replace $`V_{c}`$ with the group-relative credit
$`V_{c}-V_{i}`$. These centered credits average to zero under the backup
weights, so the same two useful children can receive opposite updates.
Panel (a) has a parent with two useful children. Matching $`V_{c}`$
raises both, while $`V_{c}-V_{i}`$ leaves their total probability near
the behavior policy. Panel (b) trains both on BrowseComp-Plus with

control.

##### Odds and logistic teachers.

Panels (c) and (d) keep $`V_{c}`$ and change how it becomes a Bernoulli
teacher.
Eq. [9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.1 Behavior-relative turn distributions ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
uses the odds map $`p=r/(1+r)`$, and the matching teacher
$`q=1/2+V/\tau`$ is affine in $`V`$. A parent teacher is then the
weighted mean of its child teachers, so tree backup and target
construction commute. The logistic map $`q=\sigma(V/\tau^{\prime})`$ is
also a Bernoulli teacher. Its scale $`\tau^{\prime}`$ can match the
first tree-policy-gradient update, but its parent teacher is then not
the weighted mean of its child teachers.

### 4.4  Rollout allocation and target-radius sensitivity

##### Rollout allocation.

Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Odds and logistic teachers. ‣ 4.3 Pointwise credit and the posterior map ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")(a)
varies the initialize-then-expand split at the default target radius,
comparing independent trajectories with prefix continuations from
visited histories. The rows $`(8,0)`$, $`(4,2)`$, and $`(2,6)`$ match
GRPO’s expected sampling cost, and $`(4,0)`$ and $`(2,2)`$ use half of
that budget. TreeOPSD recovers tree policy gradient at the behavior
policy, so the flat $`(8,0)`$ tree is the closest first-order control to
GRPO, and the two methods stay close on Multi-Hop QA. Moving the same
budget onto prefix continuations at $`(4,2)`$ raises both averages, and
it also outperforms the more continuation-heavy $`(2,6)`$ split. At half
budget, $`(2,2)`$ improves over flat $`(4,0)`$, and on Deep Research it
matches full-budget $`(8,0)`$, so a moderate number of prefix
continuations is a better use of that budget than extra independent
trajectories or a continuation-heavy split.

##### Target radius.

Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Odds and logistic teachers. ‣ 4.3 Pointwise credit and the posterior map ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")(b)
keeps the default tree and varies $`\rho`$, which sets how far each
frozen Bernoulli teacher may move from $`1/2`$. The first optimizer
block does not depend on $`\rho`$, because the distillation gradient
recovers tree policy gradient at the behavior policy. Later blocks close
the gap to a teacher whose distance from $`\tfrac{1}{2}`$ is set by
$`\rho`$, so the sweep measures those later updates. Both averages peak
at the default radius, and Deep Research moves more with $`\rho`$ than
Multi-Hop QA, but accuracy remains close to that peak across the full
radius sweep.

## 5  Conclusion

We presented TreeOPSD, a critic-free posterior self-distillation
framework for long-horizon agentic RLVR. It backs sparse terminal
rewards into implicit turn-level credit on a shared-prefix tree and
mitigates policy drift by matching a finite behavior-relative teacher
with Forward KL. The affine target commutes with tree backup, and the
distillation gradient recovers tree policy gradient at the behavior
policy, then stops or reverses after overshoot, so a rollout batch
supports sequential updates without a learned critic, process labels, or
an external teacher. TreeOPSD improves over matched-budget baselines at
both scales on Multi-Hop QA and Deep Research, and the gain is much
larger on the longer-horizon setting.

## Appendix A Related Work

##### RLVR and agentic RLVR.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib5); [Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib18); [Yu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib10)) trains language models from automatically checked
outcomes and has produced strong reasoning policies in mathematics,
coding, and other rule-based tasks. PPO remains a general
policy-gradient backbone ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib6)), while
GRPO removes the learned value model by estimating advantages from
response groups ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)). Later variants alter
the granularity of importance weighting ([Zheng et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib16)) or organize comparisons within larger groups ([Feng
et al., 2025](#bib.bib59)). RLVR has also moved from complete reasoning
responses to agents that interleave language, tools, and environment
observations ([Yao et al., 2022](#bib.bib4)). In this setting, a single
terminal advantage supervises a sequence of search queries, tool calls,
and answer decisions ([Jin et al., 2025](#bib.bib57); [Zheng et al.,
2025d](#bib.bib58); [Dong et al., 2026](#bib.bib21)). TreeOPSD focuses
on this turn-structured regime and derives intermediate targets directly
from verifiable outcomes rather than introducing a learned critic or
process labels.

##### Tree search and tree-based policy optimization.

Tree search lets language models compare continuations from a shared
history instead of drawing only independent complete trajectories. At
inference time, Tree-of-Thought ([Yao et al., 2023](#bib.bib36)),
language-agent search ([Zhou et al., 2023](#bib.bib37)), and MCTS-style
procedures ([Feng et al., 2023](#bib.bib56)) use branching to improve
planning or answer selection. Offline methods turn reasoning trees into
supervised or preference data ([Lai et al., 2024](#bib.bib17)), while
online methods bring the tree into RL for exploration, value estimation,
local comparisons, or rollout allocation ([Hou et al.,
2025](#bib.bib19); [Ji et al., 2026](#bib.bib20); [Yang et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib38); [Zong et al., 2026](#bib.bib22); [Dong et al.,
2026](#bib.bib21); [Zou et al., 2026a](#bib.bib23)). These approaches
differ in where branches are collected and how sibling outcomes become
gradients. TreeOPSD takes a shared-prefix tree as input, backs terminal
evidence into turn credit, and converts that credit into a finite target
for the current policy. Its contribution lies in optimizing the acquired
tree rather than choosing where the tree should branch.

##### Sample-efficient RLVR and rollout allocation.

A parallel line of work reduces rollout cost by deciding which examples
receive sampling budget. Offline curation fixes the training set before
optimization ([Ye et al., 2025](#bib.bib84); [Li et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib85); [Wang et al., 2025](#bib.bib86)). Online methods
filter prompts whose rollouts are uninformative ([Yu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib10); [Bae et al., 2026](#bib.bib87)) or predict prompt
difficulty before sampling ([Zheng et al., 2025c](#bib.bib88); [Qu et
al., 2025](#bib.bib79); [Gao et al., 2025](#bib.bib80); [Mao et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib71); [Qu et al., 2026b](#bib.bib72)), much as automated
model evaluation predicts test accuracy from unlabeled data ([Peng et
al., 2024](#bib.bib91)). POPO replays effective groups from earlier
policies in place of zero-variance groups ([Mao et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib73)), and budget allocation varies the number of rollouts
per prompt ([Li et al., 2025b](#bib.bib89); [Yao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib90)). In supervised fine-tuning, online batch selection
scores samples by utility and diversity ([Zou et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib74)). TRACE extends allocation from prompt roots to
turn-level prefixes with a shared success predictor ([Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23)). These methods decide which rollouts to acquire, and
TreeOPSD decides how an acquired tree updates the policy, so the two
compose without changing the teacher in
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Teacher construction ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").

##### Credit assignment under sparse rewards.

Credit assignment under delayed rewards is a long-standing RL problem.
Temporal-difference learning ([Sutton et al., 1998](#bib.bib7)),
potential-based shaping ([Ng et al., 1999](#bib.bib55)), and return
decomposition ([Arjona-Medina et al., 2019](#bib.bib8)) provide denser
signals by estimating state values or redistributing terminal returns.
For language-model reasoning, process supervision and process reward
models score intermediate steps ([Uesato et al., 2022](#bib.bib9);
[Lightman et al., 2024](#bib.bib39); [Wang et al., 2024](#bib.bib60)),
and recent work derives progress signals from Monte Carlo continuations,
answer likelihoods, or implicit process objectives ([Setlur et al.,
2025](#bib.bib61); [Yuan et al., 2024](#bib.bib62)). Long-horizon LLM
methods further use trajectory graphs ([Li et al., 2026a](#bib.bib42)),
vine-style continuations ([Kazemnejad et al., 2025](#bib.bib43)), or
alternative reward aggregation ([Cheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib44)) to
localize responsibility within a rollout. Rollout trees ([Ji et al.,
2026](#bib.bib20); [Zong et al., 2026](#bib.bib22); [Yang et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib38)) provide a related source of counterfactual evidence
because sibling suffixes keep the prefix fixed while varying the future.
TreeOPSD backs this evidence through the sampled tree and preserves
value shared by multiple useful continuations instead of removing it
through sibling centering.

##### Behavior-relative targets and posterior distillation.

KL-regularized RL ([Ouyang et al., 2022](#bib.bib11)),
maximum-a-posteriori policy optimization ([Abdolmaleki et al.,
2018](#bib.bib51)), and preference optimization ([Rafailov et al.,
2023](#bib.bib12); [Meng et al., 2024](#bib.bib14)) improve a policy
while anchoring it to a reference distribution. Mirror-descent and
mirror-ascent analyses make the corresponding target-and-projection
structure explicit ([Beck and Teboulle, 2003](#bib.bib52); [Vaswani et
al., 2022](#bib.bib53); [Asad et al., 2025](#bib.bib54)), and recent
work applies this view to group-based language-model updates on finite
response sets ([Qu et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)). On-policy distillation
instead trains on student-generated samples using a teacher distribution
([Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib47); [Gu et al., 2024](#bib.bib48);
[Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib45); [Kaur et al., 2026](#bib.bib50)) or
privileged continuation information ([Nguyen et al., 2026](#bib.bib46)).
TreeOPSD joins these perspectives at the turn level. It represents each
observed turn by a behavior-relative Bernoulli, related to relative
density-ratio estimation ([Yamada et al., 2013](#bib.bib1); [Cranmer et
al., 2015](#bib.bib2)) and contrastive source classification
([Goodfellow et al., 2014](#bib.bib3); [Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib15)),
then obtains a closed-form teacher from backed-up credit and matches it
with Forward KL. Independent turn targets avoid treating a sparse
sibling sample as a closed continuation simplex, while the finite
teacher supplies a destination that fixed-credit policy gradients do not
specify.

## Appendix B Theoretical Analysis

### B.1  Calibrated affine targets on a fixed tree

We condition on one prompt, its rollout tree, and the frozen behavior
policy that sampled that tree. The terminal advantages $`A_{k}`$,
backed-up turn credits $`V_{i}`$, and child weights $`\omega_{i,c}`$ are
fixed during the policy update. For $`K\geq 2`$ terminal trajectories
and target radius $`0<\rho<1/2`$, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
B_{K}:=\frac{K-1}{\sqrt{K}},\qquad\tau:=\frac{B_{K}}{\rho},\qquad q_{i}:=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{V_{i}}{\tau},\qquad\phi_{i}:=\operatorname{logit}(q_{i}).
``` |  | (18) |

##### Lemma B.1 (Depth-independent bound).

For every terminal occurrence $`k`$ and every backed-up turn $`i`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|A_{k}|\leq B_{K},\qquad|V_{i}|\leq B_{K},\qquad\left|q_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\rho.
``` |  | (19) |

##### Proof.

Let $`\xi_{k}:=R_{k}-\overline{R}`$ and let $`\widehat{\sigma}_{R}`$ be
the sample standard deviation of the $`K`$ terminal rewards. When
$`\widehat{\sigma}_{R}>0`$, we have $`\sum_{k}\xi_{k}=0`$ and
$`\sum_{k}\xi_{k}^{2}=(K-1)\widehat{\sigma}_{R}^{2}`$. Hence

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\xi_{k}^{2}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\ell\neq k}\xi_{\ell}\right)^{2}\leq(K-1)\sum_{\ell\neq k}\xi_{\ell}^{2}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(K-1)\left((K-1)\widehat{\sigma}_{R}^{2}-\xi_{k}^{2}\right),`$ |  | (20) |

which gives
$`|A_{k}|=|\xi_{k}|/\widehat{\sigma}_{R}\leq(K-1)/\sqrt{K}=B_{K}`$. This
is Samuelson’s inequality ([Samuelson, 1968](#bib.bib65)). Equality
holds when one reward differs from the other $`K-1`$ rewards, which are
equal. If $`\widehat{\sigma}_{R}=0`$, every $`A_{k}`$ is zero by
convention. Because each internal credit is a convex combination of its
children, $`|V_{i}|\leq B_{K}`$, and
Eq. [18](#A2.E18 "Equation 18 ‣ B.1 Calibrated affine targets on a fixed tree ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
gives the target-radius bound. $`\square`$

##### Backup-consistent target map.

Suppose a map $`f:[-B_{K},B_{K}]\to(0,1)`$ commutes with every finite
convex combination, satisfies $`f(-v)=1-f(v)`$, and maps $`B_{K}`$ to
$`1/2+\rho`$. Symmetry then maps $`-B_{K}`$ to $`1/2-\rho`$. Writing any
$`v\in[-B_{K},B_{K}]`$ as the convex interpolation

|     |                                                         |     |      |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       v=(1-t)(-B_{K})+tB_{K},\qquad t=\frac{v+B_{K}}{2B_{K}},
       ```                                                      |     | (21) |

then gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f(v)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\rho}{B_{K}}v=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{v}{\tau}.
``` |  | (22) |

Thus the affine teacher is the unique symmetric map with this radius.
Applying it before or after any normalized tree backup leaves the parent
target unchanged.

##### Proposition B.2 (Trust-region properties and closed-form teacher).

Parameterize any Bernoulli distribution $`\operatorname{Ber}(q)`$ by its
log-odds $`s=\log\frac{q}{1-q}`$, so that $`q=\sigma(s)`$ and
$`1-q=\sigma(-s)`$. The divergence from the on-policy behavior anchor
$`\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)`$ satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\psi(s):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(\tfrac{1}{2})\|\operatorname{Ber}(\sigma(s))\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\log\bigl(4\sigma(s)\sigma(-s)\bigr)=\log\cosh\frac{s}{2}.
``` |  | (23) |

The penalty $`\psi(s)`$ has three structural properties: (i) *Local
quadratic trust region:* $`\psi(0)=\psi^{\prime}(0)=0`$ and
$`\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)=\sigma(s)(1-\sigma(s))\in(0,1/4]`$, giving the
Taylor expansion
$`\psi(s)=\frac{1}{8}s^{2}-\frac{1}{192}s^{4}+\mathcal{O}(s^{6})`$
around the on-policy point $`s=0`$. (ii) *Globally bounded slope:* Its
derivative
$`\psi^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\tanh\frac{s}{2}=\sigma(s)-\frac{1}{2}=q-\frac{1}{2}\in(-1/2,1/2)`$
is linear in $`q`$ and uniformly bounded for all $`s\in\mathbb{R}`$.
(iii) *Unique affine maximizer:* Substituting $`\psi(s)`$ into
Eq. [10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Teacher construction ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
gives the strictly concave objective
$`\mathcal{J}_{i}(s)=V_{i}s-\tau\log\cosh(s/2)`$ with derivatives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\prime}(s)=V_{i}-\tau\left(\sigma(s)-\frac{1}{2}\right),\qquad\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\prime\prime}(s)=-\tau\sigma(s)(1-\sigma(s))<0.
``` |  | (24) |

Because $`\psi^{\prime}(s)=q-1/2`$ is linear in $`q`$, the stationary
condition $`\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\prime}(s_{i}^{\star})=0`$ yields the exact
affine teacher $`\sigma(s_{i}^{\star})=1/2+V_{i}/\tau=q_{i}`$ required
by
Eq. [22](#A2.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ Backup-consistent target map. ‣ B.1 Calibrated affine targets on a fixed tree ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
(whereas the exponential-tilting objective
$`V_{i}q-\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\operatorname{Ber}(q)\|\operatorname{Ber}(\tfrac{1}{2}))`$
yields a nonlinear logistic teacher $`q=\sigma(V_{i}/\tau)`$ that fails
to commute with tree backup). By Lemma B.1 and $`\rho<1/2`$, we have
$`\tau>2|V_{i}|`$, so $`s_{i}^{\star}=\phi_{i}`$ is interior and finite,
with target likelihood ratio

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{i}^{\star}=e^{s_{i}^{\star}}=\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}=\frac{\tau+2V_{i}}{\tau-2V_{i}}\in\left[\frac{1-2\rho}{1+2\rho},\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\right].\qquad\square
``` |  | (25) |

### B.2  Exact Forward-KL aggregation

Let $`\mathcal{D}_{i}`$ be the terminal descendants of turn $`i`$. For a
descendant $`u`$, let $`\alpha_{i,u}`$ be the product of the normalized
child weights along the unique path from $`i`$ to $`u`$. If
$`\mathcal{U}`$ is any complete cut below $`i`$, repeated application of
the backup in
Eq. [5](#S2.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 2.3 Tree-based turn credit ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}=1,\qquad V_{i}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}V_{u},\qquad q_{i}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}q_{u}.
``` |  | (26) |

The last identity follows from the affine credit-to-target map proved
above.

##### Proof of Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").

Write
$`d_{\mathrm{B}}(a\|b):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\operatorname{Ber}(a)\|\operatorname{Ber}(b))`$
and let $`H_{\mathrm{B}}`$ denote binary entropy. When every descendant
teacher on the cut is matched to the same ancestor probability
$`p_{\theta,i}\in(0,1)`$,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{u}\|p_{\theta,i})`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}H_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{u})-q_{i}\log p_{\theta,i}-(1-q_{i})\log(1-p_{\theta,i})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{u}\|q_{i})+d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{i}\|p_{\theta,i}).`$ |  | (27) |

The first term on the right is independent of $`\theta`$. Therefore

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{u}\|p_{\theta,i})=\nabla_{\theta}d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{i}\|p_{\theta,i}),\qquad\arg\min_{p\in(0,1)}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i,u}d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{u}\|p)=q_{i}.
``` |  | (28) |

Setting $`\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{D}_{i}`$ (or
$`\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{C}_{i}`$) yields
Eqs. [14](#S3.E14 "Equation 14 ‣ Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")–[15](#S3.E15 "Equation 15 ‣ Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").
Hence one backed-up teacher produces the exact same gradient on the
shared turn as all descendant teachers, while each descendant turn
retains its own policy score and target. $`\square`$

### B.3  On-policy tangent and off-policy drift analysis

##### Variational duality and Fenchel–Young form.

Define the convex log-partition potential and its Fenchel conjugate on
$`(-\tau/2,\tau/2)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Omega_{\tau}(s):=\tau\log\left(2\cosh\frac{s}{2}\right),\qquad\Omega_{\tau}^{\star}(V):=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\{Vs-\Omega_{\tau}(s)\}=-\tau H_{\mathrm{B}}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{V}{\tau}\right).
``` |  | (29) |

For $`q_{i}=q(V_{i})=1/2+V_{i}/\tau`$ and
$`p_{\theta,i}=\sigma(s_{\theta,i})`$, expanding
$`\tau d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{i}\|\sigma(s_{\theta,i}))`$ yields the
Fenchel–Young loss identity of [Blondel et al. (2020)](#bib.bib63):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tau d_{\mathrm{B}}\bigl(q(V_{i})\|\sigma(s_{\theta,i})\bigr)=\Omega_{\tau}(s_{\theta,i})+\Omega_{\tau}^{\star}(V_{i})-V_{i}s_{\theta,i}=\mathcal{J}_{i}(Q_{i}^{\star})-\mathcal{J}_{i}(P_{\theta,i}).
``` |  | (30) |

Summing
Eq. [30](#A2.E30 "Equation 30 ‣ Variational duality and Fenchel–Young form. ‣ B.3 On-policy tangent and off-policy drift analysis ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
across $`i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}`$ with weights $`L_{i}/L_{x}`$ proves the
second equality in
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").
Moreover, defining the tree-weighted proximal RL objective
$`\mathcal{J}_{x}(\theta):=\frac{1}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}L_{i}\mathcal{J}_{i}(P_{\theta,i})`$,
Eq. [30](#A2.E30 "Equation 30 ‣ Variational duality and Fenchel–Young form. ‣ B.3 On-policy tangent and off-policy drift analysis ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
implies that for any two parameter iterates $`\theta,\theta^{\prime}`$
on a fixed rollout batch,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{J}_{x}(\theta^{\prime})-\mathcal{J}_{x}(\theta)=\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta^{\prime}).
``` |  | (31) |

Thus every sequential mini-batch step that reduces the Forward-KL
distillation loss $`\mathcal{L}_{x}`$ monotonically improves the
proximal objective $`\mathcal{J}_{x}`$ by the exact same amount.

##### Proof of Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning").

For token $`j`$ in turn $`i`$, differentiating $`s_{\theta,i}`$ in
Eq. [8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ 3.1 Behavior-relative turn distributions ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
contributes a factor $`1/L_{i}`$, which cancels the turn-length weight
$`L_{i}`$ in
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{x}}{\partial\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\tau L_{i}}{L_{x}}\frac{\partial d_{\mathrm{B}}(q_{i}\|\sigma(s_{\theta,i}))}{\partial s_{\theta,i}}\frac{\partial s_{\theta,i}}{\partial\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})}=\frac{\tau L_{i}}{L_{x}}\bigl(p_{\theta,i}-q_{i}\bigr)\frac{1}{L_{i}}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{L_{x}}\left[V_{i}-\tau\left(p_{\theta,i}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right].`$ |  | (32) |

Define the off-policy effective ascent weight

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Gamma_{i}(s):=\tau\bigl(q_{i}-\sigma(s)\bigr)=V_{i}-\tau\left(\sigma(s)-\frac{1}{2}\right)=V_{i}-\frac{\tau}{2}\tanh\frac{s}{2}.
``` |  | (33) |

Substituting $`q_{i}=r_{i}^{\star}/(1+r_{i}^{\star})`$ and
$`\sigma(s_{\theta,i})=r_{\theta,i}/(1+r_{\theta,i})`$ gives the
self-normalized ratio form in
Eq. [17](#S3.E17 "Equation 17 ‣ Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i})=\tau\left(\frac{r_{i}^{\star}}{1+r_{i}^{\star}}-\frac{r_{\theta,i}}{1+r_{\theta,i}}\right)=\tau\frac{r_{i}^{\star}-r_{\theta,i}}{(1+r_{i}^{\star})(1+r_{\theta,i})}.
``` |  | (34) |

The three properties in
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
follow directly: (i) At $`\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}`$,
$`s_{\theta,i}=0`$ and $`\Gamma_{i}(0)=V_{i}`$, so

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left.-\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{x}(\theta)\right|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}=\frac{1}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}\sum_{j=1}^{L_{i}}V_{i}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})=g_{x}^{\mathrm{tree}}.
``` |  | (35) |

(ii) Because $`\sigma`$ is $`1/4`$-Lipschitz and $`\sigma(0)=1/2`$, the
additive drift correction satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|\Gamma_{i}(s)-V_{i}|=\tau\left|\sigma(s)-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\frac{\tau}{4}|s|,\qquad|\Gamma_{i}(s)|=\tau|q_{i}-\sigma(s)|\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\rho\right)\tau,
``` |  | (36) |

where the uniform bound uses $`q_{i}\in[1/2-\rho,1/2+\rho]`$ and
$`\sigma(s)\in(0,1)`$. Unlike unclipped importance-weighted coefficients
$`r_{\theta,i}V_{i}=e^{s_{\theta,i}}V_{i}`$, which grow exponentially
when $`s_{\theta,i}>0`$, $`\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i})`$ remains bounded
for all $`r_{\theta,i}\in(0,\infty)`$. (iii) Because $`\sigma`$ is
strictly increasing and $`q_{i}=\sigma(\phi_{i})`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Gamma_{i}(0)=V_{i},\qquad\Gamma_{i}(\phi_{i})=0,\qquad\operatorname{sign}\Gamma_{i}(s)=\operatorname{sign}(\phi_{i}-s)=\operatorname{sign}(r_{i}^{\star}-r_{\theta,i}).\qquad\square
``` |  | (37) |

##### Off-policy scalar contraction and parameter-drift bound.

By the mean-value theorem, for any score $`s\in\mathbb{R}`$ there exists
$`\xi`$ between $`s`$ and $`\phi_{i}`$ such that
$`\Gamma_{i}(s)=\tau\sigma^{\prime}(\xi)(\phi_{i}-s)`$ with
$`0<\sigma^{\prime}(\xi)\leq 1/4`$. A gradient step
$`s^{(t+1)}=s^{(t)}+\eta\frac{L_{i}}{L_{x}}\Gamma_{i}(s^{(t)})`$ on the
turn score therefore satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
s^{(t+1)}-\phi_{i}=\bigl(1-\kappa_{t}\bigr)\bigl(s^{(t)}-\phi_{i}\bigr),\qquad\kappa_{t}:=\eta\frac{\tau L_{i}}{L_{x}}\sigma^{\prime}(\xi_{t})\in\left(0,\frac{\eta\tau L_{i}}{4L_{x}}\right].
``` |  | (38) |

Whenever $`0<\eta\leq\frac{4L_{x}}{\tau L_{i}}`$, we have
$`\kappa_{t}\in(0,1]`$, so $`s^{(t)}`$ contracts monotonically toward
the target log-odds $`\phi_{i}=\log r_{i}^{\star}`$ without
overshooting. Finally, in parameter space, let
$`\bm{d}_{i}(\theta):=\sum_{j=1}^{L_{i}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i,j}\mid h_{i,j})`$
and compare the TreeOPSD update direction
$`\bm{g}_{x}^{\mathrm{OPSD}}(\theta)`$ with the fixed-credit direction
$`\bm{g}_{x}^{\mathrm{fixed}}(\theta)`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bm{g}_{x}^{\mathrm{OPSD}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}\Gamma_{i}(s_{\theta,i})\bm{d}_{i}(\theta),`$ | $`\displaystyle\bm{g}_{x}^{\mathrm{fixed}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}V_{i}\bm{d}_{i}(\theta).`$ |  | (39) |

Applying
Eq. [36](#A2.E36 "Equation 36 ‣ Proof of Theorem . ‣ B.3 On-policy tangent and off-policy drift analysis ‣ Appendix B Theoretical Analysis ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
gives the off-policy parameter-drift bound

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|\bm{g}_{x}^{\mathrm{OPSD}}(\theta)-\bm{g}_{x}^{\mathrm{fixed}}(\theta)\right\|\leq\frac{\tau}{4L_{x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}|s_{\theta,i}|\,\|\bm{d}_{i}(\theta)\|.
``` |  | (40) |

Thus TreeOPSD and fixed-credit optimization coincide at the on-policy
first mini-batch ($`s_{\theta,i}=0`$) and depart proportionally to the
off-policy score drift $`|s_{\theta,i}|`$ accumulated across subsequent
mini-batches.

## Appendix C Discussion

### C.1  Tree acquisition, credit assignment, and policy optimization

Tree-based agent RL involves three decisions that are often coupled in
one algorithm. A rollout procedure first decides which prompts or
prefixes receive continuations. A credit-assignment rule then maps
terminal outcomes on that tree to turn values. Policy optimization uses
those values to update the current LLM. TreeOPSD handles the last step:
it takes a sampled tree and normalized child weights, converts the
backed-up values into behavior-relative targets, and matches the current
policy to those targets. The same target construction can be paired with
random branching, learned rollout allocation, or other tree samplers.

### C.2  Independent turn targets and pairwise preferences

A categorical distribution over sampled siblings is invariant to a
common shift of their logits or rewards. It can rank the observed
continuations, but it removes value shared by all of them. This is
appropriate when the sampled set is treated as a closed choice set. In
an LLM, however, a few sampled siblings cover only a small part of the
continuation space. If every observed sibling is useful relative to the
behavior policy, a sibling-normalized update cannot raise them together.
TreeOPSD instead assigns each observed turn its own behavior-relative
Bernoulli target, allowing the shared policy to increase all useful
turns while retaining their relative differences. Each target describes
where one observed turn should move relative to its behavior likelihood.
These targets do not form a normalized action distribution over the full
vocabulary or continuation space.

Step-level preference optimization is a boundary case. For two siblings
with centered credits $`V_{+}=v`$ and $`V_{-}=-v`$, the TreeOPSD
behavior-policy tangent is proportional to the usual pairwise direction
$`\nabla\log\pi(a^{+})-\nabla\log\pi(a^{-})`$. The objectives differ
after that initial point. A hard pairwise preference keeps enlarging the
preferred margin, whereas TreeOPSD assigns both turns finite soft
targets and stops when those targets are reached. When the pair has
nonzero mean credit, TreeOPSD also retains the common update that
pairwise centering removes.

### C.3  Finite-target feedback under sequential optimization

Rollout batches are generated by a frozen behavior policy, but later
optimizer blocks run after earlier blocks have already changed the
shared LLM parameters. A fixed-credit surrogate continues to apply the
collection-time coefficient $`V_{i}`$ even when the likelihood of turn
$`i`$ has already moved. TreeOPSD keeps the teacher fixed and recomputes
the student probability, replacing $`V_{i}`$ by the residual
$`\Gamma_{i}=\tau(q_{i}-p_{\theta,i})`$. The first block therefore
follows the tree-policy-gradient tangent. Later blocks reduce, stop, or
reverse the update according to the current student–teacher gap.

This feedback differs from importance-ratio clipping. Clipping limits
how strongly a sampled action can move, but it does not specify a
destination. The TreeOPSD teacher supplies that destination in a
behavior-relative coordinate. The radius $`\rho`$ bounds the target
probability and, with it, the geometric-mean likelihood ratio of the
observed turn. It sets the finite target that later blocks pursue after
updates on other prompts have already moved the shared policy.

### C.4  Limitations and future work

##### Allocation-aware teachers.

Theorems [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
and [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (On-policy tangent and off-policy drift correction). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
condition on the sampled tree, so TreeOPSD can train on trees from
learned allocators such as DPS ([Mao et al., 2026a](#bib.bib71)), GPS
([Qu et al., 2026b](#bib.bib72)), and TRACE ([Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23)), while its teachers still come only from verifier
outcomes and behavior likelihoods. The allocator also sets the scale of
those teachers. Under the Samuelson calibration, the root-mean-square of
$`q_{i_{k}}-1/2`$ over the $`K`$ leaves equals $`\rho/\sqrt{K-1}`$
whenever the rewards are not all equal, about $`0.075`$ at the default
$`\rho=0.25`$ and $`K=12`$, and only a tree with a single dissenting
reward reaches the full radius. Allocators that favor mixed outcomes
move trees toward balanced rewards, and budget allocators vary $`K`$
across prompts ([Li et al., 2025b](#bib.bib89); [Yao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib90)). A radius set from the realized credits or from $`K`$
would let allocation and teacher scale be designed together. It keeps
both theorems, since the first-block tangent does not depend on $`\tau`$
and
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Tree-consistent targets and descendant loss aggregation). ‣ 3.3 Distribution matching and off-policy analysis ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
only needs every $`q_{i}`$ in $`(0,1)`$.

##### Tree replay.

Each tree currently serves $`E=4`$ optimizer blocks before it is
discarded. POPO saves rollouts by replaying effective groups from
earlier policies in place of zero-variance groups ([Mao et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib73)), and the same replay fits TreeOPSD. A target ratio
$`r_{i}^{\star}`$ is defined relative to the policy that collected its
tree, so a replayed teacher still satisfies
Eq. [12](#S3.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ 3.2 Teacher construction ‣ 3 TreeOPSD ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning"),
and the residual $`\tau(q_{i}-p_{\theta,i})`$ shrinks or reverses on
turns that the current policy has already moved toward or past their
targets. The residual also scores stored turns with one forward pass,
which could drive utility- and diversity-aware batch selection over a
replay buffer ([Zou et al., 2025a](#bib.bib74)). Since each $`V_{i}`$
averages continuations from the collecting policy, replayed credit ages
as the policy improves, and weighting stored trees by their
off-policiness, as POPO does for groups, would account for this aging.

##### Continual agentic post-training.

A tree whose rewards all coincide has $`q_{i}=1/2`$ at every turn, so in
later blocks it pulls its own turns back toward
$`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, much as Learning without Forgetting
distills the previous model’s outputs ([Li and Hoiem,
2017](#bib.bib82)). On-policy RL forgets less than SFT, and its
forgetting tracks the KL shift on the new task ([Shenfeld et al.,
2026](#bib.bib92)). TreeOPSD shares the on-policy sampling behind this
result, and its teachers also stay within $`\rho`$ of $`1/2`$. RL
post-training still forgets across task sequences, however, because KL
measured on current-task data does not control drift on prior-task
distributions ([Luo et al., 2026](#bib.bib93)). Mixing a few stored
trees from earlier environments, together with their logged behavior
probabilities, into later batches would extend the $`q_{i}=1/2`$ anchor
to prior tasks without new rollouts. On the parameterization side,
continual-learning methods mitigate the stability–plasticity dilemma
through parameter regularization ([Kirkpatrick et al.,
2017](#bib.bib81)), fly-olfactory-circuit designs ([Zou et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib75); [Zou et al., 2026b](#bib.bib76)), and guided random
projections ([Li et al., 2026b](#bib.bib77)). FlyLoRA limits inter-task
interference in LoRA ([Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib83)) by using a frozen
sparse random projection as an implicit router over rank-wise experts
([Zou et al., 2025c](#bib.bib78)), and TreeOPSD applies to such
parameterizations unchanged because it only reweights token
log-likelihood gradients. The residual already accounts for drift among
prompts within a batch
(Appendix [C.3](#A3.SS3 "C.3 Finite-target feedback under sequential optimization ‣ Appendix C Discussion ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")),
and a decorrelated parameterization would limit interference across
environments that never share a batch.

## Appendix E Experimental and implementation details

We follow Search-R1 ([Jin et al., 2025](#bib.bib57); [Zou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib23)) for Multi-Hop QA and OpenResearcher ([Li et al.,
2026d](#bib.bib34); [Tao et al., 2026](#bib.bib64)) for Deep Research.

### E.1  Datasets, environments, and rewards

#### E.1.1  Multi-Hop QA

##### Training data and environment.

We use all 90,447 questions from the HotpotQA distractor training split
([Yang et al., 2018](#bib.bib26)). The agent searches a local E5
retrieval server ([Wang et al., 2022](#bib.bib24)) built over a
Wikipedia dump ([Karpukhin et al., 2020](#bib.bib25)). Each tool call
accepts up to four queries and returns the top three passages for each
query. We cap a rollout at five assistant turns, including the final
answer, which leaves at most four tool-calling turns.

##### Evaluation benchmarks.

We evaluate on the 7,405-question HotpotQA validation split, the
12,576-question 2WikiMultiHopQA development split ([Ho et al.,
2020](#bib.bib27)), the 2,417-question MuSiQue development split
([Trivedi et al., 2022](#bib.bib28)), and all 125 Bamboogle test
questions ([Press et al., 2023](#bib.bib29)).

##### Reward function.

Training uses thresholded token F1. A parsed answer receives its
token-level F1 when that score is at least $`0.3`$ and receives zero
otherwise. Validation records both F1 and exact match.
Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4.2 Main results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-HorizonAgentic Reinforcement Learning")
and the training curves use exact match (EM).

#### E.1.2  Deep Research

##### Training data and environment.

OpenResearcher provides 16 synthesized trajectories for each of 6,102
MiroVerse questions ([Li et al., 2026d](#bib.bib34)). We keep one prompt
per unique question and do not place the released teacher responses in
the policy input. Training and BrowseComp-Plus ([Chen et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib30)) evaluation use closed-corpus retrieval. BrowseComp
([Wei et al., 2025](#bib.bib31)), GAIA ([Mialon et al.,
2024](#bib.bib32)), and xbench-DeepSearch ([Chen et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib33)) use the same search, open, and find tools with a
Serper backend.

##### Evaluation benchmarks.

Final evaluation covers all 830 BrowseComp-Plus test questions, 1,266
BrowseComp test questions, 103 text-only questions from the GAIA
development split, and 100 xbench-DeepSearch test questions. To limit
temporal variation in live search, we interleave the open-web methods
within the same evaluation window and archive the raw search responses
with each generation.

##### Reward function.

The training reward is exact match, with a $`0.1`$ bonus for a
well-formed final-answer span.

### E.3  Training and evaluation settings

##### Optimization and systems.

All runs use bfloat16 and SGLang on one eight-GPU node. Qwen3-4B uses
FSDP. Qwen3-30B-A3B uses FSDP for Multi-Hop QA and FSDP2 for Deep
Research, with rollout tensor parallelism of four on 96GB H20 GPUs. We

$`10^{-6}`$, and weight decay $`0.01`$. The Adam coefficients are
$`(0.9,0.999)`$ for Multi-Hop QA and $`(0.9,0.98)`$ for Deep Research.
We use no auxiliary KL penalty or entropy regularization, and sample
training rollouts with temperature $`1.0`$ and top-$`p`$ $`1.0`$.

##### Task-specific settings.

For Multi-Hop QA, collection uses 256 prompts split into four optimizer
blocks of 64. Prompt and response lengths are capped at 2,048 and 6,144
tokens, retrieved observations at 16,384 characters, and each rollout at
five assistant turns including the answer. Evaluation decodes one answer
per question every five updates with temperature $`0.6`$ and top-$`p`$
$`0.95`$. For Deep Research, collection uses 128 prompts split into four
blocks of 32, with prompt, response, and tool-observation limits of
4,096, 16,384, and 2,048 tokens. Training allows 60 assistant turns.
BrowseComp-Plus evaluation allows 80 and greedily decodes one trajectory
per question every five updates.

We evaluate every five policy updates through step 80 on Multi-Hop QA
and step 70 on BrowseComp-Plus, and report one checkpoint per run in
that window. In

the serialized trajectory stores two chat entries per assistant

200 assistant turns, and a 131,072-token response budget. The reported
average weights the four datasets equally.

### E.4  Baselines and rollout-budget control

##### Controlled methods.

ReAct ([Yao et al., 2022](#bib.bib4)) evaluates the shared
initialization without parameter updates. GRPO ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib5)) standardizes terminal rewards over complete rollouts
from each prompt. GSPO ([Zheng et al., 2025b](#bib.bib16)) replaces
token-level importance ratios with a sequence-level ratio while
retaining group-relative terminal advantages. TreePO uses the same
initialize-then-expand trees, likelihood-weighted credits $`V_{i}`$,
unique-turn reconstruction, prompt normalization, and optimizer blocks
as TreeOPSD, but applies the fixed ascent coefficient $`V_{i}/L_{x}`$
throughout optimization instead of matching a finite posterior target.
Every trainable method applies four actor optimizer blocks per collected
prompt batch.

##### Rollout budget.

GRPO and GSPO use $`G=8`$ rollouts per prompt. TreePO and TreeOPSD begin
with $`M=4`$ complete trajectories, select $`N=2`$ branch-anchor
occurrences from each, and sample one fresh terminal suffix per
occurrence. Keeping the roots gives $`M(N+1)=12`$ terminal leaves. A
continuation randomly starts from an intermediate prefix, so its
expected generation length is roughly half that of a root rollout, and
the expected cost $`M(1+N/2)=8`$ matches $`G`$.

### F.2  Deep Research (OpenResearcher)

RL construction reads one OpenResearcher record per unique question and
discards the teacher messages ([Li et al., 2026d](#bib.bib34)). All
reported runs present only the question as the user turn, together with
the system string below. The training row routes browser.search,
browser.open, and browser.find to the fixed OpenResearcher corpus.
Evaluation rows keep the same interface and select the fixed
BrowseComp-Plus corpus or the Serper backend.
````
