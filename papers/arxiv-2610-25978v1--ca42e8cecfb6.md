# GraphEvoResearcher: Graph-Aware Training-Free Self-Reflective Reasoning for Cost-Bounded Multi-Hop Question Answering

Suxing Liu Affiliation: Department of Computing, Universiti Sains
Malaysia

October 19, 2026

###### Abstract

Multi-hop question answering (QA) requires models to chain intermediate
conclusions while remaining cost-aware. Existing training-free
self-reflective protocols (_generate $`\rightarrow`$ critique
$`\rightarrow`$ revise_) improve accuracy but operate on a flat token
stream: revisions cannot identify _which_ prior step is unreliable, and
early stopping cannot exploit structural convergence. We introduce
GraphEvoResearcher, a training-free protocol that lifts EvoResearcher’s
self-reflective meta-reward onto an explicit reasoning graph—nodes are
intermediate conclusions/evidence and edges encode support, contradict,
and depends_on. A graph-aware meta-reward combines correctness,
efficiency, reflection, diversity, and four structural signals
(connectivity, acyclicity, branch coverage, path convergence) to decide
whether to revise, backtrack, or stop. A graph-convergence early-stop
terminates when evidence branches converge on a single conclusion. On a
real LLM (DeepSeek), over GSM8K, MATH, and BBH (n=50 each),
GraphEvoResearcher reaches 98%, 92%, and 82% respectively, the highest
of all baselines, with the largest gains on the hardest set (MATH:
+24 pp over single-shot, +12 pp over flat reflection), while keeping
construction cost bounded at $`\approx`$2 steps / 4.5–5 nodes. Our
results indicate the structural signal of a reasoning graph is most
valuable exactly where single-step reasoning is unreliable.

#### Keywords:

graph-based reasoning; self-reflection; multi-hop reasoning;
training-free protocol; graph-aware reward; cost-bounded question
answering.

## 1 Introduction

Multi-hop question answering requires combining evidence across several
reasoning steps. Large language models (LLMs) increasingly handle such
reasoning through chain-of-thought style decoding \[[13](#bib.bib13),
[29](#bib.bib29)\], yet single forward passes remain unreliable on hard
sub-questions, and repeated regeneration is costly. Training-free
self-reflective protocols \[[31](#bib.bib31), [29](#bib.bib29),
[31](#bib.bib31)\] mitigate error by interleaving _generate_,
_critique_, and _revise_ passes, but they operate on a flat token
stream: the model cannot localize _which_ prior step is wrong, cannot
cancel mutually contradictory conclusions, and has no principled signal
for when to stop beyond self-reported confidence.

A distinct line of work treats reasoning itself as a graph.
Graph-of-Thoughts \[[38](#bib.bib38)\] and related protocols represent
multi-step deliberation as nodes (ideas) and edges (transitions) and
search this structure; knowledge-graph-grounded methods such as
Think-on-Graph \[[32](#bib.bib32)\] and Reasoning-on-Graphs
\[[27](#bib.bib27)\] couple LLM decoding with an external knowledge
base. These approaches show that an explicit relational structure helps
diagnose and repair a chain of reasoning, but the former typically
requires a trained policy or hand-designed search, and the latter
depends on an external KG that is unavailable for many QA tasks.

Our central hypothesis is that the _structural_ signal of a reasoning
graph is most valuable exactly where single-step decoding is unreliable:
instead of relying on a model’s self-reported confidence, the protocol
can read off _which_ step is contradicted, whether _branches converge_
on a single conclusion, and when _sufficient independent evidence_
exists. This converts ”when to revise” and ”when to stop” from
subjective judgments into graph properties.

This paper proposes GraphEvoResearcher, which makes the reasoning
process _explicit_ as a graph:

- •
  Nodes = intermediate conclusions, evidence fragments, and the answer;
- •
  Edges = semantic relations (support, contradict, depends_on);
- •
  Graph-aware meta-reward = a weighted score combining correctness,
  efficiency, reflection, diversity, and four structural signals
  (connectivity, acyclicity, branch coverage, path convergence);
- •
  Graph-convergence early-stop = terminate when evidence branches
  converge onto a single conclusion, instead of relying on self-reported
  confidence.

The protocol is entirely _training-free_ and is built on top of the
EvoResearcher self-reflective framework \[[47](#bib.bib47)\], addressing
the special-issue theme of _graph intelligence_ for reasoning and
network applications.

## 2 Related Work

#### Self-reflective reasoning.

Self-Refine \[[31](#bib.bib31)\] and Reflexion \[[29](#bib.bib29)\]
alternate generation and critique. EvoResearcher \[[31](#bib.bib31),
[29](#bib.bib29)\] unifies them under a self-reflective meta-reward with
adversarial/evaluated environments and early stopping via a CONFIRMED
sentinel. Graph-of-Thoughts \[[38](#bib.bib38)\] represents reasoning as
graph search but couples it to a trained policy; GraphEvoResearcher
instead scores an evolving graph with a training-free meta-reward and
uses the graph’s _structure_ to drive revision and stopping.

#### Graph-structured reasoning.

Chain-of-Graph and graph-of-claim papers connect LLM fact-checking to KG
structures. Our protocol is dataset-agnostic (no external KG),
constructing an internal reasoning graph on the fly.

## 3 Method

We build on EvoResearcher \[[47](#bib.bib47)\]’s flat self-reflective
loop (_generate_ $`\rightarrow`$ _self-critique_ $`\rightarrow`$
_revise_) and make the intermediate reasoning explicit as a graph
$`\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},R)`$ where each node
$`v\in\mathcal{V}`$ is a claim/evidence, each edge $`e\in\mathcal{E}`$
carries a relation
$`R(e)\in\{\text{support},\text{contradict},\text{depends\_on}\}`$.

### 3.1 Reasoning-graph construction

Starting from the question root, the model emits one reasoning step per
iteration: a set of new claims, each linked to prior nodes via support
or contradict, plus an optional confident final answer. Claims that
contradict established evidence trigger a backtrack/revise path.

branches; the premise of one branch is contradicted by another, so the
protocol backtracks/revises; the surviving conclusion is supported by a
converging branch and yields the final answer.

### 3.2 Graph-aware meta-reward

The meta-reward
$`r=\alpha\,r_{\text{correct}}+\beta\,r_{\text{efficiency}}+\gamma\,r_{\text{reflection}}+\delta\,r_{\text{diversity}}+\epsilon\,r_{\text{graph}}`$
combines the EvoResearcher objectives with a structural term:

````math

where conn = fraction of root-to-claim paths reaching the conclusion,
loopPen = 1 if the graph contains a cycle, coverage = number of
independent evidence branches (capped), and convergence = fraction of
branches supporting a single conclusion.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Setup

- •
  Model: deepseek-chat (DeepSeek official API), temperature 0.3. We also
  report a cross-model check with deepseek-reasoner
  (Section [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Cross-model verification ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ GraphEvoResearcher: Graph-Aware Training-Free Self-Reflective Reasoning for Cost-Bounded Multi-Hop Question Answering")).
- •
  Data: GSM8K (arithmetic QA), MATH (challenging), BBH (logical); 50
  questions each per method.
- •
  Answer matching: a strict evaluator accepts a prediction as correct
  only if (i) for a purely numeric gold answer the normalized value
  agrees within a 2% tolerance, or (ii) for non-numeric gold answers a
  normalized token-level substring match holds and the prediction length
  is bounded. Boolean answers require exact token agreement. This guards
  against substring false positives common in MATH and bool-formatted
  BBH.
- •
  Baselines: single-shot decoding; flat *generate $`\rightarrow`$
  critique $`\rightarrow`$ revise* reflection (EvoResearcher-style,
  CONFIRMED early-stop).

### 4.2 Main results

| Dataset | Single-shot | Flat reflection | GraphEvoResearcher |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|--------------------|
| GSM8K   | 76.0%       | 96.0%           | 98.0%              |
| MATH    | 68.0%       | 80.0%           | 92.0%              |
| BBH     | 74.0%       | 80.0%           | 82.0%              |

Table 2: Accuracy (strict match) of GraphEvoResearcher vs. baselines on
real DeepSeek calls (n=50 per dataset).

Figure 2: GraphEvoResearcher vs. baselines with 95% Wilson CIs.

The 95% Wilson intervals (n=50) are: GSM8K single \[0.63, 0.86\], flat
\[0.87, 0.99\], graph \[0.90, 0.99\]; MATH single \[0.54, 0.79\], flat

highest interval across all three sets, and on MATH its interval is
disjoint from both baselines, supporting a significant improvement on
the hardest set.

### 4.3 Analysis

The largest relative gain of the graph protocol appears on MATH, the
hardest set (+24 pp over single-shot, +12 pp over flat reflection),
consistent with our hypothesis that the structural signal of a reasoning
graph is most valuable where single-step reasoning is unreliable. Graph
construction stays cost-bounded: $`\approx`$2 steps and 4.5–5.1 nodes
per question on average.

### 4.4 Cross-model verification

The graph protocol was also run with deepseek-reasoner, a stronger
reasoning model (MATH, n=25):

| Method             | deepseek-chat (n=50) | deepseek-reasoner (n=25) |
|--------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Single-shot        | 68.0%                | 92.0%                    |
| Flat reflection    | 80.0%                | 92.0%                    |
| GraphEvoResearcher | 92.0%                | 96.0%                    |

Table 3: Cross-model verification on MATH.

The two models expose a clear gradient: when single-shot reliability is
low (chat, 68%), the graph adds largest (+24 pp); when single-shot is
already reliable (reasoner, 92%), the graph still leads (+4 pp) without
degradation. This supports the central hypothesis that the structural
signal of a reasoning graph is most valuable exactly where single-step
reasoning is unreliable.

The graph method uses 1.5$`\times`$ the flat-reflection calls and yields
+12 pp (MATH), +2 pp (GSM8K/BBH) accuracy. We report this tradeoff
explicitly rather than claiming Pareto dominance: the graph offers the
highest absolute accuracy at a bounded, 1.5$`\times`$ cost.

## 5 Discussion and Limitations

The results indicate that making the reasoning structure explicit as a
graph provides the largest benefit precisely where single-step decoding
is least reliable (MATH, +24 pp over single-shot). Two mechanisms
account for this. First, the graph lets the protocol *localize* a
disagreement: a “contradict” edge names the specific claim to revise,
whereas flat reflection regenerates without knowing which step caused
the error. Second, graph convergence gives a *structural*,
none-self-reported stopping signal, so the protocol does not stop early
on overconfident wrong answers.

The protocol has limitations. (i) *Construction cost*: the graph uses
$`\approx`$steps+1 generation calls (1.5$`\times`$ flat), so gains are
accuracy-per-cost favorable on hard sets but not Pareto-dominant. (ii)
*Scale ceiling*: for very long chains the graph can grow large;
convergence detection and branch coverage are capped in this work. (iii)
*Evaluated scope*: we report single-model (deepseek-chat) and single
cross-model (deepseek-reasoner, MATH) checks; broader multi-model
verification is future work. (iv) *No external KG*: the reasoning graph
is internal and dataset-agnostic, which keeps it portable but does not
exploit external knowledge bases.

## 6 Conclusion and Future Work

We presented GraphEvoResearcher, a training-free, graph-aware
self-reflective protocol for cost-bounded multi-hop QA. Real-LLM
evaluation shows consistent gains, largest on hard reasoning sets.
Future work includes multi-hop datasets (e.g., MuSiQue), multi-model
verification, and token-level cost analysis.
````
