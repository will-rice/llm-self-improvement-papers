###### Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) remain vulnerable against jailbreak attacks
and harmful-content prompts, particularly under adversarial distribution
shifts where static alignment pipelines fail to generalize. Although
introspective methods such as STAIR strengthen refusal consistency, they
depend on fixed self-evaluation policies that cannot rapidly adapt to
evolving manipulation strategies. To overcome these limitations this
paper introduces META-IR, a meta-introspective reasoning framework that
enhances safety alignment through meta-learning–driven adaptation. Its
employs a dual-loop architecture: an inner introspective loop that
performs structured risk analysis, self-assessment, and reflective
correction via SI-MCTS, and an outer meta-learning loop that optimizes
the introspection policy across diverse jailbreak task families to
enable rapid generalization to unseen attacks. A dynamic jailbreak
simulation module further exposes the model to progressively harder
adversarial prompts, enabling continual self-improvement without human
supervision. Experiments across multiple models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
and Qwen-2-7B-Instruct) and a broad suite of safety, helpfulness,
generalization, and introspection metrics demonstrate that META-IR
substantially improves adaptation speed, robustness, and reasoning
stability compared with all baseline models. These results establish
META-IR as an effective pathway toward building LLMs capable of
sustained, adaptive safety alignment under dynamic and evolving threat
environments¹¹ 1

## I Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) achieve strong performance across reasoning
and dialogue tasks but remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks that
deliberately bypass safety controls to elicit harmful content
\[[7](#bib.bib1)\]. Standard alignment pipelines such as supervised
fine-tuning (SFT) \[[15](#bib.bib2)\], reinforcement learning from human
feedback (RLHF) \[[12](#bib.bib3)\], and direct preference optimization
(DPO) \[[13](#bib.bib4)\] behave reliably on simple harmful instructions
but rely on fixed safety policies that degrade under
distribution-shifted adversarial prompts \[[7](#bib.bib1)\]. When
attackers exploit role-play framing, forced prefixes, or disguised
harmful intent, LLMs often revert to unsafe or inconsistent
\[[17](#bib.bib5)\].

Recent introspective approaches like STAIR improve refusal reliability
by encouraging System-1 style reasoning as in Fig.
[1](#S1.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ I Introduction ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM"),
yet their reasoning process is static and cannot adapt when adversarial
methods evolve \[[22](#bib.bib6)\]. Static introspection is particularly
brittle against prompts that manipulate initial responses, embed harmful
intent in narrative structures, or exploit linguistic variations not
seen during training \[[17](#bib.bib5)\].

To address these limitations, we propose META-IR: Meta Introspective
Reasoning framework for adaptive safety alignment which introduces a
dual-loop architecture. An inner loop performs structured risk analysis
via adaptive SI-MCTS \[[14](#bib.bib7)\], refining reasoning
trajectories through safety helpfulness introspection optimization,
while an outer meta-learning loop updates\[[2](#bib.bib8)\] the
introspection policy across diverse jailbreak families to enable rapid
adaptation. A dynamic jailbreak simulation engine further generates
progressively harder adversarial prompts, preventing overfitting and
supporting continuous model improvement.

Fig. 1: Under adaptive jailbreaks, a standard aligned model is
manipulated into unsafe output, whereas Meta-IR uses dual-loop
introspective reasoning to detect hidden intent, correct intermediate

Experiments on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2-7B-Instruct show that
META-IR achieves substantial improvements in harmfulness reduction,
violation rates, refusal stability, and generalization compared to
STAIR, SFT, RLHF, DPO, and self-rewarding baselines. META-IR-3
consistently delivers the lowest safety errors and the strongest
introspective reliability and meta-adaptation scores, outperforming all
baselines under both static and adaptive jailbreak attacks. These
results confirm that META-IR provides robust, adaptive safety alignment
in dynamic adversarial environments.

The contributions of this work are: 1) introducing META-IR, a
meta-introspective reasoning framework that integrates structured
introspection with meta-learning for adaptive safety alignment; 2)
developing SI-MCTS, a safety-aware introspective MCTS guided by a
composite safety–helpfulness–introspection reward; 3) designing a
dynamic jailbreak simulation engine that generates evolving adversarial
prompts for continuous improvement; and 4) providing a comprehensive
evaluation protocol showing that META-IR achieves state-of-the-art
robustness under both static and distribution-shifted jailbreak attacks.

## II Meta-IR Framework

Meta-IR aligns $`\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)`$ via structured introspection,
SI-MCTS based self-generation, and meta-learning, yielding robust safety
adaptation under evolving jailbreak attacks (see Supplementary Materials
Section. I).

![Refer to caption](2611.10089v1/Figure_2.png)

Fig. 2: Meta-IR has three stages: (1) structured introspective reasoning
for prompt analysis, hidden-intent detection, risk evaluation, and safe
trajectory generation; (2) dual-loop self-improvement, where SI-MCTS
generates trajectories, builds safety-prioritized preference data, and
meta-learns the introspection policy across jailbreak families; and (3)
meta-adaptive decoding, where the learned reward model guides best-of-N
and beam search to select a safe, helpful final answer.

### II-A Structured Introspective Reasoning

To shift from instinctive refusal to deliberate System 2 reasoning, we
introduce structured introspection for progressive risk analysis and
safety assessment.

Let $`x`$ denote an input prompt and let $`y_{\mathrm{IR}}`$ denote an
introspective reasoning sequence composed of $`n`$ structured reasoning
steps followed by a final answer, written as
$`y_{\mathrm{IR}}=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n},f)`$. Each reasoning step
$`s_{i}`$ contains a short title summarizing the purpose of the step and
a detailed explanation describing the model’s analysis, and is enclosed
within the special markers \<\|Reasoning*step\|\> $`s*{i}`$
\<\|/Reasoning_step\|\> to maintain structural consistency. After
completing all reasoning steps, the model outputs the final conclusion
$`f`$, which is wrapped inside the markers \<\|Output\|\> $`f`$
\<\|/Output\|\>, producing a complete introspective trace that separates
the reasoning process from the final decision.

The introspective steps are required to identify potential risks
embedded in the prompt, including role-play framing, forced-prefix
generation, disguised harmful intent, or adversarial instruction
templates; if any risks are detected in $`\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}`$, the
final answer $`f`$ must provide a clear and policy-consistent refusal.
To construct training examples, we prompt a teacher model to generate
structured introspective responses for each input $`x`$, producing the
dataset $`\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{IR}}=\{(x,y_{\mathrm{IR}})\}`$, where
each $`y_{\mathrm{IR}}`$ follows a multi-stage template for risk
analysis and safety justification. This dataset is then used for
supervised fine-tuning to align the model’s introspective reasoning
style with the structured format.

### II-B Meta-IR Dual Loop Implementation

Meta-IR improves safety-aware reasoning through dual-loop across diverse
jailbreak families.

Inner Loop: SI-MCTS. Given an introspective reasoning trace
$`y_{\mathrm{IR}}`$ produced in the previous stage, the inner loop
applies Safety-Informed Monte Carlo Tree Search (SI-MCTS) to explore
alternative reasoning paths and to evaluate their safety. Each candidate
trajectory compute a composite reward that integrates helpfulness $`H`$,
safety $`S`$, and introspection quality $`I`$, defined as
$`R(H,S,I)=F(S)H+S+\lambda I`$, where $`F(S)`$ modulates the
contribution of helpfulness based on safety and $`\lambda>0`$ controls
the impact of introspective clarity. This reward guides node selection
and pruning, ensuring that unsafe or policy-violating branches are
removed while high-safety, high-consistency reasoning paths are
expanded. Let $`\hat{y}_{\mathrm{IR}}`$ denote the refined trajectory
generated by the SI-MCTS search:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\hat{y}_{\mathrm{IR}}=\mathrm{SI\text{-}MCTS}\bigl(y_{\mathrm{IR}};\theta_{r}\bigr)
``` |  | (1) |

Outer Loop: Meta-Learning. To enable fast adaptation to previously
unseen adversarial patterns, the outer loop performs meta-learning over
a task distribution $`\mathcal{T}`$, where each task
$`\tau\sim\mathcal{T}`$ corresponds to a jailbreak family such as
role-play deception, forced-prefix prompts, or subtle harmful-intent
masking. For each task $`\tau`$, the refined introspection trace
$`\hat{y}_{\mathrm{IR}}`$ is evaluated using the same composite reward,
yielding a task-level score $`R_{\tau}`$. Meta-IR then updates the
introspection policy parameters $`\theta_{r}`$ by maximizing the
expected reward across tasks:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta_{r}\leftarrow\theta_{r}+\alpha_{\mathrm{meta}}\nabla_{\theta_{r}}\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\mathcal{T}}\bigl[R_{\tau}\bigr]
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\alpha_{\mathrm{meta}}`$ is the meta step size. This update
enables the reasoning policy to generalize beyond the jailbreak
strategies observed during training and improves robustness under
distribution shift. By refining introspective trajectories with SI-MCTS
in the inner loop and optimizing across jailbreak task families in the
outer loop, Meta-IR learns a safety-aware introspection policy that
remains aligned under evolving and unseen adversarial attacks.

Reward Mechanism and Self-Improvement. Meta-IR enhances safety alignment
through a dual-loop self-improvement process that couples an inner-loop
search refinement with an outer-loop meta-learning update, both driven
by a safety-prioritized composite reward. Each response compute a
helpfulness score $`H\in[-1,1]`$, a safety score $`S\in[-1,1]`$, and an
introspection-quality score $`I\in[-1,1]`$. The overall reward is
defined as

|     |                              |     |     |
|-----|------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       R(H,S,I)=F(S)\,H+S+\lambda I
       ```                           |     | (3) |

where $`F(S)`$ modulates the contribution of helpfulness based on safety
and $`\lambda>0`$ controls the impact of introspective quality. This
formulation enforces safety as the dominant objective while still
encouraging helpful and clearly reasoned responses that accurately
identify role-play framing, hidden malicious intent, and forced-prefix
jailbreak strategies.

In the *inner loop*, Safety-Informed MCTS (SI-MCTS) \[[14](#bib.bib7)\]
expands and evaluates structured introspective trajectories using the
composite reward $`R(H,S,I)`$ to guide node selection and pruning,
thereby producing safe, policy-consistent introspection traces. In the
*outer loop*, Meta-IR performs meta-learning over a distribution of
jailbreak task families. For each batch of tasks
$`\tau\sim\mathcal{T}`$, Meta-IR aggregates introspective trajectories
and updates the introspection policy parameters $`\theta_{r}`$ via the
task-level objective

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
J(\theta_{r})=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\mathcal{T}}\left[R(H_{\tau},S_{\tau},I_{\tau})\right]
``` |  | (4) |

where $`H_{\tau},S_{\tau},I_{\tau}`$ denote the aggregated scores for
task $`\tau`$. *Optionally* composite reward $`R`$ can be converted into
pairwise preferences and optimized with step-level DPO
\[[13](#bib.bib4)\] to stabilize the base model update. This dual-loop
refinement enables rapid generalization to unseen jailbreaks.

### II-C Meta-Adaptive Response Generation

After dual-loop refinement, Meta-IR uses the meta-updated policy
$`\pi_{\theta+\theta_{r}}`$ for test-time safe response generation.
Given an input $`x`$, it produces multiple candidate continuations with
diverse decoding strategies and scores each partial or complete
trajectory using a process reward model $`r_{\phi}`$ trained with the
Bradley-Terry model \[[12](#bib.bib3)\] to assess safety and usefulness.
Meta-IR first applies Best-of-N sampling \[[9](#bib.bib9)\], selecting
the candidate with the highest process reward, and then further handles
difficult cases with reward-guided Beam Search \[[19](#bib.bib10)\],
which expands promising reasoning paths while pruning unsafe
continuations. The final output is the highest-reward sample or beam,
enabling robust safe generation under unseen jailbreaks.

## III Experimental Setup

Detail Experiments in Supplementary Materials Sect. II.

Models and Datasets. We use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2-7B-Instruct
as the base LLMs for safety alignment. Both employed for ablation
analysis, and cross-model generalization. Our experiments are
constructed from a 50k-sample seed corpus $`D`$ drawn from AdvBench
\[[24](#bib.bib11)\], HarmBench \[[11](#bib.bib12)\], and WildJailbreak
\[[4](#bib.bib13)\]: we include all harmful instructions from AdvBench
and all behavior prompts from HarmBench (approximately 2k combined), and
subsample the remaining 48k examples from WildJailbreak to balance
harmful (vanilla and adversarial) and benign look-alike prompts.
Baseline systems are trained on the full labeled corpus $`D`$, while
META-IR additionally requires a structured introspection corpus
$`D_{\text{IR}}`$. At the end construct a 20k-sample introspective
dataset consisting of 10k safety-oriented and 10k helpfulness-oriented
prompts converted into our introspective reasoning format. Each META-IR
inner-loop update uses 5k safety and 5k helpfulness introspection
samples, and to expose the model to distribution-shifted adversarial
behavior, we introduce 1k and 2k adversarial jailbreak prompts (from the
adversarial split of WildJailbreak) in the final two meta-adaptation
iterations.

Baselines. We compare META-IR against eight widely used safety-alignment
baselines: (i) Base, an instruction-tuned model without introspection or
self-generation; (ii) CoT, which adds supervised chain-of-thought but
lacks true introspective structure \[[18](#bib.bib16)\]; (iii) SFT/RLHF,
representing standard non-introspective alignment pipelines
\[[15](#bib.bib2), [12](#bib.bib3)\]; (iv) DPO, a
preference-optimization objective without dynamic introspection
\[[13](#bib.bib4)\]; (v) SACPO, which uses a learned safety critic but
no structured introspective reasoning or iterative self-generation
\[[16](#bib.bib14)\]; (vi) Self-Rewarding (RLOO), which adds lightweight
self-generated signals but remains non-introspective
\[[20](#bib.bib15)\]; and (vii) STAIR-SFT, which provides fixed-format
introspective reasoning without meta-level adaptation
\[[22](#bib.bib6)\].

Evaluation. We evaluate using a unified suite of metrics covering
Harmlessness + Safety, Helpfulness + Generalization, and Introspection +
Meta-Learning. Harmlessness and safety are assessed with five
failure-oriented indicators: Adv↓ (attack success rate)
\[[17](#bib.bib5)\], Harm↓ (overall harmfulness) \[[21](#bib.bib17)\],
WJ-H↓ (WildJailbreak harmful responses) \[[5](#bib.bib18)\], SVR↓
(safety-violation rate) \[[1](#bib.bib19)\], and RST↓ (refusal-stability
under perturbations) \[[7](#bib.bib1)\]. Helpfulness and generalization
are measured using WJ-B↑ (benign helpfulness) \[[5](#bib.bib18)\], MGS↑
(robustness to paraphrasing, role-play, and distribution shifts)
\[[3](#bib.bib20)\], MTB↑ (MTBench instruction quality)
\[[23](#bib.bib21)\], Elo↑ (Arena Elo pairwise preference)
\[[10](#bib.bib22)\], and BBH↑ (Big-Bench Hard reasoning accuracy)
\[[6](#bib.bib23)\]. Introspection and meta-learning are motivated by
prior work on introspection and meta-adaptation; we evaluate
introspective reliability and adaptive safety behavior using five
metrics: IA↑ (Introspection Accuracy), REAR↑ (Risk-Explanation
Attribution Recall), MCR↓ (Meta-Confusion Rate), JIR↓
(Jailbreak-Introspection Failure Rate), and MTA↑ (Meta-Training
Adaptation) \[[2](#bib.bib8), [3](#bib.bib20), [8](#bib.bib24)\].

Details Analysis in Supplementary Materials Section. III

Main Result. Meta-IR consistently outperforms all baselines on both
models, achieving the strongest overall balance of safety, harmlessness,
helpfulness, generalization, introspection, and meta-learning. Table
[I](#S3.T1 "TABLE I ‣ III Experimental Setup ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM")
summarizes the overall performance across both models, showing that
META-IR-3 consistently delivers the strongest alignment, reasoning

and +19.5%, respectively.

### IV-A Comprehensive Performance Analysis

Safety and Robustness.
Fig. [5](#S4.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ IV Results and Analysis ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM")
shows that SFT, RLHF, and DPO achieve only moderate reductions in
harmfulness (WJ-HL), adversarial vulnerability (Adv), and safety
violations (SVR), and remain unstable under harmful prompts. STAIR-SFT
improves robustness via structured introspection but still experiences
elevated violation rates. Meta-IR provides substantially stronger safety
performance across all metrics, with META-IR-3 yielding the lowest
harmfulness and violation levels and maintaining stable refusal behavior
under aggressive red-teaming. This demonstrates that integrating
introspection, self-generated corrections, and meta-learning produces a
more robust safety policy.

Introspection Reasoning Quality.
Fig. [5](#S4.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ IV Results and Analysis ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM")
evaluates introspective reliability (REAR), mistake-cause reasoning
(MCR), and jailbreak-risk attribution (JIR). While DPO and
Self-Rewarding offer partial improvements, their reasoning-error
profiles remain high; STAIR-SFT reduces some inconsistencies but does
not fully address attribution errors. Meta-IR variants improve
monotonically across all introspective metrics, with META-IR-3 achieving
the highest REAR and the lowest MCR and JIR. These results confirm that
Meta-IR’s dual-loop design inner-loop introspection with outer-loop
meta-adaptation substantially strengthens internal safety reasoning.

Meta-Generalization to Adaptive Jailbreaks. As shown in
Fig. [5](#S4.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ IV Results and Analysis ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM"),
SFT and DPO generalize poorly to unseen jailbreak styles, and
Self-Rewarding and STAIR-SFT offer limited resilience under adaptive
attacks. In contrast, META-IR-1, META-IR-2, and META-IR-3 consistently
improve meta-generalization, with META-IR-3 achieving the highest MTA
stability and the best MTB and BBH performance. This indicates that
Meta-IR not only improves in-distribution safety but also adapts
effectively to adversarial conditions unseen.

### IV-B Ablation Analysis

We ablate Meta-IR on LLAMA-3.1-8B and Qwen-2-7B to measure the
contribution of each component
(Table [II](#S4.T2 "TABLE II ‣ IV-B Ablation Analysis ‣ IV Results and Analysis ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM")).
Removing structured introspection, meta-learning, SelfGen, or adaptive
jailbreak handling consistently harms safety, introspection, and
meta-generalization. For LLAMA-3.1-8B, disabling introspection causes
large drops in REAR (0.902 $`\rightarrow`$ 0.611) and increases in
MCR/JIR, while removing meta-learning weakens both introspection and
MTA. Eliminating SelfGen raises SVR and reduces generalization.
Qwen-2-7B shows similar degradations, with REAR and JIR deteriorating
when IR or meta-learning are removed. Across both backbones, Meta-IR-3
remains the strongest, achieving the lowest SVR and reasoning errors and
the highest MTA. These results confirm that Meta-IR’s components operate
synergistically and are jointly required for robust safety alignment.

## V Related Work

As summarized in
Table [III](#S5.T3 "TABLE III ‣ V Related Work ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM"),
existing alignment methods against jailbreak attacks can be broadly
grouped into static alignment, self-generated alignment, and
reasoning-based alignment. Early approaches such as SFT
\[[15](#bib.bib2)\], RLHF \[[12](#bib.bib3)\], and DPO
\[[13](#bib.bib4)\] rely on fixed supervision or preference signals and
therefore remain static after training, which limits their robustness
under adaptive jailbreak attacks \[[3](#bib.bib20), [8](#bib.bib24),
[1](#bib.bib19)\]. Later methods such as SACPO \[[16](#bib.bib14)\] and
self-rewarding alignment \[[20](#bib.bib15)\] introduce self-generated
feedback, but they still lack meta-adaptive learning and show limited
generalization to evolving attack distributions \[[10](#bib.bib22),
[11](#bib.bib12)\]. Reasoning-based methods such as CoT
\[[18](#bib.bib16)\] and STAIR \[[22](#bib.bib6)\] improve intermediate
reasoning or introspection ability, yet their safety policies also
remain fixed and cannot explicitly adapt to new jailbreak families
\[[19](#bib.bib10), [2](#bib.bib8), [17](#bib.bib5)\]. In contrast,
Meta-IR uniquely combines self-generated signals, search-based
refinement, and meta-adaptive learning within a structured introspective
reasoning framework \[[2](#bib.bib8)\], making it the only method in
Table [III](#S5.T3 "TABLE III ‣ V Related Work ‣ META-IR: Meta-Introspective Reasoning for Adaptive Safety Alignment in LLM")
that explicitly supports adaptive safety alignment and robust
generalization against evolving jailbreak attacks.

## VI Conclusion

We presented META-IR, a meta-learning framework that integrates
structured introspective reasoning, self-generated corrective signals,
and outer-loop adaptation to enhance the safety and robustness of large
language models. Experiments across harmfulness, introspection quality,
and meta-generalization show that META-IR consistently outperforms SFT,
RLHF, DPO, Self-Rewarding, and STAIR-SFT, producing safer and more
stable responses while adapting effectively to unseen jailbreak
strategies. Ablation studies further verify the contribution of each
component, with the full META-IR model achieving the best balance
between safety and utility. Overall, META-IR offers a principled and
scalable approach to continuous safety improvement, enabling LLMs to
remain robust and trustworthy under evolving adversarial conditions.
````
