# A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Models via On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation

###### Abstract

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has substantially
improved Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs, but its outcome-level rewards
provide only sparse supervision. On-Policy Distillation offers dense
token-level feedback, yet requires a stronger compatible teacher, while
on-policy self-distillation relies on hand-designed privileged
information. We observe that the MoE architecture itself offers a way to
resolve this dilemma, as increasing the activation budget per token
exposes latent expert capacity and yields a potentially stronger teacher
within the model. However, naive expansion degrades performance because
newly co-activated experts are not optimized to work together. We
therefore propose MoEvolve, a bidirectional distillation framework
following a recovery-then-improvement strategy: we first restore the
activation-expanded high-$`k`$ model and further strengthen it by
exploiting its additional active capacity, then reverse the direction of
distillation to transfer the enhanced capabilities back into the
original low-$`k`$ configuration, retaining its inference-time
activation budget. Across diverse MoE models and benchmarks, MoEvolve
improves the initial models by up to $`22.2`$ domain-average points and
outperforms same-budget RLVR baselines by up to $`6.5`$ points. Notably,
the final low-$`k`$ models often surpass their activation-expanded
teachers.

## 1 Introduction

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) has become a prominent architecture for
state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) ([Team et al.,
2026](#bib.bib7); [GLM-5-Team et al., 2026](#bib.bib15), e.g.,). By
activating only a small subset of experts per token, MoE models offer a
favorable capability-efficiency trade-off ([Shazeer et al.,
2017](#bib.bib2); [Fedus et al., 2022](#bib.bib3); [DeepSeek-AI,
2026](#bib.bib17)).

Post-training methods such as Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable
Rewards (RLVR) have improved MoE LLMs on reasoning tasks ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib18); [Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib19); [Xiaomi,
2026](#bib.bib16)), but outcome-level rewards provide sparse supervision
for intermediate steps. Process-level feedback provides a natural source
of denser supervision, but training Process Reward Models (PRMs)
requires costly step-level annotations ([Yuan et al.,
2025](#bib.bib30)).

Recently, On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has emerged as a promising
alternative ([Lu and Lab, 2025](#bib.bib11)). By supervising
trajectories sampled from the student’s current policy with token-level
teacher logits, OPD provides dense feedback without requiring a
specifically trained PRM ([Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [Hou et al.,
2026](#bib.bib10)). However, OPD depends on a strong teacher model
sharing compatible tokenizers and output spaces with the student model
([Lu and Lab, 2025](#bib.bib11)), which may not always be available or
affordable in practice. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) sidesteps
this by using the student model itself as teacher with _privileged
information_ (e.g., partial solutions, hints) ([Zhao et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib12)), but this shifts the burden to designing privileged
signals: they may require unavailable ground-truth solutions, and can
encourage shortcut memorization rather than transferable capability
learning, thereby hurting generalization ([Zhao et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib29); [Shen et al., 2026](#bib.bib28)).

In this paper, we observe that MoE LLMs offer a natural way out of this
dilemma. A typical MoE routes each token to only $`k`$ of its $`N`$
experts, leaving substantial expert capacity inactive. Increasing the
activation budget to $`k^{\prime}>k`$ exposes each token to additional
expert transformations and computation ([Geva et al., 2021](#bib.bib8);
[Dai et al., 2022](#bib.bib14); [Huang et al., 2024](#bib.bib31)),
creating an activation-expanded variant with the potential to serve as a
stronger, fully compatible teacher. However, naively increasing $`k`$ in
a pretrained MoE can trigger _mode collapse_, as newly co-activated
experts are not optimized to work together ([Gu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib1); [Wang et al., 2025a](#bib.bib47)). We find that this
degradation can be effectively repaired by applying OPD from the
original low-$`k`$ model, whose dense token-level supervision restores
the behavior disrupted by activation expansion. However, OPD alone is
bounded by the capability of its teacher and offers limited incentive to
exploit the additional active capacity. We therefore follow it with
RLVR, which further strengthens the recovered high-$`k`$ model beyond
the original one.

We then reverse the direction of distillation, using the strengthened
high-$`k`$ model as the teacher to improve the original low-$`k`$ model.
This transfers the gains from activation expansion back into the
original inference configuration, preserving its activation budget and
inference-time computation. We term the resulting bidirectional
distillation framework MoEvolve. It enables MoE self-improvement without
requiring either a stronger external teacher, as in OPD, or
hand-designed privileged information, as in OPSD.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

- •
  We propose MoEvolve, a self-improvement framework for MoE LLMs that
  exploits latent expert capacity to construct a stronger, fully
  compatible teacher within the model itself, and then distills the
  acquired capabilities back into the original low-$`k`$ configuration
  without increasing inference-time computation.
- •
  We introduce a recovery-then-improvement strategy for constructing a
  stronger activation-expanded teacher: first restoring the expert
  coordination disrupted by activation expansion, and then exploiting
  the additional active capacity to improve the recovered model beyond
  its original capability.
- •
  Extensive experiments across four MoE LLMs and twelve benchmarks
  spanning mathematics, code, and scientific knowledge show consistent
  gains at unchanged inference cost, with improvements of up to $`22.2`$
  domain-average points over the initial models and up to $`6.5`$ points
  over strong same-budget RLVR baselines. Notably, the final low-$`k`$
  models often surpass their activation-expanded teachers, suggesting
  that reverse distillation can produce genuine capability gains beyond
  lossy compression.

## 2 Related Work

##### Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs.

MoE replaces the dense feed-forward network in each Transformer layer
with expert FFNs and a router that activates only the top-$`k`$ experts
per token ([Shazeer et al., 2017](#bib.bib2); [Fedus et al.,
2022](#bib.bib3)). Prior work studies expert specialization and load
balancing ([Huang et al., 2024](#bib.bib31); [Muennighoff et al.,
2025](#bib.bib4)). Distillation has also been used to compress MoE
teachers into smaller models ([Kim et al., 2025](#bib.bib45); [Kim et
al., 2026](#bib.bib46)) and to reduce expert activation through
self-distillation ([Lv et al., 2026](#bib.bib48)). For this paper, the
key observation is that simply raising the activation budget $`k`$ can
hurt performance because newly co-activated experts were never optimized
to cooperate ([Gu et al., 2025](#bib.bib1); [Wang et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib47)). This motivates a way to restore coordination after
expansion.

##### On-Policy Distillation (OPD).

Knowledge distillation transfers the behavior of a teacher model into a
student ([Hinton et al., 2015](#bib.bib32); [Sanh et al.,
2019](#bib.bib33)). OPD trains the student on trajectories sampled from
itself, which reduces exposure bias and provides dense token-level
supervision through the teacher’s logits ([Lu and Lab,
2025](#bib.bib11); [Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). ExOPD further adds
reward extrapolation so that the student can benefit more fully from the
stronger teacher ([Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). However, OPD relies
on a strong teacher with a compatible tokenizer and output space. Such a
teacher may not always be available or affordable in practice ([Lu and
Lab, 2025](#bib.bib11)). To avoid this external dependency, OPSD
leverages privileged information to act as its own teacher, distilling a
model to itself without increasing inference cost ([Zhao et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib12); [Zhao et al., 2026b](#bib.bib29); [Shen et al.,
2026](#bib.bib28)). Such privileged signals often include partial
solutions, hints, or reference answers ([Zhao et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib12)). Designing these signals can be task-specific, may
require unavailable ground-truth solutions, and can hurt generalization
by encouraging imitation of reference trajectories ([Zhao et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib29); [Shen et al., 2026](#bib.bib28)).

## 3 Preliminaries

We review the three components underlying our method. The MoE
formulation defines the activation budget, whose expansion from $`k`$ to
$`k^{\prime}`$ enables us to construct an internal teacher. OPD provides
the objectives for restoring cooperation in the expanded model and
transferring its gains back to the original budget. SAPO ([Gao et al.,
2025](#bib.bib23)) is used to improve the MoE model while mitigating
training instability caused by mismatch between training and rollout.

##### Mixture-of-Experts.

Given a hidden representation $`\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$, the
router produces affinity scores

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
g_{i}(\mathbf{h})=\frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{h})}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{h})},
``` |  | (1) |

where $`\mathbf{w}_{i}`$ is the routing vector of expert $`i`$, and
$`N`$ is the total number of experts. Let
$`\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})`$ denote the top-$`k`$ experts selected by
the router. The MoE layer outputs
$`\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})}\tilde{g}_{i}(\mathbf{h})\,E_{i}(\mathbf{h})`$,
where
$`\tilde{g}_{i}(\mathbf{h})=g_{i}(\mathbf{h})/\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})}g_{j}(\mathbf{h})`$
are normalized routing weights. Since only $`k\ll N`$ experts are
activated per token, cost depends on $`k`$ rather than $`N`$, allowing
capacity to scale without proportional compute. We denote the original
model by $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ and the activation-expanded variant by
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$. $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ and
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ are two separate model copies with
identical initial expert and routing parameters. They differ only in the
activation budget, namely $`k`$ versus $`k^{\prime}`$.

##### On-Policy Distillation.

Given a prompt $`\mathbf{x}`$, the student samples a response
$`\mathbf{y}\sim\pi_{S}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x})`$, and OPD minimizes the
reverse KL divergence between the teacher and student on that response

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}_{\text{OPD}}(\pi_{T},\pi_{S})=\min_{\theta_{S}}\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{D},\,\mathbf{y}\sim\pi_{S}}\Big[\\
&\qquad D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_{S}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\,\|\mathrm{sg}\bigl[\pi_{T}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\bigr]\big)\Big].\end{split}
``` |  | (2) |

Here the KL is at the token level over next token distributions along
the student-sampled prefix, $`\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]`$ denotes stop-gradient
on the teacher logits, and $`\mathcal{D}`$ is the prompt distribution.
Standard OPD fixes the teacher signal and the KL regularization to equal
weight. Generalized On-Policy Distillation (G-OPD) generalizes this view
by adding a reference policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ and a reward scaling
factor $`\lambda`$ ([Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G\text{-}OPD}}(\pi_{S},\pi_{T},\pi_{\mathrm{ref}},\lambda)\\
&\quad=\max_{\theta_{S}}\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{D},\,\mathbf{y}\sim\pi_{S}}\Bigg[\lambda\log\frac{\pi_{T}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})}\\
&\qquad\quad-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{S}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\right)\Bigg].\end{split}
``` |  | (3) |

Standard OPD is the special case $`\lambda=1`$. G-OPD uses $`\lambda>1`$
for reward extrapolation, termed ExOPD, giving extra weight to a strong
teacher. Prior work demonstrates that ExOPD significantly outperforms
standard OPD when distilling between models of the same family ([Yang et
al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). In our setting, the teacher and student are
naturally paired copies of the same MoE models with different activation
budgets, so we use ExOPD in the reverse-distillation stage.

##### Soft Adaptive Policy Optimization (SAPO).

SAPO ([Gao et al., 2025](#bib.bib23)) replaces hard clipping in GRPO
([Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib19)) with a smooth temperature-controlled
gate. For $`G`$ responses sampled from the behavior policy, it optimizes

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{SAPO}}(\theta)=\max_{\theta}\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{D},\,\mathbf{y}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x})}\Bigg[`$ |  | (4) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{G|\mathbf{y}_{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|\mathbf{y}_{i}|}f^{\mathrm{SAPO}}_{i,t}(r_{i,t})\hat{A}_{i,t}\Bigg],`$ |  |  |

where $`r_{i,t}`$ is the token-level importance ratio, $`\hat{A}_{i,t}`$
is the group relative advantage, and
$`f^{\mathrm{SAPO}}_{i,t}(r)=\frac{4}{\tau_{i}}\sigma(\tau_{i}(r-1))`$
with $`\tau_{i}=\tau_{\mathrm{pos}}`$ for positive advantages and
$`\tau_{i}=\tau_{\mathrm{neg}}`$ otherwise. We set
$`\tau_{\mathrm{neg}}>\tau_{\mathrm{pos}}`$ to downweight high-variance
negative updates, yielding a smooth trust region that stabilizes RL on
MoE models.

## 4 MoEvolve: Bidirectional On-Policy Optimization for MoE Self-Distillation

We now present MoEvolve, our activation-expansion based training
framework, illustrated in
Figure [1](#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ On-Policy Distillation (OPD). ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").
Starting from a trained MoE model $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$, the framework
proceeds in two phases. Forward Distillation expands the activation
budget from $`k`$ to $`k^{\prime}`$ and runs Expert Cooperation
Restoration Distillation (ECRD), which takes $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ as the
teacher to restore coordination among the newly co-activated experts.
Backward Distillation then flips the direction of supervision. It first
applies Capability Unlocking via Reinforcement Learning (CU) to push the
recovered activation-expanded model beyond $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ under the
larger budget, yielding a stronger internal teacher
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$, and then performs Reverse
Distillation for Capability Transfer (RD), which distills that unlocked
capability back into the original $`k`$-expert budget. Together, these
two phases produce an improved $`k`$-activation model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\star}`$ with unchanged inference cost.

### 4.1 Forward Distillation

Increasing the number of activated experts widens the active
intermediate representation of each MoE layer
(Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
and exposes each token to additional expert transformations and
computation, providing additional representational capacity. However,
directly raising the activation budget of a trained model from $`k`$ to
$`k^{\prime}`$ yields an activation-expanded model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ whose co-activated experts were never
trained to work together. As a result, $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$
suffers mode collapse and can perform *worse* than $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$
despite using more FFN capacity ([Gu et al., 2025](#bib.bib1); [Wang et
al., 2025a](#bib.bib47)). Our first step therefore restores expert
cooperation, corresponding to the ECRD stage in
Figure [1](#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ On-Policy Distillation (OPD). ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

Many recovery choices are possible at this stage, including supervised
fine-tuning (SFT) ([Guha et al., 2026](#bib.bib25); [Team et al.,
2025](#bib.bib26)), rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib18)), reinforcement learning (RL), and OPD. In our method,
the ECRD is implemented with OPD: the original model $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$
serves as the teacher and the activation-expanded model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ serves as the student. We choose this ECRD
design because SFT requires reference solutions from an external source,
which does not fit our goal of self-improvement without an external
teacher. RFT can use verified samples from $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$, but it
still trains on a fixed off-policy set and is limited by the behaviors
captured in that set. Direct RL avoids such targets, yet outcome-only
rewards provide sparse sequence-level feedback. With uncoordinated
experts, this easily leads to entropy collapse and stagnant reward, as
shown in
Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Training Instability without Expert Cooperation Restoration ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").
By contrast, ECRD samples on-policy from $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$
and uses $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ as an internal teacher, providing dense
supervision without external labels.

Concretely, in ECRD we optimize the objective in
Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "In On-Policy Distillation. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
with $`\pi_{T}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}`$ and
$`\pi_{S}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}}`$. The trajectories are sampled
from the student $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ itself, so the teacher
supervises exactly the states the activation-expanded model actually
visits. This quickly pulls the newly co-activated experts back into a
cooperative regime. In practice this step rapidly recovers the
performance lost to mode collapse, so that $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$
at least matches $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ while now genuinely leveraging its
larger activation budget.

### 4.2 Backward Distillation

#### 4.2.1 Capability Unlocking via Reinforcement Learning (CU)

ECRD restores cooperation, but it has yet to harness the stronger
representational capacity brought by activating more experts to improve
the model’s capability. To unlock this capacity and obtain a stronger
teacher, we continue training $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ with
reinforcement learning using SAPO ([Gao et al., 2025](#bib.bib23)),
corresponding to the CU stage in
Figure [1](#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ On-Policy Distillation (OPD). ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").
Because the experts already cooperate after ECRD, RL optimization
becomes stable and effective. This step allows
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ to realize the benefit of the larger
activation budget and *surpass* the original model $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$,
at the temporary cost of activating $`k^{\prime}`$ experts. We denote
the resulting strengthened activation-expanded model by
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$.

#### 4.2.2 Reverse Distillation for Capability Transfer (RD)

After the CU step we obtain a stronger model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$, but it activates $`k^{\prime}>k`$
experts and therefore remains more expensive at inference time. The
final step transfers this gain back to the original activation budget by
*reversing* the distillation direction. We use ExOPD, instantiated by
the objective in
Eq. ([3](#S3.E3 "In On-Policy Distillation. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")),
with $`\pi_{S}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}`$,
$`\pi_{T}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}}`$,
$`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}`$, and $`\lambda>1`$. As
discussed in
§[3](#S3 "3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
ExOPD has shown stronger distillation performance than OPD on the same
family of models, motivating its use here. This fits our paired-model
setting: $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$ and $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$
share the tokenizer, vocabulary, and architecture, and mainly differ in
the capability recovered through the larger activation budget. The
teacher-reference log-ratio in
Eq. ([3](#S3.E3 "In On-Policy Distillation. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
can therefore provide a clean dense signal for amplifying the useful
changes in $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$ while anchoring the
student to the original $`k`$-activation model. The final model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\star}`$ keeps the original $`k`$ experts per token,
preserving the inference speed and activation cost.

## 5 Experiments

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

##### Models

We evaluate our method on four widely used open-source MoE LLMs spanning
different model scales and expert configurations: OLMoE-1B-7B
([Muennighoff et al., 2025](#bib.bib4)), DeepSeek-V2-Lite ([DeepSeek-AI
et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)), GPT-OSS-20B ([OpenAI et al.,
2025](#bib.bib6)), and Qwen3-30B-A3B ([Yang et al., 2025](#bib.bib27)).
These models cover a diverse range of MoE architectures and activation
patterns, enabling us to assess whether our method generalizes across
different model scales and expert configurations. Detailed model
configurations are provided in
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Model Details ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

##### Evaluation

We evaluate on three complementary domains to comprehensively assess the
model’s capabilities. Mathematics measures the model’s capacity for
symbolic reasoning, Code generation reflects its mastery of procedural
logic and Scientific and general knowledge (ScienceQA) gauges the
model’s broad knowledge retention and application. Together, these
diverse domains show that the improvements from MoEvolve are not limited
to a specific task or problem setting. For mathematics we use AMC23
([math-ai, 2025b](#bib.bib37)) together with GSM8K ([Cobbe et al.,
2021](#bib.bib34)), MATH500 ([Hendrycks et al., 2021b](#bib.bib35)),
Minerva Math ([Lewkowycz et al., 2022](#bib.bib36)), and PolyMath-Low
(Poly-L) ([Wang et al., 2025b](#bib.bib41)) for OLMoE-1B-7B and
DeepSeek-V2-Lite, and the harder PolyMath-Medium (Poly-M) ([Wang et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib41)) and AIME-2024/2025/2026 ([math-ai,
2024](#bib.bib38); [math-ai, 2025a](#bib.bib39); [math-ai,
2026](#bib.bib40)) for GPT-OSS-20B and Qwen3-30B-A3B, matching benchmark
difficulty to backbone strength. For code generation we use HumanEval
(HEval) ([Chen et al., 2021](#bib.bib42)), MBPP (full and
MBPP-Sanitized) ([Austin et al., 2021](#bib.bib43)), and
LiveCodeBench-v6 (LCB) ([Jain et al., 2025](#bib.bib44)). For ScienceQA
we use GPQA-main ([Rein et al., 2023](#bib.bib49)), ARC-Challenge
([Clark et al., 2018](#bib.bib50)), and MMLU-STEM ([Hendrycks et al.,
2021a](#bib.bib51)). We report pass@1 ([Chen et al., 2021](#bib.bib42))
across all benchmarks, sampling $`10`$ generations per problem. Detailed
evaluation settings are provided in
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Evaluation Details ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

##### Training Datasets and Settings

We train on three domains, matching the three evaluation domains. For
mathematics we use OpenMathInstruct-2 ([Toshniwal et al.,
2025](#bib.bib20)) and DeepMath-103K ([He et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)),
for code generation we use the code split of Eurus-2-RL-Data ([Cui et
al., 2026](#bib.bib22)), and for ScienceQA we use the MMLU auxiliary
training set ([Hendrycks et al., 2021a](#bib.bib51)). Detailed training
settings are in
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Implementation Details ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

##### Baselines

We compare against representative methods that improve
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ *without* raising its activation budget: (1) SFT,
supervised fine-tuning of $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ on the reference solutions
of the mathematics corpus, i.e. the Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct solutions of
OpenMathInstruct-2 ([Toshniwal et al., 2025](#bib.bib20)) and the
DeepSeek-R1 traces of DeepMath-103K ([He et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)), the
only domain shipping such labels, hence run on mathematics only ([Guha
et al., 2026](#bib.bib25); [Team et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)). (2) RFT,
rejection fine-tuning of $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ using its own
verifier-verified responses as labels ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib18)).
(3) RL, reinforcement learning directly on $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ using
SAPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib18); [Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib19)).
(4) OPSD, on-policy self-distillation where the teacher is
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ itself prompted with privileged information ([Zhao
et al., 2026a](#bib.bib12); [Zhao et al., 2026b](#bib.bib29); [Shen et
al., 2026](#bib.bib28); [Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)). Detailed
privileged information settings are given in
Appendix [C.2](#A3.SS2 "C.2 Training Configuration ‣ Appendix C Implementation Details ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

### 5.3 Expert Cooperation Restoration Methods for the Activation-Expanded Model

We next study how best to recover the activation-expanded model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ after its activation budget is expanded.
Applying RL directly to $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ diverges because
the newly co-activated experts have not yet learned to cooperate, RL
must optimize on top of a collapsed routing distribution and training
collapses
(Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Training Instability without Expert Cooperation Restoration ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")).
Figure [2](#S5.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 5.3 Expert Cooperation Restoration Methods for the Activation-Expanded Model ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")
compares three stable recovery strategies. SFT fine-tunes
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ on the reference solutions shipped with the
training set. RFT first samples responses from the original model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ on the same prompts, keeps those whose final answer
is verified correct, and fine-tunes $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ on
them. ECRD takes $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ as the teacher and
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ as the student and distills token-level
supervision on the student’s own trajectories
(Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "In On-Policy Distillation. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))).

As shown in
Figure [2](#S5.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 5.3 Expert Cooperation Restoration Methods for the Activation-Expanded Model ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
both SFT and RFT underperform ECRD. They are off-policy, since they
fine-tune $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ on a fixed set of target
trajectories. SFT uses external reference solutions and RFT uses the
original model’s own verified responses, but in either case the training
targets come from a policy different from the one
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ samples from at inference. This causes
exposure bias and errors accumulate once $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$
generates outside the imitation set. As an on-policy method, ECRD
samples trajectories directly from $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ and
supervises them using the teacher’s token-level logits. By providing
supervision on the states visited by the student, ECRD aligns the
training distribution with the student’s inference distribution. This is
why ECRD restores expert cooperation more faithfully than the off-policy
alternatives.

## 6 Analyses

### 6.1 Analysis of Expert Cooperation after Activation Expansion

To understand what ECRD repairs,
Figure [4](#S6.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 6.1 Analysis of Expert Cooperation after Activation Expansion ‣ 6 Analyses ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")
examines the gate weights at the router level. An MoE layer outputs
$`\sum_{e}g_{e}\,E_{e}(x)`$, so an expert contributes only in proportion
to its gate weight $`g_{e}`$, and expanding the budget to
$`k^{\prime}{=}N`$ activates every expert without changing the router.
On Qwen3-30B-A3B the default $`k`$ experts therefore still carry
$`26.2\%`$ of the gate mass, about $`4.2\times`$ the uniform-weight
reference $`1/N`$, while the newly activated ones stay below it and
contribute little to the layer output. ECRD neutralises this profile by
moving weight from the head to the lower-ranked experts, suggesting
restored cooperation among the co-activated experts.

Figure 4: Gate weights on Qwen3-30B-A3B during cooperation restoration.
(a) Mean gate weight per expert before and after ECRD, with experts
sorted by their base-model weight. The dashed line marks the
uniform-weight reference $`1/N`$. (b) Mean gate weight of the head, mid,
and tail groups over ECRD training steps in multiples of $`1/N`$.

##### Router ablation.

To further examine which components contribute to recovery, we run the
ECRD stage on Qwen3-30B-A3B while updating only the router, only the
experts, or only the router and experts, and compare them with full
parameter ECRD. Training only the router fails to recover the model,
with entropy staying extremely low and reward declining. As shown in
Table [3](#S6.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Router ablation. ‣ 6.1 Analysis of Expert Cooperation after Activation Expansion ‣ 6 Analyses ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
updating only the experts already recovers the model. Jointly updating
the experts and the router recovers most of the capability of full
parameter ECRD ($`90.4\%`$). This suggests that recovery relies largely
on adapting the co-activated experts. The remaining gap to full
parameter ECRD indicates that other components also contribute. Recovery
therefore depends not only on reweighting the experts through the
router, but also on adapting the experts to their new co-activated
partners.

### 6.2 Why the Teacher Must Be Activation-Expanded

A natural alternative to the activation-expanded teacher
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$ is a teacher obtained by applying
RL directly to $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ under the original activation budget.
We compare the two from two perspectives: the capability ceiling each
teacher can reach, and how much of that capability is transferred to the
final low-$`k`$ model.

##### The activation-expanded teacher reaches a higher capability ceiling.

We compare the two teachers by their training dynamics and held-out
performance. Under the same training budget, CU on the recovered
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ converges to a higher reward with higher
entropy, and outperforms RL on $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ across all domains
and backbones. Detailed comparisons are deferred to
Appendix [J](#A10 "Appendix J Why CU unlocks a higher capability ceiling ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

##### The stronger teacher is also the better teacher.

A stronger model is not automatically a better teacher for reverse
distillation, so we compare the two teacher choices directly. Our
results show that the activation-expanded
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$ consistently serves as the better
teacher, allowing more of the unlocked capability to be transferred back
to the original activation budget. Detailed results are deferred to
Appendix [K](#A11 "Appendix K Analysis of Teacher Choice ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

### 6.3 How Much Capability Survives the Transfer

Our experiments reveal an interesting phenomenon: the final
$`k`$-activation student $`\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\star}`$ can even *surpass*
the activation-expanded teacher $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$
while still activating only $`k`$ experts. Detailed comparisons and
discussion are deferred to
Appendix [L](#A12 "Appendix L Analysis of Student Surpassing the Teacher ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

## 7 Conclusion

In this work, we introduced MoEvolve, a framework that builds a stronger
teacher from an MoE model itself without relying on a stronger external
teacher or human annotations at the process level. The gains from the
expanded model are then distilled back to the original activation
budget. Across a wide range of MoE models and benchmarks, our method
consistently outperforms the initial models and strong RL baselines at
no additional inference cost. Our analysis further shows that the
reverse-distilled model can surpass its own activation-expanded teacher.
Together, these findings offer a practical recipe for turning the latent
capacity of MoE models into real performance gains.

## Limitations

The proposed MoEvolve framework has not yet been validated on
substantially larger MoE models. It has also not been tested on broader
tasks such as open-ended generation, agentic tasks, or long-context
settings. Whether the observed gains transfer to these regimes remains
open. Moreover, constructing a stronger internal teacher relies on
training procedures. Thus, such a teacher cannot be obtained in a
training-free setting. We currently lack a method to directly unlock the
latent expert capacity without additional supervision or optimization.

## Ethical Considerations

All experiments in this work are conducted on publicly available
datasets and open-weight MoE models commonly used in natural language
processing research. No human subjects, personal data, or sensitive
content are involved. We acknowledge that large language models may
carry risks of misuse, bias, or hallucination, and that our framework,
which strengthens a model using only its own latent expert capacity, may
inherit such behaviors from the underlying backbones.

## Appendix B Evaluation Details

### B.1 Datasets

All evaluation datasets use English for natural-language content.

#### B.1.1 Scientific and General Knowledge (ScienceQA)

##### GPQA-main

It contains multiple choice questions written by experts at the graduate
level in biology, physics, and chemistry (CC BY 4.0 license) ([Rein et
al., 2023](#bib.bib49)). We use the main subset to evaluate scientific
knowledge and reasoning.

##### ARC-Challenge

It is the challenging subset of the AI2 Reasoning Challenge, containing
multiple-choice science questions that require knowledge and reasoning
beyond simple retrieval (CC BY-SA 4.0 license) ([Clark et al.,
2018](#bib.bib50)).

##### MMLU-STEM

It comprises the STEM subjects of MMLU, evaluating knowledge through
multiple-choice questions across scientific and technical disciplines
(MIT license) ([Hendrycks et al., 2021a](#bib.bib51)). We use the test
questions from these subjects.

#### B.1.2 Mathematics

##### AMC23

It contains problems from the 2023 American Mathematics Competitions,
covering high school mathematics and reasoning in mathematical
competitions ([math-ai, 2025b](#bib.bib37)). We use the dataset for
research evaluation, subject to MAA’s copyright and applicable usage
requirements.¹¹ 1 MAA’s official AMC website:
[https://maa.org/student-programs/amc/](https://maa.org/student-programs/amc/).

##### GSM8K

It consists of grade-school mathematics word problems requiring
multi-step arithmetic reasoning (MIT license) ([Cobbe et al.,
2021](#bib.bib34)). We use its test split to evaluate OLMoE-1B-7B and
DeepSeek-V2-Lite.

##### MATH500

It is an evaluation subset of MATH containing 500 problems, spanning
subjects such as algebra, geometry, and number theory (MIT license)
([Hendrycks et al., 2021b](#bib.bib35)).

##### Minerva Math

It contains undergraduate mathematics and science problems collected
from MIT OpenCourseWare, with numerical or symbolic solutions that can
be automatically verified (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license for the source course
materials) ([Lewkowycz et al., 2022](#bib.bib36)).

##### PolyMath

It is a multilingual mathematical reasoning benchmark organized into
four difficulty levels (Apache-2.0 license for the official repository)
([Wang et al., 2025b](#bib.bib41)). We use only its English problems,
selecting PolyMath-Low for OLMoE-1B-7B and DeepSeek-V2-Lite and
PolyMath-Medium for GPT-OSS-20B and Qwen3-30B-A3B.

##### AIME

We use problems from the 2024, 2025, and 2026 American Invitational
Mathematics Examinations (Apache-2.0) ([math-ai, 2024](#bib.bib38);
[math-ai, 2025a](#bib.bib39); [math-ai, 2026](#bib.bib40)).

#### B.1.3 Code Generation

##### HumanEval

It evaluates Python function generation from natural-language docstrings
and function signatures (MIT license) ([Chen et al., 2021](#bib.bib42)).

##### MBPP and MBPP-Sanitized

MBPP contains basic Python programming tasks with descriptions in
natural language and unit tests (CC BY 4.0 license) ([Austin et al.,
2021](#bib.bib43)). We evaluate both the full and sanitized versions,
with the latter providing a manually reviewed subset with improved task
descriptions.

##### LiveCodeBench-v6

LiveCodeBench collects recent programming competition problems from
LeetCode, AtCoder, and Codeforces to evaluate code generation while
reducing contamination risk (Creative Commons license; specific variant
and version unspecified) ([Jain et al., 2025](#bib.bib44)). We use the
lite version of the code generation benchmark (v6), which prunes and
samples test cases to reduce dataset size while aiming to preserve
evaluation performance.

### B.2 Decoding and Scoring

All benchmarks are zero-shot at temperature $`1.0`$, top-$`p`$ $`1.0`$,
and a maximum generation length of $`11{,}000`$ tokens. We sample $`10`$
generations per problem and report the average pass@1 accuracy of each
model on each benchmark. We adopt Math-Verify²² 2
[https://github.com/huggingface/Math-Verify](https://github.com/huggingface/Math-Verify)
as a rule-based verifier to validate answer correctness on the
mathematics benchmarks, where a response is correct when its extracted
final answer exactly matches the reference and equivalent surface forms
(e.g., different LaTEX spellings of the same expression, or a fraction
and its decimal) are accepted. On the multiple-choice ScienceQA
benchmarks the extracted option letter has to match the reference
exactly, and on the code generation benchmarks a response counts as
correct only if it passes *all* unit tests of the problem.

Except for the study in
Appendix [I](#A9 "Appendix I Experiments on the Effect of the Number of Activated Experts ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
all experiments of Ours use the maximum feasible intermediate budget
$`k^{\prime}`$.

## Appendix C Implementation Details

### C.1 Training Datasets

We train on three domains, matching the three evaluation domains. For
mathematical reasoning, we mix $`25`$K problems from
[OpenMathInstruct-2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/OpenMathInstruct-2)
([Toshniwal et al., 2025](#bib.bib20)) (CC BY 4.0 license), synthesized
from the GSM8K and MATH training sets and therefore easy to medium
grade-school and high-school exercises, with $`25`$K samples from
[DeepMath-103K](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zwhe99/DeepMath-103K)
([He et al., 2025](#bib.bib21)) (MIT license), drawn from a broader pool
of competition and university-level material, for a total of $`50`$K
training instances. Mixing the two difficulty levels keeps the binary
reward informative on the weak, just-expanded model while leaving
headroom for the stronger ones. For code generation, we use the code
split of
[Eurus-2-RL-Data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PRIME-RL/Eurus-2-RL-Data)
([Cui et al., 2026](#bib.bib22)) (MIT license), which consists of
$`25`$K samples. For scientific and general knowledge, we randomly
sample $`50`$K instances from the
[MMLU-auxiliary_training_set](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cais/mmlu/viewer/auxiliary_train)
([Hendrycks et al., 2021a](#bib.bib51)) (MIT license), which is disjoint
from the MMLU-STEM test split used for evaluation. The same data drives
all the steps, so all methods share one training corpus. To rule out
evaluation contamination, we checked all three corpora against the
held-out benchmarks. OpenMathInstruct-2 is synthesized from the GSM8K
and MATH *training* splits and is therefore disjoint from their test
sets by construction. We additionally verified that no problem has a
near-duplicate (5-gram Jaccard $`\geq 0.8`$) in the AMC23 or
AIME-2024/2025/2026 test sets. For Eurus-2-RL-Data, we checked its
problem statements against LiveCodeBench-v6 with the same near-duplicate
criterion. The MMLU auxiliary training set is a held-out split that is
explicitly disjoint from the MMLU-STEM test set used for ScienceQA
evaluation. No overlap was found after these checks. All training
datasets use English for natural-language content.

### C.3 EMA-Based Stopping Criterion for ECRD

Since the sole purpose of the ECRD step is to re-establish expert
cooperation, we do not fix the number of distillation steps in advance.
Instead, we switch to capability recovery when the distillation
objective stops making meaningful progress. To avoid reacting to noisy
single-step fluctuations, we first maintain an exponential moving
average (EMA) of the absolute distillation objective. Let
$`a_{s}=|\mathcal{J}_{\text{OPD}}^{(s)}|`$ at step $`s`$. We set
$`e_{1}=a_{1}`$ and update

|     |                                       |     |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       e_{s}=\alpha e_{s-1}+(1-\alpha)a_{s},
       ```                                    |     | (5) |

where $`\alpha`$ is the EMA coefficient. We then perform convergence
checks every $`W`$ steps. If $`b`$ is the beginning of the current
window and $`s=b+W`$, we compute the relative EMA drop

|     |                                                 |     |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       r_{s}=\frac{e_{b}-e_{s}}{\max(e_{b},\epsilon)}.
       ```                                              |     | (6) |

A window is marked as converged when $`r_{s}<\rho`$, where $`\rho`$ is a
minimum relative-drop threshold. Otherwise, the convergence counter is
reset. After each check, the window baseline is rolled forward to the
current $`(e_{s},s)`$. Once $`P`$ consecutive windows are marked as
converged, we regard the distillation objective as having reached a
plateau and transition to capability recovery. The complete ECRD
procedure is summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ C.3 EMA-Based Stopping Criterion for ECRD ‣ Appendix C Implementation Details ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation").

1: MoE $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$, target budget $`k^{\prime}>k`$, prompts
$`\mathcal{D}`$, EMA coefficient $`\alpha`$, window size $`W`$,
threshold $`\rho`$, patience $`P`$

2: Recovered activation-expanded model $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$

3: Initialize $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ by changing only the
activation budget from $`k`$ to $`k^{\prime}`$

4: $`e\leftarrow\textsc{None}`$; $`b\leftarrow 1`$; $`c\leftarrow 0`$
$`\triangleright`$ EMA, window start, converged-window counter

5: for distillation step $`s=1,2,\ldots`$ do

6:   Sample trajectories from student $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ on
prompts from $`\mathcal{D}`$

7:   Update $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ with teacher
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ using OPD

8:   $`a_{s}\leftarrow|\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPD}}^{(s)}|`$

9:   $`e\leftarrow a_{s}`$ if $`e`$ is None, otherwise
$`e\leftarrow\alpha e+(1-\alpha)a_{s}`$

10:   if $`s-b=W`$ then

11:    $`r\leftarrow(e_{b}-e)/\max(e_{b},\epsilon)`$

12:    $`c\leftarrow c+1`$ if $`r<\rho`$, otherwise $`c\leftarrow 0`$

13:    $`e_{b}\leftarrow e`$; $`b\leftarrow s`$

14:    if $`c=P`$ then break

15:    end if

16:   end if

17: end for

18: return recovered $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$

## Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets

This section formalizes the intuition used in
§[4](#S4 "4 MoEvolve: Bidirectional On-Policy Optimization for MoE Self-Distillation ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
namely that raising the activation budget from $`k`$ to $`k^{\prime}`$
widens the active intermediate representation of every MoE layer,
exposing more pretrained parameters to each token. We follow the
notation of
§[3](#S3 "3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
where $`\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ is the hidden representation,
$`\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})`$ is the set of top-$`k`$ experts selected
by the router, and $`\tilde{g}_{i}(\mathbf{h})`$ are the normalized
routing weights obtained from
Eq. ([1](#S3.E1 "In Mixture-of-Experts. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")).
Modern MoE LLMs instantiate each expert $`E_{i}`$ as a gated
feed-forward network. Writing $`d_{\mathrm{e}}`$ for the intermediate
width of a single expert,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
E_{i}(\mathbf{h})=W_{2,i}\big(\sigma(W_{g,i}\mathbf{h})\odot W_{u,i}\mathbf{h}\big),
``` |  | (7) |

where $`W_{g,i},W_{u,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{e}}\times d}`$,
$`W_{2,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\mathrm{e}}}`$, $`\sigma`$ is an
elementwise activation, and $`\odot`$ is the elementwise product. We
write
$`\mathbf{z}_{i}(\mathbf{h})=\sigma(W_{g,i}\mathbf{h})\odot W_{u,i}\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{e}}}`$
for the intermediate activation of expert $`i`$. To make the activation
budget explicit we also write $`\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{h})`$ for the
layer output $`\mathbf{y}`$ of
§[3](#S3 "3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")
under budget $`k`$. Substituting
Eq. ([7](#A4.E7 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
into that output gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{h})=\!\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})}\!\!\tilde{g}_{i}(\mathbf{h})\,W_{2,i}\,\mathbf{z}_{i}(\mathbf{h}),
``` |  | (8) |

which is a sum of matrix-vector products. Since
$`\tilde{g}_{i}(\mathbf{h})`$ is a scalar, it can be absorbed into the
output projection as
$`\widehat{W}_{2,i}(\mathbf{h})=\tilde{g}_{i}(\mathbf{h})W_{2,i}`$,
which turns
Eq. ([8](#A4.E8 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
into

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{h})=\!\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})}\!\!\widehat{W}_{2,i}(\mathbf{h})\,\mathbf{z}_{i}(\mathbf{h}).
``` |  | (9) |

Let $`\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{h})=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}`$. Block matrix
multiplication states that for any
$`A_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\mathrm{e}}}`$ and
$`v_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{e}}}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}v_{j}=\big[A_{1}\cdots A_{k}\big]\big[v_{1}^{\top}\cdots v_{k}^{\top}\big]^{\top},
``` |  | (10) |

so the concatenated form below is an identity rather than an additional
assumption. Applying
Eq. ([10](#A4.E10 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
to
Eq. ([9](#A4.E9 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
with $`A_{j}=\widehat{W}_{2,i_{j}}(\mathbf{h})`$ and
$`v_{j}=\mathbf{z}_{i_{j}}(\mathbf{h})`$ yields

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{h})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}_{2}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})\,\mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h}),\quad\text{with}`$ |  | (11) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\big[\widehat{W}_{2,i_{1}}(\mathbf{h})\cdots\widehat{W}_{2,i_{k}}(\mathbf{h})\big],`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\big[\mathbf{z}_{i_{1}}(\mathbf{h})^{\top}\cdots\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}(\mathbf{h})^{\top}\big]^{\top},`$ |  |  |

where
$`\mathbf{W}_{2}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times kd_{\mathrm{e}}}`$
and
$`\mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{R}^{kd_{\mathrm{e}}}`$.
It remains to check that $`\mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})`$ is
itself a gated feed-forward activation of width $`kd_{\mathrm{e}}`$.
Because $`\sigma`$ and $`\odot`$ act elementwise, they commute with
vertical concatenation, so stacking the per-expert projections into
$`\mathbf{W}_{g}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}},\mathbf{W}_{u}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{kd_{\mathrm{e}}\times d}`$
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})=\sigma\big(\mathbf{W}_{g}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathbf{h}\big)\odot\big(\mathbf{W}_{u}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathbf{h}\big).
``` |  | (12) |

Combining
Eqs. ([11](#A4.E11 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
and ([12](#A4.E12 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
gives the explicit form

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{h})=\mathbf{W}_{2}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}(\mathbf{h})\big(\sigma(\mathbf{W}_{g}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathbf{h})\odot\mathbf{W}_{u}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\mathbf{h}\big),
``` |  | (13) |

so the top-$`k`$ MoE layer is exactly a gated feed-forward layer whose
intermediate width is $`kd_{\mathrm{e}}`$, with a block-structured and
routing-dependent output projection.
Eq. ([12](#A4.E12 "In Appendix D Intermediate Activation Width under Larger Activation Budgets ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"))
therefore makes the role of the activation budget explicit. Since
$`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ and $`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}`$ share the same
expert and routing parameters and differ only in how many experts are
activated, moving from $`k`$ to $`k^{\prime}`$ widens the active
intermediate representation from $`\mathbb{R}^{kd_{\mathrm{e}}}`$ to
$`\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}d_{\mathrm{e}}}`$ and adds
$`(k^{\prime}-k)\,d_{\mathrm{e}}`$ active intermediate features per
token without introducing any new parameter. Activation expansion thus
exposes additional capacity that is already encoded in the pretrained
weights but left unused under the original budget, which ECRD and CU are
designed to exploit.

For MoEvolve, the curves are taken from the RD stage, where

almost all inherited expert capacity.

## Appendix H Ablation on the Reverse-Distillation Objective

We ablate the reverse-distillation objective in
Eq. ([3](#S3.E3 "In On-Policy Distillation. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")),
where the recovered activation-expanded model
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$ is the teacher and the original
$`k`$-activation model $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ is the student. We compare
three ways to transfer the teacher’s capability back into the original
activation budget. RFT imitates the teacher’s own correct trajectories
off-policy. OPD instead supervises the student’s own trajectories with
the token-level KL objective in
Eq. ([2](#S3.E2 "In On-Policy Distillation. ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation")),
on-policy but still bounded by the teacher. ExOPD adds reward
extrapolation ([Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)), letting the student go
beyond the teacher.

As shown in
Table [6](#A7.T6 "Table 6 ‣ Appendix G Analysis of Training Dynamics and Expert Load ‣ A Stronger Teacher Within: Strengthening MoE Modelsvia On-Policy Bidirectional Distillation"),
the ranking ExOPD $`>`$ OPD $`>`$ RFT is consistent across backbones.
RFT$`\to`$OPD gains about $`+0.1`$ to $`+0.8`$ average points, while
adding reward extrapolation yields a further $`+1.1`$ to $`+1.5`$
points. This is expected because the activation-expanded teacher
$`\mathcal{M}_{k^{\prime}}^{\star}`$ is stronger than the
original-budget student, leaving $`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ more room to
explore and improve, while reward extrapolation turns that
teacher–student gap into a cleaner token-level learning signal than
plain OPD or the off-policy targets used by RFT.

shows that the student surpasses the teacher in most settings. On

$`\mathcal{M}_{k}`$ to explore and improve, allowing it to go beyond
simple teacher matching relative to the reference model while adapting
````
