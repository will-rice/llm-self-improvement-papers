###### Abstract

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful approach
for training large language model agents on multi-step tasks, yet
reliance on terminal outcome rewards creates two credit-assignment
problems, particularly in long-horizon tasks. First, same-outcome
rollout groups provide no learning signal from terminal rewards. Second,
terminal rewards provide only trajectory-wide feedback, making it
difficult to identify which decisions caused a failure. Recent work
supplements terminal rewards with finer-grained information from
trajectory analysis, such as natural-language reflections on
intermediate decisions and errors. However, natural-language diagnoses
are difficult to use directly for credit assignment: their error claims
may be unreliable, and they do not quantify how much each error should
affect learning. We propose Self-Diagnosis-guided Terminal Credit
Redistribution (FAULT), which turns diagnosed errors into explicit
step-level credit anchored by terminal outcomes. FAULT checks diagnostic
evidence and learns relative error costs from task outcomes. During
training, the policy and self-diagnoser co-evolve, while error costs are
updated online from recent outcomes. On ALFWorld, FAULT recovers
learning signals from same-outcome groups, reaching 95% signal coverage
versus 41% for GRPO and 72% for GiGPO, while better localizing credit to
specific error steps. Across two model scales, FAULT delivers strong
improvements on the long-horizon ALFWorld and WebShop tasks while
remaining competitive on short-horizon Search-based QA.

## 1 Introduction

Large language model agents solve increasingly complex tasks through
sequences of decisions: they reason, interact with external
environments, observe feedback, and adapt their subsequent actions
\[[48](#bib.bib36)\]. Agentic reinforcement learning improves such
behavior by optimizing these interaction trajectories, typically using
rewards based on the final task outcome \[[53](#bib.bib17)\]. Terminal
outcome supervision is widely used \[[17](#bib.bib19), [27](#bib.bib20),
[9](#bib.bib21), [39](#bib.bib22), [10](#bib.bib2)\] because final
outcomes are often easier to verify than intermediate decisions, but it
creates a fundamental credit-assignment problem: a single reward must
supervise many intermediate decisions that may have contributed very
differently to the final result. As trajectories grow longer, the reward
becomes increasingly distant from the decisions it is meant to improve.

This limitation is especially pronounced in group-relative RL methods
such as GRPO \[[33](#bib.bib1), [13](#bib.bib18)\], which normalize
terminal rewards across multiple rollouts of the same task, and
broadcast each rollout-level advantage to all of its steps. This
produces two distinct credit-assignment limitations. First, when all
rollouts in a group receive the same terminal outcome, their normalized
advantages vanish, so the group provides no outcome-driven learning
signal, even if the trajectories differ substantially in their
intermediate decisions. In our preliminary ALFWorld \[[35](#bib.bib5)\]
runs, such same-outcome groups account for 59–64% of training groups.
Second, even when a group contains both successes and failures, the
resulting trajectory-level advantage does not identify which
intermediate decisions were responsible for the outcome: all steps
within a rollout receive the same signal. As trajectories become longer,
this makes it increasingly difficult to distinguish decisive errors from
otherwise reasonable actions.

A natural way to address these limitations is to introduce process-level
signals that provide finer-grained feedback than terminal rewards
\[[22](#bib.bib15), [37](#bib.bib34), [32](#bib.bib35)\]. One line of
work constructs numerical signals for intermediate steps, using learned
reward models \[[41](#bib.bib27)\], Monte Carlo estimates
\[[6](#bib.bib26)\], hindsight-based scores \[[23](#bib.bib44),
[46](#bib.bib47)\], or comparisons across related states
\[[10](#bib.bib2)\]. However, many such signals are still derived from
terminal outcomes and therefore lose discriminative power when outcomes
are identical. A complementary line of work analyzes trajectories in
natural language, using reflection \[[34](#bib.bib14), [55](#bib.bib39),
[56](#bib.bib46), [40](#bib.bib3)\], hindsight \[[1](#bib.bib40)\], or
error diagnosis to identify what went wrong and where
\[[52](#bib.bib29), [54](#bib.bib30)\]. These analyses provide richer
information about intermediate decisions, but remain descriptive: they
are not directly expressed as quantitative step-level credit, and their
error claims require heavy verification before being used for learning.

diagnoser and prices co-evolve through shared model updates and online

Turning process information into effective credit assignment requires
solving two key challenges. First, the process signal must be
trustworthy: an error diagnosis can misidentify either what went wrong
or where it occurred, causing credit to be assigned to the wrong step.
This is a practical concern in agent traces, where even strong models
can struggle to jointly identify and localize errors. Second,
identifying an error does not determine how much that error should
matter for learning. Different error types may have very different
effects on task success, and multiple errors within the same trajectory
should not deserve equal weight. Thus, useful step-level credit requires
both verifiable localization of errors and outcome-grounded calibration
of their relative importance.

We propose Self-Diagnosis-guided Terminal Credit Redistribution (FAULT),
which turns self-diagnosed errors into explicit step-level credit, while
retaining terminal outcomes as the source of supervision. The key idea
is to separate error localization from error valuation: self-diagnosis
identifies what went wrong and where, while terminal outcomes determine
how costly different error types are. FAULT first performs diagnosing,
which converts trajectory analyses into structured error records and
verifies each diagnosis against evidence from the cited step. It then
performs pricing, which learns relative error costs from observed task
outcomes, and uses these costs to finally redistribute a bounded penalty
budget across the diagnosed steps. Because the learned error costs can
be applied even when all rollouts receive the same terminal outcome,
FAULT can distinguish trajectories that would otherwise receive
identical credit, and can concentrate learning signal on the steps most
in need of correction.

We summarize how FAULT addresses the two credit-assignment problems and
how these improvements translate into downstream performance in Figure
[1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
First, FAULT substantially increases training signal coverage: on
ALFWorld, $`95\%`$ of rollout groups provide non-negligible credit
contrast, compared with $`41\%`$ for GRPO and $`72\%`$ for GiGPO.
Second, FAULT better localizes credit to the decisions responsible for
failure, achieving a decisive-step MRR of $`0.494`$, compared with
$`0.303`$ for GiGPO and $`0.266`$ for random ranking. Finally, across
two model scales, FAULT achieves the strongest improvements on the
longer-horizon ALFWorld \[[35](#bib.bib5)\] and WebShop
\[[47](#bib.bib37)\] tasks while remaining competitive on the much
shorter Search-based QA tasks. These results demonstrate the
effectiveness of our finer-grained credit assignment becoming more
important as trajectories grow longer.

## 2 Method

We present Self-Diagnosis-guided Terminal Credit Redistribution (FAULT),
a framework for explicit step-level credit assignment in agentic RL. A
cold start initializes a shared actor and diagnoser through diagnosis
SFT and learns initial error prices; RL then locates errors through
structured self-diagnosis, fits their prices from terminal outcomes, and
redistributes a bounded penalty across the diagnosed steps.

### 2.1 Problem Formulation

An LLM policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ solves task $`q\sim\mathcal{D}`$ through
repeated interaction with an external world. At step $`t`$, it generates
response $`a_{q,i,t}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid o_{q,i,t})`$ from context
$`o_{q,i,t}`$ containing the task, interaction history and feedback so
far; the world executes the response and returns feedback, appended to
the context. Each response-feedback pair forms an _active interaction
step_. Task $`q`$’s $`i`$-th rollout is
$`\tau_{q,i}=(o_{q,i,1},a_{q,i,1},\ldots,o_{q,i,T_{q,i}},a_{q,i,T_{q,i}})`$,
with $`T_{q,i}`$ such steps. Following group-based RL, we sample $`G`$
rollouts per task from frozen behaviour policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{old}}`$.

Each trajectory receives terminal reward $`R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}`$ only
at termination, giving the outcome objective

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
J_{\mathrm{out}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\mathcal{D}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{q,i}\sim\pi_{\theta}}\big[R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}\big].
``` |  | (1) |

This reward is the only judgement guaranteed correct; we preserve it and
redistribute only its credit across steps.

### 2.2 Cold Start: Verifiable Diagnosis and Diagnosis SFT

Before RL, a one-time cold start fixes the error taxonomy, teaches the
policy to produce verifiable diagnoses of its completed trajectories,
and estimates initial error prices. Pricing errors from terminal
outcomes
(§[2.3](#S2.SS3 "2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
and penalizing diagnosed steps
(§[2.4](#S2.SS4 "2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
require diagnoses that are countable across trajectories and checkable
against each one. Free-form reflections lack this structure, so we map
error claims to fixed classes and check evidence from their cited steps.

Teacher diagnosis. Teacher model GPT-5.6 Sol \[[25](#bib.bib55)\] reads
initial-policy trajectories and outcomes, recording each error’s label,
step and an exact quote from that step.

Verification. We check each report’s step and quote against the
trajectory. Accepted labels form a fixed taxonomy, from which we select
$`K`$ classes with enough examples for pricing:
$`\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{K}\}`$ (Appendix
[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Cold Start: Taxonomy, Diagnosis SFT and Initial Prices ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Teacher and later policy diagnoses of $`\tau_{q,i}`$ are represented as
entries $`(k,t,e)`$: error class, step and quote. Verified entries of
priced class $`k`$ give count $`n_{q,i,k}`$ and per-step rate
$`x_{q,i,k}=n_{q,i,k}/T_{q,i}`$; the rates form $`\mathbf{x}_{q,i}`$
(Appendix
[7.2](#S7.SS2 "7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

Diagnosis SFT. For each cold-start example
$`(q,i)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sft}}`$ with an accepted teacher
diagnosis, we fine-tune $`\pi_{\theta}`$ to reproduce its verified text
$`\mathbf{d}_{q,i}`$ given trajectory $`\tau_{q,i}`$ and success label
$`y_{q,i}\in\{0,1\}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta)=-\,\mathbb{E}_{(q,i)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sft}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{|\mathbf{d}_{q,i}|}\log\pi_{\theta}\big(d_{q,i,\ell}\mid\tau_{q,i},y_{q,i},\mathbf{d}_{q,i,<\ell}\big),
``` |  | (2) |

Only diagnosis tokens are supervised; prompts include the frozen
taxonomy, trajectory and outcome (Appendix
[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Cold Start: Taxonomy, Diagnosis SFT and Initial Prices ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
The checkpoint initializes the RL actor and diagnoser; the same verified
diagnoses and outcomes initialize prices via the estimator in
§[2.3](#S2.SS3 "2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
No teacher is used thereafter.

### 2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing

Locating an error does not reveal its cost, so FAULT regresses terminal
outcomes on verified error rates
(§[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Cold Start: Verifiable Diagnosis and Diagnosis SFT ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
to estimate an outcome association coefficient $`\beta_{k}`$ for each
class. A more negative $`\beta_{k}`$ links higher error rates to worse
outcomes, holding other features fixed. We convert these coefficients to
nonnegative relative error prices $`w_{k}`$
(§[2.4](#S2.SS4 "2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

Tasks differ in difficulty, so we compare successful and failed rollouts
within each task. We take the observed number of successes as given and
use error rates to explain which rollouts succeed, without separately
estimating each task’s baseline success rate. For group $`q`$, let
$`\mathcal{Y}_{q}`$ be the successes retained by the diagnosis gate
(Appendix
[7.2](#S7.SS2 "7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
and $`\mathcal{A}_{q}`$ all candidate success sets $`\Gamma`$ drawn from
all retained trajectories with $`|\Gamma|=|\mathcal{Y}_{q}|`$. This
gives the observed success-set probability and likelihood objective over
informative groups $`\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{info}}`$, containing both
successes and failures:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\nolimits_{q}(\bm{\beta})=\frac{\exp\big(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{Y}_{q}}\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{q,i}\big)}{\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}\exp\big(\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{q,i}\big)},\qquad\bm{\beta}^{\star}=\argmin_{\bm{\beta}}\Big[-\!\!\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{info}}}\!\!\log\Pr\nolimits_{q}(\bm{\beta})\Big].
``` |  | (3) |

For binary outcomes, these coefficients are fitted by bounded
quasi-Newton optimization with a small-sample correction and shrinkage
toward a fixed anchor: initially on teacher-diagnosed trajectories
(§[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Cold Start: Verifiable Diagnosis and Diagnosis SFT ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
then periodically on a sliding window of recent RL rollouts (derivation,
estimator, acceptance test and update rule in Appendix
[7.3](#S7.SS3 "7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

### 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution

Verified errors can differ even when all rollouts in a group succeed or
fail, and can provide step-specific process signals when priced. In each
batch, a frozen policy generates and diagnoses a rollout group per task.
Using fixed error prices, we set each trajectory’s bounded total
penalty, redistribute it to diagnosed steps, and use each step’s share
to form group-relative advantages for policy learning.

Self-diagnosis of on-policy rollouts. After each rollout, the actor
diagnoses its completed trajectory given the observed outcome, using the
format of
§[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Cold Start: Verifiable Diagnosis and Diagnosis SFT ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
Verified errors are aggregated into error-rate vector
$`\mathbf{x}_{q,i}`$.

Bounded budget. We cap the total penalty below the success–failure
reward gap for binary outcomes, so steps from successful trajectories
retain higher episode scores than those from failures. Before this cap,
fixed prices turn each trajectory’s verified diagnosis into a score
$`S_{q,i}\in[0,1]`$ and budget $`P_{q,i}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
P_{q,i}=\lambda_{u}S_{q,i}=\lambda_{u}\,\clip\!\Big(\sum_{k}w_{k}\,x_{q,i,k},\,0,\,1\Big),\quad w_{k}=\begin{cases}|\beta_{k}|/\max_{j:\,\beta_{j}<0}|\beta_{j}|,&\beta_{k}<0,\\[1.0pt]
0,&\beta_{k}\geq 0.\end{cases}
``` |  | (4) |

Here $`x_{q,i,k}=n_{q,i,k}/T_{q,i}`$ is the verified error rate, and
$`w_{k}`$ is the nonnegative error price derived from $`\beta_{k}`$
(§[2.3](#S2.SS3 "2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Using rates rather than counts avoids rewarding the RL actor for shorter
trajectories solely because longer ones accumulate more diagnosed
errors. $`\lambda_{u}`$ sets batch $`u`$’s penalty strength; Appendices
[7.3](#S7.SS3 "7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
and
[7.4](#S7.SS4 "7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
detail the price mapping, schedule and cap.

Conserved redistribution. The budget is split in proportion to step
localization weights $`h_{q,i,t}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{q,i,t}=P_{q,i}\,\frac{h_{q,i,t}}{\sum_{r=1}^{T_{q,i}}h_{q,i,r}},\qquad h_{q,i,t}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{V}_{q,i,t}}w_{k(e)},
``` |  | (5) |

Here $`\mathcal{V}_{q,i,t}`$ contains verified errors cited at step
$`t`$, and $`k(e)`$ is entry $`e`$’s class. Their summed prices give the
raw step penalty weight $`h_{q,i,t}`$; normalization to $`P_{q,i}`$
gives $`\delta_{q,i,t}`$, the penalty allocated to step $`t`$ for the
policy update (exact form in Appendix
[7.5](#S7.SS5 "7.5 Conserved Step Redistribution ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

Dual-channel advantage. Task-wide comparisons provide overall outcome
guidance but use a baseline shared across different contexts. We
therefore add comparisons within similar contexts to give each decision
a more relevant reference. Following GiGPO \[[10](#bib.bib2)\], the
episode channel compares each active step with $`\Omega_{q}`$, all
task-$`q`$ step samples $`(j,r)`$; the step channel uses the
anchor-state group $`\mathcal{H}(o_{q,i,t})\subseteq\Omega_{q}`$ of
samples with the same or similar pre-response context (Appendix
[7.6](#S7.SS6 "7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Both channels use the same step penalty; group centering cancels any
penalty shared by all its samples. The episode channel centers terminal
reward minus the step’s share within the task group,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i,t}=E_{q,i,t}-\frac{1}{|\Omega_{q}|}\!\sum_{(j,r)\in\Omega_{q}}\!E_{q,j,r},\qquad E_{q,i,t}=R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-\delta_{q,i,t},
``` |  | (6) |

and the step channel centers discounted return minus the step penalty
within the anchor-state group,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A^{\mathrm{step}}_{q,i,t}=\widetilde{Z}_{q,i,t}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}(o_{q,i,t})|}\!\sum_{(j,r)\in\mathcal{H}(o_{q,i,t})}\!\widetilde{Z}_{q,j,r},\qquad\widetilde{Z}_{q,i,t}=\gamma^{\,T_{q,i}-t}R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-\delta_{q,i,t},
``` |  | (7) |

Policy update. The joint step advantage
$`A_{q,i,t}=A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i,t}+\omega A^{\mathrm{step}}_{q,i,t}`$,
with step-channel weight $`\omega`$, is broadcast uniformly to step
$`t`$’s response tokens $`\mathcal{U}_{q,i,t}`$. The actor minimizes the
clipped policy-gradient objective over all $`N`$ response tokens in the
batch:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{actor}}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(q,i,t)}\ \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{U}_{q,i,t}}\Big[\min\big(\varrho_{\ell}A_{q,i,t},\ \clip(\varrho_{\ell},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\,A_{q,i,t}\big)-\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}\,D^{\mathrm{LV}}_{\ell}\Big],
``` |  | (8) |

where
$`\varrho_{\ell}=\pi_{\theta}(a_{\ell}\mid c_{\ell})/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(a_{\ell}\mid c_{\ell})`$
is token $`\ell`$’s importance ratio against the rollout policy given
context $`c_{\ell}`$, and $`D^{\mathrm{LV}}_{\ell}`$ is a low-variance
KL estimate to frozen reference policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ at that
token (Appendix
[7.6](#S7.SS6 "7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

Co-evolution of diagnoser, prices and policy. The diagnoser shares the
actor’s weights, but its diagnosis tokens receive no policy gradient,
preventing a direct RL shortcut of reporting fewer errors to avoid
penalties. After each actor update, the batch’s verified records
$`(q,\mathbf{x}_{q,i},y_{q,i})`$ enter a sliding window; with enough
informative groups, prices are periodically refitted online via Eq.
([3](#S2.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
with the regularization and smoothing of Appendix
[7.3.5](#S7.SS3.SSS5 "7.3.5 Online Refits and Price Updates ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
The updated model generates and diagnoses new rollouts, and the latest
prices turn these diagnoses into step penalties for subsequent policy
updates, closing the loop (Algorithm
[1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 7.7 Algorithm ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
in Appendix
[7.7](#S7.SS7 "7.7 Algorithm ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

## 3 Experiments

### 3.1 Experimental Setting

We evaluate FAULT on three benchmarks. Further details are in Appendix
[8](#S8 "8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

Benchmarks. ALFWorld \[[35](#bib.bib5)\] spans six text-based household
task families; we train on standard games and test on unseen tasks
within $`30`$ steps. In WebShop \[[47](#bib.bib37)\], the agent searches
$`1{,}000`$ products and buys one matching a natural-language request
within $`15`$ steps. Search-based QA follows Search-R1
\[[17](#bib.bib19)\]: the agent searches for evidence to answer
questions from NQ \[[20](#bib.bib48)\], TriviaQA \[[18](#bib.bib49)\],
PopQA \[[24](#bib.bib50)\], HotpotQA \[[45](#bib.bib51)\],
2WikiMultiHopQA \[[15](#bib.bib52)\], MuSiQue \[[36](#bib.bib53)\] and
Bamboogle \[[26](#bib.bib54)\].

Baselines. Vanilla is the backbone without task-specific training. GRPO
\[[33](#bib.bib1)\] uses group-normalized outcome rewards and GiGPO
\[[10](#bib.bib2)\] adds step credit from anchor-state comparisons; both
start from the raw backbone. SEED \[[40](#bib.bib3)\] uses its own
teacher-written hindsight-skill SFT data and distils skill-conditioned

### 3.3 Analysis

We analyze signal coverage and strength, step-level credit, and training
trajectory length.

#### 3.3.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups

##### Signal coverage.

On ALFWorld, each prompt’s eight rollouts form a *same-outcome* group
(all-fail or all-success) or a *mixed* group. Same-outcome groups make
up $`59`$–$`64\%`$ of training groups, rising to about $`75\%`$ by the
end of GiGPO and FAULT training. To compare scales, we measure each
group’s credit gap, its highest minus lowest credit value. Mixed groups
provide outcome contrast, so a group is usable if its gap exceeds
$`5\%`$ of that method’s mean mixed-group gap, allowing weak but
non-negligible signals (Appendix
[9.1](#S9.SS1 "9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
From FAULT’s diagnosis-SFT checkpoint, GRPO, GiGPO and FAULT find usable
signal in $`41.4\%`$, $`72.5\%`$ and $`95.1\%`$ of all groups (solid
bars, Figure
[1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(a)).

##### Signal strength.

In each $`20`$-update window, we average the credit gaps of all-success,
all-fail and mixed groups separately. Dividing each mean by the method’s
mixed-group mean in that window expresses signal strength relative to
mixed groups. We then weight each ratio by that group type’s share of
the window’s groups. The three contributions sum to the signal-retention
Index; mixed groups contribute exactly their share, as their ratio is
one (Appendix
[9.1](#S9.SS1 "9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
From the first to last window, FAULT’s all-success share rises from
$`11.6\%`$ to $`70.3\%`$, while its mixed and all-fail shares fall from
$`42.2\%`$ to $`24.1\%`$ and $`46.3\%`$ to $`5.6\%`$. Its all-success
contribution rises from $`0.184`$ to $`0.668`$, keeping the total near
$`0.9`$ (Figure
[3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
At $`72.5\%`$ coverage, GiGPO’s pooled Index is $`0.421`$ (same-outcome
contribution $`0.063`$), near GRPO’s $`0.419`$, versus FAULT’s $`0.821`$
(same-outcome contribution $`0.449`$).

##### Why outcome credit falls short.

All mixed groups qualify, but same-outcome groups expose the limits of
outcome credit. GRPO uses none because subtracting the group mean
cancels equal rewards. GiGPO uses about $`75\%`$ of all-success but no
all-fail groups: discounting separates rollouts by steps left to success
from shared anchor states, while failures return zero. FAULT uses
$`90\%`$ and $`100\%`$, respectively, as its diagnostic penalties
distinguish trajectories despite equal outcomes (derivations in Appendix
[9.1](#S9.SS1 "9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Successes can still contain correctable process errors: the audit finds
$`61.85\%`$ of FAULT’s successes ($`8{,}719/14{,}097`$) penalized while
keeping their success labels (Appendix
[9.2](#S9.SS2 "9.2 Penalties on Successful Trajectories ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
The strength comparison uses budget gaps for FAULT and advantage gaps
for baselines, so the pooled $`0.821`$ is a contrast proxy, not a
common-scale advantage estimate (Appendix
[9.1](#S9.SS1 "9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

#### 3.3.2 Step-Level Localization of Credit

Section
[3.3.1](#S3.SS3.SSS1 "3.3.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 3.3 Analysis ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
asked whether each group keeps a training signal. Here we ask how this
signal is split across a trajectory’s steps and whether it falls on the
right steps.

##### Credit placement.

An independent LLM judge reads each sampled failed training trajectory
and marks its decisive error step, the earliest step whose correction
would most likely turn the failure into a success. Each method ranks a
trajectory’s steps by the penalty it gave them. Figure
[1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(b)
reports the mean reciprocal rank of the judge’s step,
$`\mathrm{MRR}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}1/\mathrm{rank}_{i}`$ (allowing
one step of error), and Figure
[4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(a)
shows the offset of the most penalized step from the judge’s step
(Appendix
[9.3](#S9.SS3 "9.3 Step-Level Localization: Measurement and Additional Views ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
FAULT reaches an MRR of $`0.494`$ versus $`0.266`$ for random ranking,
and its most penalized step is exactly the judge’s step in $`82`$ of
$`300`$ trajectories. GiGPO reaches $`0.303`$ with $`21`$ exact matches,
and its most penalized step usually comes several steps after the
judge’s; GRPO stays close to random.

##### Credit concentration.

The normalized entropy measures how evenly a trajectory’s credit is
spread over its steps. We normalize each step’s absolute credit (penalty
shares for FAULT, advantages for baselines) into a distribution and
divide its entropy by the log of the step count, so it is $`1`$ for an
even spread and $`0`$ when all credit is on one step (Appendix
[9.3](#S9.SS3 "9.3 Step-Level Localization: Measurement and Additional Views ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
On same-outcome groups, the mean normalized entropy is $`0.29`$ for
FAULT versus $`0.72`$ for GiGPO and $`0.91`$ for GRPO, and $`28\%`$ of
FAULT’s penalized trajectories put the whole penalty on one step (Figure
[4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(b)).

##### Why step credit differs.

GRPO gives every step of a trajectory the same credit. GiGPO’s credit
varies across steps but comes from comparing returns at states shared by
several rollouts: a failed rollout is penalized at every state that a
successful rollout of the same task also reached, and in all-fail groups
this difference disappears (Proposition
[2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Credit in same-outcome groups). ‣ 9.1.2 Credit under Equal Terminal Rewards ‣ 9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
FAULT penalizes only the steps cited by verified diagnoses. This
explains both results: equal credit keeps GRPO near random and the most
spread out, GiGPO’s state-based credit is more concentrated but rarely
hits the decisive error, and FAULT’s credit is both the most
concentrated and the best placed. Appendices
[9.3](#S9.SS3 "9.3 Step-Level Localization: Measurement and Additional Views ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),
[9.4](#S9.SS4 "9.4 Penalties on Wasted Steps ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
and
[11.1](#S11.SS1 "11.1 Credit in an All-Fail Group ‣ 11 Case Studies ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
give more results.

### 3.5 Diagnostic Quality Before and During RL

Assessment protocol. We assess teacher diagnoses and taxonomy coverage
on the $`2{,}880`$ ALFWorld cold-start trajectories collected with
Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, and evaluate the diagnosis-SFT checkpoint on the
$`200`$ stratified held-out trajectories (Appendix
[8.3.1](#S8.SS3.SSS1 "8.3.1 ALFWorld ‣ 8.3 Cold-Start Data and Initialization ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Separately, we test for degradation during RL as $`\lambda`$ anneals
using $`17`$ frozen checkpoints at updates $`0`$–$`160`$ and $`n{=}600`$
diagnoses per checkpoint. This assessment uses single-seed training
rollouts and a fixed reference from the original teacher’s diagnoses,
unchanged throughout training.

Cold-start results. Evidence verification retains $`13{,}820`$ of
$`14{,}484`$ teacher error mentions ($`95.4\%`$); every rejected mention
fails the quote check. Successful and failed trajectories contain an
average of $`3.4`$ and $`5.1`$ verified errors, respectively, showing
that the teacher reports process errors even in successful trajectories.
The frozen taxonomy covers $`90.6\%`$ of accepted mentions. On the
held-out set, the initial self-diagnoser achieves L1 precision $`0.81`$
and recall $`0.56`$, above the respective thresholds of $`0.75`$ and
$`0.50`$. Dominant-error step accuracy is $`0.48`$ under a
$`\pm 2`$-step tolerance, below the $`0.60`$ threshold, and the
off-table rate is $`0.31`$, above the $`0.10`$ limit. Only precision and
recall meet their criteria; step localization and off-table predictions
remain limitations.

## 4 Related Work

Outcome-based agentic RL. Agentic RL typically trains LLM policies from
terminal rewards \[[53](#bib.bib17)\], often with GRPO
\[[33](#bib.bib1)\], which gives each rollout’s group-normalized outcome
advantage to all its steps. Same-outcome groups therefore yield no
outcome-driven gradient \[[51](#bib.bib6), [43](#bib.bib25)\]. Agentic
variants change how rollouts are sampled or compared \[[8](#bib.bib24),
[10](#bib.bib2)\], but still derive credit from outcomes.

Process-based agentic RL. Step rewards improve on outcome rewards in
mathematical reasoning \[[22](#bib.bib15), [37](#bib.bib34)\], and agent
RL now scores steps with learned models, rollouts or hindsight
\[[6](#bib.bib26), [41](#bib.bib27), [19](#bib.bib28), [23](#bib.bib44),
[46](#bib.bib47)\]. Learned step rewards invite reward hacking
\[[13](#bib.bib18)\], and GiGPO’s outcome credit vanishes in all-fail
groups \[[10](#bib.bib2)\]. Language analyses of finished trajectories
can instead guide later attempts \[[34](#bib.bib14), [55](#bib.bib39)\],
correct training data \[[52](#bib.bib29), [54](#bib.bib30)\] or be
distilled into the policy \[[40](#bib.bib3)\]. Yet the analysis stays
text, its error claims are unchecked, and its weight is not learned from
outcomes. FAULT keeps the terminal reward as the trusted signal and
closes these gaps with verified diagnoses, outcome-fitted prices and
bounded step credit (Appendix
[12](#S12 "12 Extended Related Work ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

## 5 Conclusion

Agentic RL has advanced rapidly in training agents for multi-step tasks,
yet terminal rewards offer limited signals in same-outcome groups and
little guidance on individual errors, particularly in long-horizon
tasks. Natural-language diagnoses provide process information but need
verification and quantification. FAULT addresses this gap by checking
diagnostic evidence and learning relative error costs from terminal
outcomes to assign explicit step-level credit. The policy and diagnoser
co-evolve during RL. On ALFWorld, FAULT raises usable signal coverage to
$`95\%`$, versus $`41\%`$ for GRPO and $`72\%`$ for GiGPO, and achieves
a decisive-step MRR of $`0.494`$, versus GiGPO’s $`0.303`$. At both
model scales, it leads in ALFWorld success rate and WebShop task score
while remaining competitive on shorter-horizon Search-based QA. Its
ALFWorld training trajectories are also shorter than those of GRPO and
SEED.

### 7.1 Cold Start: Taxonomy, Diagnosis SFT and Initial Prices

Before RL, a one-time cold start produces a frozen error taxonomy, a
diagnosis-SFT checkpoint and initial error prices. Appendix
[8.5](#S8.SS5 "8.5 Diagnosis Prompt ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
gives the diagnosis prompt; Appendix
[8](#S8 "8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
provides the data and training settings.

Teacher diagnosis. We collect training trajectories from the base policy
and, optionally, early checkpoints trained with outcome-only RL. A
teacher diagnoses each trajectory once using greedy decoding, given its
task, outcome, reward and step count. Each error report identifies a
mechanism through a free-form label, a brief explanation, cited steps, a
verbatim quote and a severity hint. The teacher is used only during cold
start.

Taxonomy construction. An LLM organizes the accepted labels into L1
error families and L2 mechanisms. Starting from an empty table, a growth
pass processes labels in descending frequency, matching existing
mechanisms or adding new classes when uncertain. A second pass assigns
the remaining labels to existing classes or marks them as off-table.
Rare L2 classes are merged back into their L1 families, and the taxonomy
is frozen. We select $`K`$ pricing classes $`\mathcal{C}`$ from the
retained L2 classes and L1 fallback categories using minimum verified
counts and frequency-rank thresholds. Selection ensures data support;
the fitted price may still be zero.

Diagnosis SFT. Teacher diagnoses mapped to the frozen taxonomy become
the structured targets $`\mathbf{d}_{q,i}`$. We reserve a held-out set
stratified by checkpoint, outcome and task type; the remaining diagnoses
form $`\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sft}}`$, supplemented during training by a
few action-replay examples from successful trajectories. Each diagnosis
input contains the rules, taxonomy, task, outcome and trajectory, with
the teacher’s JSON as its target. We fine-tune all model parameters
using Eq.
([2](#S2.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 2.2 Cold Start: Verifiable Diagnosis and Diagnosis SFT ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"));
the diagnosis targets contain no prices, scores or advantages.

Initial prices. The verified teacher diagnoses also provide the error
rates used with terminal outcomes to fit $`\widehat{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}`$
and obtain $`\mathbf{w}^{(1)}`$. Appendix
[7.3.4](#S7.SS3.SSS4 "7.3.4 Cold-Start Fit and Initialization ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
details this fit, which learns prices separately from diagnosis SFT.

### 7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation

Online diagnosis provides error rates for pricing and step evidence for
penalty allocation.

Diagnosis generation. Before each actor update, the unchanged rollout
policy samples $`M`$ diagnoses per completed trajectory at a fixed
temperature, given the frozen taxonomy, task, outcome and trajectory.
Each diagnosis is JSON containing the reported outcome and a bounded
list of entries $`(k,t,e)`$: class, step and quote. Free-form labels
outside the taxonomy are logged separately and excluded from scoring.

Entry verification. An entry is verified when its class belongs to the
frozen taxonomy (L2, or an L1 fallback when L2 is absent), its step
$`t\in\{1,\ldots,T_{q,i}\}`$ was executed, and its quote meets the
minimum length and matches a verbatim, case-sensitive substring of that
step’s text (observation, response and environment feedback). Sampling
settings and verification thresholds are listed in Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

Trajectory selection. A trajectory passes the gate when at least two
diagnoses are parsable JSON and a majority of the parsable diagnoses
correctly report its outcome. If it fails, it is excluded from price
fitting; its score $`S_{q,i}`$ is filled with the mean score of
same-outcome trajectories in the batch and marked as imputed.

Error aggregation. We map verified entries to pricing classes. Let
$`\mathcal{M}_{q,i}`$ be the parsable diagnoses of $`\tau_{q,i}`$,
$`M_{q,i}=|\mathcal{M}_{q,i}|`$, and $`n_{q,i,m,k}`$ the number of
verified class-$`k`$ entries in diagnosis $`m`$, counting repeated
entries separately. We average these counts across all parsable
diagnoses and normalize by the number of executed steps:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
n_{q,i,k}=\frac{1}{M_{q,i}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{q,i}}n_{q,i,m,k},\qquad x_{q,i,k}=\frac{n_{q,i,k}}{T_{q,i}}.
``` |  | (9) |

Averaging retains a verified error reported by only one diagnosis, but
reduces its contribution. We also record $`k^{*}_{q,i}`$, the class of
the earliest verified error across all parsable diagnoses. The rates
form the pricing record $`(q,\mathbf{x}_{q,i},y_{q,i})`$; for graded
outcomes, the normalized reward replaces $`y_{q,i}`$ as defined in
Appendix
[7.3.2](#S7.SS3.SSS2 "7.3.2 Regularized Coefficient Estimation ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

### 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing

We estimate how verified error rates relate to terminal outcomes while
accounting for differences in task difficulty. We first remove the
task-specific intercept, derive the regularized estimators, and then
describe how their coefficients produce initial prices and online
updates.

#### 7.3.1 Conditional Likelihood

Let $`\mathcal{G}_{q}`$ contain the trajectories retained for fitting in
group $`q`$, with error-rate vectors $`\mathbf{x}_{q,i}`$ and binary
outcomes $`y_{q,i}`$. The observed success set is
$`\mathcal{Y}_{q}=\{i\in\mathcal{G}_{q}:y_{q,i}=1\}`$, with size
$`s_{q}=|\mathcal{Y}_{q}|`$. We use a logistic model with shared error
coefficients $`\bm{\beta}`$ and a task-specific intercept
$`\alpha_{q}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{q,i}=\Pr(y_{q,i}=1\mid\mathbf{x}_{q,i},q)=\sigmoid(\alpha_{q}+\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{q,i}).
``` |  | (10) |

Conditioning on $`s_{q}`$ removes $`\alpha_{q}`$ without estimating a
separate difficulty parameter for every group \[[5](#bib.bib9)\]. To
show this, define the candidate success sets and their feature sums as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}_{q}=\{\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}:|\Gamma|=s_{q}\},\qquad\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma}=\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\mathbf{x}_{q,i}.
``` |  |

Assuming conditionally independent outcomes under the model, the
probability that exactly the trajectories in $`\Gamma`$ succeed is

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Pr(\Gamma\mid\alpha_{q},\bm{\beta})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in\Gamma}p_{q,i}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\setminus\Gamma}(1-p_{q,i})`$ |  | (11) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\!\big(|\Gamma|\alpha_{q}+\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma}\big)}{\prod_{i\in\mathcal{G}_{q}}\big[1+\exp(\alpha_{q}+\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{q,i})\big]},`$ |  |  |

where conditioning on the group’s feature vectors is implicit. The
denominator is common to all candidate sets. After conditioning on the
observed success count, this denominator cancels, followed by the common
factor $`\exp(s_{q}\alpha_{q})`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Pr\!\left(\mathcal{Y}_{q}\,\middle|\,\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}_{q}}y_{q,i}=s_{q}\right)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\Pr(\mathcal{Y}_{q}\mid\alpha_{q},\bm{\beta})}{\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}\Pr(\Gamma\mid\alpha_{q},\bm{\beta})}`$ |  | (12) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(s_{q}\alpha_{q})\exp(\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{f}_{q,\mathcal{Y}_{q}})}{\exp(s_{q}\alpha_{q})\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}\exp(\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma})}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{f}_{q,\mathcal{Y}_{q}})}{\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}\exp(\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma})}=\Pr\nolimits_{q}(\bm{\beta}).`$ |  |  |

This recovers Eq.
([3](#S2.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
using only within-group comparisons. If all trajectories succeed or all
fail, $`\mathcal{A}_{q}`$ contains one set and
$`\Pr\nolimits_{q}(\bm{\beta})=1`$, so the group contributes neither
loss nor gradient to this estimator. The binary fit therefore uses
$`\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{info}}`$, the groups containing both outcomes.

#### 7.3.2 Regularized Coefficient Estimation

Binary outcomes. Write
$`\ell(\bm{\beta})=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{info}}}\log\Pr\nolimits_{q}(\bm{\beta})`$.
For each candidate set, let $`p_{q,\Gamma}(\bm{\beta})`$ denote its
conditional probability from Eq.
([12](#S7.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ 7.3.1 Conditional Likelihood ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
with $`\Gamma`$ in place of $`\mathcal{Y}_{q}`$. Differentiating the
log-sum-exp normalizer gives

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{q}(\bm{\beta})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}p_{q,\Gamma}(\bm{\beta})\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma},`$ |  | (13) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\nabla\ell(\bm{\beta})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{info}}}\big(\mathbf{f}_{q,\mathcal{Y}_{q}}-\bm{\mu}_{q}\big),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\bm{\beta})=-\nabla^{2}\ell(\bm{\beta})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{info}}}\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}p_{q,\Gamma}(\bm{\beta})\big(\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma}-\bm{\mu}_{q}\big)\big(\mathbf{f}_{q,\Gamma}-\bm{\mu}_{q}\big)^{\top}.`$ |  |  |

Thus the information matrix is the sum of the within-group covariances
of candidate feature sums. To stabilize estimation from limited data, we
fit

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\beta}}(\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\argmin_{\|\bm{\beta}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{\max}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{price}}(\bm{\beta}),`$ |  | (14) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{price}}(\bm{\beta})`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\ell(\bm{\beta})-\tfrac{1}{2}\logdet\mathcal{I}(\bm{\beta})+\rho\|\bm{\beta}-\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{2}^{2}.`$ |  |  |

The first term fits within-task outcomes; the Firth correction addresses
small-sample bias and separation \[[11](#bib.bib7), [14](#bib.bib8)\];
and the final term shrinks uncertain coefficients toward
$`\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. The log-determinant requires
positive-definite information on the fitted coordinates; regularization
does not make indistinguishable error classes identifiable. We enumerate
candidate sets exactly and solve the bounded objective with L-BFGS-B
\[[3](#bib.bib10), [57](#bib.bib11)\]. The resulting
$`\widehat{\bm{\beta}}`$ is the regularized numerical estimate used by
the method, whereas $`\bm{\beta}^{\star}`$ in Eq.
([3](#S2.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 2.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
denotes the unpenalized estimator.

Graded outcomes. For graded rewards, let
$`z_{q,i}=\clip((R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-R_{\min})/\Delta R,0,1)`$. We
replace the binary likelihood by squared-error regression with
unpenalized group intercepts:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\min_{\bm{\beta},\{\alpha_{q}\}}\sum_{q,i}\big(z_{q,i}-\alpha_{q}-\bm{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{q,i}\big)^{2}+\rho\|\bm{\beta}-\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{2}^{2},
``` |  | (15) |

where the sum covers the retained trajectories in the fitting data. For
fixed $`\bm{\beta}`$, setting the derivative with respect to
$`\alpha_{q}`$ to zero gives
$`\widehat{\alpha}_{q}=\bar{z}_{q}-\bm{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{q}`$,
where the bars denote means within group $`q`$. Substituting this
expression removes the intercepts by centring both features and rewards:
$`\mathbf{x}^{c}_{q,i}=\mathbf{x}_{q,i}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{q}`$ and
$`z^{c}_{q,i}=z_{q,i}-\bar{z}_{q}`$. Let $`\mathbf{X}_{c}`$ contain the
centred feature rows and $`\mathbf{z}_{c}`$ the centred rewards. The
reduced objective and its normal equations yield

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ridge}}(\bm{\beta})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{z}_{c}-\mathbf{X}_{c}\bm{\beta}\|_{2}^{2}+\rho\|\bm{\beta}-\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{2}^{2},`$ |  | (16) |
|  | $`\displaystyle(\mathbf{X}_{c}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{c}+\rho\mathbf{I})\widehat{\bm{\beta}}(\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}_{c}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{c}+\rho\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\beta}}(\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=(\mathbf{X}_{c}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{c}+\rho\mathbf{I})^{-1}(\mathbf{X}_{c}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{c}+\rho\bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{ref}}).`$ |  |  |

Here $`\mathbf{I}`$ is the identity matrix and $`\rho>0`$ makes the
system invertible. This ridge estimator replaces the likelihood and
Firth terms for graded outcomes; the price mapping and update rules
below are shared by both branches.

#### 7.3.3 From Coefficients to Prices

Negative coefficients associate higher error rates with lower outcomes
within a task. We use their magnitudes as relative costs and assign no
reward to positive coefficients:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{w}_{k}=\max(-\beta_{k},0)\,\sigma_{k},\qquad w_{k}=\begin{cases}\widetilde{w}_{k}/\max_{j}\widetilde{w}_{j},&\max_{j}\widetilde{w}_{j}>0,\\[1.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
``` |  | (17) |

At cold start, $`\sigma_{k}`$ is the population standard deviation of
error rate $`k`$ over all cold-start trajectories, including zeros and
groups excluded from the binary likelihood. Then
$`|\beta_{k}|\sigma_{k}`$ measures the change in the linear predictor
associated with one standard deviation of that rate. For online refits,
$`\sigma_{k}=1`$, recovering Eq.
([4](#S2.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
When any price is positive, the most expensive class has price one;
$`\lambda_{u}`$ controls the overall penalty strength (Appendix
[7.4](#S7.SS4 "7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
These prices summarize predictive associations with outcomes, not causal
effects.

#### 7.3.4 Cold-Start Fit and Initialization

Cold-start fitting uses the teacher-diagnosed trajectories of Appendix
[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Cold Start: Taxonomy, Diagnosis SFT and Initial Prices ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),
with $`M_{q,i}=1`$ in Eq.
([9](#S7.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Each group $`q`$ consists of the rollouts of one task from one
checkpoint. To separate error density from the additional association
between trajectory length and outcome, the initial fit includes a length
covariate:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{q,i}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}_{q,i}\\
T^{\mathrm{fit}}_{q,i}\end{pmatrix},\qquad T^{\mathrm{fit}}_{q,i}=\frac{T_{q,i}}{T^{\max}},\qquad\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\beta}\\
\beta_{T}\end{pmatrix}.
``` |  | (18) |

Unlike the group intercept, this covariate can vary within a group and
is therefore retained after intercept removal. We apply the appropriate
estimator of Appendix
[7.3.2](#S7.SS3.SSS2 "7.3.2 Regularized Coefficient Estimation ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
to these extended features, using a zero reference and fixed shrinkage
strength $`\rho`$; the binary optimizer also starts at zero and bounds
the length coefficient. The coefficient $`\beta_{T}`$ controls for
length in fitting but never enters the price vector.

Let $`\widehat{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}`$ contain only the $`K`$ error
coefficients from this fit. Equation
([17](#S7.E17 "Equation 17 ‣ 7.3.3 From Coefficients to Prices ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
with the cold-start scales, gives the initial prices
$`\mathbf{w}^{(1)}`$. We also retain the non-positive part of the fitted
error coefficients as a fixed reference for online estimation:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bm{\beta}^{(0)}=\min\big(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},\mathbf{0}\big),
``` |  | (19) |

where the minimum is coordinate-wise. Thus
$`\widehat{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}`$ is the initial fit, $`\bm{\beta}^{(0)}`$
is the frozen reference, and $`\mathbf{w}^{(1)}`$ is the initial price
vector. Online refits use only the $`K`$ error rates, without the length
covariate.

#### 7.3.5 Online Refits and Price Updates

Fitting recent trajectories. Batch $`u`$ uses the fixed prices
$`\mathbf{w}^{(u)}`$. After its actor update, the verified records
$`(q,\mathbf{x}_{q,i},y_{q,i})`$ with non-imputed scores enter a sliding
window of the last $`W`$ updates; graded outcomes use $`z_{q,i}`$ in
place of $`y_{q,i}`$. Refits begin at $`u_{0}`$ and recur every
$`\Delta u`$ updates. The binary fit requires enough mixed-outcome
groups and successes; only classes with sufficient non-zero observations
and within-group variation are fitted (thresholds in Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
If these checks fail or a fit is rejected, the previous prices remain in
use. Numerical binary fits require convergence, a finite objective, and
finite, feasible coefficients; implementation differences are recorded
in Appendix
[7.7](#S7.SS7 "7.7 Algorithm ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

For the $`v`$-th accepted refit, we use the current window to obtain
$`\widehat{\bm{\beta}}^{(v)}=\widehat{\bm{\beta}}(\bm{\beta}^{(0)})`$
with shrinkage strength $`\rho_{u}`$, which decreases over training. The
reference stays fixed at $`\bm{\beta}^{(0)}`$; each binary optimization
also starts there. The previous online coefficients enter only the
subsequent smoothing step, not the shrinkage target.

Updating the deployed coefficients. Let $`\mathcal{K}_{v}`$ be the
classes included in the accepted refit. Starting from
$`\bm{\beta}^{(0)}`$, update the deployed coefficients by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\beta^{(v)}_{k}=\begin{cases}\nu\beta^{(v-1)}_{k}+(1-\nu)\hat{\beta}^{(v)}_{k},&k\in\mathcal{K}_{v}\ \text{and}\ \hat{\beta}^{(v)}_{k}\leq 0,\\[2.0pt]
\beta^{(v-1)}_{k},&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad\nu=0.7.
``` |  | (20) |

This exponential moving average gives weight $`0.7`$ to the previous
value and $`0.3`$ to the new fit. An unfitted class or a positive new
coefficient keeps its previous value. We map $`\bm{\beta}^{(v)}`$ to
prices with $`\sigma_{k}=1`$ in Eq.
([17](#S7.E17 "Equation 17 ‣ 7.3.3 From Coefficients to Prices ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"));
the new prices take effect from batch $`u+1`$, so a batch is never
scored using a fit to its own outcomes. The smoothing acts on
coefficients; the change in scaling after cold start means that it does
not guarantee continuity of the normalized prices.

### 7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget

With error prices fixed within a batch, we compute a trajectory score,
scale it over training, and cap the resulting penalty before
distributing it across steps. The cap preserves the ordering of binary
outcomes in the episode channel, as shown below.

#### 7.4.1 Trajectory Score

For a trajectory that passes the diagnosis gate, the batch prices
$`\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}^{(u)}`$ and soft counts from Eq.
([9](#S7.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
give

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{raw}}_{q,i}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{k}n_{q,i,k}+(b-1)w_{k^{*}_{q,i}},`$ |  | (21) |
|  | $`\displaystyle S_{q,i}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\clip\!\left(\frac{S^{\mathrm{raw}}_{q,i}}{T_{q,i}},0,1\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\clip\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{k}x_{q,i,k}+\frac{(b-1)w_{k^{*}_{q,i}}}{T_{q,i}},0,1\right).`$ |  |  |

Here $`b\geq 1`$ adds weight to the earliest verified error class
$`k^{*}_{q,i}`$; the extra term is zero when no verified error exists.
We use a first-error penalty factor of $`b=1.5`$ in all experiments,
adding $`0.5\,w_{k^{*}_{q,i}}`$ to $`S^{\mathrm{raw}}_{q,i}`$. Setting
$`b=1`$ recovers the score in Eq.
([4](#S2.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
The rate term is independent of length at fixed error rates; the
first-error term retains an explicit $`1/T_{q,i}`$ dependence. Both
successful and failed trajectories are scored; trajectories that fail
the diagnosis gate use the imputed score of Appendix
[7.2](#S7.SS2 "7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

#### 7.4.2 Penalty Schedule

Let $`u=1,\ldots,U`$ index actor updates and $`0\leq U_{\mathrm{w}}<U`$
be the warm-up length. We measure $`\overline{T}^{(0)}`$, the mean
executed trajectory length in the first rollout batch, before any actor
update. The non-negative strengths $`\lambda_{\mathrm{warm}}`$ and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{peak}}`$ are fixed multiples of
$`\overline{T}^{(0)}`$ (Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning");
benchmark variants in Appendix
[8.4](#S8.SS4 "8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
This scales a per-step error score to a trajectory-level penalty using
the initial trajectory length. We keep $`\overline{T}^{(0)}`$ fixed so
that later changes in trajectory length do not change this global scale.
The penalty strength is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lambda_{u}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{\mathrm{warm}},&u\leq U_{\mathrm{w}},\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{\lambda_{\mathrm{peak}}}{2}\Big[1+\cos\!\Big(\pi\,\dfrac{u-U_{\mathrm{w}}}{U-U_{\mathrm{w}}}\Big)\Big],&u>U_{\mathrm{w}}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (22) |

The warm-up strength is constant; the remaining updates follow the
cosine branch, with $`\lambda_{U}=0`$ so that the diagnostic penalty
vanishes at the final update.

#### 7.4.3 Bounded Budget and Order Preservation

Let $`\Delta R=R_{\max}-R_{\min}>0`$ be the terminal reward range, equal
to the success–failure reward gap for binary outcomes. For
$`0\leq\eta<1`$, the trajectory budget is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
P_{q,i}=\min\big(\lambda_{u}S_{q,i},\eta\Delta R\big),\qquad 0\leq P_{q,i}\leq\eta\Delta R.
``` |  | (23) |

This budget is nondecreasing in $`S_{q,i}`$, with ties once the cap is
reached; Appendix
[7.5](#S7.SS5 "7.5 Conserved Step Redistribution ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
distributes it across steps without changing its total.

###### Proposition 1 (Episode-channel order preservation).

Fix a task $`q`$ with binary terminal rewards
$`R_{\min}+\Delta R\,y_{q,i}`$ and episode scores
$`E_{q,i,t}=R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-\delta_{q,i,t}`$. For any successful
trajectory $`i`$, failed trajectory $`j`$, and their respective steps
$`t`$ and $`r`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i,t}-A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,j,r}=E_{q,i,t}-E_{q,j,r}\geq(1-\eta)\Delta R>0.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

The conserved split has non-negative shares, so
$`0\leq\delta_{q,i,t}\leq P_{q,i}`$. Using Eq.
([23](#S7.E23 "Equation 23 ‣ 7.4.3 Bounded Budget and Order Preservation ‣ 7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle E_{q,i,t}-E_{q,j,r}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\Delta R-\delta_{q,i,t}+\delta_{q,j,r}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq\Delta R-P_{q,i}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq(1-\eta)\Delta R>0.`$ |  |

Both episode advantages subtract the same task-group mean in Eq.
([6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
leaving their difference unchanged. ∎

The proposition concerns the diagnostic episode channel; additional
reward terms in Appendix
[7.6](#S7.SS6 "7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
are outside its scope. For graded outcomes, the same argument gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
E_{q,i,t}-E_{q,j,r}\geq R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,j}-\eta\Delta R.
``` |  |

This bound guarantees strict order when the original reward gap exceeds
the penalty cap.

### 7.5 Conserved Step Redistribution

Given the bounded budget $`P_{q,i}`$ from Appendix
[7.4](#S7.SS4 "7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),
we use the diagnosed error locations to determine each step’s share
without changing the total penalty.

#### 7.5.1 Localization Weights

Let $`\mathcal{V}_{q,i,t}`$ collect the verified entries citing step
$`t`$ across all parsable diagnoses, retaining repeated reports as
separate entries. Each entry contributes its class price, giving

|     |                                                         |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       h_{q,i,t}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{V}_{q,i,t}}w_{k(e)}\geq 0.
       ```                                                      |     |

Thus a class reported at the same step by three diagnoses contributes
$`3w_{k}`$; the relative support across steps determines their
allocation weights. Quote length and severity hints do not affect these
weights. When at least one weight is positive, we boost the earliest
such step:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
t^{*}_{q,i}=\min\{t:h_{q,i,t}>0\},\qquad\widetilde{h}_{q,i,t}=\big[1+(b_{\mathrm{loc}}-1)\mathds{1}\!\left[t=t^{*}_{q,i}\right]\big]h_{q,i,t}.
``` |  | (24) |

If all weights are zero, we set $`\widetilde{h}_{q,i,t}=0`$ for every
step. We use $`b_{\mathrm{loc}}=1.5`$ (Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Unlike the score-level boost $`b`$ in Appendix
[7.4](#S7.SS4 "7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),
which can increase the total budget, $`b_{\mathrm{loc}}`$ changes only
its allocation. The boosted step is the earliest with positive price,
which need not be the earliest verified error.

#### 7.5.2 Normalized Allocation and Conservation

The complete allocation rule is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{q,i,t}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle P_{q,i}\frac{\widetilde{h}_{q,i,t}}{\sum_{r=1}^{T_{q,i}}\widetilde{h}_{q,i,r}},&\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{T_{q,i}}\widetilde{h}_{q,i,r}>0,\\[9.0pt]
P_{q,i}\mathds{1}\!\left[t=T_{q,i}\right],&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (25) |

This is Eq.
([5](#S2.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
with the boosted weights and a fallback for missing locations. A
positive budget without a positive localization weight can arise from
score imputation (Appendix
[7.2](#S7.SS2 "7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"));
it is assigned to the last active step. Both branches use non-negative
proportions summing to one, so

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\leq\delta_{q,i,t}\leq P_{q,i},\qquad\sum_{t=1}^{T_{q,i}}\delta_{q,i,t}=P_{q,i}.
``` |  | (26) |

With localized evidence, zero-weight steps receive no penalty; when
$`P_{q,i}=0`$, all shares vanish. Redistribution therefore changes where
the budget is charged, while preserving the total set by the rate-based
score, schedule and cap.

### 7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update

The step shares $`\delta_{q,i,t}`$ enter two comparisons: an episode
channel across the task’s rollouts and a step channel across similar
pre-response contexts. Their combined advantage then supervises the
response tokens of each step.

#### 7.6.1 Comparison Groups and Local Scores

The task group contains all active step samples,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Omega_{q}=\{(j,r):1\leq j\leq G,\ 1\leq r\leq T_{q,j}\},\qquad|\Omega_{q}|=\sum_{j=1}^{G}T_{q,j}.
``` |  |

Each step counts once, so longer trajectories contribute more samples to
the episode baseline. For the step channel,
$`\mathcal{H}(o_{q,i,t})\subseteq\Omega_{q}`$ contains samples clustered
with $`o_{q,i,t}`$ by pre-response context similarity. A context joins a
cluster when its text similarity to the representative meets the
threshold in Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning");
otherwise it starts a new cluster. These groups approximate comparable
contexts rather than asserting identical latent states.

We first compute the return from the environment rewards, then subtract
the diagnostic share locally:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle Z^{\mathrm{env}}_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{r=t}^{T_{q,i}}\gamma^{r-t}R^{\mathrm{env}}_{q,i,r},`$ |  | (27) |
|  | $`\displaystyle E_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-\delta_{q,i,t},\qquad\widetilde{Z}_{q,i,t}=Z^{\mathrm{env}}_{q,i,t}-\delta_{q,i,t}.`$ |  |  |

For purely terminal rewards,
$`Z^{\mathrm{env}}_{q,i,t}=\gamma^{T_{q,i}-t}R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}`$,
recovering Eq.
([7](#S2.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
The share is not added to the environment reward and discounted back to
earlier steps: the diagnosis has already identified where to charge it.
When the base recipe includes a rule-based invalid-action penalty, it
also enters $`E_{q,i,t}`$; the displayed equations omit this additional
term.

#### 7.6.2 Centred Advantages and the Effect of Diagnosis

Following the two comparison groups, we define

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mu^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{q}|}\sum_{(j,r)\in\Omega_{q}}E_{q,j,r},`$ | $`\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=E_{q,i,t}-\mu^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q},`$ |  | (28) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mu^{\mathrm{step}}(o)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}(o)|}\sum_{(j,r)\in\mathcal{H}(o)}\widetilde{Z}_{q,j,r},`$ | $`\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{step}}_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\widetilde{Z}_{q,i,t}-\mu^{\mathrm{step}}(o_{q,i,t}),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle A_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i,t}+\omega A^{\mathrm{step}}_{q,i,t}.`$ |  |  |  |  |

On ALFWorld and WebShop, both channels subtract a mean without dividing
by a standard deviation, keeping advantages in reward units;
Search-based QA also divides by the standard deviation (Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
A singleton anchor group has $`A^{\mathrm{step}}=0`$, while its episode
advantage can remain non-zero.

To isolate the effect of diagnosis, hold the rollouts, comparison groups
and other reward terms fixed, and let $`A^{(0)}_{q,i,t}`$ be the joint
advantage with $`\delta=0`$. For a non-empty comparison set
$`\mathcal{G}\subseteq\Omega_{q}`$, write
$`\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}}=|\mathcal{G}|^{-1}\sum_{(j,r)\in\mathcal{G}}\delta_{q,j,r}`$.
Subtracting the two versions of Eq.
([28](#S7.E28 "Equation 28 ‣ 7.6.2 Centred Advantages and the Effect of Diagnosis ‣ 7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{q,i,t}-A^{(0)}_{q,i,t}=-\big(\delta_{q,i,t}-\bar{\delta}_{\Omega_{q}}\big)-\omega\big(\delta_{q,i,t}-\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{H}(o_{q,i,t})}\big).
``` |  | (29) |

Thus each channel lowers a step’s advantage relative to its own
comparison group when its penalty exceeds that group’s mean; a uniform
penalty within a group cancels. The two channels compare the same local
cost against different reference sets, rather than assigning two
separate budgets.

#### 7.6.3 Token-Level Policy Update

For each response-token position $`\ell\in\mathcal{U}_{q,i,t}`$, we use
the same joint advantage, $`A_{\ell}=A_{q,i,t}`$, in Eq.
([8](#S2.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
The loss averages over the $`N=\sum_{(q,i,t)}|\mathcal{U}_{q,i,t}|`$
response tokens in the batch; context, feedback and diagnosis tokens
receive no policy loss. The importance ratio is
$`\varrho_{\ell}=\pi_{\theta}(a_{\ell}\mid c_{\ell})/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(a_{\ell}\mid c_{\ell})`$;
both $`\pi_{\mathrm{old}}`$ and the advantages are held fixed during the
update. In the experiments, the clipped surrogate additionally uses dual
clipping for negative advantages \[[50](#bib.bib16)\], and the loss
includes the low-variance KL term
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}D^{\mathrm{LV}}_{\ell}`$ relative to the frozen
policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ \[[31](#bib.bib12)\]. The clipping and KL
settings are listed in Table
[4](#S8.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 8.4 Training Configuration and Hyper-parameters ‣ 8 Additional Experimental Setup ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

## 8 Additional Experimental Setup

This appendix expands the benchmarks, baselines, evaluation protocols
and implementation of
§[3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

### 8.1 Benchmarks and Baselines

Benchmarks. ALFWorld comprises six families of household tasks (Pick,
Look, Clean, Heat, Cool and Pick2); we train on its standard training
games and evaluate on the unseen split. WebShop tests product search,
inspection and purchase against natural-language requests. Search-based
QA follows Search-R1 \[[17](#bib.bib19)\] and draws questions from NQ
\[[20](#bib.bib48)\], TriviaQA \[[18](#bib.bib49)\], PopQA
\[[24](#bib.bib50)\], HotpotQA \[[45](#bib.bib51)\], 2WikiMultiHopQA
\[[15](#bib.bib52)\], MuSiQue \[[36](#bib.bib53)\] and Bamboogle
\[[26](#bib.bib54)\].

Baselines. Vanilla uses the instruction-tuned backbone without
task-specific post-training. GRPO and GiGPO start from that backbone;
GiGPO follows its upstream recipe, including $`150`$ updates on
ALFWorld. SEED uses its own teacher-written hindsight-skill SFT data and
skill-distillation objective. FAULT starts from diagnosis SFT, and the
Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 GRPO (diag-SFT) and GiGPO (diag-SFT) controls
share this checkpoint with diagnostic shaping disabled but diagnosis
monitoring retained.

### 8.2 Evaluation Protocols

All reported metrics are percentages, with higher values better; each
result comes from one training run, so differences are point estimates
rather than claims of statistical significance.

ALFWorld. The unseen split has $`134`$ tasks: Pick $`24`$, Look $`18`$,
Clean $`31`$, Heat $`23`$, Cool $`21`$ and Pick2 $`17`$. Each task is
evaluated three times by a single evaluation worker, with greedy
decoding (temperature $`0`$) and at most $`30`$ active steps per
evaluation. We average the three success indicators for each task, then
average across all $`134`$ tasks, so task families contribute in
proportion to their size.

WebShop. We use the small catalogue of $`1{,}000`$ products and $`500`$
held-out goals. Evaluation runs $`8`$ batches of $`32`$ goals ($`256`$
episodes) with greedy decoding, at most $`15`$ steps and a two-step
history window. Goals are sampled without replacement within a batch but
may recur across batches, so the strict success rate counts successful
episodes, not distinct goals. The task score is the environment’s graded
score, averaged over episodes and scaled to $`[0,100]`$; the strict
success rate is the fraction of episodes ending in a purchase of score
one.

Search-based QA. The agent gathers evidence with a search tool before
answering questions from the seven datasets above. We report exact-match
accuracy for each dataset and their unweighted mean, rather than pooling
all questions into one accuracy.

#### 8.3.1 ALFWorld

The following cold-start setup describes Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.

Data and teacher diagnosis. We sample eight rollouts for each of $`180`$
training tasks from two actor checkpoints: the base model and a
checkpoint after $`20`$ GRPO updates. Each checkpoint contributes

Taxonomy construction. Using GPT-5.6 Sol for both induction passes, we
construct a frozen taxonomy with eight L1 families and $`25`$ L2
mechanisms. From the L2 mechanisms and eight L1 fallback categories, we
retain classes with at least ten verified occurrences and frequency
ranks in the top $`60\%`$ of candidates, yielding $`K=20`$ pricing
classes.

Diagnosis-SFT data. We reserve $`200`$ trajectories, stratified by
checkpoint, outcome and task type, for assessment. The remaining
$`2{,}680`$ examples, supplemented by a few action-replay examples from
successful trajectories, train the diagnosis-SFT model with the settings
in Table

rollouts from the same task and checkpoint, or $`792`$ trajectories,
with the extended features of Appendix
[7.3.4](#S7.SS3.SSS4 "7.3.4 Cold-Start Fit and Initialization ‣ 7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
Cold-start feature scales $`\sigma_{k}`$ are computed over all
$`2{,}880`$ trajectories. The resulting prices are reported in Table
[7](#S10.T7 "Table 7 ‣ 10.3 Initial Prices and Online Price Dynamics ‣ 10 Training Dynamics ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

### 9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups

We first define the measurements used in
§[3.3.1](#S3.SS3.SSS1 "3.3.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 3.3 Analysis ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),

#### 9.1.1 Coverage and Relative Signal Strength

Groups and measured quantities. A group $`g`$ contains $`G=8`$ rollouts
of one task at one update. Each run has $`16`$ groups per update over
$`160`$ updates. Write
$`c(g)\in\{\mathrm{mix},\mathrm{succ},\mathrm{fail}\}`$ for mixed,
all-success and all-fail groups, respectively, and let $`N_{c}`$ be the
number of groups of type $`c`$, with $`N=\sum_{c}N_{c}`$. For coverage,
the measured credit is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\kappa_{s}=\begin{cases}A_{q,i,t},&\text{GRPO/GiGPO: step sample }s=(i,t),\\[2.0pt]
S_{q,i},&\text{FAULT{}: trajectory sample }s=i.\end{cases}
``` |  | (30) |

Here $`A_{q,i,t}`$ is the optimizer advantage, whereas $`S_{q,i}`$ is
the diagnostic score of Appendix
[7.4](#S7.SS4 "7.4 Score, Penalty Schedule and Order-Preserving Budget ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),
before multiplication by $`\lambda_{u}`$. Thus FAULT’s coverage measures
available diagnostic contrast, not the magnitude of its final policy
advantage.

Coverage. Define the within-group range and the usable-signal indicator
by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{g}=\max_{s\in g}\kappa_{s}-\min_{s\in g}\kappa_{s},\qquad\mathrm{usable}(g)=\mathds{1}\!\left[r_{g}>\tau\bar{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}\right],
``` |  | (31) |

where each method uses its own mixed-group mean:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bar{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mix}}}\sum_{g:\,c(g)=\mathrm{mix}}r_{g},`$ |  | (32) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{Coverage}(\tau)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{g}\mathds{1}\!\left[r_{g}>\tau\bar{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}\right].`$ |  | (33) |

The measured runs have $`N_{\mathrm{mix}}>0`$ and
$`\bar{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}>0`$. Mixed groups supply outcome contrast and
set a within-method reference scale. We use $`\tau=0.05`$; “usable”
denotes a contrast above this threshold, not independently verified
correctness or a guaranteed policy improvement.

Signal-retention Index. For signal strength, let $`r_{g}^{\mathrm{I}}`$
use the same range definition, but replace $`S_{q,i}`$ by the injected
budget $`P_{q,i}`$ for FAULT; the baselines still use $`A_{q,i,t}`$. In
a $`20`$-update window $`w`$, let $`N_{w,c}`$ count groups of type $`c`$
and let $`\bar{r}^{\mathrm{I}}_{w,c}`$ be their mean range. For a
positive mixed-group mean, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
I_{w}=\sum_{c\in\{\mathrm{mix},\mathrm{succ},\mathrm{fail}\}}\frac{N_{w,c}}{N_{w}}\,\frac{\bar{r}^{\mathrm{I}}_{w,c}}{\bar{r}^{\mathrm{I}}_{w,\mathrm{mix}}},\qquad N_{w}=\sum_{c}N_{w,c}.
``` |  | (34) |

An absent group type contributes zero. Each term combines a type’s
frequency with its contrast relative to mixed groups; the mixed
contribution is exactly its frequency. Figure
[3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
normalizes each window separately. The run-pooled Index is recomputed
from all groups, rather than averaged across windows. Because FAULT uses
trajectory budgets and the baselines use step advantages, this is a
comparison of contrast proxies, not a common-scale measure of optimizer
credit. Redistribution and centring can change how budget differences
enter the update, so the proxy alone does not provide an upper bound on
advantage contrast.

#### 9.1.2 Credit under Equal Terminal Rewards

ALFWorld gives terminal reward $`R_{\max}=10`$ for success and
$`R_{\min}=0`$ for failure. The following result isolates terminal
rewards and diagnostic penalties; it omits the shared invalid-action
penalty and other reward terms (Appendices
[7.6](#S7.SS6 "7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
and
[11.1](#S11.SS1 "11.1 Credit in an All-Fail Group ‣ 11 Case Studies ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
For a fixed task $`q`$, use the step set $`\Omega_{q}`$ and anchor
groups of Appendix
[7.6](#S7.SS6 "7.6 Dual-Channel Advantage and Policy Update ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").
For any non-empty comparison set $`\mathcal{G}\subseteq\Omega_{q}`$,
write
$`\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{G}}=|\mathcal{G}|^{-1}\sum_{(j,r)\in\mathcal{G}}\delta_{q,j,r}`$.
For $`\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(o_{q,i,t})`$, define the centred discount
factor

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\psi_{q,i,t}=\gamma^{T_{q,i}-t}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}|}\sum_{(j,r)\in\mathcal{H}}\gamma^{T_{q,j}-r}.
``` |  | (35) |

###### Proposition 2 (Credit in same-outcome groups).

Fix a rollout group with purely terminal rewards
$`R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}=\bar{R}`$ for all $`i`$, discount
$`0<\gamma<1`$, and step-channel weight $`\omega\geq 0`$. Assume that
the non-empty anchor groups form a fixed partition of $`\Omega_{q}`$,
and that normalization scales are finite and strictly positive. Omitting
additional reward terms, the following hold.

1.  (i)
    GRPO and the GiGPO episode channel have zero outcome advantage:

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    A^{\mathrm{GRPO}}_{q,i}=A^{\mathrm{GiGPO},\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i}=0.
    ``` |  | (36) |
2.  (ii)
    Within an anchor group $`\mathcal{H}`$, GiGPO’s step advantage is

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    A^{\mathrm{GiGPO},\mathrm{step}}_{q,i,t}=\frac{\bar{R}}{\xi_{\mathcal{H}}}\,\psi_{q,i,t},\qquad\xi_{\mathcal{H}}>0,
    ``` |  | (37) |

    where $`\xi_{\mathcal{H}}`$ is the group’s common normalization
    scale. It vanishes everywhere when $`\bar{R}=0`$. For
    $`\bar{R}\neq 0`$, it vanishes throughout $`\mathcal{H}`$ if and
    only if every member has the same remaining length $`T_{q,j}-r`$.
3.  (iii)
    FAULT has the channel and joint advantages

    |  |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|----|
    |  | $`\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{ep}}_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\big(\delta_{q,i,t}-\bar{\delta}_{\Omega_{q}}\big),`$ |  | (38) |
    |  | $`\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{step}}_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\bar{R}\,\psi_{q,i,t}-\big(\delta_{q,i,t}-\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{H}}\big),`$ |  |  |
    |  | $`\displaystyle A_{q,i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\omega\bar{R}\,\psi_{q,i,t}-\big(\delta_{q,i,t}-\bar{\delta}_{\Omega_{q}}\big)-\omega\big(\delta_{q,i,t}-\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{H}}\big).`$ |  |  |

    When $`\bar{R}=0`$, the joint advantage is zero at every sample if
    and only if $`\delta_{q,i,t}`$ is constant over $`\Omega_{q}`$.

###### Proof.

For (i), let $`\mu_{q}=G^{-1}\sum_{i}R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}`$ and
$`\sigma_{q}=\sd(R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,1},\ldots,R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,G})`$.
Equal rewards give $`\mu_{q}=\bar{R}`$ and $`\sigma_{q}=0`$, hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A^{\mathrm{GRPO}}_{q,i}=\frac{R^{\mathrm{out}}_{q,i}-\mu_{q}}{\sigma_{q}+\zeta}=\frac{\bar{R}-\bar{R}}{\zeta}=0,\qquad\zeta>0.
``` |  |

The GiGPO episode channel uses the same centred outcome. For (ii),
purely terminal rewards give

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Z_{q,i,t}=\sum_{r=t}^{T_{q,i}}\gamma^{r-t}R^{\mathrm{env}}_{q,i,r}=\bar{R}\gamma^{T_{q,i}-t},\qquad\frac{Z_{q,i,t}-\bar{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}}{\xi_{\mathcal{H}}}=\frac{\bar{R}}{\xi_{\mathcal{H}}}\psi_{q,i,t},
``` |  |

where $`\bar{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}`$ is the anchor-group mean. For
$`\bar{R}\neq 0`$, all centred returns vanish exactly when all discount
factors agree. Since $`d\mapsto\gamma^{d}`$ is strictly decreasing for
$`0<\gamma<1`$, this is equivalent to equal remaining lengths.

For (iii), substituting the common reward into Eqs.
([6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))–([7](#S2.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 2.4 Self-Evolving RL with Step-Level Credit Redistribution ‣ 2 Method ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"))
gives $`E_{q,i,t}=\bar{R}-\delta_{q,i,t}`$ and
$`\widetilde{Z}_{q,i,t}=\bar{R}\gamma^{T_{q,i}-t}-\delta_{q,i,t}`$.
Centring these scores over $`\Omega_{q}`$ and $`\mathcal{H}`$,
respectively, and adding the channels gives Eq.
([38](#S9.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ Item (iii) ‣ Proposition 2 (Credit in same-outcome groups). ‣ 9.1.2 Credit under Equal Terminal Rewards ‣ 9.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
When $`\bar{R}=0`$, write $`m=\bar{\delta}_{\Omega_{q}}`$ and
$`m_{\mathcal{H}}=\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{H}}`$, so

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{q,i,t}=-(m_{\mathcal{H}}-m)-(1+\omega)(\delta_{q,i,t}-m_{\mathcal{H}}).
``` |  |

If all advantages are zero, averaging within each anchor group gives
$`m_{\mathcal{H}}=m`$; since $`1+\omega>0`$, every share then equals
$`m`$. The converse follows by substitution. ∎

The result concerns advantage contrast, not semantic correctness or a
guaranteed gradient improvement. A singleton anchor group has zero
step-channel advantage for both GiGPO and FAULT, although FAULT’s
episode channel can remain non-zero. Equal outcomes do not force
diagnostic contrast to vanish, but uniform shares do; different error
labels need not yield different shares. In all-success groups, discount
and diagnostic terms can also cancel.

This subsection defines the localization and concentration measures used
in
§[3.3.2](#S3.SS3.SSS2 "3.3.2 Step-Level Localization of Credit ‣ 3.3 Analysis ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),

[4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning"),

[11](#S9.F11 "Figure 11 ‣ 9.3.3 Concentration of Step Credit ‣ 9.3 Step-Level Localization: Measurement and Additional Views ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning").

#### 9.3.1 Evaluation Protocol

Step penalties. For a trajectory of $`T=T_{q,i}`$ steps, define the
non-negative credit assigned against step $`t`$ as

|     |                                                             |     |      |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       b_{t}=\begin{cases}\delta_{q,i,t},&\text{FAULT{}},\\[2.0pt]
       \max(-A_{q,i,t},0),&\text{GRPO and GiGPO},\end{cases}
       ```                                                          |     | (39) |

where $`A_{q,i,t}`$ is the optimizer advantage. GRPO broadcasts its
outcome advantage across steps; the additional invalid-action penalty
makes $`b_{t}`$ constant or two-valued.

Blind reference labels. We use $`300`$ failed training trajectories of
FAULT, $`300`$ from a same-configuration GiGPO rerun, and $`48`$ from
GRPO, sampled evenly across eight $`20`$-update windows and from both
all-fail and mixed groups. Every failure reaches the $`T=30`$ step
limit. An independent LLM, Qwen3.8-Max \[[30](#bib.bib56)\], sees only
the action and feedback text, without method names or penalties, and
selects the earliest decisive step $`j`$ whose correction it judges most
likely to turn failure into success. These are model judgments, not
human or counterfactually verified labels. A blind relabel of $`48`$
trajectories agrees within one step in $`75\%`$ of cases.

#### 9.3.2 Localization Metrics and Results

Rank steps by decreasing $`b_{t}`$, breaking ties uniformly at random.
Allowing one step of error around the judge’s label, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{J}=\{t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}:|t-j|\leq 1\},\qquad\mathrm{rank}^{\star}=\min_{t\in\mathcal{J}}\mathrm{rank}(t).
``` |  | (40) |

The ranking and signed-offset metrics are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{MRR}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}^{\star}}\right],\qquad\mathrm{hit@}k=\Pr(\mathrm{rank}^{\star}\leq k),\qquad\mathrm{off}=\min\!\left(\argmax_{t}b_{t}\right)-j.
``` |  | (41) |

MRR and hit@$`k`$ average over trajectories and $`20{,}000`$ random
tie-breaks per trajectory; the offset instead uses the first maximizer.
Positive offsets place the maximum penalty after the judged step.
Constant $`b_{t}`$ gives the uniform random-ranking reference.

Localization results. FAULT achieves MRR $`0.494`$, hit@$`1`$
$`34.1\%`$, and $`82`$ exact step matches; GiGPO achieves $`0.303`$,
$`11.9\%`$, and $`21`$ exact matches. On its $`48`$ trajectories, GRPO
achieves MRR $`0.306`$ and hit@$`1`$ $`15.3\%`$, compared with $`0.266`$
and $`9.9\%`$ for random ranking. Figure
[1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(b)
places GRPO at the random reference for its uniform outcome credit; the
measured value includes the invalid-action penalty, which sometimes
marks the decisive step. In $`73\%`$ of GRPO trajectories, the maximum
penalty is tied, so its offset distribution is omitted from Figure
[4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.1 Experimental Setting ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(a).
GiGPO’s median offset is $`+5`$ steps. Figure
[11](#S9.F11 "Figure 11 ‣ 9.3.3 Concentration of Step Credit ‣ 9.3 Step-Level Localization: Measurement and Additional Views ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")(a)
extends hit@$`k`$ to $`k=1,\ldots,12`$: FAULT leads most at small $`k`$,
while the curves approach each other as $`k`$ grows. Even random
nomination of $`10`$ out of $`30`$ steps covers the tolerance window
about $`72\%`$ of the time.

#### 9.3.3 Concentration of Step Credit

For FAULT, set $`c_{t}=\delta_{q,i,t}`$; for the baselines, set
$`c_{t}=A_{q,i,t}`$. For $`T\geq 2`$ and $`\sum_{t}|c_{t}|>0`$, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
w_{t}=\frac{|c_{t}|}{\sum_{r=1}^{T}|c_{r}|},\qquad H^{*}=-\frac{1}{\ln T}\sum_{t:w_{t}>0}w_{t}\ln w_{t},\qquad\mathrm{ENS}=\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{t}^{2}\right)^{-1}.
``` |  | (42) |

The normalized entropy $`H^{*}\in[0,1]`$ is zero for concentration on
one step and one for a uniform allocation. The effective number of
steps, ENS, is the inverse Herfindahl index: it equals $`1`$ and $`T`$
in these two cases. For FAULT these statistics describe the diagnostic

### 9.4 Penalties on Wasted Steps

Step classes and measurement. We use two mechanically identified
classes: invalid or missing actions, and format-valid no-ops whose next
feedback is “Nothing happens.” Invalid actions take precedence; the last
step cannot be labeled a no-op without a subsequent observation. All
failed trajectories reach the $`30`$-step limit, so these steps consume
the interaction budget without recorded progress. The labels identify
wasted interactions, not whether correcting one step would have changed
the outcome. For the steps $`\mathcal{B}`$ of one class in a trajectory,
its targeting ratio is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}}b_{t}/\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t}}{|\mathcal{B}|/T},
``` |  |

using Eq.
([39](#S9.E39 "Equation 39 ‣ 9.3.1 Evaluation Protocol ‣ 9.3 Step-Level Localization: Measurement and Additional Views ‣ 9 Additional Experimental Results ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Ratios and top-1 rates use failed trajectories with positive total
negative credit and class counts strictly between $`0`$ and $`T`$;
eligibility therefore depends on the method and class. We average the
ratio equally over these trajectories, with one denoting uniform
allocation. Top-1 hits average uniformly over tied maximizers of
$`b_{t}`$.

Invalid actions. Among eligible trajectories, both baselines achieve
$`100\%`$ top-1 targeting of invalid actions in all-fail groups; over
the pooled all-fail and mixed-group sample, GRPO reaches $`100\%`$
compared with $`48.6\%`$ for FAULT. The shared invalid-action penalty
directly marks these steps, so this result alone does not demonstrate
diagnostic understanding. In the recorded recipe, an invalid action
receives $`-0.1`$ and a valid action receives zero before advantage
construction (Appendix
[11.1](#S11.SS1 "11.1 Credit in an All-Fail Group ‣ 11 Case Studies ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).

Format-valid no-ops. FAULT assigns $`3.67\times`$ the uniform share of
negative credit to no-ops, compared with $`0.48\times`$ for GRPO and
$`0.57\times`$ for GiGPO (Figure

The baselines’ no-op top-1 hit rates are $`0.1\%`$ and $`4.3\%`$, below
their uniform references of $`7.2\%`$ and $`7.7\%`$. In the analysed
all-fail groups, their no-op negative credit is zero, although a valid
step can still have a non-negative advantage after centring. Thus FAULT
targets a class of wasted steps not directly flagged by the
invalid-action reward; the contrast goes beyond locating format
violations.

## 12 Extended Related Work

This section expands Section
[4](#S4 "4 Related Work ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")
along the same three lines.

Outcome-based agentic RL. Agentic RL treats an LLM as a policy that acts
over many turns and learns from the rewards it observes
\[[53](#bib.bib17)\]. Following the success of GRPO for reasoning models
\[[33](#bib.bib1), [13](#bib.bib18)\], it has been applied with GRPO,
PPO or their variants to search \[[17](#bib.bib19)\], tool use
\[[27](#bib.bib20), [9](#bib.bib21)\], web navigation
\[[39](#bib.bib22)\], and games and embodied text environments
\[[38](#bib.bib23), [10](#bib.bib2)\]. GRPO needs no critic: it
normalizes terminal rewards across the rollouts of a task and assigns
each rollout’s advantage to every step. This is simple and scales well,
but the advantage of a step reflects the whole trajectory rather than
that step.

Two consequences follow for long-horizon tasks. With binary rewards, a
group whose rollouts all succeed or all fail has zero advantage for
every rollout; DAPO filters such groups out \[[51](#bib.bib6)\], while
[Yang et al. \[43\]](#bib.bib25) give all-fail groups a fallback
advantage from first-visit observation coverage. In our ALFWorld runs,
these same-outcome groups make up between $`59\%`$ and $`64\%`$ of
training groups (Section
[3.3.1](#S3.SS3.SSS1 "3.3.1 Learning Signal in Same-Outcome Groups ‣ 3.3 Analysis ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
Within a failed rollout, every step receives the same credit, so the
decisive mistake is not singled out. Agent-specific methods such as ARPO
\[[8](#bib.bib24)\] and GiGPO \[[10](#bib.bib2)\] change how rollouts
are sampled or compared, but their credit is still computed from
outcomes.

Numeric process signals. Process supervision assigns rewards to
intermediate steps. In mathematical reasoning, step rewards from human
labels \[[22](#bib.bib15)\], automatic rollout labels
\[[37](#bib.bib34)\] or progress estimates \[[32](#bib.bib35)\] improve
on outcome-only rewards. Agentic RL has adopted the idea: process reward
models for agents learn step values from Monte Carlo rollout returns
\[[6](#bib.bib26)\] or temporal-difference estimates
\[[41](#bib.bib27)\], and recent methods score steps with prefix-aware
reward models or the agent’s own hindsight \[[19](#bib.bib28),
[23](#bib.bib44), [46](#bib.bib47)\]. These signals are dense and can
separate rollouts that share an outcome.

Their difficulty is trust. A learned step reward is itself a model that
the policy can exploit; DeepSeek-R1 avoids neural process rewards partly
for this reason \[[13](#bib.bib18)\], and further optimizing an agent
PRM can lower the true success rate \[[6](#bib.bib26)\]. GiGPO avoids a
learned model by comparing actions taken from the same environment state
\[[10](#bib.bib2)\]. Its outcome-based credit, however, comes from
discounted returns, so it vanishes in all-fail groups and, when present,
spreads over the steps after a wrong turn (Section
[3.3.2](#S3.SS3.SSS2 "3.3.2 Step-Level Localization of Credit ‣ 3.3 Analysis ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")).
FAULT keeps the terminal reward as the only trusted signal: step
penalties come from verified diagnoses, and their prices are a few
coefficients fitted to outcomes.

Language analysis of trajectories. Language methods analyze completed
trajectories to state what went wrong and where. The analysis can guide
another attempt through context or memory \[[34](#bib.bib14),
[55](#bib.bib39), [16](#bib.bib42), [49](#bib.bib38),
[12](#bib.bib41)\], earn a reward when a retry succeeds
\[[1](#bib.bib40)\], produce corrected training trajectories
\[[52](#bib.bib29), [54](#bib.bib30), [21](#bib.bib43)\], or be
distilled into the policy \[[44](#bib.bib45), [56](#bib.bib46),
[2](#bib.bib4), [40](#bib.bib3)\]. SEED is closest to our setting: one
checkpoint acts, analyzes its rollouts into hindsight skills and learns
from them by on-policy distillation, with strong gains on ALFWorld
\[[35](#bib.bib5)\] and WebShop \[[47](#bib.bib37)\]. Such analysis
carries the information that outcome credit lacks.

Three gaps keep it from solving the two credit-assignment problems
above. First, the analysis rarely sets RL credit: it serves as context,
a training target or an object of reward, even when pooled across
trajectories as in SAMULE \[[12](#bib.bib41)\]. GRSD
\[[56](#bib.bib46)\] does use pooled reflections to rescale each step’s
outcome advantage, but this advantage is still zero in same-outcome
groups, so equal outcomes still receive equal outcome credit. Second,
error claims are not checked at the stated step; on TRAIL, the best
evaluated model reaches only about $`11\%`$ joint accuracy in
identifying and locating errors in agent traces \[[7](#bib.bib31)\],
even though error taxonomies are available \[[4](#bib.bib32),
[58](#bib.bib33)\]. Third, the weight of the analysis is set by fixed
loss coefficients, mixing schedules or a single retry, not learned from
outcomes. FAULT closes these gaps with diagnoses restricted to a fixed
taxonomy and checked by quotes (Appendix
[7.2](#S7.SS2 "7.2 Online Self-Diagnosis, Verification and Aggregation ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
prices fitted to outcomes within each task (Appendix
[7.3](#S7.SS3 "7.3 Terminal-Anchored Error Pricing ‣ 7 Method Details ‣ My FAULT: Self-Diagnosis as Credit Assignment inSelf-Evolving Agentic Reinforcement Learning")),
and a bounded penalty budget redistributed over verified error steps.
````
