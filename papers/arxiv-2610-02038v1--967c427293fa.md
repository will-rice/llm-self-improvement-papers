# Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control

Yimeng Liu Affiliation:  Michigan State University    Mi Zhang
Affiliation:  Ohio State University    Younsuk Dong Affiliation: 
Michigan State University    Zhichao Cao Affiliation:  Michigan State
University

###### Abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly combine reasoning, tool
use, and action, but most evidence comes from episodic tasks with
relatively immediate feedback and reset failures. Long-running physical
control operates in a different regime: actions alter future states,
errors compound across decisions, and an agent must improve from
experience without being allowed to rewrite the physical rules that make
execution safe. We study this regime through irrigation, where daily
decisions interact with soil-water dynamics over entire growing seasons.
We present Mímir, a physics-grounded LLM agent organized around two
repair timescales. At the fast timescale, a structured physical
interface and deterministic simulator turn an LLM output into a proposal
that we numerically check, revise, and subject to bounded deterministic
action selection before execution. At the slow timescale, recurrent
failure patterns are consolidated into persistent contextual principles
that condition future proposals, while the physical model, evaluator,
and execution constraints remain immutable. Under a common retrospective
evaluator across multiple sites, crops, and years, Mímir attains the
lowest reported aggregate control cost among the evaluated references
and uses about 51% less irrigation than the historical schedule replay.
The ablation study show higher control cost when forward simulation,
verified revision, or persistent context is removed; model-scale and
model-family studies show no monotonic gain from increasing LLM size.
The resulting lesson show that persistent physical agents can combine
semantic reasoning with bounded, evidence-driven self-improvement while
reserving physical truth and actuator authority for explicit numerical
mechanisms.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as agents that
interleave reasoning, tool use, memory, and action [Yao et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib36). In embodied settings, language models can ground
high-level decisions in perception, affordances, and external tools [Ahn
et al. (2022)](#bib.bib1); [Driess et al. (2023)](#bib.bib7); [Huang
et al. (2022)](#bib.bib14); [Huang et al. (2023)](#bib.bib15). However,
most agent evaluations remain episodic: a task starts, the agent acts
for a finite horizon, and the episode terminates. Persistent physical
control is different. Each action changes the state on which later
decisions depend; consequences can be delayed; and a locally plausible
mistake can persist through the physical system long after the
language-model call that produced it. A physical agent must therefore do
more than generate a reasonable action. It need to remain grounded in
numerical dynamics, repair bad decisions before actuation, and
accumulate useful experience without allowing that experience to erode
execution safety.

Irrigation provides a concrete instance of this regime. A controller
repeatedly decides how much water to apply under evolving soil, crop,
weather, and forecast conditions. Irrigation changes root-zone water
storage and therefore the context for future decisions; excessive
application consumes water and can increase drainage, whereas
insufficient application can accumulate crop-water deficit [Allen et al.
(1998)](#bib.bib2). The effect of today’s decision may only become
evident after subsequent evapotranspiration, rainfall, and additional
control actions. Physics-based scheduling, model predictive control
(MPC), sensor-driven irrigation, and reinforcement learning already
provide strong solutions when the state, model, objective, and
controller are sufficiently specified [Allen et al. (1998)](#bib.bib2);
[Delgoda et al. (2016)](#bib.bib4); [Garcia et al. (1989)](#bib.bib13);
[Dong et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6); [Yu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib37); [Ding &
Du (2024)](#bib.bib5). We therefore do _not_ ask whether an LLM should
replace these numerical controllers. We ask a narrower
systems-and-learning question: _if an LLM is introduced as a reasoning
component of a long-running physical controller, which responsibilities
can be adaptive and which must remain explicit, numerical, and
externally enforceable?_

![Refer to caption](2610.02038v1/teaser.png)

Figure 1: Mímir Physical-Embedded Irrigation Control.

This question exposes three coupled challenges. Q1: physical grounding.
Language models operate naturally over semantic representations, whereas
irrigation is governed by quantities with units, conservation
relationships, state transitions, and admissible action bounds. The
agent should not be asked to reconstruct these relationships implicitly
from prose. Q2: decision repair. A physically feasible action is not
necessarily a desirable action. Multiple feasible actions can induce
different delayed stress, drainage, and water-use consequences, so a
first language-model proposal should be treated as a hypothesis to
verify but not a device command. Q3: experience-driven adaptation.
Recurrent decision errors may become visible only after repeated
operation. A persistent agent should learn from those errors, but
learning in a physical system raises an additional governance question:
what is allowed to change, and what must remain invariant?

We present Mímir, a two-timescale, physics-grounded LLM agent that
answers these questions by separating _reasoning authority_ from
_physical authority_. At the fast timescale, a deterministic soil-water
model converts observations into a structured physical state and
feasible action region. The LLM proposes and revises a decision;
programmatic simulation exposes the consequences of that proposal; a
deterministic selector ranks a bounded candidate set; and runtime
assurance retains final execution authority. At the slow timescale,
recurring corrections and outcome patterns are distilled into persistent
contextual principles that can bias future proposals. Importantly, this
adaptation don’t need to modify the transition model, evaluator, action
bounds, or execution gate. The LLM therefore contributes contextual
interpretation, proposal generation, and revision.

Recent continual and self-improving agents increasingly treat memory,
prompts, and other framework components as objects that can change from
experience without updating model weights [Kim et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib19); [Sridhar et al. (2026)](#bib.bib29); [Ren et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib27); [Feng et al. (2026)](#bib.bib11). Mímir adopts a
deliberately bounded version of this idea: experience may repair future
reasoning context, but not the trusted physical substrate. Fast-loop
repair ends when verification no longer changes the proposal or its
fixed budget is exhausted; slow-loop memory changes only when recurring
evidence crosses a configured trigger. We therefore use _bounded
adaptation_. This boundary distinguishes Mímir from language-only
refinement [Madaan et al. (2023)](#bib.bib25); [Du et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib8); [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib34) and from broader
self-adaptive physical agents [Kaushik et al. (2026)](#bib.bib17):
language agreement is not physical evidence, memory cannot relax
execution constraints, and an LLM proposal never directly becomes an
actuator command.

We evaluate Mímir retrospectively using field-observed data from
Michigan, Texas, and Colorado spanning multiple crops and years. In the
reported multi-site comparison, Mímir attains an aggregate control cost
of $`100.94\pm 1.80`$ with $`2.58\pm 0.12`$ mm irrigation under the
common evaluator, while the historical schedule replay uses
$`5.32\pm 0.57`$ mm. Mechanism-specific matched ablations show higher
control cost when forward simulation, verified proposal revision, or
persistent context is removed. A separate nine-season sequence exhibits
substantial seasonal variation without sustained divergence. That shows
the ability of long-horizon operational stability. Finally, performance
across the LLM sizes remains in the same broad range across several
model families, indicating that the control architecture surrounding the
language model materially shapes behavior.

Our contributions are threefold:

- •
  Physics-grounded agent interface. We formulate and instantiate an
  interface in which language reasoning operates over explicit physical
  state and deterministic transition tools, while state evolution,
  feasible actions, and execution constraints remain outside the
  language model.
- •
  Simulator-grounded decision repair. We develop a bounded
  propose-verify-revise mechanism in which an LLM proposal is confronted
  with numerical rollout evidence before a deterministic selector ranks
  executable candidates.
- •
  Bounded experience-driven adaptation. We integrate evidence-triggered
  persistent context that can repair future proposal priors from
  recurring experience while leaving the physical model, evaluator, and
  runtime constraints immutable.

#### Irrigation and long-horizon control.

Irrigation scheduling is commonly formulated as a physical control
problem linking soil-water availability, crop demand, weather forcing,
and irrigation actions. FAO-56 provides widely used evapotranspiration
and crop-water formulations [Allen et al. (1998)](#bib.bib2); MPC
optimizes future actions under explicit dynamics and
constraints [Delgoda et al. (2016)](#bib.bib4); [Garcia et al.
(1989)](#bib.bib13); and learning-based approaches optimize policies
from simulated or observed interaction [Yang et al. (2020)](#bib.bib35);
[Ding & Du (2024)](#bib.bib5); [Kelly & Foster (2021)](#bib.bib18).
These approaches are strong control baselines and, when the control
problem is fully specified, do not require language reasoning.
Agricultural LLM systems increasingly incorporate sensor and weather
context for irrigation advice or decision support [Kandamali et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib16); [Yu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib37). Our question is
different: how should a language-model agent participate in repeated
closed-loop control when numerical prediction and execution authority
must remain explicit?

#### Grounded, embodied, and verifiable LLM agents.

Grounded agents couple language reasoning to external state, tools, or
executable capabilities. SayCan grounds plans in robot affordances [Ahn
et al. (2022)](#bib.bib1), PaLM-E incorporates embodied
observations [Driess et al. (2023)](#bib.bib7), and ReAct interleaves
reasoning with actions and observations [Yao et al. (2022)](#bib.bib36).
Iterative methods such as Self-Refine and multi-agent debate revisit an
initial generation through critique or interaction [Madaan et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib25); [Du et al. (2024)](#bib.bib8), while VIRF couples
an LLM planner to a deterministic logic tutor for verifiable plan
repair [Wu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib34). Mímir shares the principle that
generative proposals should be externally checked, but continuous
physical control imposes a different contract: numerical simulation
determines state consequences, deterministic logic ranks bounded
actions, and runtime assurance remains independent of language-model
agreement.

#### Persistent memory, continual learning, and self-improving agents.

A growing literature studies agents that accumulate experience.
Reflexion stores verbal feedback [Shinn et al. (2023)](#bib.bib28),
Voyager accumulates reusable skills [Wang et al. (2023)](#bib.bib33),
online continual learning has been studied for embodied
instruction-following agents [Kim et al. (2024)](#bib.bib19), and
experience retrieval improves long-horizon robotic control [Sridhar
et al. (2026)](#bib.bib29). Agentic Context Engineering (ACE) treats
context as an evolving playbook [Zhang et al. (2025)](#bib.bib38). More
recent work frames changes to prompts, memory, tools, or controller
frameworks as forms of agent self-improvement [Ren et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib27), and proposes broader self-evolving embodied systems
that adapt memory, task knowledge, or models over time [Feng et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib11). Concurrent physical-agent work similarly
investigates self-adaptive crop management with verification and
reflection [Kaushik et al. (2026)](#bib.bib17).

Mímir occupies a deliberately narrower point in this design space. It
performs no model-weight update, no online world-model revision, and no
autonomous relaxation of safety rules. Experience can change only the
contextual framework used for future proposals. This boundedness is
important in physical control because continual adaptation and runtime
assurance solve different problems: the former determines what the agent
should remember or reconsider, while the latter determines what it is
allowed to execute. Control barrier functions, Simplex-style runtime
assurance, and safe RL provide complementary mechanisms for maintaining
explicit safety conditions [Ames et al. (2019)](#bib.bib3); [Mehmood
et al. (2022)](#bib.bib26); [Wachi & Sui (2020)](#bib.bib32). The
contribution of Mímir is a two-timescale architecture that permits
experience-driven reasoning repair while preserving an immutable
numerical control boundary.

## 3 Problem Formulation

We study persistent physical control in which an agent repeatedly
observes an evolving environment, proposes an action, and receives
feedback only through subsequent state evolution. At decision step
$`t`$, observations $`o_{t}`$ are mapped by a fixed physical abstraction
to state

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       s_{t}=\phi(o_{t};\theta_{\mathrm{phys}}),
       ```                                        |     | (1) |

and the process evolves as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       s_{t+1}=f(s_{t},a_{t},w_{t}),\qquad a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}(s_{t}),
       ```                                                              |     | (2) |

where $`w_{t}`$ denotes exogenous forcing and $`\mathcal{A}(s_{t})`$ is
the feasible action set. Over horizon $`T`$, the controller seeks low
cumulative physical cost,

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}c(s_{t},a_{t})\right],\qquad\text{s.t. Eq.~\ref{eq:problem_dynamics}}.
``` |  | (3) |

Irrigation instantiates this problem with root-zone water state,
precipitation, and atmospheric demand as forcing, and irrigation depth
as the action.

Our objective is to decompose control into an adaptive semantic layer
and a trusted numerical layer. Let $`x_{t}`$ denote the structured
decision context and $`m_{e}`$ the persistent context for episode or
season $`e`$. The LLM produces a proposal

|     |                                |     |     |
|-----|--------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       z_{t}=g_{\theta}(x_{t},m_{e}),
       ```                             |     | (4) |

while the executed action is determined only after physical evaluation
and assurance,

|     |                                               |     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       a_{t}=\mathcal{D}(s_{t},z_{t};f,\mathcal{A}),
       ```                                            |     | (5) |

where $`\mathcal{D}`$ denotes bounded rollout, candidate selection,
runtime assurance, and actuation gating. This decomposition makes the
allocation of authority explicit: $`g_{\theta}`$ can influence what is
proposed or reconsidered, but $`f`$, $`\mathcal{A}`$, and
$`\mathcal{D}`$ determine physical consequence and executable action.

Persistent experience acts on a slower timescale. Let $`\tau_{e}`$
denote the completed trajectory and outcome summary for episode $`e`$,
and let $`q_{e}`$ be the configured evidence trigger extracted from that
experience. We write

|     |                                                              |     |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       m_{e+1}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}(m_{e},\tau_{e}),&q_{e}=1,\\
       m_{e},&q_{e}=0,\end{cases}
       ```                                                           |     | (6) |

where $`\mathcal{G}`$ modifies only the contextual framework supplied to
future LLM calls. In particular, $`\theta`$, $`f`$,
$`\theta_{\mathrm{phys}}`$, the evaluator, and runtime constraints are
fixed by construction.
Equation [6](#S3.E6 "In 3 Problem Formulation ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
formalizes the limited sense in which the agent can improve from
experience: the reasoning context can be repaired when recurring
evidence warrants an update, but the trusted physical substrate cannot
self-modify.

This exposes three questions. Q1 How should $`x_{t}`$ show physically
meaningful state and constraints to language reasoning? Q2 how should a
proposal $`z_{t}`$ be checked and repaired before it can influence
$`a_{t}`$? Q3 how should $`m_{e}`$ accumulate experience without
changing physical truth or execution authority? Mímir addresses these
questions through a physics-grounded interface, simulator-grounded
decision repair, and bounded cross-episode adaptation.

Figure 2: Mímir as a two-timescale physical agent. In the daily loop,
observations are converted to a structured physical state; the LLM
proposes or revises a decision; runtime assurance gates execution. In

## 4 Mímir Design

Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Problem Formulation ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
shows the central design principle: adaptive reasoning and physical
authority are intentionally asymmetric. The language model may propose,
explain, and revise. Numerical tools determine predicted consequences.
Deterministic logic constrains and ranks actions. Runtime assurance
retains final actuator authority. Persistent memory may influence future
reasoning, but it cannot expand what the physical controller permits.
This creates two repair loops: a fast loop corrects individual proposals
using physical evidence, while a slow loop consolidates recurring
experience into contextual principles. Neither loop can alter physical
state-transition rules or execution constraints.

### 4.1 Physics-Grounded Agent Interface

Mímir exposes a structured numerical state. Let $`S_{t}`$ denote
root-zone water storage, $`P_{t}`$ precipitation, $`ET_{t}`$
evapotranspiration, $`I_{t}`$ irrigation, and $`D_{t}`$ drainage. The
controller-side model uses the lightweight balance

|     |                                         |     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{t+1}=S_{t}+P_{t}+I_{t}-ET_{t}-D_{t}.
       ```                                      |     | (7) |

The reasoning context contains root-zone storage, field capacity
($`FC`$), wilting point, crop stage, evapotranspiration, and
decision-time forecast information. This representation is an interface
contract in which units, state variables, and admissible quantities are
fixed before querying the LLM.
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Physical Model and Execution Constraints ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
specifies the physical abstraction and its assumptions.

The state also induces an execution-feasible action interval. With
anticipated retained precipitation $`\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{eff},t+1}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
I_{t}^{\max}=\max\!\left(0,FC-S_{t}-\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{eff},t+1}\right),\qquad\mathcal{A}_{t}=[0,I_{t}^{\max}].
``` |  | (8) |

The final command is projected into this interval,

|     |                                                 |     |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       a_{t}=\Pi_{\mathcal{A}_{t}}(\widetilde{a}_{t}).
       ```                                              |     | (9) |

This is an upper-bound execution constraint. The LLM cannot expand
$`\mathcal{A}_{t}`$, alter units, or change the transition parameters
used by the controller.

### 4.2 Simulator-Grounded Decision Repair

Physical feasibility alone does not determine which feasible action is
preferable. Mímir therefore treats language output as a proposal to be
repaired if physical evidence contradicts its rationale. Given
structured context $`x_{t}`$, the proposer generates

|     |                                                  |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \widetilde{a}_{t}^{(0)}=\mathrm{Propose}(x_{t}).
       ```                                               |     | (10) |

At repair round $`k`$, programmatic tools evaluate the proposed
consequence,

|     |                                                             |     |      |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       e_{t}^{(k)}=\mathrm{Verify}(s_{t},\widetilde{a}_{t}^{(k)}),
       ```                                                          |     | (11) |

and the proposal can be revised using the resulting numerical evidence,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{a}_{t}^{(k+1)}=\mathrm{Revise}(x_{t},\widetilde{a}_{t}^{(k)},e_{t}^{(k)}).
``` |  | (12) |

The implementation names the proposal and verification roles “Farmer”
and “Physicist”. The distinction is between generative proposal and
programmatic verification. The fast repair loop terminates when
verification no longer changes the proposal or when the fixed round
budget is reached, so the agent cannot deliberate indefinitely.

The repaired proposal remains only an *anchor*. A deterministic selector
constructs a finite set $`\mathcal{C}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{t}`$
around the anchor while retaining reference candidates such as zero
irrigation and the admissible refill bound. Each candidate is rolled out
over the control horizon,

|     |                                                            |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \widehat{\tau}_{t}(a)=\mathrm{Rollout}(s_{t},a,w_{t:t+H}),
       ```                                                         |     | (13) |

and assigned fixed rollout cost $`J_{t}(a)`$. The selector chooses

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
a_{t}^{\star}=\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{t}}\left(N_{\mathrm{viol}}(a),J_{t}(a)\right),
``` |  | (14) |

using lexicographic priority on modeled physical violations and then
rollout cost. The selected candidate passes runtime assurance and a
trusted fallback path before the final projection in
Eq. [9](#S4.E9 "In 4.1 Physics-Grounded Agent Interface ‣ 4 Mímir Design ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control").

Language can therefore change the search prior, but not physical
evidence or execution authority. The corresponding ablation tests
whether numerical feedback improves the LLM-conditioned proposal, not
whether language agents replace optimization.

### 4.3 Bounded Experience-Driven Adaptation

The fast loop repairs an individual decision, but persistent agents also
encounter repeated failure modes. A proposal may repeatedly overreact to
forecast uncertainty, require the same verifier correction, or reach the
same runtime safeguard across multiple episodes. Mímir therefore
maintains a slower adaptation loop that converts recurring experience
into reusable contextual knowledge.

Let $`\tau_{e}`$ be the completed trajectory and outcome summary for
episode or season $`e`$. We implement $`\mathcal{G}`$ in
Eq. [6](#S3.E6 "In 3 Problem Formulation ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
using Agentic Context Engineering [Zhang et al. (2025)](#bib.bib38).
Deliberation traces, repeated verifier corrections, control-cost
patterns, and runtime interventions provide evidence from which we can
distill compact principles. A principle records the context in which a
pattern occurred, the corrective tendency, relevant exceptions, and its
supporting evidence. When the configured recurrence trigger is not met,
the slow loop performs no update; thus adaptation is event-driven rather
than an unconditional rewrite after every season.

Memory can condition later proposals or provide a soft anchor, but
cannot rank or execute actions. Across updates,

|     |                           |     |      |
|-----|---------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       m_{e}\rightarrow m_{e+1},
       ```                        |     | (15) |

while

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g_{\theta}\text{'s weights},\quad f_{\mathrm{phys}},\quad\theta_{\mathrm{phys}},\quad\mathcal{A}_{t},\quad\text{Evaluator},\quad\text{Runtime Assurance}
``` |  | (16) |

remain fixed. Memory cannot access future held-out outcomes, relax a
safety bound, change the scoring rule, or bypass deterministic
selection.

We call this *bounded experience-driven adaptation*: framework-level
self-improvement in which accumulated experience can change future
reasoning context, but not model weights, the physical world model, or
runtime constraints. The central question is therefore not only whether
a physical agent can learn, but *which parts are permitted to learn and
under what evidence*.
Appendix [H](#A8 "Appendix H Bounded Experience-Driven Adaptation ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
gives the full memory contract.

## 5 Experimental Setup

### 5.1 Datasets and Protocol

The corpus contains approximately 6,938 days, or 19 crop-years, spanning
four environments: Michigan potato under humid, variable rainfall [Dong
et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6); Bushland, Texas maize under high evaporative
demand [Evett et al. (2024)](#bib.bib9); Colorado LIRF maize under
limited-irrigation treatments [Trout & Bausch (2017)](#bib.bib30); and a
separate Colorado sunflower sequence for the nine-season analysis [Trout
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib31). Available streams include hourly weather
forcing, irrigation records, evapotranspiration-related variables, and
soil-water observations.
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Dataset Provenance and Preprocessing ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
summarizes their role and describes provenance and preprocessing.

For each eligible chronological segment, the first 60% is reserved for
calibration or baseline training and the remaining 40% for evaluation;
samples are never shuffled. Decisions are issued every 24 h using a
168 h history and a 24 h forward horizon, while the physical model
integrates hourly. At time $`t`$, a method can use only information
available by that decision time. All methods share the same forecast
construction, actuator limits, feasible action bounds, and evaluation
window. The protocol forbids future reads, initialization or repair of
persistent context from future held-out outcomes, test-based seed
selection, and overlap between calibration and evaluation windows. The
nine-season sequence preserves chronological order and is analyzed
separately from the main aggregate.

### 5.2 Reference Controllers and Comparison Contract

We report four reference classes. Historical irrigation replays recorded
irrigation actions under the common evaluator and serves as a management
reference; it is not a comparison of realized crop yield. MAD-50
irrigates after 50% management-allowable depletion and refills toward
field capacity subject to the common action limit [FAO
(2017)](#bib.bib10). ET-Replace replaces accumulated evapotranspiration
loss using the same causal weather information. DRLIC provides the
reinforcement-learning reference [Ding & Du (2024)](#bib.bib5).
Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Reference Controller Implementations ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
additionally describes zero irrigation, fixed-threshold control, and a
deterministic physics-search/MPC reference used diagnostically to
separate language-conditioned anchoring from numerical action search.

### 5.3 Metrics

The primary metric is seasonal physical control cost,

|     |                                |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       C=w_{W}W+w_{S}S+w_{R}R+w_{L}L,
       ```                             |     | (17) |

where $`W`$ is irrigation applied, $`S`$ accumulated soil-water stress
deficit, $`R`$ runoff, and $`L`$ deep percolation/leaching. All terms
are computed from the same hourly trajectory and use a single fixed set
of weights across sites, controllers, and language-model backbones.
Lower $`C`$ is better. We call this quantity *control cost*. It is an
absolute trajectory objective. We separately report irrigation applied
in millimeters; lower water use is interpreted only jointly with control
cost, because a controller can save water by accepting larger stress.
Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Metric Definition ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
defines the components and aggregation protocol.

### 5.4 Implementation Details

Unless stated otherwise, Mímir uses a DeepSeek-14B backbone served
locally through Ollama. Fast-loop decision repair is capped at three
proposal-verification-revision rounds. Every proposal is followed by
deterministic candidate construction, rollout scoring, runtime
assurance, fallback if required, and the final actuation gate; the LLM
never writes directly to the actuator.

The slow loop changes only persistent context. Recurrence triggers,
memory budget, activation/reset rules, and the representation of stored
principles are fixed by configuration within each matched experiment. A
season with no qualifying recurring pattern produces no contextual
update. For model-scale and model-family studies, only the
language-model checkpoint changes; prompts, physical state, simulator,
action bounds, repair budget, candidate selection, and evaluation
protocol remain fixed.
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Language-Model Interface ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
reports prompting and parsing details, and
Appendix [H](#A8 "Appendix H Bounded Experience-Driven Adaptation ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
details the persistent-context update contract.

### 6.2 Performance of Explicit Physical Grounding

We ablate two distinct responsibilities of the physical substrate:
predicting delayed consequences and enforcing executable bounds.
Removing forward simulation increases control cost from $`149.5`$ to
$`730.5`$ on its matched slice, while irrigation falls from $`3.29`$ to
$`0.45`$ mm. The joint decrease in water and increase in cost is
consistent with under-irrigation whose delayed deficit is not
represented during proposal evaluation. Removing the execution shield
increases cost from $`147.2`$ to $`259.9`$ while mean irrigation changes
comparatively little. The second result indicates a different role:
execution bounding can alter costly tail behavior without determining
the controller’s mean irrigation level. Together, the two comparisons
support the bounded claim that explicit physical prediction and
execution constraints materially affect behavior under the tested
conditions. Neither comparison guarantees future agronomic safety. The
direct/zero-shot LLM diagnostic used a different metric configuration
and is therefore reported only in
Appendix [J](#A10 "Appendix J Component Ablations ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control");
its scalar value is not combined with the matched ablations.

repair improves the evaluated objective on this slice relative to the
corresponding single-pass proposal. It does not establish that free-form
multi-agent debate is superior to every alternative inference-time
strategy, nor that the language model replaces deterministic search.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented Mímir, a physics-grounded LLM agent for long-horizon
irrigation control. The design begins from a simple premise: persistent
physical agents need the ability to adapt, but not every part of a
physical controller should be equally free to change. Mímir therefore
separates three responsibilities. A structured physical interface
defines state and feasible actions; simulator-grounded decision repair
lets language-model proposals be challenged with numerical evidence
before deterministic selection; and bounded cross-episode context
carries recurring experience forward without changing the physical model
or execution constraints. Across the reported multi-site and multi-year
evaluation, matched ablations show that physical simulation, verified
repair, and persistent context each affect control behavior. The
resulting contribution shows one way to build a persistent physical
agent in which reasoning can evolve from experience while physical truth
and actuator authority remain explicitly governed.

## Appendix A Physical Model and Execution Constraints

### A.1 Root-Zone State and Units

All water quantities are represented as equivalent water depth in
millimeters. When volumetric soil moisture $`\theta_{t}`$ (m³/m³) is
available, the controller maps it to root-zone storage as

|     |                                    |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{t}=\theta_{t}Z_{\mathrm{root}},
       ```                                 |     | (18) |

with $`Z_{\mathrm{root}}=600\,\mathrm{mm}`$ in the reported
configuration. This fixed depth is part of the controller abstraction.

The structured controller state contains root-zone storage $`S_{t}`$,
field capacity $`FC`$, wilting point $`WP`$, crop-stage information,
evapotranspiration, observed precipitation, and the decision-time
precipitation input. We keep three quantities conceptually separate
throughout the system: (i) physical storage limits, (ii) the agronomic
stress threshold used by the evaluator, and (iii) the feasible action
bound used by the controller.

### A.2 Water-Balance Transition

The controller-side model evolves root-zone storage according to

|     |                                         |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{t+1}=S_{t}+P_{t}+I_{t}-ET_{t}-D_{t},
       ```                                      |     | (19) |

where $`P_{t}`$, $`I_{t}`$, $`ET_{t}`$, and $`D_{t}`$ denote
precipitation, irrigation, evapotranspiration, and drainage/deep
percolation, respectively. The bucket approximation uses

|     |                                                           |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       D_{t}=\max\!\left(0,\,S_{t}+P_{t}+I_{t}-ET_{t}-FC\right).
       ```                                                        |     | (20) |

The model is intentionally lightweight. It is used to construct and
verify bounded irrigation decisions. Reference evapotranspiration is
computed from the available meteorological forcing using FAO-56
Penman–Monteith where the required variables are available [Allen et al.
(1998)](#bib.bib2); otherwise, the implementation follows a
deterministic fallback path fixed before evaluation and shared by all
controllers.

### A.3 Effective Precipitation and Causal Forecast Input

The controller can map forecast precipitation to retained root-zone
input using the fixed transform

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
P_{\mathrm{eff}}=\begin{cases}P\frac{125-0.2P}{125},&P\leq 250,\\
125+0.1P,&P>250.\end{cases}
``` |  | (21) |

Here $`P`$ denotes the precipitation quantity supplied through the
controller’s forecast interface, expressed in millimeters for the
corresponding decision interval. We treat
Eq. [21](#A1.E21 "In A.3 Effective Precipitation and Causal Forecast Input ‣ Appendix A Physical Model and Execution Constraints ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
strictly as a fixed implementation heuristic in the controller
interface. Every controller receives the same transformed causal input,
and no controller may access realized future rainfall beyond the frozen
forecast interface.

### A.4 Feasible Action and Execution Gate

Before actuation, the controller computes the refill upper bound

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
I_{t}^{\max}=\max\!\left(0,\,FC-S_{t}-\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{eff},t+1}\right),
``` |  | (22) |

which induces the legal action interval

|     |                                      |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{A}(S_{t})=[0,I_{t}^{\max}].
       ```                                   |     | (23) |

Given a proposal $`\widetilde{I}_{t}`$, the final actuation gate applies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
I_{t}=\Pi_{\mathcal{A}(S_{t})}(\widetilde{I}_{t})=\min\!\left(\max(0,\widetilde{I}_{t}),I_{t}^{\max}\right).
``` |  | (24) |

This mechanism provides execution-feasibility and upper-bound
protection. It does not guarantee avoidance of future crop-water deficit
under arbitrary forecast error. Mass-balance checks and the device-level
fallback/watchdog path remain external to the LLM and cannot be modified
by persistent context.

## Appendix C Evaluation Protocol and Leakage Controls

### C.1 Chronological Split

For the main comparison, the first 60% of each eligible chronological
segment is reserved for calibration or baseline training and the
remaining 40% for evaluation. Time series are never shuffled. Each
control decision uses a 168-hour history and a 24-hour control horizon.
The physical process is integrated at hourly resolution, and the next
controller invocation occurs after 24 hours.

The nine-season sequence is analyzed as a preserved chronological
sequence.

### C.2 Causal Information Policy

The evaluation protocol enforces the following leakage guards:

- •
  no controller may access observations after the current decision time;
- •
  persistent context may not be initialized or updated from future
  held-out outcomes;
- •
  held-out results may not be used to select a favorable random seed;
- •
  calibration/training and evaluation windows do not overlap.

The forecast interface is identical for all methods. In retrospective
replay, all controllers receive the same causal precipitation input and
the same precipitation-persistence construction where that forecast rule
is used.

### C.3 Matched Controller and Component Comparisons

For every controlled comparison, the initial state, weather sequence,
observation history, physical action range, metric definition, and
random seed are held fixed. Changing the controller does not change the
environmental forcing or the evaluation rule.

Each ablation is interpreted only against the full configuration
evaluated on the same matched slice. Several ablations were produced on
different matched slices, so their absolute full-system values differ.
Consequently, ablation effect sizes are interpreted within row and are
not ranked across rows.

## Appendix D Metric Definition

### D.1 Seasonal Physical Control Cost

The frozen evaluator computes

|     |                                |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       C=w_{W}W+w_{S}S+w_{R}R+w_{L}L,
       ```                             |     | (25) |

where

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle W`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{t}I_{t},`$ |  | (26) |
|  | $`\displaystyle S`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\max(0,S_{\mathrm{stress}}-S_{t}),`$ |  | (27) |
|  | $`\displaystyle R`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{t}R_{t},`$ |  | (28) |
|  | $`\displaystyle L`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{t}L_{t}.`$ |  | (29) |

Here $`W`$ is irrigation applied, $`S`$ is accumulated storage deficit
below the configured stress threshold, $`R`$ is runoff, and $`L`$ is
deep percolation/leaching. All components are computed from the same
hourly trajectory. The weights $`(w_{W},w_{S},w_{R},w_{L})`$ are fixed
globally across sites, controllers, and language-model backbones.

Because
Eq. [25](#A4.E25 "In D.1 Seasonal Physical Control Cost ‣ Appendix D Metric Definition ‣ Mímir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control")
is an absolute trajectory objective.

#### Frozen numerical weights.

The exact numerical values of $`(w_{W},w_{S},w_{R},w_{L})`$ are taken
from the frozen experiment configuration associated with the reported
results. They should be inserted here verbatim from that configuration

## Appendix E Reference Controller Implementations

### E.1 Historical Irrigation Replay

The historical reference replays recorded irrigation actions under the

### E.2 Zero Irrigation and Fixed Threshold

A zero-action controller provides a lower-water diagnostic for
identifying degenerate water-saving behavior. A fixed-threshold
controller irrigates from the current soil-water state using a
pre-specified threshold and has no learned or language-model state.

### E.3 MAD-50

MAD-50 computes available water from field capacity and the configured
lower agronomic threshold and triggers irrigation after 50% allowable
depletion. When triggered, it refills toward field capacity subject to
the shared actuator limit. It receives no privileged future information.

### E.4 ET Replacement

ET-Replace accumulates evapotranspiration loss since the previous
irrigation event and requests replacement of that loss, clipped to the
same feasible action interval as the other controllers. Weather input
and ET computation follow the same causal interface used by Mímir.

### E.5 Deterministic Physics-Search/MPC Diagnostic

A deterministic physics-search controller enumerates a finite candidate
action set under the same controller-side physical model and selects the
candidate with the minimum rollout objective. We use this controller
diagnostically to distinguish language-conditioned anchoring from
numerical action search.

### E.6 DRLIC and Learning References

The reproduced DRLIC reference uses the common state/action interface
and the same physical action limits as Mímir in the reported comparison.
Learning-based values are treated as comparable only when the trained
policy is replayed through the common evaluator; externally reported
rewards are not mixed with the manuscript’s control-cost metric.

## Appendix F Language-Model Interface

The main configuration uses a frozen DeepSeek-14B model through a local
Ollama client. The prompt contains the structured physical state,
actuator limits, forecast information, and active persistent principles
and requests structured JSON output.

The primary decoding configuration uses greedy decoding with temperature
zero where applicable, a fixed software seed, think=False, format=json,
and num_predict=2048. No model-weight update, fine-tuning, or gradient
step occurs during control or across seasons.

Only designated action fields are parsed from the model response.
Malformed responses enter a deterministic fallback path and cannot
bypass candidate selection, runtime assurance, or the final actuation
gate. Per-call telemetry records success/error state, retry count,
latency, prompt length, response length, and model identifier.
Experiment-level metadata additionally record the software revision and
data hash.

## Appendix G Simulator-Grounded Decision Repair

### G.1 Proposal–Verification–Revision Loop

At decision time, Mímir executes the bounded repair process

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle a_{t}^{(0)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{Propose}(x_{t}),`$ |  | (31) |
|  | $`\displaystyle e_{t}^{(k)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{Verify}(s_{t},a_{t}^{(k)}),`$ |  | (32) |
|  | $`\displaystyle a_{t}^{(k+1)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{Revise}(x_{t},a_{t}^{(k)},e_{t}^{(k)}).`$ |  | (33) |

The loop terminates when physical verification no longer changes the
proposed action or after the fixed three-round budget.

“Farmer” and “Physicist” are implementation role names. The former
proposes an irrigation action and rationale; the latter invokes
numerical tools and returns structured physical feedback. The
methodological contribution is the separation of proposal generation
from programmatic physical verification, not the role names themselves
or free-form debate in isolation.

### G.2 Bounded Candidate Selection

The deliberation output does not directly determine the executed action.
Instead, it provides an anchor for a deterministic candidate selector.

Without a language-conditioned anchor, the selector constructs
candidates over the legal action interval and retains physically
meaningful references, including zero irrigation and refill toward field
capacity. With an LLM/ACE anchor, the selector searches a bounded
neighborhood around that anchor while retaining the same reference
actions. A large raw LLM output cannot enlarge the legal action
interval.

Each daily candidate is expanded over the 24-hour horizon, rolled out by
the fixed controller-side model, and evaluated by the frozen rollout
objective. Selection minimizes

|     |                                         |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \left(N_{\mathrm{viol}}(a),J(a)\right),
       ```                                      |     | (34) |

lexicographically, where $`N_{\mathrm{viol}}`$ is the modeled
physical-violation count and $`J`$ is the rollout cost. The selected
action is subsequently passed to runtime assurance, fallback logic if
required, and the final actuation gate.

The fast-loop execution sequence is therefore:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\text{proposal}\rightarrow\text{verification/revision}\rightarrow\text{anchor}\rightarrow\text{bounded search}\rightarrow\text{physical rollout}\rightarrow\text{selection}\rightarrow\text{runtime assurance}\rightarrow\text{actuation gate}.
``` |  |

### G.3 Representative Trace

A representative trace illustrates the interaction:

> State: $`S_{t}=19.7\,\mathrm{mm}`$, $`WP=10.5\,\mathrm{mm}`$,
> $`FC=71.8\,\mathrm{mm}`$, $`ET_{t}=3.8\,\mathrm{mm}`$,
> $`\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{eff},t+1}=0\,\mathrm{mm}`$.
> Proposal: $`8.0\,\mathrm{mm}`$.
> Verification: the simulated next state remains above the configured
> lower threshold; additional irrigation raises modeled drainage risk.
> Revision: $`4.0\,\mathrm{mm}`$.
> Verification: the zero-irrigation rollout remains feasible under the
> same short-horizon model.
> Final revision: $`0.0\,\mathrm{mm}`$.

## Appendix H Bounded Experience-Driven Adaptation

The slow loop modifies the contextual framework supplied to future
language-model calls without changing model weights or the physical
controller. Let $`\tau_{e}`$ denote the completed trajectory and outcome
summary for episode/season $`e`$, $`m_{e}`$ the persistent context, and
$`q_{e}`$ a configuration-controlled recurrence/evidence trigger. The
update takes the form

|     |                                                              |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       m_{e+1}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}(m_{e},\tau_{e}),&q_{e}=1,\\
       m_{e},&q_{e}=0.\end{cases}
       ```                                                           |     | (35) |

The system is not forced to rewrite its context after every episode: if
no recurring pattern satisfies the configured evidence condition, no
contextual update occurs.

ACE implements $`\mathcal{G}`$ as a soft principle/anchor memory used by
the reasoning layer. Deliberation traces, repeated verifier corrections,
control-cost patterns, and runtime-assurance interventions can provide
evidence for a candidate principle. A stored principle contains an
identifier, source context, trigger, suggested action tendency,
exception, supporting evidence, version, and activation state.

The adaptation pathway preserves the following invariants:

- •
  no language-model weight update;
- •
  no modification of physical parameters, transition equations, or
  evaluation rules;
- •
  no relaxation of action or runtime-assurance bounds;
- •
  no future held-out observations in persistent context;
- •
  no bypass of candidate selection, runtime assurance, fallback, or the
  actuation gate.

Accordingly, “adaptation” denotes a change in future reasoning context.
This is scaffold/framework-level.

### H.1 Stopping Conditions

At the fast timescale, proposal repair stops when physical verification
no longer changes the proposal or the fixed three-round budget is
reached. At the slow timescale, no persistent memory update occurs
unless the recurrence/evidence trigger is satisfied.

#### Frozen adaptation configuration.

Memory-update thresholds, recurrence rules, memory budget, and reset
policy are fixed within each matched comparison and recorded in the
corresponding frozen experiment configuration. Their exact values should
be copied verbatim from the frozen configuration into the final archival
supplement.

## Appendix N Discussion and Limitations

#### What the LLM contributes.

The LLM in Mímir is neither the physical model nor the final optimizer.
It maps structured state, forecast, crop-stage information, and
persistent guidance to a context-conditioned proposal and can revise
that proposal after numerical feedback. Physical rollout evaluates
consequences, deterministic selection ranks bounded candidates, and
runtime assurance retains final execution authority. The paper therefore
studies how adaptive semantic reasoning can be composed with trusted
numerical control; it does not establish that an LLM is necessary for
every irrigation problem or that it is superior to the best
deterministic optimizer.

#### Bounded self-improvement and stopping conditions.

Mímir implements scaffold-level, experience-driven adaptation.
Persistent principles can change future proposals, but updates are
evidence-triggered and cannot modify model weights, the physical
transition model, the evaluator, or the feasible-action set. Fast-loop
repair terminates when physical verification no longer changes the
proposal or the fixed round budget is reached. Slow-loop adaptation is a
no-op when no recurring pattern satisfies the configured update trigger.
These semantics prevent open-ended reflection from becoming part of
actuation.

We do not claim that persistent memory converges, improves
monotonically, or is immune to forgetting. Mímir should therefore be
viewed as a bounded step toward persistent or lifelong physical agency.

#### Implication for embodied physical agents.

Agriculture is a persistent embodied-control problem: actions modify a
physical state, feedback is delayed, environmental forcing changes over
time, and experience accumulates across repeated interaction[Liu et al.
(2025a)](#bib.bib21); [Liu et al. (2024)](#bib.bib20); [Liu et al.
(2025c)](#bib.bib23); [Liu et al. (2025b)](#bib.bib22); [Liu et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib24); [Gan et al. (2023)](#bib.bib12).. The transferable
design lesson is a separation of authority across timescales: semantic
reasoning may propose and adapt; numerical models predict consequences;
deterministic logic constrains execution; and persistent context can
repair future reasoning without rewriting the trusted physical
substrate. Whether the same decomposition transfers to other
long-horizon physical domains remains an empirical question for future
work.
