# Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts

###### Abstract

Harness engineering and self-evolving agents improve language-model
agents quickly, yet their gains live outside the model. The harness is
re-sent and re-paid on every call, and the standard route to
internalization is a weight update that is costly to iterate and fixed
once merged. We propose compiling the harness instead. Latent Harness
distills an external condition into a rank-8 activation shift. The shift
is applied at the output of every decoder block of a frozen backbone, at
0.03–0.05% of parameters. The condition is either an explicit
process-level behavior instruction or the behavioral contract of an
agentic harness. At inference the compiled bundle carries no harness
text and adds no tokens. It exposes three runtime controls for injection
strength, injection phase, and shift-space mixing. Compiled behaviors
recover the source-harness gain in 8 of 10 preregistered settings. Where
the explicit source pays the heaviest per-call token tax, the compiled
form exceeds it. The same bundles transfer zero-shot across tasks (5 of
7 pairs positive) and compose in shift space, while stacked text
instructions interfere destructively. Used as a search space, they lift
budget-matched harness search by 1.45$`\times`$. On three agentic
benchmarks the operator aligns policy to the harness contract. Under
matched data, capacity, and effective strength it outperforms
same-capacity LoRA, which needs 11.1$`\times`$ the parameters to reach
the same band, and it regresses nothing where the base policy is already
strong. Across 1B-to-8B models the method skeleton is invariant while
gain locations move with the model-task pair, which delimits where
compiled harnesses should be deployed. Our code is available at
[https://github.com/rlisml/latentharness](https://github.com/rlisml/latentharness).

## 1 Introduction

The performance of a language-model agent is increasingly set by its
harness, meaning the instructions, tool policies, memory, and
verification scaffolds wrapped around model calls. Recent work searches
over program spaces ([Hu et al., 2025](#bib.bib42); [Zhang et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib44)) and over modular design spaces ([Shang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib43); [Zhang et al., 2025a](#bib.bib45)), and some of it
constrains the search to safety ([Zheng et al., 2025](#bib.bib46)). A
second line makes harnesses improve themselves, either by synthesizing
code harnesses from failures ([Lou et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)), by editing
executable runtime harnesses ([Shao et al., 2026](#bib.bib11)), or by
optimizing harness and model jointly ([Lee et al., 2026](#bib.bib5)).
Surveys document how broad this engineering surface has become ([Meng et
al., 2026](#bib.bib14)). Where the resulting gains end up is almost
always one of two places, in text that surrounds the model or in the
model’s weights.

Both carry structural costs. A textual condition is a recurring expense.
Even a 49-token process instruction is re-sent on every call, the
behavior exists only while the text is present, and serving several
harnesses multiplies prompt length. Weight updates internalize at a
different price. Training-style policy alignment such as SFT, RLOO, and
GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib47)), co-evolution frameworks that
update policy weights alongside the harness ([Chen et al.,
2026c](#bib.bib12); [Chen et al., 2026b](#bib.bib2); [Hebbar et al.,
2026](#bib.bib13)), and self-evolving agents that write acquired skills
into fine-tuned or RL-trained weights ([Huang et al., 2026](#bib.bib17);
[Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib20); [Zhang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib19)) all
modify millions of parameters per behavior. They also bind one behavior
to one set of edited weights and fix the intervention strength at merge
time.

We propose a third destination and compile the harness. At compile time
the frozen model produces its own supervision under the external
condition. The supervision is either teacher traces generated with an
explicit behavior instruction prepended, or decision pairs harvested
from trajectories that succeeded under an agentic harness. A rank-8
low-rank shift is then trained at the output of every decoder block and
exported as a per-layer bundle. At run time the bundle is injected into
the residual stream after each block. The model then behaves as if the
condition were present, without the condition text, at zero additional
tokens, with every backbone parameter bit-identical. We call the
compiled artifact a _latent harness_.

summarizes the pipeline.

Compilation preserves behavior more faithfully than we expected. Across
preregistered settings the compiled shift recovers most of the
explicit-harness gain. In the settings where the text harness pays the
heaviest per-call token tax, the compiled form exceeds it, because the
internalized behavior pays that tax once, at compile time. The compiled
object is the behavior itself and does not reduce to generic task skill.
Preregistered behavioral markers move with the scores, accuracy-only
adapters dissociate from behavior, shuffled pairings fail to reproduce
it, and a harmful harness compiles into a harmful latent with the same
fidelity. Compiled bundles are also reusable. They transfer zero-shot
across tasks, they compose in shift space while stacked text
instructions interfere destructively, and they lift budget-matched
harness search by 1.45$`\times`$. The same operator aligns policy on
three agentic benchmarks. Under matched data, capacity, and effective
strength it outperforms same-capacity LoRA, and on a benchmark where the
base policy is already strong it regresses nothing. Our contributions
are as follows.

- •
  A compilation abstraction for harness gains. One operator, a
  post-block rank-8 shift on a frozen backbone at 0.03–0.05% of
  parameters, compiles two kinds of external conditions into an artifact
  that needs no condition text and no tokens at inference. The artifact
  exposes runtime controls for strength, phase, and mixing.
- •
  Evidence that compilation preserves the behavior. 8 of 10
  preregistered settings recover at least half of the source gain. A
  three-chain protocol over scores, preregistered markers, and controls
  attributes the recovery, including symmetric replication of harmful
  signatures. Where the text source pays the heaviest token tax, the
  compiled form exceeds it.
- •
  Evidence that compiled bundles are reusable. Frozen bundles transfer
  zero-shot across tasks (5 of 7 pairs positive, 59–74%
  out-of-distribution recovery). They compose exactly in shift space,
  where text stacking is destructive, and they deliver a 1.45$`\times`$
  search gain at a matched evaluation budget.
- •
  Policy compilation on agent benchmarks. At 1.012$`\times`$ parameters
  and matched data and strength, the shift beats same-capacity LoRA on
  ToolHop (60.00% vs. 54.74%, paired net $`+5`$). Full-capacity LoRA
  needs 11.1$`\times`$ (4B) and 3.09$`\times`$ (8B) parameters to reach
  the same band. API-Bank shows zero regression. The skeleton is
  invariant from 1B to 8B while gains move with the model-task pair.

## 2 Compiling behaviors into post-block shifts

### 2.1 The post-block injection site

Let the frozen policy be an autoregressive transformer $`\pi_{\theta}`$
with $`L`$ decoder blocks. In residual-stream notation, block $`l`$
computes $`x^{l+1}=x^{l}+f_{l}(x^{l})`$. We inject at the _post-block_
site, the point where everything block $`l`$ computed meets everything
blocks $`l{+}1,\dots,L`$ will read. For every layer we train a down
projection $`D_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}`$ and an up projection
$`U_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}`$ with rank $`r{=}8`$, and replace the
block output by

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\tilde{x}^{l+1}_{i}\;=\;x^{l+1}_{i}\;+\;s\,\alpha\,U_{l}\big(\varphi(D_{l}\,x^{l+1}_{i})\big)\,\mathbf{1}[i\in\Phi],
``` |  | (1) |

where $`s`$ is a global runtime strength, $`\alpha`$ is a fixed training
scale, $`\varphi`$ is a bottleneck nonlinearity, and the phase set
$`\Phi`$ selects which token positions receive the shift. Trainable
parameters total $`2rdL`$, between 0.029% and 0.042% of the backbone
across the five models we study
(Table [3](#A1.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Appendix A Experimental protocol details ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
in the appendix).

Two properties make this site the right compilation target. A
perturbation of the residual trunk is read by every subsequent layer, so
one parameter influences far more computation than a perturbation inside
a single linear map, which propagates only within its layer. The site is
also the unique junction at which any first-order correction to a
block’s behavior can be expressed, which explains why two very different
compile sources succeed under the same operator. [Adila et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib3) show that weight updates can be viewed as activation
shifts. We compile conditions that never required a weight update into
the same substrate. The operator is first-order kin to LoRA ([Hu et al.,
2021](#bib.bib38)), since both inject rank-at-most-$`r`$ perturbations,
which is what makes capacity-aligned comparison possible
(Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Policy compilation on agentic benchmarks ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
The bottleneck nonlinearity is $`\varphi=\mathrm{SiLU}`$ with
zero-initialized $`U_{l}`$ for instruction compilation and the identity
for policy compilation. Both choices work, indicating low sensitivity to
this detail.

### 2.2 Compile source A: explicit behavior instructions

The first source is a registry of seven process-level behavior
instructions. Each is a short prompt prefix of at most 60 tokens,
single-pass and tool-free, and each targets a diagnosed error mode. The
seven cover independent verification, numbered decomposition,
write-the-expression-first evaluation, concise answering, option
elimination, evidence quoting, and test-interface consultation. Every
entry is registered with a frozen hash and carries a predicted
behavioral marker, which is a deterministic parse or regex count on the
raw output such as decomposition-step count or interface-alignment rate.
An entry enters the study only if screening shows a significant explicit
gain
(Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Explicit-harness screening and the MBPP format floor ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).

The teacher is the frozen model with the instruction prepended. It
produces greedy traces that are cached in full and filtered by
correctness. The student is the same frozen model plus the shift of
Eq. [1](#S2.E1 "In 2.1 The post-block injection site ‣ 2 Compiling behaviors into post-block shifts ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts"),
trained on prompts that are *byte-identical* to the unconditioned base
prompts, so the instruction text never appears in any student input
(asserted per batch). The distillation loss is token-level cross-entropy
on filtered teacher traces for generation tasks and an option-level KL
on cached teacher answer distributions for multiple-choice tasks. No
gold labels enter this configuration. Two capacity-matched controls
share the budget, a gold-label task adapter and a joint objective
(Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Experimental protocol details ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).

### 2.3 Compile source B: harness-conditioned strategies

The second source is the behavioral contract of an agentic harness. The
frozen base policy rolls out on the training pool under the official
harness pairing. Each trajectory decomposes into per-step decision
pairs, whose prompt is the full interaction history rendered so far and
whose completion is the action taken. The *strict tier* keeps pairs only
from trajectories whose final answer is correct. The choice of target
distribution for behavioral cloning motivates this filter ([Torabi et
al., 2018](#bib.bib1)). Failed trajectories are draws from the base
policy itself, and training on them pulls the target back toward the
base policy. We observed this effect directly. A balanced tier costs 3.2
accuracy points and inflates action counts by 51%, while the strict tier
gains 3.2 points with 3% inflation. Training applies prefix-masked
cross-entropy to action tokens with hyperparameters identical to the
LoRA comparison configuration
(Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Experimental protocol details ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).

### 2.4 Runtime interface

Compilation exports a bundle $`\{U_{l}D_{l}\}_{l=1}^{L}`$ in fp32 per
layer. Load-back is bit-exact and the engine’s weights are untouched.
Three controls operate at inference without retraining. The global
strength $`s`$ in
Eq. [1](#S2.E1 "In 2.1 The post-block injection site ‣ 2 Compiling behaviors into post-block shifts ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
is hot-swappable within a serving session. The phase
$`\Phi\in\{\text{prompt},\text{generation},\text{both}\}`$ selects which
positions receive the shift. Shift-space mixing combines two compiled
bundles as $`\alpha Z_{a}+\beta Z_{b}`$, realized by a rank-16 merged
bundle with tensor-level error at most $`10^{-5}`$. Mixing must be
defined on induced shifts rather than factor sums, because the
bottleneck nonlinearity makes factor-space addition the wrong algebra
(Section [3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Compiled bundles are reusable objects ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
demonstrates the consequences). Two checks keep the measurements valid.
If metrics are bit-identical across two strengths the injection is
silently off, which we check per configuration. A bounded parse
protocol, independent of configuration, repairs or rejects malformed
tool-call JSON identically everywhere.

### 3.1 Setup

Instruction compilation uses Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (M1), Qwen3-4B ([Yang
et al., 2025](#bib.bib48)) (M2), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (M3), and an
independent replication track on Llama-3.2-1B (base). The evaluation
tasks are BoolQ ([Clark et al., 2019](#bib.bib51)), ARC-Challenge
([Clark et al., 2018](#bib.bib52)), GSM8K ([Cobbe et al.,

Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B on ToolHop ([Ye et al., 2025](#bib.bib39))
($`n{=}95`$), SearchQA over HotpotQA ([Yang et al., 2018](#bib.bib40))

training. Statistics follow one discipline. Criteria are preregistered,
selection uses validation only, and the test set is touched once. We
report paired bootstrap 95% CIs ($`B{=}10{,}000`$) over 3 training seeds
for instruction settings, and at least 2 independent full evaluations

##### Capacity-aligned comparison.

We align three variables at once. The LoRA control acts on q/k/v with
rank 3 and 1.49M parameters (1.012$`\times`$ ours), trains on the same
829 ToolHop decision pairs, and applies the same effective intervention
multiplier (0.5).
Figure [2(a)](#S3.F2.sf1 "In Figure 2 ‣ 3.2 Policy compilation on agentic benchmarks ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
shows the outcome. The shift reaches 60.00% (paired net $`+5`$ over
base) while the capacity-aligned LoRA nets zero, and full-capacity LoRA
(rank 8, all linear projections, 16.4M parameters, 11.1$`\times`$ ours)
reaches 62.11%. At 8B, LoRA r11 with 3.09$`\times`$ our parameters
matches the best shift configuration (62.63% vs. 62.46%). The efficiency
gap follows from the site. A post-block parameter is read by every
downstream layer, whereas an in-layer parameter is not. The shift also
stays scorable at full strength ($`s{=}1.0`$, net $`+1`$), whereas
merged LoRA deltas at enlarged coefficients produced no scorable outputs
under the earlier protocol. The runtime strength control therefore
doubles as a safety margin that preserves the tool-call contract.

##### Stability.

Agentic evaluation under greedy decoding still varies from run to run,
so single runs mislead. The both@0.25 configuration reproduced 55/95
correct in two independent runs, an identical aggregate score despite 14
per-item flips, and it is the only configuration whose every run exceeds
every base run
(Figure [2(b)](#S3.F2.sf2 "In Figure 2 ‣ 3.2 Policy compilation on agentic benchmarks ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
Its path score (0.713) is the highest of all configurations, and it
triggered no rejected tool calls, which indicates that prompt-phase
injection does not strain the tool-call contract. Gains also come
without action inflation (14.41 steps vs. base 11.80 and full-capacity
LoRA 14.61).

##### Zero regression where base is strong.

On API-Bank, a short-chain and format-heavy benchmark where the base
policy is already strong, the generation-phase shift ties base
bit-for-bit across three base runs (0.7647), while the capacity-aligned
LoRA nets $`-2`$ with all three runs below base
(Figure [2(c)](#S3.F2.sf3 "In Figure 2 ‣ 3.2 Policy compilation on agentic benchmarks ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
At 8B every shift configuration is at or above base, and both@0.25 adds
$`+4.4`$ points. Preserving an already-good policy is a property that
weight updates did not provide at matched capacity.

distills the test-consult behavior from only 20 correct teacher traces
(teacher pass@1 is 0.073 at the format floor) and gains $`+31.7`$ test
points. Recovery ratios divide by the explicit gain, so settings with a
small explicit source produce large ratios ($`R{=}5.86`$ here, against
an explicit gain of $`+5.4`$ points). We therefore read this setting by
its absolute delta, and the observation we defend is that the
information needed to install a behavior is far smaller than the
information needed to solve the task. On M2$`\times`$MBPP the compiled
form lands $`+19`$ to $`+29`$ points above its explicit source
(0.660–0.687 vs. 0.396–0.470), and there the denominator is not small.
The explicit harness itself gains $`+0.36`$ to $`+0.43`$ over a
near-zero base. The token tax explains the difference. The explicit
harness spends decode budget at every call, while the compiled behavior
spends it once at compile time and nothing per call. This also places
Latent Harness alone at the zero-token point of the cost-performance
frontier, far from parameter-matched soft prompts that pay 256–576
tokens on every call
(Figure [3(b)](#S3.F3.sf2 "In Figure 3 ‣ 3.3 Instruction compilation preserves the source gain ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
The pattern replicates on an independent track with a base
(non-instruct) 1B model, which recovers the source gain in both settings
(Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D Recovery summaries and independent replication ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).

Accuracy alone cannot attribute a compiled artifact. A gold-label
adapter trained at matched capacity is the *strongest* accuracy
configuration (up to $`+0.65`$), yet it leaves every behavioral marker
at base level

We therefore preregistered three evidence chains. The score chain is
Table [2](#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.3 Instruction compilation preserves the source gain ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts").
The marker chain shows behavior moving with scores. Decomposition steps
rise from 0 to 4.6 against an explicit source of 4.5, interface
alignment from 0.039 to 0.97, and answer-line rate from 0.525 to 1.00,
each matching score recovery in magnitude

The control chain closes the alternatives. Shuffling teacher traces
across harnesses flattens every setting and every marker, and
parameter-matched soft prompts trained on the same distillation signal
are weaker everywhere they can be compared while paying hundreds of
tokens per call.

The sharpest test of specificity is symmetric. If the bundle encodes
harness semantics, harmful harnesses should compile as faithfully as
helpful ones. They do. A verification harness that hurts M1 on GSM8K
($`-10.7`$ points explicit) distills to $`-6.7`$ points with
verify-action marker recovery 1.00, and a harmful M2 pairing reproduces
$`-4.2`$ with marker recovery 1.09. The negative signature even
transfers out of distribution to SVAMP ($`-7.9`$ points,
Figure [4(c)](#S3.F4.sf3 "In Figure 4 ‣ 3.4 What the bundle carries ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
Whatever the bundle carries, it carries the harness’s behavior rather
than a generic improvement.

### 3.5 Compiled bundles are reusable objects

(a) Zero-shot transfer

(b) Composition algebra

(c) Budget-matched search

Figure 5: Reuse of compiled behaviors. (a) Cross-task transfer of frozen
bundles. (b) Text stacking against shift-space composition. (c) Search
gain at matched evaluation budgets.

##### Transfer.

Freezing five compiled bundles and evaluating seven preregistered
(source $`\to`$ target) pairs with no retraining and no target-side
tuning, five pairs transfer positively with CIs excluding zero
(Figure [5(a)](#S3.F5.sf1 "In Figure 5 ‣ 3.5 Compiled bundles are reusable objects ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
Decomposition transfers to ARC with $`+22.0`$ points, and the marker
follows (steps 1.10 $`\to`$ 5.20). Two out-of-distribution rows into
ARC-Easy recover 59% and 74% of their source gains. One transfer flips
the sign usefully. The test-consult harness is harmful on ARC in text
form ($`-19.3`$), yet its compiled form gains $`+6.7`$, because
compilation distills the behavior as the teacher expresses it under that
task, which differs from the instruction’s literal text. The
verification bundle transfers no score on ARC at all, but it moves its
marker from 0.027 to 0.727 against 0.740 in explicit form, which is
behavior migration on its own. Gold-label task adapters, by contrast,
transfer at $`-0.15`$ and $`-0.35`$. Task skills overfit, and compiled
behaviors do not.

##### Composition.

Two compiled behaviors for M2$`\times`$MBPP (decompose, compute) combine
exactly in shift space through the rank-16 merge. Every nonzero point of
a 25-point $`(\alpha,\beta)`$ validation grid lands in 0.60–0.67 against
a base of 0.02, and the frozen test combination reaches $`+0.624`$, no
worse than the best single component, with both marker signatures
intact. Stacking the same two instruction texts in context is
destructive instead, at $`+0.088`$, a near-total collapse caused by
mixed compliance with two mandated structures. The right algebra for
behaviors is the shift space.

##### Search.

If bundles compose, a library of them turns harness search from discrete
text rewriting into continuous coefficient selection. With evaluation
budgets pinned at $`T{=}1300`$ validation evaluations per configuration
on M2$`\times`$MBPP, text-space search reaches $`+0.430`$ test gain,
latent library search reaches $`+0.624`$, and the full loop of text
mutation, compilation, and coefficient search reaches $`+0.610`$. The
compiled library alone accounts for the 1.45$`\times`$ multiplier
(Figure [5(c)](#S3.F5.sf3 "In Figure 5 ‣ 3.5 Compiled bundles are reusable objects ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).

### 3.6 Where the intervention acts, and when it fails

(a) Phase decomposition

(b) Strength response

(c) Without the harness

Figure 6: Mechanism. (a) Phase decomposition of the gain. (b) Strength
response with plateau and cliff. (c) ToolHop with the harness removed,
by configuration.

##### The gain does not depend on the harness text.

The policy gain does not require the harness to be present. With the
harness removed and the protocol otherwise unchanged, both@0.25 still
delivers $`+9.5`$ points on ToolHop (paired net $`+11`$/$`+7`$, with
both runs above every base run), while the capacity-aligned LoRA stays
at base level
(Figure [6(c)](#S3.F6.sf3 "In Figure 6 ‣ 3.6 Where the intervention acts, and when it fails ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
Removing the harness enlarges the policy gain rather than shrinking it,
which indicates partial substitution. The shift performs structural
corrections that the harness would otherwise impose, and it does so for
behaviors the harness text does not cover.

##### The action site is the representation layer.

Prompt-phase injection changes every downstream computation through
attention and the KV cache, while generation-phase injection changes
token-level dynamics only. Decomposing the two
(Figure [6(a)](#S3.F6.sf1 "In Figure 6 ‣ 3.6 Where the intervention acts, and when it fails ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts"))
shows that prompt positions contribute $`+8.4`$ to $`+8.5`$ points,
near-constant across strengths and about 60% of the total. Instruction
compilation is the limiting case of the same fact, since the student
trains on byte-identical prompts and still acquires the behavior
entirely through representation-level correction.

##### Strength has a wide safe plateau and a sharp cliff.

Sweeping 13 strength values with two repetitions each
(Figure [6(b)](#S3.F6.sf2 "In Figure 6 ‣ 3.6 Where the intervention acts, and when it fails ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts"))
gives an inverted U with a 0.35–0.70 plateau. Within the plateau all
seven points fall within 2.6 points of each other, and the peak reaches
$`+14.2`$ over base. Beyond the plateau the failure is specific and
diagnosable. Trajectories complete normally, but has_valid_answer
collapses monotonically (1.000 $`\to`$ 0.284), a termination failure
driven by prompt-side over-injection. Generation-only injection never
triggers it at any tested strength. The optimal strength is
condition-dependent (0.25 with the harness, 0.35–0.70 without), which is
why it is exposed as a runtime control and not baked into weights.

##### What survives a scale change.

Re-running the identical pipeline on Qwen3-8B preserves the skeleton.
Zero-weight adaptation works, the parameter-efficiency ordering holds
(LoRA needs 3.09$`\times`$ parameters to match), the failure family is
the same with an earlier onset, and API-Bank remains regression-free.
What moves is the gain location. ToolHop gains spread across several
strengths instead of one, and SearchQA flips from no gain at 4B to
$`+7.5`$–$`9.2`$ points at 8B
(Appendix [I](#A9 "Appendix I Cross-scale account ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")).
Applicability is therefore established per model-task pair, and the
bundle’s runtime controls are what make per-pair deployment practical.

## 4 Related work

##### Harness engineering and self-evolution.

Automated harness design searches over programs ([Hu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib42); [Zhang et al., 2025b](#bib.bib44)) and over modular
pipelines ([Shang et al., 2025](#bib.bib43); [Zhang et al.,
2025a](#bib.bib45)), and some variants constrain the search to safety
([Zheng et al., 2025](#bib.bib46)). Self-improving systems synthesize or
edit harnesses from execution feedback ([Lou et al., 2026](#bib.bib8);
[Shao et al., 2026](#bib.bib11); [Zhang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7); [Lin
et al., 2026a](#bib.bib9); [Karten et al., 2026](#bib.bib10)), optimize
harness and model end-to-end ([Lee et al., 2026](#bib.bib5)), or compile
harness logic into code and skill programs ([Ning et al.,
2026](#bib.bib6); [Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib16); [Chen et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib4)). Self-evolving agents typically internalize what they
learn through weight updates. Examples include RL-trained reasoning from
zero data ([Huang et al., 2026](#bib.bib17)), recursive skill-augmented
RL ([Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib20)), memory skills ([Zhang et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib19)), and self-referential improvement loops ([Yin et
al., 2025](#bib.bib21)), while [Lin et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib15)
disentangle harness updates from harness benefits. HarnessForge ([Chen
et al., 2026b](#bib.bib2)) co-evolves harnesses and policies and
provides the benchmarks, harness pairings, and training protocol we
adopt. Its training-style configurations (SFT, RLOO, GRPO) and its
co-evolution objective all write gains into weights. Latent Harness is
complementary. It moves a fixed harness’s behavioral content into
activations, keeps the backbone frozen, and leaves the harness-evolution
loop free to iterate on text.

##### Parameter-efficient adaptation.

LoRA ([Hu et al., 2021](#bib.bib38)), prefix-tuning ([Li and Liang,
2021](#bib.bib49)), and prompt tuning ([Lester et al.,
2021](#bib.bib50)) attach low-rank or prompt-side parameters to a frozen
backbone. Our operator shares the low-rank parametrization but acts at
the post-block junction of the residual stream. Our comparisons hold
data, capacity, and effective strength fixed, so they isolate the effect
of the site from that of the budget. Behavioral cloning ([Torabi et al.,
2018](#bib.bib1)) and data-efficient reasoning distillation ([Zhang et
al., 2026c](#bib.bib18)) are output-mimicking relatives of ours. The
differences are strict correctness filtering of the source and a
preregistered behavior-level attribution protocol on the target.

##### Activation steering and representation engineering.

Adding direction vectors to activations ([Turner et al.,
2024](#bib.bib26); [Turner et al., 2023](#bib.bib30)), contrastive
activation addition ([Rimsky et al., 2024](#bib.bib22)), representation
engineering ([Zou et al., 2023](#bib.bib27); [Bartoszcze et al.,
2025](#bib.bib23)), inference-time intervention ([Li et al.,
2023a](#bib.bib32)), function vectors ([Todd et al., 2024](#bib.bib34)),
and learned representation interventions ([Wu et al., 2024](#bib.bib28))
study how latent-space edits change model behavior. Systematic
evaluations find difference-vector methods unreliable and learned
interventions stronger ([Tan et al., 2024](#bib.bib31); [Wu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib24); [Stoehr et al., 2024](#bib.bib35)), and later work
extends the setting to refusal steering ([Sheng et al.,
2026](#bib.bib25)), defense ([Zou et al., 2024](#bib.bib33)), subspace
models ([Zhao et al., 2025](#bib.bib36)), hallucination detection ([Park
et al., 2025](#bib.bib29)) and decision operators ([Li and Chen,
2026](#bib.bib54)). Our work follows the learned-intervention regime.
[Adila et al. (2026)](#bib.bib3) formalize weight updates as activation
shifts. We use the post-block shift as a compilation target for external
conditions and add three things that steering work has not addressed,
namely behavior-level attribution, composition algebra, and transfer
together with agent-benchmark policy alignment under controlled
capacity.

## 5 Limitations

Gain locations move with the model-task pair. ToolHop gains concentrate
or spread across strengths, and SearchQA flips direction from 4B to 8B.
We therefore claim per-pair applicability only, and the runtime controls
exist to support per-pair tuning. Strength has a hard ceiling. Beyond
the plateau the policy stops producing valid answers, the failure is
prompt-side specific, and the onset moves earlier with scale, so
deployments must sweep $`s`$ before fixing it. Our policy results are
single-round alignment of a base policy to a fixed harness, not
multi-round co-evolution, so the dagger rows of
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.1 Setup ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
are protocol-distinct from the published co-evolution numbers.
Evaluation pools are small (95, 50, and 34 items). We mitigate this with
repeated full evaluations and per-item paired analysis, but absolute
deltas of a few points should be read with that limitation in mind.
Instruction compilation covers short process-level instructions.
Harnesses too complex to state as text are handled by the trajectory
route.

## 6 Conclusion

We treated the harness as a compilation source and not as a prompt to
repeat or a skill to fine-tune. A rank-8 shift at the post-block
junction of a frozen backbone compiles both explicit behavior
instructions and agentic-harness strategies into bundles that need no
condition text, no tokens, and no weight change. Strength, phase, and
mixing stay exposed at run time. Compiled behaviors recover and
sometimes exceed their sources, they transfer and compose in shift
space, they align policy at a fraction of LoRA’s parameters under
matched conditions, and they fail in a diagnosable and bounded way. The
compilation view turns harness engineering from a per-call expense into
a reusable asset, since every harness the community evolves becomes a
candidate bundle in a shared library.

#### Reproducibility statement

All numbers here come from preregistered protocols with fixed seeds,
validation-only selection, single test touches, and append-only result
ledgers. Policy configurations average at least two independent full
evaluations.
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Experimental protocol details ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
details the harness registry, training configurations, the definition of
each configuration, the parse protocol, and the statistical procedures.
Appendix [I](#A9 "Appendix I Cross-scale account ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
documents cross-scale runs and protocol notes.

#### Ethics statement

Compilation is semantically faithful in both directions. Harmful
harnesses distill into harmful bundles as readily as helpful ones, which
we report as an attribution finding without claiming a capability.
Deployment should check compile sources against a registry, preregister
evaluation criteria, and audit exported bundles. A practical advantage
of the runtime form is that a bundle can be inspected, tuned down, or
removed without touching model weights.

layers and all tokens, with a SiLU bottleneck for instruction
compilation and a linear map for policy compilation. The backbone is
frozen, with every parameter verified identical to the original.

##### Harness registry.

Seven single-pass, tool-free, process-level instructions (VERIFY,
DECOMPOSE, COMPUTE, CONCISE, ELIMINATE, QUOTE, TESTCONSULT) each stay at
most 60 tokens. Each is registered by a content hash with a named target
error mode and preregistered markers, computed by deterministic parsing
of raw outputs (verify-action rate, decomposition-step count,
arithmetic-expression rate, answer-line rate, option-coverage rate,
evidence-quote rate, interface-alignment rate, clean-stop rate). By
construction, markers never fire on base-prompt echo, and they are
checked to that effect.

##### Instruction compilation.

Teacher traces are greedy generations of the frozen model with the
harness prepended. All outputs are cached, and correctness filtering
selects the imitation target at training time. The student trains on
$`n{=}\min(600,|\text{pool}|)`$ examples for 2 epochs at lr
$`9{\times}10^{-4}`$ with 3 seeds $`\{42,43,44\}`$. The loss is
prompt-masked token cross-entropy on filtered traces for generation
tasks or an option-level KL against cached teacher distributions for
multiple choice, with option-string mirroring locked by unit test. Hard
per-step assertions cover three properties. Backbone parameters stay
identical to the original, the optimizer holds adapter parameters only,
and no harness substring appears in any student input while harness text
is present in every teacher prompt.

##### Policy compilation.

Strict-tier decision pairs come from harness-conditioned rollouts
(ToolHop 1,042 pairs, 829 train / 213 val, SearchQA 670, API-Bank 327).
Training uses prefix-masked cross-entropy on completions with lr
$`10^{-4}`$, 3 epochs, batch size 1 with gradient accumulation 2, max
length 4,096, and bf16 with gradient checkpointing. The LoRA comparison
configurations use identical hyperparameters. The capacity-aligned
configuration is rank 3 on q/k/v (1.49M parameters, 1.012$`\times`$)
with effective multiplier $`\tfrac{\alpha_{L}}{r_{L}}k=0.5`$ matched to
the shift’s $`s\,\alpha=0.5`$. The full-capacity configuration is rank 8
on all linear projections (16.4M), and at 8B the reference configuration
is rank 11 (7.30M, 3.09$`\times`$).

##### Evaluation.

Decoding is greedy throughout. Instruction settings use dataset-native
extract-and-match or sandboxed pass@1 with preregistered extraction, and
we report paired bootstrap 95% CIs with $`B{=}10{,}000`$. Policy runs
use the HarnessForge evaluator fields as produced, with at least 2
independent full evaluations per adopted configuration, per-item paired
net differences, and a bounded deterministic parse protocol that repairs
once and then rejects, identically across configurations. Rejection
counts are reported as behavioral signals. Injection validity is
verified per configuration by the scale-signature check, in which
bit-identical metrics across strengths indicate silent non-injection.

##### Configuration definitions (instruction track).

BASE applies no intervention. EXPLICIT prepends the harness text and
serves as the recovery denominator. SOFT-PROMPT is a parameter-matched
soft prefix trained on the same distillation signal. TASK-ADAPTER uses
gold-label cross-entropy at matched capacity. OURS distills without gold
labels. SHUFFLED pairs teacher traces with the wrong harness and serves
as the semantic-ablation control.

## Appendix C Attribution details and per-model discussion

Table [2](#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.3 Instruction compilation preserves the source gain ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts")
compresses three model-specific patterns. On M1, the two ARC settings
recover the explicit gain (ratios 0.83 and 1.23 on moderate
denominators), while the MBPP setting adds $`+31.7`$ points from only 20
correct teacher traces. Its ratio of 5.86 reflects the small
$`+5.4`$-point explicit denominator, so we read the setting by its
absolute delta. The parameter-matched soft-prompt control trained on the
same signal reaches 0.079 and pays 512 tokens per call, which shows that
the distillation signal is necessary and that the activation site makes
it sufficient. On M2, all three MBPP settings exceed their explicit
sources (0.660–0.687 vs. 0.396–0.470). The explicit harness spends
decode budget on mandated structure at every call within a 512-token
limit, while the compiled form spends that budget on code. On M3, the
two BoolQ settings bracket the criterion. Concise answering recovers
0.71 of a large explicit gain (0.809 $`\to`$ 0.891 explicit, ours
0.774), while test-consult recovers 1.20 with 98% validation recovery
and a seed range of 0.015, the most stable setting in the study. Across
all settings the TASK-ADAPTER control is the strongest accuracy
configuration and the weakest behavioral one, which is the dissociation
that motivates the three-chain protocol in
Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 What the bundle carries ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts").

## Appendix E Harmful-signature transfer and the task-skill contrast

Figure 10: (a) Harmful-signature transfer to SVAMP. (b) Transfer
contrast between compiled behaviors and gold-label task adapters.

Two observations close the attribution argument. The harmful VERIFY
signature is mobile. Distilled from a GSM8K pairing at $`-6.7`$ points,
it transfers out of distribution to SVAMP at $`-7.9`$, the same mobility
positive signatures show. Harm and help moving through the same channel
is strong evidence that the bundle encodes harness semantics rather than
task utility. The transfer profiles also differ in kind
(Figure [10](#A5.F10 "Figure 10 ‣ Appendix E Harmful-signature transfer and the task-skill contrast ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts"),
right). Task adapters trained on gold labels transfer negatively
($`-0.15`$ to BoolQ, $`-0.35`$ to GSM8K), while compiled behaviors
transfer at zero or positive gain. Bundles behave like reusable
behaviors, and task adapters behave like overfit task skills.

## Appendix F Composition and search details

Figure 11: (a) The 25-point shift-space composition grid. (b) The three
budget-matched search configurations.

The composition grid
(Figure [11](#A6.F11 "Figure 11 ‣ Appendix F Composition and search details ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts"),
left) sweeps $`\alpha,\beta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}`$ on validation.
Every nonzero point lands in 0.60–0.67 against a base of 0.02, so no
mixing ratio interferes with either component. The merge that realizes
$`\alpha Z_{a}+\beta Z_{b}`$ is exact, using rank-16 vertical and
horizontal stacking with the coefficients on the up blocks and a
tensor-level error of at most $`10^{-5}`$. Composing in parameter space
after orthogonal alignment instead deviates from the shift-space
composition by roughly 37% relative error and loses over half the marker
signal, which is why we locate the correct algebra in shift space. The
search configurations (right) are text-only mutation and screening over
instruction rewrites, coefficient search over a fixed compiled library
with nonnegative coefficients capped at 2, and the full loop that
mutates text, compiles it, then searches coefficients. All three receive
$`T{=}1300`$ validation evaluations. The library configuration wins
because composition makes its search space continuous and reusable
instead of discrete and disposable.

## Appendix G Policy-compilation details

(left) shows every ToolHop configuration including the full-capacity

## Appendix H Strength response and failure staging

Figure 13: (a) Failure staging at high strength. (b) The same failure
family at 4B and 8B.

The high-strength failure proceeds in two stages
(Figure [13](#A8.F13 "Figure 13 ‣ Appendix H Strength response and failure staging ‣ Latent Harness: Compiling Agent Behaviors into Post-Block Activation Shifts"),
left). Between $`s{=}0.7`$ and $`0.8`$ the policy terminates early, with
action counts falling from 10.6 to 6.5 to 5.3 while has_valid_answer
begins to slip. From $`s{=}0.9`$ the behavior degenerates into cycling,
where action counts rebound to 8–9.5 but valid answers fall to 0.34 and
then 0.28. Trajectories always complete and tool-call parsing never
fails, so this is a policy-level termination failure. It is also
prompt-side specific. Generation-only injection holds has_valid_answer
at 1.000 at every strength tested. The 8B curve (right) shows the same
family with an earlier onset (collapse by $`s{=}0.875`$), consistent
with the interpretation that the phase boundary is set by the ratio of
shift magnitude to the policy’s behavioral margin, which shrinks as
models get stronger.
````
