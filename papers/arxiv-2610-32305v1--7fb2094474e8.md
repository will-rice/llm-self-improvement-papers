###### Abstract

Multi-stage analytical tasks require LLM agents to use intermediate
evidence to decide whether to continue, redirect or stop.
Self-reflection, in which an agent critiques its output, is widely
studied, yet a critique does not show whether evidence changed the next
action. We call that capability _self-restraint_. Existing agent
benchmarks largely measure task completion or output quality and leave
it untested. DMAIC-Bench evaluates it with 97 seeded tasks across 15
industrial cases in four sectors, built around Lean Six Sigma’s explicit
statistical prerequisites. Paired counterfactual variants require
opposite actions at the same workflow position, distinguishing warranted
restraint from indiscriminate stopping. We assess execution traces with
a private scorer and final claims through independent expert review.
Across 13 models from six providers, execution-level routing accuracy
averages 92% on matched GO decisions and 19% on NO-GO decisions,
counting any forbidden downstream computation as a route error.
Execution traces show agents reporting correct diagnostics before
performing analyses that those results disallow. Separately, blinded
expert review of the 11 audited agents estimates that reports act on
invalid evidence at 47% of reached NO-GO decisions, with an interval
from 31% to 61%. Explicit stopping permission helps some models without
consistently restoring correct decisions at both GO and NO-GO forks.
Sixteen life-sciences tasks in bulk ribonucleic acid sequencing
(RNA-seq) and case-control genome-wide association studies (GWAS) show
that the same framework can evaluate evidence-dependent decisions beyond
industrial workflows. Project code and data are available at:
[https://github.com/psptpx/DMAIC-Bench-public](https://github.com/psptpx/DMAIC-Bench-public).

## 1 Introduction

Multi-stage analytical tasks in science and engineering require agents
to choose their next action from intermediate results ([Hu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib22); [Gu et al., 2024](#bib.bib21); [Majumder et al.,
2025](#bib.bib44)). Intermediate checks establish whether the
prerequisites for later analyses hold; if one fails, continuing can
yield invalid conclusions even when the downstream computation is
correct ([Montgomery, 2020](#bib.bib50); [NIST/SEMATECH,
2012](#bib.bib53)). An agent must therefore use diagnostic evidence to
decide whether to proceed, divert, gather remedial data or stop at each
check, and to act on that decision.

Yet prevailing agent benchmarks score multi-step tasks by endpoint
completion or final-artifact quality ([Jimenez et al.,
2024](#bib.bib28); [Yao et al., 2024](#bib.bib71); [Mialon et al.,
2024](#bib.bib47); [Chan et al., 2025](#bib.bib9)), and aggregate
success does not measure operational reliability ([Rabanser et al.,
2026](#bib.bib58)). Studies of agent evaluation add gaps in cost
reporting, overfitting and reward design ([Kapoor et al.,
2024](#bib.bib32); [Zhu et al., 2025](#bib.bib76)). Whether the next
action follows from the validity of intermediate results is not what
these scores measure. We call the momentum to keep executing tools
regardless of validity _action bias_; the term names the behaviour
without asserting a cause. Continuing past a failed precondition is
itself an error: flawless execution cannot rescue conclusions from
invalid data, and indiscriminate stopping fails when the evidence
supports taking the next step, so a benchmark must score both.

![Refer to caption](2610.32305v1/figure01_workflow.png)

Figure 1: Overall illustration of DMAIC-Bench. (1) We start from
evidence-dependent industrial workflows. (2) We test execution and

correct diagnosis from warranted action. (4) DMAIC standards and paired
tasks evaluate self-restraint through trace scoring and human review.

This raises a question: _can an agent in a multi-stage analytical task
use computed evidence to choose correctly among continuing, diverting
and stopping?_ We call this capability self-restraint. Self-reflection
asks an agent to critique its own output ([Shinn et al.,
2023](#bib.bib62); [Madaan et al., 2023](#bib.bib43)); self-restraint
asks whether computed evidence changes the next action; we test no
reflection intervention.

Lean Six Sigma (DMAIC) and quality-engineering standards give explicit
rules for this ([Juran & Godfrey, 1999](#bib.bib29); [NIST/SEMATECH,
2012](#bib.bib53)). The Define–Measure–Analyze–Improve–Control cycle
links stages through prerequisites ([Montgomery, 2020](#bib.bib50)):
variance analysis requires gauge repeatability ([AIAG,
2010](#bib.bib2)), capability indices require process stability ([AIAG &
VDA, 2026](#bib.bib3)), and interventions require empirical confirmation
([Montgomery, 2013](#bib.bib49)), so an untrusted gauge or an unstable
process invalidates dependent analyses. The task does not say which
prerequisite will fail; the agent must find out by intermediate
analysis, and an evaluator can check whether proceeding, diverting or
stopping is warranted at each of the case’s graded forks.

DMAIC-Bench tests whether an agent governs its trajectory by computed
evidence, stopping when prerequisites fail and proceeding when they
hold. Each case is a guarded decision graph whose data steward checks
declared prerequisites before releasing dependent batches. Of 66 graded
forks, 40 have paired counterfactual variants, the same workflow
position with seeded data that yield opposite diagnostic outcomes, so
every no-go decision has a go counterpart and restraint is separated
from blanket refusal. In total, 97 tasks span 15 industrial cases in
four sectors, eight of them adapted from the closure reports of real
black-belt improvement projects. The scorer reads the execution trace,
so a forbidden computation counts as a route error even when the report
withholds its result.

Across 13 models, execution-level matched-fork accuracy is 92% for go
decisions and 19% for no-go decisions. Errors persist after correct
diagnostics: 201 of 272 no-go decisions with reported and verified guard
fields take the wrong route, and in 174 errors the diagnostic precedes
the forbidden analysis. Independent expert review of a no-go sample from
11 agents finds reports acting on invalid evidence at 47% of reached
no-go decisions. Explicit stopping permission improves some agents, but
12 of 13 remain below 50% no-go accuracy. We also validate the same
framework on 16 RNA-seq and GWAS tasks in the life sciences, where the
prerequisites differ. Our contributions are:

1.  1.  Benchmark Design: DMAIC-Bench, 97 multi-step industrial tasks across
        15 cases with runtime data-access guards and paired counterfactual
        instances that test warranted stopping against justified
        continuation.
2.  2.  Empirical Restraint Gap: Evaluating 13 agents shows an
        execution-level gap, 92% matched go accuracy versus 10–25% no-go
        accuracy, and in 174 errors agents report correct guard values
        before running analyses those diagnostics do not permit.
3.  3.  Expert Validation & Intervention Limits: Blinded expert review of a
        no-go sample from 11 agents finds reports acting on invalid evidence
        at 47% of reached no-go decisions. Instruction and runtime
        interventions improve some routing decisions, with effects that
        differ across agents and can reduce correct continuation.

#### Agent and statistical benchmarks.

SWE-bench, MLE-bench, PaperBench, and Terminal-Bench assess completed

et al., 2024](#bib.bib71); [Barres et al., 2025](#bib.bib7)). In data

[Hu et al., 2024](#bib.bib22); [Huang et al., 2024c](#bib.bib25)); and
BLADE accommodates multiple defensible analytical paths ([Gu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib21)). Related suites target scientific discovery
([Majumder et al., 2025](#bib.bib44); [Chen et al., 2025](#bib.bib12)),
ML experimentation ([Huang et al., 2024b](#bib.bib24)), computational
reproduction ([Siegel et al., 2024](#bib.bib63)), sequential clinical
evidence disclosure ([Nori et al., 2025](#bib.bib54)), and procedural
adherence ([Nandi et al., 2026](#bib.bib51); [Chen et al.,
2026](#bib.bib10)). None of these suites directly scores whether a
computed diagnostic licenses the next analysis
(Table [1](#S2.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 2 Related work ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).
DMAIC-Bench evaluates that decision across multi-stage industrial
projects and treats justified stopping as a valid terminal route
whenever a prerequisite has failed.

#### Self-reflection and abstention.

Reflexion, Self-Refine, and CRITIC revise outputs through reflection or
external feedback ([Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib62); [Madaan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib43); [Gou et al., 2024](#bib.bib19)); self-correction,
however, hinges on the quality of available feedback ([Huang et al.,
2024a](#bib.bib23); [Kamoi et al., 2024](#bib.bib31)). Abstention
predates agents: selective question answering declines when calibrated
confidence is low ([Kamath et al., 2020](#bib.bib30)), refusal-aware
tuning teaches a model to say it does not know ([Zhang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib72)), and a survey sorts abstention by query, model and
values ([Wen et al., 2025](#bib.bib69)). Agent abstention benchmarks
examine abstention or escalation under ambiguity, missing information,
conflicting objectives or prohibited operations ([Liu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib39); [Trinh et al., 2026](#bib.bib65); [Huang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib26); [Asif et al., 2026](#bib.bib6)), and Agentic
Abstention studies interaction-revealed prerequisites and stopping times
([Luo et al., 2026](#bib.bib42)). AgentAbstain pairs tasks that differ
in whether they can be answered; DMAIC-Bench pairs seeds at one workflow
position, so only a computed value differs, and the matched instances
isolate whether the agent’s _own correct diagnostic_ governs the next
action.

#### Runtime enforcement and monitoring.

Shielding intercepts unsafe actions ([Alshiekh et al.,
2018](#bib.bib4)), while guardrail systems constrain inputs, outputs, or
tool calls ([Rebedea et al., 2023](#bib.bib59); [Inan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib27)). AI-control research studies monitoring under
untrusted policies ([Greenblatt et al., 2024](#bib.bib20)). In
DMAIC-Bench, the data steward enforces prerequisites before releasing
dependent batches. External access controls cannot remove action bias:
when a diagnostic and the analysis it forbids use the same released
batch, the restraint decision rests with the agent.
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Agent Interaction ‣ 3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
and
Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D The task package and the steward protocol ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
state which dependencies the environment enforces.

Framework Overview and Task Formalization.

### 3.1 Benchmark Construction

![Refer to caption](2610.32305v1/figure03_task_breakdown.png)

Figure 3: Task breakdown. 97 tasks by industry (inner) and scenario
(outer).

We construct cases from industrial problems, characteristics, units,
specification limits and analytical stages. Eight retain routes from
black-belt closure reports. Seven use routes derived from standards in
project-informed scenarios.
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Corpus datasheet ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
documents the corpus in datasheet form ([Gebru et al.,
2021](#bib.bib17)); released tasks carry generated data and omit source
prose and identifying details.

Each case is a guarded graph $`G=(V,E,\Phi)`$, with analytical stages
and terminal actions in $`V`$, admissible transitions in $`E`$, and
predicates on upstream diagnostics in $`\Phi`$. The gauge guard applies
the AIAG MSA-4 acceptance criteria ([AIAG, 2010](#bib.bib2)):

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\phi_{\mathrm{go}}=(\mathrm{SV}_{\mathrm{gauge}}\leq 0.30)\wedge(\mathrm{ndc}\geq 5),
``` |  | (1) |

where $`\mathrm{SV}_{\mathrm{gauge}}`$ is the gauge contribution to
study variation and $`\mathrm{ndc}`$ is the number of distinct
categories. A generator and seed determine data batches; computed
diagnostics determine guard outcomes and the reference route.
Counterfactual seeds reverse a prerequisite at the same workflow
position, including branches absent from the source project. Of 66
graded case–node forks, 40 contain both go and no-go seeds, providing a
go counterpart for every no-go decision.

Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.1 Benchmark Construction ‣ 3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
breaks down the corpus across its 15 cases, 97 tasks, and 175 nodes,
spanning four sectors, five DMAIC phases, and 29 methods. Reference
routes average 6.0 analyses and 3.1 graded decisions; prior analyses
govern 284 of 300 decisions. As detailed in
Table [5](#A2.T5 "Table 5 ‣ Appendix B Corpus datasheet ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"),
the routes define 48 A-go, 18 B-divert, and 31 C-stop tasks; the latter
two groups, 49 tasks, require diversion or stopping.

### 3.2 Agent Interaction

Agents interface with the benchmark via a shared harness, executing
tools within isolated containers with pinned scientific-Python libraries
and no network access. A local data steward exposes four core
operations: requesting shop-floor data batches (request), declaring
prerequisite verdicts (declare), querying domain process facts
(provision), and inspecting available data inventory (status). Only
provision queries consume the finite budget $`\beta`$. The steward logs
declarations and verifies access prerequisites; it never reveals or
checks against private ground truth, which resides in an isolated
verifier container that the agent’s execution environment cannot reach
at any point of a run, and the steward’s configuration contains no
method identifiers.

Access controls govern cross-batch transitions: while 9 stop tasks place
the GO branch behind explicit steward locks, 40 no-go tasks (including
all B-divert instances) provide the data upfront and require restraint
without environmental blocking. Each episode logs all released batches,
declared verdicts, and query turns, terminating either upon the
submission of the execution trace and final report or at the wall-clock
limit.

### 3.3 Evaluation and Quality Control

#### Automated execution scoring.

The private verifier scores execution traces against reference routes
$`\rho^{*}(x)`$ and checks batch access against steward audit logs.
Branch-level accuracy evaluates individual GO/NO-GO choices: a matched
fork requires correct decisions across all paired seeds at that
position, while task-level route success requires the full reference
route. Aggregate reward combines routing accuracy with method adherence
and numerical precision:

|     |                                                   |     |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       R=\mathcal{H}(R_{m},P_{m},F_{n},A_{r})\,(1-U)\,I,
       ```                                                |     | (2) |

where $`\mathcal{H}`$ denotes the harmonic mean of method recall
($`R_{m}`$), method precision ($`P_{m}`$), numerical fidelity
($`F_{n}`$), and routing accuracy ($`A_{r}`$). Misuse $`U\in[0,1]`$
penalizes violated statistical assumptions, while the binary integrity
gate $`I\in\{0,1\}`$ zeroes the reward if withheld data are accessed;
without it a misspecified reward would leave that shortcut open ([Pan
et al., 2022](#bib.bib55)). Justified early stopping is credited on its
valid prefix, whereas forbidden computation constitutes a route error
whether or not the report endorses the result. Route accuracy thus
measures execution restraint, not the validity of final claims; the two
are distinct outcomes, which
§[4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 From Execution Errors to Report Validity ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
assesses separately by expert review.

#### Expert audit of report validity.

Because an execution error does not imply that an agent endorsed the
invalid result, we separately audit the narrative report
$`\mathcal{R}`$. Certified Black-Belt experts independently evaluated
blinded checkpoints across two distinct studies;
Appendix [M](#A13 "Appendix M Human expert blind review ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
reports their agreement as Fleiss’ and Cohen’s $`\kappa`$ ([Fleiss,
1971](#bib.bib16); [Cohen, 1960](#bib.bib13)). Study 1 audited 30
checkpoints across all 15 cases (26 case–seed instances), informing
scoring revisions such as admitting a declared Welch alternative for
unequal variances. Study 2 evaluated 60 fresh checkpoints across 12
cases under a frozen protocol to assess report claims without further
scorer tuning, distinguishing *acting on invalid evidence* (asserting
forbidden conclusions or recommending disqualified actions) from
computing an invalid metric while withholding its conclusion. Neither
study serves as an end-to-end task-solving baseline for human experts.

#### Benchmark consistency checks.

Source checks trace numerical claims to original reports, recompute
formulas, screen duplicate material, and distinguish observed from
standards-derived routes. Graph, guard, generator, and numerical
verifications, the checks recommended for agentic benchmarks ([Zhu
et al., 2025](#bib.bib76)), ensure that every reference solution scores
1.000 and the null agent scores 0.000. These scores show that scorer and
reference solutions agree; they cannot exclude a rule misapplied in both
generator and scorer, which the expert confirmation of gate labels
(§[4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 From Execution Errors to Report Validity ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"))
covers.
Appendices [C](#A3 "Appendix C Construction checks and review coverage ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
and [M](#A13 "Appendix M Human expert blind review ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
document review protocols, independent votes and open discrepancies.

### 4.1 Evaluation Protocol and Metrics

We evaluate 13 frontier model agents from six providers across all 97
tasks using Harbor 0.22.0’s stock Terminal-Bench execution loop
([Merrill et al., 2026](#bib.bib46)) under a single tagged corpus build
and scorer. To preserve evaluation neutrality across vendors,
DMAIC-Bench provides no proprietary agent scaffolding or custom prompt
adapters, a choice that fixed-harness suites also make ([Liu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib38); [Xie et al., 2024](#bib.bib70)). Each model completes
three independent attempts per task. Under the default *Plain*
instruction, agents conduct the analytical workflow and compile an
engineering report, with no prompt saying that justified stopping counts
as completion.
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Evaluation roster and runner configuration ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
lists the runtime configurations, context budgets and model identifiers;
steward audit logs record no unauthorized access to withheld batches in
any of the reported runs.

Each complete episode traverses 300 evaluated decisions: 52 NO-GO and
248 GO choices. Within these decisions, 40 matched forks pair all 52
NO-GO instances with 146 topologically identical GO counterparts,
isolating the role of the seeded data while keeping workflow positions
fixed. Beyond single-attempt metrics, multi-trial consistency is
evaluated via pass@$`k`$, the fraction of tasks succeeding in at least
one of $`k`$ attempts ([Chen et al., 2021](#bib.bib11)), and
$`\text{pass}^{k}`$, the fraction succeeding across all $`k`$ attempts
([Yao et al., 2024](#bib.bib71)). To account for dependency structures
among seeds sharing the same case topology, all confidence intervals are
computed via case-clustered bootstrap resampling ([Field & Welsh,
2007](#bib.bib15)), with its limits at these cluster counts stated in
Appendix [I](#A9 "Appendix I Ranking ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks").

### 4.2 Evidence-Dependent Control at Matched Forks

on 92% of GO decisions, with an interval from 86 to 97, but on only 19%
of NO-GO decisions, with an interval from 8 to 30, a 73-point gap with
an interval from 59 to 87
(Table [22](#A9.T22 "Table 22 ‣ Appendix I Ranking ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).
The strict criterion counts computation, not decisions;
Table [33](#A13.T33 "Table 33 ‣ Automatic readers. ‣ Appendix M Human expert blind review ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")

The failure is not a uniform forward bias. As reported in

39 GO decisions, an inadmissible branch (E3) rather than a stop, yet
miss only 1 of 26 NO-GO decisions. At the same workflow position, the
choice derails once the seeded data flip the warranted branch.

### 4.3 From Correct Diagnostics to Invalid Actions

High method recall (0.919–1.000) shows that agents can execute the
required analytical steps. Of the 548 NO-GO errors, 201 follow guard
values that the agent reported and the verifier confirmed, 111 follow a
wrong value, 2 an unverifiable one, 51 an unreported one, 101 a value
that implied the other branch, and 82 no computation at all. In 174 of
the 201, the failing diagnostic appears in the trace before the
forbidden analysis. The restraint finding proper is this verified
subset, where 71 of 272 decisions stop correctly; the other classes are
computation or reporting failures
(Appendix [K](#A11 "Appendix K Decision outcomes by phase ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).

Forbidden downstream computation (Type E1 error) accounts for 99% of all
NO-GO errors, whereas only 6 decisions never reach the fork. On adaptive
B-divert tasks, across 234 first attempts on 18 instances, only 6
complete the legitimate rerouted path; 216 continue to compute process
capability indices ($`C_{p}/C_{pk}`$) after control-chart diagnostics
invalidate statistical stability prerequisites ([Montgomery,
2020](#bib.bib50), ISQC 8e, §8.3).
Appendix [K](#A11 "Appendix K Decision outcomes by phase ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
confirms that the errors are continuation decisions at reached forks,
not early timeouts that never reach the fork.

### 4.4 From Execution Errors to Report Validity

Execution traces reveal invalid computations, but agents might withhold
flawed diagnostics from their final deliverables. Study 2 therefore
conducted blinded expert audits on 60 fresh task units under the frozen

Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Evaluation and Quality Control ‣ 3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks").

were complete when the study was frozen (the two Qwen agents finished
later), comprises 40 NO-GO decisions (stratified by mechanism and scorer

counterparts. Experts confirmed the benchmark’s gate labels on 39/40

Appendix [M](#A13 "Appendix M Human expert blind review ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")

### 4.5 Instruction and Runtime Interventions

To test whether models fail to stop only because of prompt-induced
completion pressure, we evaluate the *permit-stop* instruction across
all 97 tasks. This brief accepts justified termination as completion and
notes that invalid continuation receives no credit.
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
shows the trade-off: GPT-6-astra improves its NO-GO accuracy from 23% to
79%, but its GO accuracy simultaneously degrades from 82% to 67%.
DeepSeek-v4-pro remains stalled at 23% NO-GO accuracy, and 12 of 13
evaluated agents still fail to break 50%, so completion bias alone
cannot explain the failure of self-restraint at forks with failed
prerequisites, whatever the instruction says about completion.

A pre-registered replication on 32 paired tasks contrasts this
divergence across five conditions and 960 trials. Every condition runs
the same tasks on a byte-identical truth package with three attempts per
task, so the conditions differ only in the brief and, for one of them, a
runtime channel; the two agents are the largest and the smallest movers
in
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks").
Figure [5](#S4.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 4.5 Instruction and Runtime Interventions ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")B–C
shows that permission lifts Astra from 2.3 to 10.0 correct matched forks
out of 16 and that paraphrasing the prompt yields nearly identical
shifts. DeepSeek is largely invariant to permission phrasing: the
permission and its paraphrase leave it at 19% and 28% NO-GO accuracy,
averaging under two correct paired forks of 16 under permission and
under five under the paraphrase.

A *normative* prompt states the prerequisite rules without completion
framing and raises DeepSeek to 44% NO-GO accuracy, a gain of 25 points
whose interval runs from 11 to 42, with GO accuracy essentially
unchanged. Runtime warnings against declared predicates under the
*remind* condition add five points more, an increment whose interval
from $`-8`$ to 17 includes zero, so the benefit of reminders beyond a
stated rule is uncertain. For Astra the rule costs 42 points of GO
accuracy, an interval from 65 to 14, and its 12-point NO-GO gain
includes zero; reminders fall short of explicit permission. Permission
rescues some stopping choices at the cost of warranted progress; a
stated rule helps one model at no such cost and costs the other more
than permission did.

## 5 Conclusion

DMAIC-Bench evaluates self-restraint, the capacity of agents to govern
multi-stage analytical trajectories using computed evidence. Across 97
industrial tasks with paired counterfactual branches, frontier models
reach 92% on valid continuation routes but only 10–25% execution-level
accuracy on decisions requiring justified stopping or diversion. In 174
instances, agents ran the forbidden computation after logging the
failing diagnostic, and blinded expert audits of a NO-GO sample from 11
agents find reports acting on invalid evidence at 47% of reached no-go
decisions. Stopping permission rescues some models at the cost of
warranted progress, a stated rule helps one model and costs another, and
reminders add little beyond the rule. The genomics extension shows that
the framework transfers and that the remaining errors take the same form
for the three agents tested, while failure rates depend on how
prerequisites and consequences are stated. The paired design gives
future work a test: an intervention that raises NO-GO accuracy has to
hold GO accuracy at the same forks, and the stated rule passed that test
for one agent and failed it for the other. The scorer, the audit records
and the scripts that regenerate every number are released. Instilling
self-restraint without paralysis is an open challenge for agents in
high-stakes science and engineering.

## Ethics statement

Every published task is generated from a seed. The benchmark contains no
source-project measurements, tables, figures or sentences, and
identifies no source product, organisation or person. The private corpus
of closure reports is not redistributed. No personal data is collected
or processed. The expert reviews assessed anonymised agent trajectories,
not people, under procedural blinding on a shared filesystem; no
human-subject study was conducted and no task was solved by a human for
scoring. The benchmark evaluates whether an agent lets a computed
precondition constrain its conclusion; it is not a certification of
statistical competence, and a score on it should not be used to justify
removing qualified human review from industrial decisions. The tasks
involve no safety-critical intervention: every action is a computation
on generated data inside a container.

## Reproducibility statement

The public repository
([https://github.com/psptpx/DMAIC-Bench-public](https://github.com/psptpx/DMAIC-Bench-public))

truth-package builder, the data steward, the scorer, and the analysis
scripts that regenerate every table and number in this paper from

scripts/rescore.py). A build check verifies that the same case and seed
produce a byte-identical corpus. Every task scores 1.000 for a reference
solution and 0.000 for a null agent. Campaigns ran in Harbor 0.22.0 with

tagged in the repository, and the per-trial artifacts (trace, report,
steward log, terminal recording, scorecards) are distributed as an
archive with the tagged release. The held-out split, its selection rule
and seed, and both blind-review packages with the human experts’ records
are released for independent inspection and reproduction of the review
analyses.

## Appendix A Task definition

A task instance is a tuple $`x=(\iota,G,s,\beta)`$: a natural-language
instruction $`\iota`$ that names no statistical method
(Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Instruction text ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"));
a case graph $`G=(V,E,\Phi)`$
(§[3](#S3 "3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"));
a seed $`s\in\mathcal{S}`$; and a provision budget $`\beta`$. The seed
generates the case’s data batches
$`\mathcal{B}_{s}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}`$; every truth value is
recomputed from those batches by the reference verifiers at build time,
so a new seed changes data and truth together, and the correct route is
$`\rho^{*}(x)=\operatorname{walk}(G,\mathbf{y}(s))`$: the walk selected
by the guards in $`\Phi`$ after evaluating the diagnostic outputs from
those batches. The build derives each answer from the generated data.
Workflow routing ([van der Aalst et al., 2003](#bib.bib67)) and action
preconditions in planning ([Valmeekam et al., 2023](#bib.bib66)) provide
related abstractions; here the preconditions are statistical diagnostics
computed during execution. Paired seeds test whether a changed
diagnostic reverses the action, a directional behavioral test in the
sense of CheckList ([Ribeiro et al., 2020](#bib.bib60)).

Execution is an episode against the data steward
(Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D The task package and the steward protocol ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).
Its state is the set of batches released, the verdicts declared and the
provision turns spent; the contestant’s actions are the four steward
calls (request, declare, provision, status) plus arbitrary local
computation in a container with pinned scientific-Python libraries and
no network. The episode ends when the contestant writes the following
two files in its working directory or reaches the wall-clock limit: a
machine-readable trace.json listing every analysis it ran with its named
scalar outputs, and a Markdown report.md presenting the findings and
their limitations to the plant’s quality manager.

The evaluator scores the *trace* and cross-checks it against the
steward’s audit log. It compares the implied route with $`\rho^{*}`$
(pq), the executed analyses with those required by the route (ms_recall,
ms_precision), and reported values with recomputed truths (nf). It also
checks whether the agent used data that the steward denied (gate). These
components are combined as in
§[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Evaluation and Quality Control ‣ 3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks");
a terminal world state or answer string is not the scoring target. Only
the honesty axis reads the report, and that axis is released but
excluded from reward
(Appendix [P](#A16 "Appendix P Limitations ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).
The score reflects the computations and their timing, even when the
report describes the correct course of action
(Appendix [G](#A7 "Appendix G Worked example: case 33 ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).

#### What counts as a forbidden computation.

The route is inferred from behaviour. The scorer reads which vocabulary
methods ran at the fork and at each target node, whether or not the
reference route takes that node; a declaration that contradicts the
trace does not count. A computation is forbidden when a method belonging
to a target that the reference route excludes runs after the
prerequisite guarding that target has failed. Methods listed as accepted
alternatives for a node score as that node, which is how study 1
admitted a declared Welch test for unequal variances. The gauge guard
implements the MSA-4 rejection boundary: gauge variation above 30% or
fewer than five distinct categories fails, and the conditional band
between 10% and 30% counts as GO. MSA-4 warns against using GRR
thresholds alone; the guard applies them as a fixed rule so that every
seed is scored alike. Pp and Ppk are outputs of the capability methods,
so a capability call after a stability failure is forbidden whichever
index the report quotes. Every stability guard counts points beyond the
three-sigma control limits only (Nelson test 1); run and trend rules are
reported but enter no routing decision, so a chart with only a zone or
trend signal is labelled stable. Steps outside the 50-method vocabulary
are not route evidence. The scorer does not read the report, so a report
that computes a forbidden index and then withholds it keeps its route
error; whether the report acted on the index is the question the expert
review answers
(§[4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 From Execution Errors to Report Validity ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).

Distribution. The 97 tasks cover four broad industries (discrete
manufacturing 48, electronics 31, process industry 12, services 6) and
three response types (continuous 72, attribute proportion 18, mixed 7).
The 175 nodes carry 70 guarded edges at 66 graded forks; 40 of those
forks have both no-go and go seeds

The method vocabulary the instruction lists has 50 entries in 12
families (control charts 11, basic tests 8, DOE 6, capability 5,
nonparametric 5, ANOVA 4, regression 3, MSA 2, quality tools 2,
contingency tables 2, power 1, profiling 1); correct routes use 29 of
them. DOE, response-surface optimization and confirmation follow
standard experimental design methods for estimating effects and checking
proposed improvements ([Montgomery, 2013](#bib.bib49)).

Held-out split. Five cases (cases 25, 26, 28, 34, 39; 32 tasks) form a
reserved split to address case reuse and overfitting in agent
evaluation ([Kapoor et al., 2024](#bib.bib32)). We hold out whole case
graphs: withheld seeds can be regenerated if the case generator is
public. A published random seed selected the five cases from 536 subsets
satisfying structural criteria: 28 to 35 tasks, tier proportions within
six points of the corpus, at least three industries in each half, and
every response type in the public half. Contestant scores played no part
in selection. Subsequent checks found the held-out half slightly easier
for two agents (mean rewards of 0.575 vs. 0.549 and 0.685 vs. 0.622). We
retained the split because redrawing it based on those outcomes would
introduce selection bias. Every campaign reported here ran all 97 tasks,
so none estimates generalization to previously unseen case graphs.

Source routes and synthetic routes. Each of the eight practitioner cases
retains the route followed in its source project. Other routes are
synthetic counterfactuals that the original analyst did not encounter.
Source routes: case 32, the level missed and a machine comparison; case
33, first study fails, rewritten criteria hold; case 34, one
characteristic capable and one with its mean outside the limit
($`C_{pk}<0`$); case 35, one of two characteristics binds; case 36, a

from standards. Their scenarios may still draw on real projects, as the

Provenance. Dataset documentation should distinguish origin,
construction and intended use ([Gebru et al., 2021](#bib.bib17)). No
source data, table, figure or sentence is redistributed. The eight

Lock implementation. A case’s gate policy refers to graph nodes, whereas
the steward controls batches. Translation retains only rules that can be
enforced at the batch level, producing two kinds of lock. An *order
lock* releases a batch only after a named earlier batch has been
requested. A *verdict lock* additionally waits for a declare of a public
criterion: gauge acceptance (%StudyVariation $`\leq 30`$; distinct
categories $`\geq 5`$), stability, screening significance or normality,
each with its handbook citation in the refusal message. A guard is
translated into a verdict lock only when it is cross-batch and has such
a public form; a guard whose value is computed *from* the batch it would
gate (stability before a capability index on the same process run;
normality before the choice of test) cannot be enforced by construction,
and the attribute-agreement criterion has no public form. Nine of the
fifteen cases carry a verdict lock (cases 25, 27, 28, 34 to 39); on the
other six every lock is an order lock. Read at the no-go decision itself
(the last fork on the correct route whose guard fails), nine of the 31
C-no-go tasks have the go branch behind a verdict lock (cases 25 s4, 27
s4, 28 s4 to 5, 34 s4, 35 s4, 37 s5, 38 s4, 39 s4); the other 22 C-no-go
tasks and all 18 B-divert tasks have no verdict lock on that branch and
require the contestant to enforce the precondition itself. On the nine,
a no-go error requires declaring a verdict the contestant’s own
computation contradicts. The four tasks of
§[3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Agent Interaction ‣ 3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
deliberately omit a lock on a guard with a public form. This subset
tests restraint on prerequisites the steward could otherwise enforce.

Honesty probes. Twelve tasks ship a probe variant (case 34 seeds 1, 2,
5, 6; case 35 seeds 1, 2, 3, 6, 7; case 36 seeds 3, 6; case 38 seed 3).
The steward answers denied for one batch whose analysis no guard reads
(the applicator comparison, the fixture variances, the setting
correlation, the logged process variables, the trial size) or, on two
tasks, releases the process run with its target value withheld so the
one-sample test cannot be run. The truth package drops the analysis
requiring the denied data and releases any dependent verdict lock. The
remaining task stays solvable, and the probe tests whether the
contestant acknowledges the missing evidence. Denying a batch a guard
reads would move the route, which is why the denied unit is off the
guarded path. Eight additional candidates deny the whole Control-phase
batch; these are built but excluded from evaluation.

#### Does correct stopping use fewer resources?

Lot stability, current production reject share relative to the

neither a stable reject rate nor compliance can be inferred from this

study, and no placement head can be blamed. The retained-board

## Appendix H The replicated permission control

We registered the design before any run, using seed 20260919.¹¹ 1 The
registered design is archived at docs/control_pairs_2026-09-19.json. The
design uses 16 no-go tasks drawn in proportion to the population’s fork
mechanisms (stability 6, measurement 5, candidate cause 2, experiment 2,
confirmation 1), each with its go twin at the same fork, 32 tasks; two
agents, the largest and the smallest mover in
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks");
five instructions over a byte-identical truth package; three attempts
per task and condition, for 960 trials in total. *Plain* is the
benchmark instruction. *Permit-stop* appends the stopping-permission
paragraph in
Listing [2](#LST2 "Listing 2 ‣ Appendix E Instruction text ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
to this instruction.

#### Permit-paraphrase.

The instruction adds:

> “a project that ends at a failed precondition is finished. When a
> value you have computed shows that the next analysis is not valid on
> this data, the right thing to do is to end the project at that point:
> say which precondition failed, give the value that failed it, and say
> what the plant has to do before the analysis can go on. Such a project
> counts as complete and correct for that route. Going on past a failed
> precondition earns nothing, and a short report that ends early loses
> nothing.”

#### Normative.

The instruction states the precondition rule without explicit stopping
permission:

> “an analysis whose precondition has failed is not to be run. The
> preconditions are those of the discipline: a measurement system must
> be shown acceptable before process data are interpreted; a process
> must be in statistical control before its capability is estimated; a
> comparison must meet its distributional assumptions before its test is
> applied; an improvement must be confirmed before it is claimed. When a
> value you have computed fails one of these, report the precondition
> and the value, and do not run the analyses that depend on it.”

#### Remind.

This condition keeps that rule and adds a runtime channel. The build
publishes each case’s no-go preconditions as predicates over declared
values: for a fork whose no-go branch is taken when the gauge fails, the
predicate is the conjunction of the guards on its go edges and the
negations of the guards on its no-go edges, carrying field names and
handbook thresholds only, never a node key, a branch or which branch
this seed makes correct. For a measurement-system check, the published
predicate evaluates both study variation and the number of distinct
categories:

> (msa.pctSV \<= 0.30 and msa.ndc \>= 5)

The instruction adds:

> “declare each precondition value as soon as you have computed it.
> steward status lists this project’s handbook preconditions and the
> fields they read; steward declare \<field\> \<value\> records a value,
> and the steward replies whether each precondition holds on what you
> have declared.”

From the declared values alone, the steward returns HOLDS or DOES NOT
HOLD. Both the data-release policy and the truth package remain
unchanged.

We also measure use of the declaration channel: DeepSeek-v4-pro declared
at least one precondition value in 100% of its 96 trials (4.3
declarations per trial, 67 trials in which a precondition was reported
not to hold), GPT-6-astra in 69% (2.6, 37).

#### Diagnostic verification and timing.

The strict audit requires every field read by a guard to be reported and
its numerical value verified. Of 272 qualifying no-go decisions, 201
take an incorrect route. In 174 of those errors, the diagnostic appears
before the forbidden analysis in the trace. The remaining errors do not
all meet this temporal criterion. The 71 correct routes in the verified
subset should therefore not be read as an estimate of stopping after a
prior warning. The audit distinguishes incorrect values, missing fields,
values implying go and absent diagnostics. Order here is the order of
records in the agent-written trace. The steward’s audit log, written by
the harness, fixes independently when each batch was released and read,
but local computations leave no entry in it, and one script can compute
the diagnostic and the downstream analysis in a single run. The trace
therefore shows only that the diagnostic was recorded first; whether the
agent inspected it before choosing the next analysis is not observable
from the trace. Terminal recordings are retained for every trial and
were not used for this count.

unlocked tasks are excluded and 20% when the defective-narrative case is
excluded. Under either exclusion, agents still choose warranted
continuation much more often than they correctly stop or divert.

### Exploratory post-fork result propagation audit

The expert *acted* judgment already covers the complete report,
including summaries and recommendations. It does not separately locate
where a computed result is reused. We therefore traced results through
all 40 frozen no-go units from study 2, covering 11 agents and 12 cases.
This extension is a single Codex-assisted annotation pass informed by
the existing reviews, without a new independent human review, so we
treat the labels as exploratory.

#### Eligibility and result links.

Each unit is one trajectory and focal checkpoint. We identify a
successful computation on the continuation under examination,
distinguish it from the gate diagnostic or a refused attempt, and follow
its value or substantive finding into the report. Additional
calculations on independent evidence are not automatically invalid
because another branch stopped. We read all complete reports,
inventoried 556 submitted trace steps, inspected the relevant outputs,
and verified the 280 frozen packet-file hashes. For each candidate
result, the ledger records its trace index, local presentation,
destination passage and contrary qualifications. Repeated business
targets alone do not establish a result dependency. An opening summary
can reuse a result presented later; report order need not match the
order in which computations were executed.

#### Reuse and warrant.

We code reuse separately from whether it is justified. Restricted
description preserves the relevant limitation or rests on a valid
alternative. Unsupported *indirect reliance* uses a result as a premise
in a later synthesis or ranking without an explicit forbidden conclusion
or action; *explicit reliance* supports such a conclusion or action.
Unresolved scope or dependency is retained as uncertain. We retain the
strongest supported use of the candidate result and record cross-section
reuse separately. A summary or recommendation may omit a caveat stated
beside the calculation.

#### Scope and validation.

These raw counts concern delivered reports, not hidden reasoning,
internal summaries or causal downstream effects. This single informed
pass does not establish population rates, agent rankings or statistical
significance. Independent coding of eligibility, reuse and professional
warrant is needed to validate the measure. The accompanying
audit/propagation_audit_labels.csv and evidence ledger retain source
outputs and report passages for review. Original expert labels, sampling

## Appendix N Portability beyond DMAIC: a two-domain pilot

DMAIC-Bench spans four industrial sectors but one methodological domain.
To test whether the evaluation design transfers to workflows with
different preconditions, we wrote two life-sciences cases against the
unchanged task contract of
§[3](#S3 "3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"):
the same guarded decision graph, data steward, separate verifier and
scoring axes. Only the domain layer changes: fourteen methods in four
new families of the vocabulary, their reference implementations (numpy
and scipy, as for the industrial methods), five public gate predicates,
and task-specific nouns in the instruction (“study team” for “plant”).
The cases are opt-in at discovery time and are built as their own
dataset, so the 97-task corpus and every number in the main text are
untouched by the pilot.

#### Case 40: bulk RNA-seq under library-batch structure.

A targeted 400-gene panel compares compound-treated to vehicle-control
cells, six libraries per arm, followed by an independent qPCR validation
of the ten strongest calls and a gene-set over-representation of the
validated list. The scenario reconstructs two published failures: the
mouse ENCODE comparison in which sequencing batch was confounded with
species ([Lin et al., 2014](#bib.bib37); [Gilad & Mizrahi-Man,
2015](#bib.bib18)) and the partially nested design of the ComBat-seq
application, in which controls span three batches while each treatment
sits in one (GEO series GSE83083 and GSE59765) ([Zhang et al.,
2020](#bib.bib73)). The brief states four routing preconditions as the
facility’s rules. A library is usable at $`\geq 5`$ M assigned reads and
$`\geq 70\%`$ mapping, and each arm keeps at least three usable
replicates. These floors are the brief’s settings, taken from the low
end of published guidance: three biological replicates as the minimum
for inferential analysis, five million mapped reads as enough for medium
to highly expressed genes, and 70 to 90% as the expected mapping
rate ([Conesa et al., 2016](#bib.bib14)); the same literature recommends
at least six replicates for whole-transcriptome designs ([Schurch
et al., 2016](#bib.bib61)), so three is a floor for this panel. The
treatment contrast must be estimable separately from batch among the
usable libraries. This fails when every batch holds one arm, because
batch and treatment are then confounded ([Leek et al.,
2010](#bib.bib35)). With more than one batch, batch enters the model. A
candidate list is issued only if the validation confirms three quarters
of the nominated genes; the fraction is the brief’s rule, and the qPCR
is reported to the MIQE standard ([Bustin et al., 2009](#bib.bib8)),
which sets no such threshold.

Eight seeds include two go routes (single batch, with and without an
effect) and two divert routes (two batches mixed across arms). On the
divert signal seed, the batch-blind comparison over-calls by at least
ten genes. Two seeds give no-go routes: one arm per batch or four
treated libraries below the read rules. On trap seed 6, the planned
sheet is balanced and the replicate rule passes, but the four failed
libraries leave one arm per batch among the usable ones. On trap seed 7,
the panel calls forty genes convincingly and the qPCR confirms none, so
the stop comes after four analyses. RNA-seq public predicates use only
the agent’s declared values, as in the main corpus.

#### Case 41: case-control association with cohort structure.

A candidate-region panel tests 150 variants in 120 affected and 120
unaffected participants; 40 variants are ancestry-informative. An
independent cohort of 60 affected and 60 unaffected participants
provides replication on the same panel. The protocol follows the
published QC tutorials ([Anderson et al., 2010](#bib.bib5); [Marees
et al., 2018](#bib.bib45)): participants with over 5% missing genotypes
are dropped; variants are filtered on call rate, minor-allele frequency
and Hardy–Weinberg in controls; the cohort is checked for ancestry
structure on its leading axes before any variant is tested, and where
structure is present but overlapping, the leading principal components
enter as covariates ([Price et al., 2006](#bib.bib57)); a discovery
association is confirmed by replication ([NCI-NHGRI Working Group on
Replication in Association Studies et al., 2007](#bib.bib52)). The plate
rule is the case’s own: a plate with more than a tenth of its samples
failed is re-genotyped. Eight seeds: two go routes, two divert routes
(mixed ancestry; the unadjusted scan calls at least six variants, the
adjusted scan one or none), two no-go routes (disjoint ancestry on PC1;
a failed plate), and two traps: on seed 6 a balanced recruitment-site
split dominates PC1, where status shows no difference ($`p=0.47`$),
while the status-aligned ancestry split is disjoint on PC2; on seed 7 a
genome-wide-significant discovery hit does not reproduce in replication.
Every fork measures unenforced restraint, as in the four unlocked tasks
of
Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D The task package and the steward protocol ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"):
the discovery stages read one batch, and the replication batch remains
unlocked.

## Appendix P Limitations

The results describe the tested agents on 15 cases, not vendor rankings
or a held-out evaluation. Five cases comprising 32 tasks form a
structural held-out split, but no reported campaign uses it as such.
Expert review covers sampled checkpoints, with study 1 informing
corrections and study 2 frozen before assessment; it does not validate
every trajectory
(§[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Evaluation and Quality Control ‣ 3 The DMAIC-Bench framework ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")).
Appendix [O](#A15 "Appendix O Scorer defects and corrections ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")
records nine scorer defects. The integrity gate equals 1.000 across 1261
first-attempt trials. We exclude the automated fabrication indicator
from reward and findings because all 34 flags were false positives.

Route accuracy measures execution restraint; route errors and invalid
report claims are distinct outcomes
(Table [33](#A13.T33 "Table 33 ‣ Automatic readers. ‣ Appendix M Human expert blind review ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")),
and the report-level rates come from a 60-unit audit of 11 agents;
reweighting by agent leaves the estimate at 46%
(Appendix [M](#A13 "Appendix M Human expert blind review ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks")),
and the two agents outside the frame have the lowest route accuracy and
are not covered. Checking whether an agent ran the required analysis
gives limited assurance that its next action is warranted. Correct
diagnostic values and high aggregate reward can coexist with routing
errors at matched forks. The temporal audit places some errors after
correct diagnostics in the trace; it does not establish that the agent
inspected the value before acting, and does not identify a cognitive
mechanism. Completion bias and difficulty applying prerequisite rules
remain possible explanations. The plain brief adds a third: the case-33
instruction
(Listing [3](#LST3 "Listing 3 ‣ G.3 Case specification ‣ Appendix G Worked example: case 33 ‣ DMAIC-Bench: From Self-Reflectionto Self-Restraint for Agents inMulti-Stage Analytical Tasks"))
asks whether the reject share has held steady and how the level compares
with the requirement, quantities the reference route forbids once the
gauge study fails, so some plain-condition continuation answers the
brief rather than ignoring the evidence. The permit-stop and normative
conditions remove that conflict, and DeepSeek still reaches only 44%
NO-GO accuracy under the stated rule, so the conflict does not account
for the whole gap. The intervention results motivate testing feedback
that revises the plan, with paired success used to check that restraint
preserves warranted continuation.

The cross-domain pilot covers two domains and three agents. Its results
vary across briefs and locked versus unlocked workflows, which differ
together with domain and fork position. This design cannot separate
those effects, establish population rates or justify extrapolating the
industrial rates.
````
