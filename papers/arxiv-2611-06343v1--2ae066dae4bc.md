# Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models

Peng Zhang^(1,2), Qian Chen², Xianyin Zhang², Yimin Tian², Ruite Fei¹,
Dongyi Lv¹, Lifan Guo^(2†), Chi Zhang², and Shuai Liu^(1∗) ¹Xi’an
Jiaotong University   ²Qwen DianJin Team, Alibaba Cloud Computing

###### Abstract

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) aim to support visual understanding and
generation within a single model. Existing post-training methods either
use understanding as a fixed teacher to improve generation, or rely on
ground-truth trajectories to supervise both understanding and
generation. Prism instead derives bidirectional supervision from
self-generated interleaved rollouts, constructing training targets
according to the reliability of their text and image chains. We propose
Prism, a bidirectional self-teaching post-training method for UMMs. Like
a prism decomposing mixed light, Prism decouples a coupled interleaved
trajectory into a text chain and an image chain, assesses the
reliability of each chain, and constructs a corresponding training
target from their reliability states. Experiments on both SenseNova-U1
and BAGEL show that Prism improves both understanding and generation. On
the generation side, GenEval improves from 85.8 to 89.2 (+3.4), and DPG
improves from 86.1 to 87.3 (+1.2) on SenseNova-U1. On the understanding
side, the total MME score improves from 2282.6 to 2311.6, and MMVP
improves from 58.0 to 62.0. Consistent improvements are observed on
BAGEL. These results highlight that Prism enables coordinated
improvements in generation and understanding by constructing
reliability-guided self-training targets from self-generated
trajectories, without relying on an external verifier or externally
annotated corrected trajectories during target construction. Code is

## 1 Introduction

With the rapid development of large language models ([Brown et al.,
2020](#bib.bib34); [Sun et al., 2021](#bib.bib35); [Bai et al.,
2023a](#bib.bib36)) and multimodal generation models ([Liu et al.,
2023](#bib.bib37); [Chen et al., 2024b](#bib.bib39); [Bai et al.,
2023b](#bib.bib38)), researchers have begun to explore unifying
understanding and generation within a single model. Unlike treating
understanding and generation as two separate models, Unified Multimodal
Models (UMMs) integrate text, images, and their interactions into a
unified architecture, enabling visual question answering and image
generation to share knowledge and training signals. Recent UMMs, from
Chameleon ([Team, 2024](#bib.bib21)) to SenseNova-U1 ([Diao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib23)), have demonstrated the feasibility of supporting both
understanding and generation within a single model through different
architectural designs. However, these models also reveal a fundamental
problem: the understanding and generation capabilities within a UMM are
asymmetric.

![Refer to caption](2611.06343v1/2_cropped.png)

(a) Some prior methods are data-driven or rely on external supervision

Figure 3: Prism’s position by ground-truth supervision and improved

Recent studies reveal an asymmetry in UMMs: a model can judge whether an
image matches a text, yet fail to generate an image that follows the
same text ([Xie et al., 2026a](#bib.bib4)). Most methods exploit this in
a fixed direction, using understanding to improve generation. GvU ([Pan
et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)) and SRUM ([Jin et al., 2025](#bib.bib2)) turn
alignment judgments into generation rewards, Internal Gap
Self-Improvement ([Han et al., 2026](#bib.bib3)) turns them into
supervision or DPO preference pairs, and RecA ([Xie et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib4)) reconstructs images from visual understanding
embeddings. Others work in reverse, generating depth, segmentation, or
reconstruction targets to sharpen perception ([Su et al.,
2026](#bib.bib5)), or fine-tuning on synthesized editing and interleaved
reasoning traces ([Zheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib6); [Gu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib7)). A further set applies preference or policy
optimization ([Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib8); [Nie et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9); [Hu et al., 2026](#bib.bib10); [Yan et al.,
2026](#bib.bib11)), and UniCorn ([Fang et al., 2026](#bib.bib12))
explores self-play. These methods either are limited to improving
generation or rely on external ground-truth pairs, reward models, or
verifiers. Prism instead derives bidirectional supervision from
self-generated interleaved trajectories, building training targets from
text and image reliability without ground-truth trajectories or an
external verifier, as illustrated in
Figure [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
and
Figure [3](#S1.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").

Prism improves both understanding and generation through self-teaching
on interleaved text-image tasks. In these trajectories, text and image
alternate across multiple steps rather than forming a single
input-output pair. Because the text and image chains are coordinated
expressions of the same underlying task, each chain can be audited
independently. An interleaved trajectory can therefore be decomposed
into a text chain that represents understanding and an image chain that
represents generation, each of which may be reliable or unreliable in
different ways.

As shown in
Figure [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models"),
when the base model generates an interleaved cooking tutorial, the first
image already shows chopped tomatoes during the ingredient-preparation
stage, while the next step again depicts cutting tomatoes. For
tutorial-style tasks, this is either redundant or indicates leakage of a
future state into an earlier step. Conversely, although not shown in
Figure [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models"),
the opposite case can also occur: the image chain may still complete the
task correctly even when the text chain contains erroneous reasoning or
descriptions, because the image does not strictly follow the faulty
text.

Therefore, the key challenge is to identify, for each sample, whether
the understanding or generation side is reliable enough to teach the
other. Prism addresses this challenge by performing sample-wise
self-teaching over interleaved trajectories, enabling bidirectional
improvement in both understanding and generation.

We propose Prism, a bidirectional self-teaching post-training method for
interleaved text-image trajectories. Prism treats each self-sampled
trajectory as a coupled multimodal signal and decomposes it into two
independently assessable views: a text chain and an image chain. By
comparing the reliability of these two chains, Prism avoids assuming a
fixed supervision direction and instead determines on a per-sample basis
whether text should supervise image, image should supervise text, or
unreliable patterns in both chains should be suppressed.

The key contributions can be summarized as follows:

- •
  Method: We introduce a bidirectional post-training paradigm that
  selects the teaching direction for each trajectory, allowing
  multimodal understanding and generation to improve each other rather
  than imposing a fixed supervision direction.
- •
  Framework: Prism provides a reliability-guided self-teaching
  framework. It constructs training targets from self-generated
  interleaved rollouts by assessing the reliability of their text and
  image chains, without ground-truth supervision or external verifiers.
- •
  Performance: Prism improves multimodal understanding and generation
  across different UMMs, including interleaved generation. The results
  demonstrate its effectiveness and generality.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Unified Understanding and Generation Models

UMM architectures can be broadly categorized into four types. Bridge
architectures ([Chen et al., 2025a](#bib.bib25); [Wu et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib28); [Song et al., 2026](#bib.bib33)) retain separate
understanding and generation backbones connected through lightweight
interfaces. Unified Transformer architectures ([Team, 2024](#bib.bib21);
[Zhou et al., 2025a](#bib.bib22); [Xie et al., 2025](#bib.bib26); [Wang
et al., 2024](#bib.bib29); [Wu et al., 2025a](#bib.bib30); [Chen et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib31); [Xie et al., 2026b](#bib.bib27)) process both
modalities within a shared Transformer backbone, with variants using
discrete image tokens, mixed autoregressive-diffusion objectives, or
decoupled visual encoders. Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT)
architectures ([Deng et al., 2025](#bib.bib24); [Diao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib23)) allocate separate expert parameters for understanding
and generation while sharing attention. Diffusion-based
architectures ([AI et al., 2026](#bib.bib32)) employ diffusion language
models to jointly model text and images.

### 2.2 UMM Post-Training

Existing UMM post-training methods follow four main directions.
Preference optimization and reinforcement learning use externally scored
pairs in HermesFlow ([Yang et al., 2026](#bib.bib8)), reward components
in Unified Interleaved GRPO ([Nie et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)) and
BRAID ([Hu et al., 2026](#bib.bib10)), or ground-truth trajectories for
VLM adaptation in S-GRPO ([Yan et al., 2026](#bib.bib11)).
Data-construction approaches include Uni-Edit ([Zheng et al.,
2026](#bib.bib6)), with 148K editing examples, and ThinkMorph ([Gu et
al., 2026](#bib.bib7)), with approximately 24K reasoning traces.
Specialized generation tasks include RecA ([Xie et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib4)), which primarily improves generation through image
reconstruction, and UniMRG ([Su et al., 2026](#bib.bib5)), which
enhances perception using externally produced depth and segmentation
targets. Self-judging methods use understanding-side feedback to guide
generation, including GvU ([Pan et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)), SRUM ([Jin et
al., 2025](#bib.bib2)), and Internal Gap Self-Improvement ([Han et al.,
2026](#bib.bib3)). UniCorn ([Fang et al., 2026](#bib.bib12)) constructs
supervision through self-play. UniRL ([Mao et al., 2025](#bib.bib42))
improves both tasks using self-generated images, but requires
pre-constructed question-answer pairs as ground-truth supervision and
applies a fixed round-trip via SFT and GRPO.

## 3 Method

Unlike prior post-training methods that rely on external supervision or
a fixed supervision direction, Prism derives bidirectional supervision
from self-generated interleaved trajectories. It constructs training
targets according to the reliability of text and image chains, without
ground-truth trajectories or external verifiers. As illustrated in
Figure [4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3 Method ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models"),
given a task prompt, the model generates an interleaved trajectory.
Frozen model copies then assess the text and image chains and map the
trajectory to one of four training actions. Only the rollout model is
updated using the resulting targets.

![Refer to caption](2611.06343v1/3_cropped.png)

Figure 4: Overview of the Prism. Given a text prompt, Prism first uses
the UMM to roll out an interleaved trajectory. It then uses the UMM in a
judging role to assess the reliability of the text chain and image
chain. Based on the resulting status, Prism selects the corresponding
processing action and loss function. All UMM instances are initialized
from the same base model. Only the rollout UMM is trainable, while the
UMMs used for judging and action selection remain frozen.

### 3.1 Preliminary

Given a text prompt $`P`$, the UMM generates an interleaved text-image
trajectory:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       \tau=(T_{1},I_{1},T_{2},I_{2},\ldots,T_{K},I_{K}),
       ```                                                 |     | (1) |

where $`T_{i}`$ and $`I_{i}`$ denote the text and image segments at step
$`i`$. For the interleaved tasks considered here, the trajectory has two
modality-specific views, the complete text chain
$`T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{K})`$, representing understanding through planning
and reasoning, and the complete image chain $`I=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{K})`$,
representing generation through visual states and cross-step
consistency. Both are projections of the same interleaved trajectory.

Standard UMM interleaved training models the text-image trajectory
autoregressively:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
p_{\theta}(\tau\mid P)=\prod_{i=1}^{K}p_{\theta}(T_{i}\mid P,T_{<i},I_{<i})\,\,p_{\theta}(I_{i}\mid P,T_{\leq i},I_{<i}).
``` |  | (2) |

The standard training loss for an interleaved trajectory is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{interleave}}(\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\lambda_{\mathrm{und}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{und}}\!\left(f_{\theta}(\cdot\mid P,T_{<i},I_{<i}),T_{i}\right)+\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}\!\left(f_{\theta}(\cdot\mid P,T_{\leq i},I_{<i}),I_{i}\right)\right],
``` |  | (3) |

where $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{und}}`$ is the token-level cross-entropy
loss for text prediction, and $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}`$ is the
flow-matching loss for image generation ([Lipman et al.,
2022](#bib.bib41)). $`\lambda_{\mathrm{und}}`$ and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}`$ are modality-balancing weights that may vary
across architectures.

### 3.2 Per-Modality Reliability Judgment

The core premise of Prism is that, under the same task prompt $`P`$, the
text chain $`T`$ and the image chain $`I`$ are two modality-specific
expressions of the same underlying task content. Based on this premise,
Prism judges the reliability of the complete text chain and complete
image chain separately:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
(v_{\mathrm{txt}},r_{\mathrm{txt}})=\mathcal{J}(P,T,q_{\mathrm{txt}}),\qquad(v_{\mathrm{img}},r_{\mathrm{img}})=\mathcal{J}(P,I,q_{\mathrm{img}}),
``` |  | (4) |

where $`\mathcal{J}`$ is a frozen copy of the base model, and
$`q_{\mathrm{txt}}`$ and $`q_{\mathrm{img}}`$ are review instructions.
The indicators $`v_{\mathrm{txt}},v_{\mathrm{img}}\in\{0,1\}`$ denote
the reliability of the understanding and generation sides, while
$`r_{\mathrm{txt}}`$ and $`r_{\mathrm{img}}`$ specify detected errors.
Prism judges the complete chains and uses the resulting states to select
a training action, as illustrated in
Figure [4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3 Method ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").

### 3.3 Reliability-Guided Supervision Construction

Based on the judgments in
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Per-Modality Reliability Judgment ‣ 3 Method ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models"),
Prism selects one of four actions for each trajectory: enhancing
cross-modal consistency for reliable trajectories, rewriting an
unreliable chain from the reliable side, or suppressing trajectories
whose two chains are unreliable.

Both pass, $`v_{\mathrm{txt}}=1,v_{\mathrm{img}}=1`$. When both chains
are reliable, the trajectory provides a reliable interleaved training
sample. Prism conditions text reconstruction on the image chain,
reinforcing cross-modal consistency throughout the trajectory. Let
$`P_{\mathrm{rec}}`$ denote the instruction for recovering $`T`$ from
image chain $`I`$ under the original task. The objective is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pass}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{und}}\bigl(f_{\theta}(T\mid P_{\mathrm{rec}},I),T\bigr).
``` |  | (5) |

Text-to-image rewriting, $`v_{\mathrm{txt}}=1,v_{\mathrm{img}}=0`$. When
the text chain is judged reliable but the image chain is not, Prism
preserves $`T`$ and uses $`P`$, $`T`$, and $`r_{\mathrm{img}}`$ to
generate a corrected image chain $`I^{+}=(I^{+}_{1},\ldots,I^{+}_{K})`$.
The resulting corrected trajectory and training objective are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tau^{+}_{\mathrm{t2i}}=(T_{1},I^{+}_{1},T_{2},I^{+}_{2},\ldots,T_{K},I^{+}_{K}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{t2i}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{interleave}}(\tau^{+}_{\mathrm{t2i}}).
``` |  | (6) |

Image-to-text rewriting, $`v_{\mathrm{txt}}=0,v_{\mathrm{img}}=1`$. When
the image chain is judged reliable but the text chain is not, Prism
preserves $`I`$ and uses $`P`$, $`I`$, and $`r_{\mathrm{txt}}`$ to
generate a corrected text chain $`T^{+}=(T^{+}_{1},\ldots,T^{+}_{K})`$.
The resulting corrected trajectory and training objective are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tau^{+}_{\mathrm{i2t}}=(T^{+}_{1},I_{1},T^{+}_{2},I_{2},\ldots,T^{+}_{K},I_{K}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i2t}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{interleave}}(\tau^{+}_{\mathrm{i2t}}).
``` |  | (7) |

Both fail, $`v_{\mathrm{txt}}=0,v_{\mathrm{img}}=0`$. When both chains
are judged unreliable, Prism does not construct a positive target.
Instead, it applies a token-level unlikelihood objective to the text
tokens in the original trajectory, reducing the probability of
reproducing its erroneous text patterns:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fail}}(\tau)=-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{n=1}^{|T_{i}|}\log\left(1-p_{\theta}(t_{i,n}\mid P,T_{<i},I_{<i},t_{i,<n})\right),
``` |  | (8) |

where $`T_{i}=(t_{i,1},\ldots,t_{i,|T_{i}|})`$ denotes the $`i`$-th text
segment and $`t_{i,<n}`$ denotes the tokens preceding $`t_{i,n}`$ within
that segment. Unlike text, image generation is optimized through
continuous flow matching, for which no direct counterpart of token-level
unlikelihood is available. We therefore restrict negative supervision to
the text tokens.

For the two rewriting cases, since the rewritten chain is newly
generated and may fail to correct the detected errors, Prism verifies
each rewritten trajectory before using it as a positive training target,
retrying failed corrections and skipping trajectories that remain
invalid.
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Verification of Rewritten Trajectories ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
details the verification procedure. During training, only the rollout
model is updated, while judgment, rewriting, and verification use frozen
copies of the base checkpoint to prevent co-evolution with the rollout
model. Jointly updating them leads to training collapse, as shown in
Appendix [D](#A4 "Appendix D The Impact of Freezing Strategy ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").

### 3.4 Training Objective

Each trajectory contributes to one of the four objectives according to
its reliability state. So the overall objective is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{pass}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pass}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{t2i}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i2t}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{fail}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fail}},
``` |  | (9) |

where $`\lambda_{\mathrm{pass}}`$, $`\lambda_{\mathrm{i2t}}`$, and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{fail}}`$ weight the both-pass, image-to-text
rewriting, and both-fail objectives, respectively. The text-to-image
rewriting objective uses unit weight.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Data. We use GPT-5.5 to generate 2.4K task prompt seeds spanning
tutorials, stories, spatial reasoning, and other task types. These
prompts provide task descriptions only. Prism generates interleaved
trajectories online and constructs training targets from the model’s own
rollouts.

Evaluation. We evaluate image generation on GenEval ([Ghosh et al.,
2023](#bib.bib13)) and DPG-Bench ([Hu et al., 2024](#bib.bib14)),
multimodal understanding on MME ([Fu et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)),
MMBench ([Liu et al., 2024](#bib.bib16)), MMMU ([Yue et al.,
2024](#bib.bib17)), MMStar ([Chen et al., 2024a](#bib.bib18)),
MMVP ([Tong et al., 2024](#bib.bib19)), and POPE ([Li et al.,
2023](#bib.bib20)), and interleaved generation on OpenING ([Zhou et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib40)). We follow the official evaluation settings for all
benchmarks and report the multiple-choice accuracy for MMMU.

Training. Since some UMMs lack interleaved text-image generation support
or publicly available model weights, we select SenseNova-U1 ([Diao et
al., 2026](#bib.bib23)) and BAGEL ([Deng et al., 2025](#bib.bib24)),
which satisfy both requirements. The main experiments use a learning
rate of 3e-5, with $`\lambda_{\mathrm{pass}}=0.5`$, and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{fail}}=0.005`$. The text-to-image rewriting objective
uses unit weight. Training is performed on 16 A800 GPUs for
approximately 24 hours.

For more details, please refer to the
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Training and Evaluation Details ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").

We evaluate Prism on SenseNova-U1 and BAGEL across image generation and
multimodal understanding benchmarks.

### 4.3 Prism Improves Interleaved Generation

We evaluate Prism on OpenING [Zhou et al. (2025b)](#bib.bib40), which
measures open-ended interleaved image–text generation with pairwise win
rates.
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.2 Prism Simultaneously Improves Understanding and Generation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
reports the win rates and zero-image rates.

Prism improves SenseNova-U1 by 2.90 to 4.24 points and BAGEL by 2.33 to
3.86 points across all tie-handling settings. Among the compared
entries, SenseNova-U1 with Prism achieves the highest win rate in all
four settings. Prism also reduces the zero-image rate of BAGEL from
30.7% to 14.8%, indicating improved ability to generate interleaved
trajectories. SenseNova-U1 already has a near-zero zero-image rate of
0.7% before training, and Prism preserves this capability with a rate of
0.9%. Overall, these results show that Prism improves interleaved
generation across two UMMs.

### 4.4 Comparison with Related Post-Training Methods

We compare Prism with SRUM and UniCorn on BAGEL in
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.2 Prism Simultaneously Improves Understanding and Generation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").
SRUM and UniCorn both improve GenEval and DPG, but reduce MME and
MMBench. On other understanding benchmarks, some scores stay flat or
drop. This shows that they do not achieve consistent gains in both
generation and multimodal understanding. By contrast, Prism improves MME
by 10 points, MMBench by 3.8 points, MMMU by 1.3 points, and MMStar by
4.6 points, while maintaining comparable MMVP performance. It also
improves GenEval by 1.8 points and DPG by 1.0 point. Results show the
four losses contribute distinctly to understanding and generation.

## 5 Conclusion and Limitations

We propose Prism, a bidirectional self-teaching method that derives
targets through text and image reliability. It improves generation,
understanding and interleaved generation on UMMs.

Despite these gains, Prism has three limitations. It targets interleaved
text-image tasks with externally provided prompt seeds, and applies
negative supervision to the complete text chain when both chains fail.
Future work will extend Prism to broader multimodal tasks, fully
self-sampled training, and localized negative supervision.

## Appendix A Training and Evaluation Details

### A.1 Implementation Details and Hyperparameters

We train all models at a resolution of $`1024\times 1024`$ for one
epoch. For the interleaved objective, the cross-entropy and
flow-matching loss weights are set to $`0.1:1`$ for SenseNova-U1 and
$`0.25:1`$ for BAGEL. We use AdamW with a learning rate of
$`3\times 10^{-5}`$ and an effective batch size of 64. The reported
training time includes online rollout generation, reliability judgment,
rewriting, and verification.

For rollout and image generation, we use 50 diffusion steps with global
normalization. The text and image classifier-free guidance scales are
set to 4.0 and 1.0, respectively. Each prompt is assigned a fixed random
seed shared across compared models.

### A.2 OpenING Arena Protocol

We follow the official pairwise arena protocol of OpenING ([Zhou et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib40)). The evaluation set contains 2,160 samples. For each
sample, two distinct entries are paired through circular rotation and
random permutation, and their presentation order is randomized to
mitigate position bias.

The compact comparison in the main paper contains eight entries and
4,320 pairwise battles, with each entry participating in 1,080 battles
on average. The full leaderboard in
Table [8](#A1.T8 "Table 8 ‣ A.2 OpenING Arena Protocol ‣ Appendix A Training and Evaluation Details ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
contains 15 entries, including our Prism-trained models, their
corresponding base models, and 11 official OpenING reference outputs. It
contains 4,143 valid pairwise battles. The number of battles per entry
varies between 507 and 615, as reported in the final column of
Table [8](#A1.T8 "Table 8 ‣ A.2 OpenING Arena Protocol ‣ Appendix A Training and Evaluation Details ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").
Since the compact comparison and the full leaderboard are evaluated in
separate arenas, their win rates are arena-relative and are not directly
comparable.

We use the official IntJudge-8B checkpoint and decoding configuration.
For each battle, IntJudge outputs one of four decisions: A, B, Tie(A),
or Tie(B). We report four tie-handling metrics:

- •
  FDT: Tie(A) is counted as a win for A, and Tie(B) is counted as a win
  for B.
- •
  w/o Tie: only battles with an explicit winner are counted.
- •
  w/ Tie (0): ties contribute zero wins to both models.
- •
  w/ Tie (.5): ties contribute 0.5 wins to both models.

We use w/ Tie (.5) as the main reporting protocol. The Human entry uses
the human-authored interleaved image–text sequences released with
OpenING and participates in the same arena protocol as model outputs.
Under the w/o Tie protocol, SenseNova-U1 with Prism achieves an 86.61%
win rate over 478 decisive outcomes from 537 battles, exceeding the base
model by 3.55 points and GPT-4o+DALL-E3 by 5.23 points.

### A.3 Image Generation Evaluation

We evaluate image generation with the official data and scoring
pipelines of GenEval ([Ghosh et al., 2023](#bib.bib13)) and
DPG-Bench ([Hu et al., 2024](#bib.bib14)). GenEval contains 553 prompts,
with four images generated per prompt. It evaluates six categories using
Mask2Former and CLIP, and reports their equal-weighted average.
DPG-Bench contains 1,065 dense prompts and uses mPLUG-VQA for
evaluation.

All images are generated at $`1024\times 1024`$ using the configuration
above. For DPG-Bench, we generate four images per prompt and arrange
them into a $`2\times 2`$ grid. The official evaluator crops and scores
each image at the native $`1024`$ resolution. All comparisons with the
base models use identical generation settings.

### A.4 Multimodal Understanding Evaluation

We evaluate multimodal understanding using the official data, prompts,
and evaluation scripts of MME, MMBench, MMMU, MMStar, POPE, and MMVP. No
task-specific prompts are added.

MME contains 14 tasks, including 10 perception tasks with a maximum
score of 2,000 and four cognition tasks with a maximum score of 800. We
report the total score in the main paper. MMBench is evaluated on its
1,176-example development split with the official option-rotation
protocol. MMMU is evaluated on its 900-example validation split,
including 847 multiple-choice and 53 open-ended questions. We report
multiple-choice accuracy. MMStar contains 1,500 examples. POPE includes
9,000 questions from the random, popular, and adversarial settings, for
which we report Accuracy and F1. MMVP contains 300 questions organized
into 150 paired examples, and is scored only when both questions in a
pair are answered correctly.

All understanding benchmarks are evaluated using a unified decoding
configuration and the official aggregation scripts.

on SenseNova-U1 Prism slightly increases the short score and slightly
decreases the long score, while on BAGEL both scores decrease. On
SenseNova-U1, the basic sub-attributes improve under both variants, the
advanced ones improve under short prompts and decrease under long ones,
and style control decreases under both.

These patterns follow from how the training organizes generation. Every
image in a trajectory is generated with the preceding text and images as
context and realizes only the incremental state of the current step, so
the training reinforces continuation from rich context. A short prompt
is close to realizing a single simple state and benefits. A long prompt
instead requires satisfying all constraints in one generation from text
alone, and style control requires the same one-shot fidelity, so neither
is reinforced and both decrease. On BAGEL, which starts weaker on
single-image instruction following, the gains do not materialize and the
decreases spread across nearly all sub-attributes.

Editing. ImgEdit-Bench and GEdit-Bench evaluate instruction-based
editing, scored by ImgEdit-Judge and VIEScore respectively ([Ye et al.,
2026](#bib.bib43); [Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib44)). As shown in
Table [10](#A2.T10 "Table 10 ‣ Appendix B Additional Capability Analysis ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models"),
editing results are mixed. On SenseNova-U1 both benchmarks decrease
slightly, with the largest per-task decrease on style change. On BAGEL,
ImgEdit-Bench slightly increases, and the increases concentrate on
content-oriented operations such as extraction and person editing, while
GEdit-Bench changes by less than 0.07.

The transfer is limited because the two tasks use the conditioning image
differently. In a trajectory, the preceding images serve as state
history, and each new image re-renders the updated state, inheriting
entities and progress semantically while sharing few pixels with its
predecessors. Instruction-based editing instead keeps most pixels of the
input image and changes only the specified region. The training
therefore reinforces semantic continuation conditioned on images but
never pixel-level preservation, which explains why content-oriented
operations improve while tasks that demand pixel fidelity stay flat or
decrease. Obtaining editing gains requires editing-specific supervision
as in editing-oriented methods such as Uni-Edit ([Zheng et al.,
2026](#bib.bib6)).

## Appendix C Detailed Reliability Judgment and Routing Analysis

underlying the results in
Table [6](#S4.T6 "Table 6 ‣ 4.5 Ablation Experiment ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").
All variants start from 2,395 prompt seeds and use the same one-epoch
optimization budget and learning rate. Base w/ Self-Training optimizes
unmodified self-generated rollouts with
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{interleave}}`$. Prism w/ Random Routing preserves
the number of trajectories assigned to each action within a minibatch
but permutes their assignments independently of the judged reliability
states. Prism w/o Rewriting retains reliability-based routing but trains
trajectories assigned to t2i and i2t on their unmodified rollouts
instead of constructing rewritten targets.

Full Prism outperforms Base w/ Self-Training and Prism w/ Random Routing

from 59.3 to 62.0. These comparisons support the value of matching
training actions to judged reliability states and constructing rewritten
targets, beyond direct self-training or preserving the overall action
mixture alone. We next examine the frequency of each reliability state,
the evolution of routing during training, and the verification of
rewritten targets.

### C.3 Verification of Rewritten Targets

A trajectory assigned to t2i or i2t is used as a positive rewritten
target only if its correction passes verification.
Table [13](#A3.T13 "Table 13 ‣ C.3 Verification of Rewritten Targets ‣ Appendix C Detailed Reliability Judgment and Routing Analysis ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
reports verification outcomes for the rewriting candidates arising from
the first-rollout cohort in
Table [11](#A3.T11 "Table 11 ‣ C.1 Empirical Routing Distribution ‣ Appendix C Detailed Reliability Judgment and Routing Analysis ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").
Of the 571 t2i candidates, 376 (65.8%) are accepted and 195 (34.2%) are
discarded. Of the 223 i2t candidates, 117 (52.5%) are accepted and 106
(47.5%) are discarded. The lower acceptance rate for i2t shows that the
two directions have different verification outcomes; neither set of
generated corrections is admitted wholesale. Discarded candidates do not
become positive training targets and are replaced with newly generated
rollouts, which are judged and routed independently.

Verification also entails different amounts of review in the two
directions. The first-rollout t2i candidates require 1,474 $`I^{+}`$
review calls, compared with 320 $`T^{+}`$ review calls for i2t
candidates. These counts include retries, rather than additional
training trajectories. Together, the acceptance rates and review counts
characterize verification as a target-selection step: routing identifies
a chain to correct, while verification determines whether the resulting

## Appendix D The Impact of Freezing Strategy

We examine the role of freezing the judge during online training by
comparing Full Prism with a variant in which the judge is updated
jointly with the rollout model. The comparison uses the same training
setup and tracks two quantities. First, the routing distribution records
how the judge assigns generated trajectories to the four actions; the
text- and image-chain pass rates are computed from these assignments.
Second, the zero-image rate is the proportion of generated interleaved
trajectories containing no image output and serves as a measure of
image-generation collapse. The jointly updated run terminates after 34
updates, whereas the frozen-judge run completes all 38 updates.

Table [16](#A4.T16 "Table 16 ‣ Appendix D The Impact of Freezing Strategy ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
shows that the two settings have similar routing distributions in the
first stage but subsequently diverge. With the frozen judge, the
text-chain pass rate increases from 70.6% to 83.5% and the image-chain
pass rate from 60.8% to 66.1%, while all four actions remain active
throughout the epoch. The Frozen judge rows reproduce the online
training statistics in
Table [12](#A3.T12 "Table 12 ‣ C.2 Routing Dynamics during Training ‣ Appendix C Detailed Reliability Judgment and Routing Analysis ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models").
With joint updates, the Pass share rises from 50.6% in the first stage
to 87.4% in updates 16–23, while i2t and Fail fall to 4.8% and 1.0%. The
increasing concentration in Pass indicates that joint updates
substantially change the routing signal.

This concentration does not persist as stable generation. In the final
two updates of the jointly updated run, the Pass share falls to 46.0%
and t2i rises to 54.0%, alongside a drop in the image-chain pass rate to
46.0%.
Table [17](#A4.T17 "Table 17 ‣ Appendix D The Impact of Freezing Strategy ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")

## Appendix E Implementation of Training Objectives

Equation 3 defines the objective for one interleaved trajectory
$`\tau=(T_{1},I_{1},\ldots,T_{K},I_{K})`$, where $`P`$ is the task
prompt, $`T_{i}`$ is the $`i`$-th text segment, and $`I_{i}`$ is the
image generated after $`T_{i}`$. Each step follows a text-first, then
image order. The trajectory-level objective sums a text loss and an
image loss over the $`K`$ interleaved steps.

For the text side, let $`T_{i}=(t_{i,1},\ldots,t_{i,|T_{i}|})`$ denote
the token sequence of the $`i`$-th text segment. We use standard
token-level next-token cross-entropy:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{und}}=-\sum_{n=1}^{|T_{i}|}\log p_{\theta}(t_{i,n}\mid P,T_{<i},I_{<i},t_{i,<n}),
``` |  | (10) |

where $`T_{<i}=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{i-1})`$ is the sequence of preceding
text segments, $`I_{<i}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{i-1})`$ is the sequence of
preceding images, and $`t_{i,<n}=(t_{i,1},\ldots,t_{i,n-1})`$ are the
previous tokens in the current text segment.

For the image side, let $`z_{i,1}`$ denote the clean target visual
representation of image $`I_{i}`$ under the native image representation
of the backbone. This may be a latent code, patch representation, or
another backbone-specific continuous representation. Let
$`z_{i,0}\sim p_{0}`$ denote a noise sample of the same shape drawn from
a fixed noise distribution. We sample an interpolation time
$`s\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]`$ and define the linear flow-matching path

|     |                                |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       z_{i,s}=(1-s)z_{i,0}+sz_{i,1},
       ```                             |     | (11) |

with target velocity

|     |                                |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       v_{i}^{\star}=z_{i,1}-z_{i,0}.
       ```                             |     | (12) |

Let

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
c_{i}=(P,T_{\leq i},I_{<i}),\qquad T_{\leq i}=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{i}),
``` |  | (13) |

denote the conditioning context for generating $`I_{i}`$. The model
predicts a velocity field $`v_{\theta}(z_{i,s},s,c_{i})`$, and the
image-generation loss is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{gen}}=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1],z_{i,0}\sim p_{0}}\left[\left\|v_{\theta}(z_{i,s},s,c_{i})-v_{i}^{\star}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right].
``` |  | (14) |

For the both-pass case, both modalities are judged reliable. We keep the
original image chain $`I=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{K})`$ and reconstruct the full
concatenated text chain $`T=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{|T|})`$, where $`T`$ is
obtained by concatenating all text segments $`T_{1},\ldots,T_{K}`$. The
recovery instruction $`P_{\mathrm{rec}}`$ and image chain jointly
condition the reconstruction:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pass}}=-\sum_{n=1}^{|T|}\log p_{\theta}(t_{n}\mid P_{\mathrm{rec}},I,t_{<n}).
``` |  | (15) |

For the t2i and i2t cases, one modality is corrected and the resulting
rewritten trajectory is verified before training. We then apply
Equation 3 to the verified corrected trajectory. Thus, every text token
is trained with the text loss and every image representation is trained
with the image loss.

For the both-fail case, the original trajectory $`\tau`$ is treated as
unreliable and is not used as a positive target. Instead, we apply
token-level unlikelihood training to the original text chain:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fail}}(\tau)=-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{n=1}^{|T_{i}|}\log\left(1-p_{\theta}(t_{i,n}\mid P,T_{<i},I_{<i},t_{i,<n})\right).
``` |  | (16) |

In implementation, the argument of the logarithm is numerically clipped
away from zero for stability. Each trajectory $`\tau`$ is assigned to
exactly one reliability case, pass, t2i, i2t, or fail. During minibatch
training, only the objective associated with the assigned case
contributes to the loss for that trajectory, while the remaining
case-specific objectives are masked out.
Eq. [9](#S3.E9 "In 3.4 Training Objective ‣ 3 Method ‣ Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models")
denotes the resulting aggregate minibatch objective.

## Appendix F Verification of Rewritten Trajectories

For the t2i and i2t cases, Prism first generates a corrected chain from
the preserved reliable chain and the detected error. Text-to-image
rewriting generates $`I^{+}`$ from $`P`$, $`T`$, and
$`r_{\mathrm{img}}`$, while image-to-text rewriting generates $`T^{+}`$
from $`P`$, $`I`$, and $`r_{\mathrm{txt}}`$. Each rewritten candidate is
verified before it is used as a positive training target. The verifier
is a frozen copy of the base model. A candidate is regenerated for at
most four attempts, including the initial generation and three retries.
Candidates that fail all attempts are discarded.

Image-side verification. For a rewritten image chain $`I^{+}`$ generated
from the preserved text chain $`T`$, the verifier receives the task
prompt $`P`$ and the candidate image sequence. A candidate is accepted
only if it contains the same number of images as the original
trajectory, satisfies the task requirements for objects, counts,
attributes, spatial relations, state changes, and step order, and is
judged valid by the verifier.

``` ltx_verbatim
You are a strict reviewer auditing only the image sequence
of an interleaved solution and its visual state progression.

Step text is not shown. You see {n_images} images in order.

TASK:
{prompt}

Decide whether this image chain delivers the progression
required by the task.

1. Base each decision on visible evidence.
2. To return invalid, cite one concrete image number and its visible error.
3. The first confirmed visible error determines invalid.
4. Determine from the task whether image count is strict or flexible.
5. Check required objects, counts, attributes, spatial relations,
   actions, visual states, lighting, and state progression.
6. A preview or final state is valid only when it does not conflict
   with an explicitly required stage.
7. Do not reject an image chain solely for style drift, texture noise,
   lighting shifts, background changes, or minor visual variation.

Return invalid for a confirmed error such as no task progress,
wrong image or stage count, wrong quantity, failed state change,
state mismatch, corrupted key subject, premature final state,
state regression, identity swap, internally impossible content,
or failure to convey a required step.

Return exactly one JSON object with a Boolean field named "valid".
````

Text-side verification. For a rewritten text chain $`T^{+}`$ generated
from the preserved image chain $`I`$, the verifier receives the task
prompt and candidate text. A candidate is accepted only if the verifier
returns valid, its interleaved marker structure is valid, its final
image reference is valid, and its length is at least half that of the
original rollout text.

```ltx_verbatim
You are auditing only the visible text of a no-think interleaved
text-and-image response. Images are audited separately.

TASK:
{prompt}

SOLUTION TEXT:
{text_chain}

Judge whether the written content and image marker structure
satisfy the task.

1. Enforce image-marker count only when the task explicitly specifies it.
2. Required facts, names, labels, directions, emotions, colors, counts,
   and object identities must be stated clearly.
3. The text must cover required objects, stages, relations, or scene changes.
4. Reject only clear text-side contradictions, skipped main events,
   impossible orderings, or unexplained state regression.
5. For exact-one-image tasks, text after the image marker must not
   introduce another scene or visual plan.
6. For tutorials, processes, stories, and lifecycle tasks, check clear
   ordering errors, missing stages, extra stages violating an explicit
   count, or state regression.

Return exactly one JSON object with a Boolean field named "valid".
When invalid, include a verbatim faulty span in the "quote" field.
```
