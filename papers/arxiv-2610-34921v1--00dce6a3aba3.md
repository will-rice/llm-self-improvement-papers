# PhysEvolve: Verifier-Grounded Self-Evolution for Physical Reasoning with Executable Worlds

Jinyuan Hu ^(†)^(†)thanks: Equal contribution.    Yilei
Jiang¹¹footnotemark: 1    Chen Tang    Minghong Cai    Xinran Liu   
Xiangyu Yue ^(†)^(†)thanks: Corresponding author: xyyue@ie.cuhk.edu.hk.

###### Abstract

Physical reasoning underpins how embodied systems understand and act in
the physical world, yet vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable
on questions about dynamics and physical causality. Progress is
critically constrained by the scarcity of grounded counterfactual
training data, as observational videos capture only the factual
trajectory that occurred. The self-evolving paradigm offers a route
beyond fixed datasets by enabling models to construct their own training
problems. Extending this paradigm to physics, however, requires an
external source of truth that can both validate model-proposed
environments and verify alternative physical outcomes under
intervention. We introduce PhysEvolve, a verifier-grounded self-evolving
framework that trains VLMs for causal physical reasoning from
model-proposed executable worlds and simulation-authored tasks. At each
round, a shared VLM proposes executable world programs and evidence
schemas; a deterministic physics engine freezes each valid world,
simulates matched baseline and body-elided counterfactual branches, and
synthesizes discrete, answer-bound visual tasks. Exact verifier feedback
from task solving updates both the shared policy via GRPO and an
adaptive curriculum over physical intents. Starting from
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, PhysEvolve achieves targeted gains across
PhysBench, CausalPhys, and ContPhy without human intervention data,
improving PhysBench Throwing by $`8.8`$ points and ContPhy
Counterfactual Removal and Goal-Pit by $`7.4`$ and $`24.4`$ points.
These gains concentrate on interaction-sensitive and altered-world
questions, demonstrating zero-shot transfer of causal principles to
unseen complex dynamics (cloth, fluid, soft bodies) and real-world
video.

## 1 Introduction

Multimodal understanding of the physical world is foundational to
embodied intelligence and robotic systems, where reliable
decision-making requires anticipating how scenes evolve and respond to
external interventions ([Black et al., 2024](#bib.bib17); [Zhou et al.,
2026](#bib.bib14)). Yet current vision-language models (VLMs) remain
substantially less reliable on physical dynamics, intervention effects,
and counterfactual outcomes than on static object recognition or spatial
relations ([Chow et al., 2025](#bib.bib8); [Tang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9); [Zheng et al., 2024](#bib.bib10)), and broader
multimodal reasoning benchmarks echo the same reliability gap on
structured logical inputs ([Yuan et al., 2026](#bib.bib47)). Closing
this gap is fundamentally bottlenecked by training data: high-quality,
verifiably annotated examples that isolate how physical outcomes change
under controlled interventions are exceptionally scarce. Real-world
videos are not only costly to annotate, but fundamentally capture only
the factual trajectory that occurred, rendering counterfactual
alternatives unobservable ([Pearl, 2009](#bib.bib18)).

A growing paradigm of self-evolving systems circumvents static data
bottlenecks by enabling models to autonomously generate the problems
from which they learn ([Zhao et al., 2025](#bib.bib1); [Xia et al.,
2025](#bib.bib28); [Gao et al., 2026](#bib.bib19)). The viability of
this paradigm hinges on an external source of truth that can
independently validate self-generated problems and verify their correct
answers as the training distribution shifts. In symbolic domains such as
mathematics and code, an interpreter provides this exact supervision: a
Proposer generates an executable specification, the interpreter computes
its deterministic output, and exact correctness supplies the Solver’s
learning signal. Physics simulators offer a natural counterpart for the
physical world by executing proposed environments and exposing their
complete internal states ([Prabhudesai et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)). This
analogy suggests an appealingly direct route to physical self-play:
retain the proposer–solver curriculum and substitute a physics simulator
for the symbolic interpreter.

However, naively mapping this paradigm to physical reasoning reveals a
fundamental structural breakdown
(Figure [1](#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3.1 Problem Formulation and Pitfalls of Naive Physical Self-Play ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).
When a shared Proposer–Solver policy proposes rigid-body scenes and
evaluates a single simulated trajectory using continuous proximity
scores, two failure modes emerge
(Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Problem Formulation and Pitfalls of Naive Physical Self-Play ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")):
(1) single-trajectory readouts cannot construct or verify intervention
tasks, starving the curriculum of causal supervision; and (2) continuous
proximity rewards inflate learnability signals for uniformly incorrect
rollouts, inducing reward hacking without improving exact physical
correctness. These findings establish two indispensable requirements for
physical self-play: the verification environment must actively execute
interventions across paired branches, and derived visual tasks must be
bound to discrete, exact ground truth.

We introduce PhysEvolve, a verifier-grounded self-evolving framework
that learns causal physical reasoning from policy-proposed executable
worlds and simulation-authored tasks
(Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3.2 Framework Overview ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).
Rather than authoring text questions directly, a task-conditioned
Proposer generates an executable _world program_ and an _evidence
schema_. A deterministic physics executor validates and freezes the
scene, executes matched baseline and body-elided counterfactual branches
under identical initial conditions, and derives an answer-bound question
from their divergent outcomes. The shared VLM then solves the task from
the baseline video, receiving exact discrete feedback. This architecture
establishes a strict information boundary: the policy guides the
exploration of physical environments, while the fixed simulator alone
determines physical validity and factual/counterfactual ground truth.
The resulting exact solver feedback simultaneously drives shared-policy
updates via GRPO and guides an adaptive curriculum over physical
reasoning intents.

Training on executor-synthesized tasks over policy-proposed worlds
produces targeted transfer across three physical-reasoning benchmarks
without any human-annotated intervention data. Starting from
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct ([Bai et al., 2025a](#bib.bib42)), PhysEvolve
improves PhysBench Throwing by $`8.78`$ percentage points and Judgment
by $`5.08`$ points. Crucially, on ContPhy, performance on Counterfactual
Removal and Goal-Pit increases by $`7.38`$ and $`24.44`$ points,
transferring causal intervention principles zero-shot to held-out
continuous media (cloth, fluid, rope, and soft bodies) entirely absent
from the rigid-body training templates. On real-world videos in
CausalPhys, PhysEvolve likewise achieves consistent gains on
Anticipation ($`+2.46`$) and Intervention ($`+2.27`$).

Our core contributions are summarized as follows:

1.  1.  Verifier-grounded physical self-play: We present PhysEvolve, the
        first self-evolving framework that trains VLMs for causal physical
        reasoning via model-proposed executable worlds, enforcing a strict
        information boundary between policy exploration and deterministic
        simulator verification.
2.  2.  Intervention-grounded task synthesis: We introduce an execution
        pipeline that executes matched baseline and body-elided branches
        under identical seeds, transforming continuous multi-body dynamics
        into discrete, answer-bound multi-choice tasks with exact verifier
        rewards.
3.  3.  Out-of-distribution causal transfer & audit: We demonstrate that
        self-play on rigid-body programs transfers zero-shot to complex
        continuous dynamics and real-world video, and provide an empirical
        audit exposing why naive single-trajectory physical self-play
        fundamentally collapses.

## 2 Related Work

#### Physical reasoning in vision-language models.

Extensive studies reveal that current multimodal models handle static
visual appearance and geometric arrangements far more reliably than
temporal prediction, physical intervention, and counterfactual reasoning
([Riochet et al., 2018](#bib.bib24); [Bear et al., 2021](#bib.bib16);
[Tung et al., 2023](#bib.bib25); [Yi et al., 2020](#bib.bib15); [Ates et
al., 2022](#bib.bib26); [Chow et al., 2025](#bib.bib8); [Tang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)). Broader multimodal reasoning benchmarks further
expose that vision-language models remain unreliable on structured
logical reasoning ([Yuan et al., 2026](#bib.bib47)), motivating
verifiable, grounded supervision rather than perception-only training.
To diagnose physical understanding beyond passive prediction,
counterfactual benchmarks introduce paired simulator-generated
alternative trajectories and intervention questions ([Baradel et al.,
2020](#bib.bib27); [Chen et al., 2022](#bib.bib23); [Patel et al.,
2022](#bib.bib22)). Recently, simulator-grounded training has emerged to
convert simulation traces into verifiable supervision: notably,
Sim2Reason synthesizes physics QA pairs from predefined simulator
rollouts and filters shortcut solutions ([Prabhudesai et al.,
2026](#bib.bib2)). Unlike offline trajectory-to-QA pipelines, PhysEvolve
autonomously synthesizes verifiable counterfactual supervision _online_
by executing paired intervention branches over active, policy-proposed
physical worlds.

#### Self-evolving agents and environment co-design.

While standard self-training bootstraps reasoning over fixed problem
sets ([Zelikman et al., 2022](#bib.bib6); [Gülçehre et al.,
2023](#bib.bib7)), self-evolving agents generate the very curricula and
environments from which they learn. Absolute Zero pioneered
shared-policy proposer–solver self-play with code interpreters ([Zhao et
al., 2025](#bib.bib1)), inspiring subsequent frameworks to expand task
substrates across multi-agent debates, geometric spaces, games, and
tool-augmented execution ([Huang et al., 2025](#bib.bib20); [Chen et
al., 2025](#bib.bib21); [Xia et al., 2025](#bib.bib28); [Liu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib33); [Wei et al., 2025](#bib.bib34); [He et al.,
2025](#bib.bib29); [Wang et al., 2025](#bib.bib30); [Li et al.,
2026](#bib.bib31)). A complementary line of modular and agentic
multimodal systems—spanning visual-to-code generation, agentic coding
for games, search-augmented image generation, multimodal deep search,
agentic perception, and failure-aware software repair—demonstrates that
decomposing perception, planning, and execution into verifiable
sub-routines scales reliably across complex tool-use environments
([Jiang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib46); [Jiang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib49);
[Feng et al., 2026a](#bib.bib48); [Jiao et al., 2026](#bib.bib51); [Fan
et al., 2026](#bib.bib52)). In parallel, unsupervised environment design
(UED) co-evolves adaptive environments alongside control policies based
on learner regret or progress ([Wang et al., 2019](#bib.bib35); [Dennis
et al., 2020](#bib.bib36); [Parker-Holder et al., 2022](#bib.bib37);
[Zala et al., 2024](#bib.bib38); [Liang et al., 2024](#bib.bib39)).
While existing self-play frameworks operate primarily in discrete
symbolic spaces, PhysEvolve shifts the frontier of self-evolution to
continuous multi-body physics, delegating the physical environment
proposal to the policy while retaining deterministic verification and
task synthesis within the executor.

#### Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) substitutes
brittle, exploitable learned reward models with deterministic,
ground-truth verifiers in domains admitting objective computation
([DeepSeek-AI, 2025](#bib.bib3); [Lambert et al., 2024](#bib.bib40)).
Verification methods have expanded from exact answer matching and
test-suite execution to hybrid dense-sparse rewards, self-consistency
checks, and formal proof checking ([Tao et al., 2026](#bib.bib4); [Zhao
et al., 2026](#bib.bib5); [Wilf et al., 2026](#bib.bib32)). Recent
multimodal RL work further studies reward design under heterogeneous or
competing objectives, including multi-task visual reasoning with
moving-average reward normalization, thinking-with-image video reward
models, and reasoning reward models for agents ([Feng et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib45); [Wang et al., 2026](#bib.bib50); [Fan et al.,
2026](#bib.bib52)). While RLVR has achieved remarkable success in
mathematical deduction and program synthesis, extending objective
verification to multimodal physical reasoning has remained an open
challenge. PhysEvolve realizes RLVR for multimodal physics by grounding
policy updates in discrete, simulation-verified causal outcomes rather
than heuristic continuous proximity metrics.

## 3 Methodology

We introduce PhysEvolve, a verifier-grounded self-evolving framework
that learns causal physical reasoning by having a shared vision–language
policy propose executable worlds while a deterministic physics executor
authors and verifies derived multimodal tasks. Below,
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Problem Formulation and Pitfalls of Naive Physical Self-Play ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
first diagnoses the structural pitfalls of naively transferring code
self-play to physical reasoning and establishes two necessary design
principles.
Sections [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Framework Overview ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")–[3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Co-Evolution Training and Adaptive Curriculum ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")

### 3.1 Problem Formulation and Pitfalls of Naive Physical Self-Play

Figure 1: Naive self-play baseline for physical reasoning. A Proposer
generates a world program; the executor simulates a single trajectory
and derives Forward, Inverse, and Parameter tasks. Graded continuous
proximity can reward a Solver that remains fundamentally incorrect,
broadcasting distorted learnability signals back to the Proposer.

In discrete symbolic domains such as mathematics and code synthesis,
self-play enables models to surpass fixed data limits by autonomously
generating and solving their own problems ([Zhao et al.,
2025](#bib.bib1)). The policy evolves by optimizing a learnability
reward that dynamically prioritizes tasks near the boundary of its
current competence. The viability of this paradigm stems from its
objectivity: a deterministic interpreter executes model-written
specifications and returns exact correctness signals without human
supervision.

Given that grounded, intervention-level annotations are exceptionally
scarce in physical reasoning, transferring this self-play paradigm to
multimodal physics by substituting a physics simulator for the symbolic
interpreter appears to be a natural solution. In a direct physical
transfer, a shared Proposer–Solver policy interacts with a rigid-body
simulator. For each proposed world $`\mathcal{W}`$, the executor
simulates one factual trajectory $`\tau`$ under seed $`\xi`$, extracts
an observation $`x`$ alongside a simulator-derived target $`y`$, and
scores the Solver’s prediction $`\hat{y}`$ via a graded similarity
metric $`s`$:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\tau=\Phi_{\xi}(\mathcal{W}),\qquad(x,y)=\operatorname{readout}(\tau),\qquad r_{\mathrm{naive}}=s(\hat{y},y).
``` |  | (1) |

Here $`\Phi_{\xi}`$ denotes deterministic simulation and $`s`$ assigns
partial credit based on continuous numerical or spatial proximity,
intended to prevent sparse, uninformative all-zero rewards early in
training. While this transfer ostensibly preserves both autonomous task
generation and verifiable supervision, empirical auditing reveals two
fatal structural failure modes.

Failure Mode 1: Single-trajectory execution cannot ground causal
interventions. Physical reasoning distinctively demands understanding
how scenes respond to counterfactual interventions—outcomes that never
occurred in the observed trajectory ([Pearl, 2009](#bib.bib18)).
However, evaluating a single trajectory $`\tau`$ restricts the task
space strictly to passive observational readouts (e.g., predicting an
object’s future position or initial velocity). In our audit of the naive
transfer baseline, exactly zero intervention or counterfactual tasks
were synthesized across 100 training rounds, causing PhysBench dynamics
accuracy to stagnate and reasoning performance to degrade
(Section [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Auditing the Single-Trajectory Interface ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).
This limitation is structural: causal ground truth cannot be inferred
from a single trajectory without paired, counterfactual branch
executions. Neither scaling model capacity nor expanding rollout volume
can produce verified labels that lie beyond the verification interface
itself.

Failure Mode 2: Graded proximity rewards induce learnability illusions.
In code and math, execution verification yields a rigorous binary
correctness signal. In continuous physics, however, substituting exact
match with graded proximity $`s`$ fundamentally distorts reinforcement
signals. Because $`s`$ scores relative closeness rather than true task
resolution, clusters of uniformly incorrect rollouts (e.g., predicting
stationary velocities or median parameters) yield intermediate rewards.
These reward distributions artificially inflate the empirical
learnability metric $`r_{\mathrm{learn}}=1-2|\bar{a}-0.5|`$, misleading
the curriculum into classifying failed, trivial tasks as productive
learning boundaries. Consequently, graded reward steadily increases
during naive training while exact physical correctness remains near zero
(Section [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Auditing the Single-Trajectory Interface ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds"),
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Q3: Necessity of branch-enabled synthesis and joint co-evolution. ‣ 4.3 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).

These failure modes establish that continuous physics cannot be treated
as a trivial drop-in replacement for discrete code interpreters.
Autonomous physical reasoning requires two non-negotiable architectural
guarantees:

1.  1.
    Branch-level verification: The executor must actively execute
    paired, counterfactual interventions (divergent simulation branches)
    under identical initial conditions to establish objective causal
    ground truth.
2.  2.
    Discrete answer binding: Derived physical tasks must map continuous
    trajectories into discrete, answer-bound problems, ensuring that
    Solver success reflects exact correctness rather than deceptive
    proximity.

### 3.2 Framework Overview

Figure 2: Overview of PhysEvolve. A shared VLM proposes an executable
world program and evidence schema. A fixed deterministic executor
validates and freezes the scene, simulates matched baseline and
body-elided branches when required, and synthesizes an answer-bound
item. Exact discrete Solver feedback updates the shared policy and the
curriculum.

Guided by these requirements, we introduce PhysEvolve
(Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3.2 Framework Overview ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).
At each round, a *Task-Conditioned Proposer* transforms a sampled
reasoning intent into an executable candidate world. Through
*Intervention-Grounded Task Synthesis*, a fixed executor establishes
factual and counterfactual outcomes via deterministic simulation and
synthesizes discrete, answer-bound multiple-choice questions. Finally,
*Co-Evolution Training* translates exact Solver correctness into joint
updates for the shared policy and an adaptive curriculum.

Let $`\pi_{\theta}`$ denote the shared vision–language policy. The
identical parameters serve as Proposer $`\pi_{\theta}^{\mathrm{P}}`$ and
Solver $`\pi_{\theta}^{\mathrm{S}}`$. The Proposer controls the
exploration of executable physical environments, while the Solver
receives only rendered baseline video observations and the resulting
question and options. Crucially, a strict *information boundary* is
enforced: the fixed physics executor alone accesses internal simulator
states, computes counterfactual branches, and binds the correct answer.
This boundary enables the policy to actively shape its own training
distribution without ever setting its own training labels, eliminating
label contamination and reward hacking.

### 3.3 Task-Conditioned Proposer

The Proposer receives a reasoning intent $`\sigma`$, which specifies the
target physical relation to probe and the evidence required to make that
relation uniquely decidable. Grounded in classical cognitive physics
([Battaglia et al., 2013](#bib.bib43); [Spelke, 1990](#bib.bib44)), we
instantiate simulation across three fundamental Newtonian paradigms: (1)
projectile motion and parabolic dynamics (*Throwing*), (2)
impulse-momentum transfer and kinematic collisions (*Collision*), and
(3) continuous kinematics and contact interactions (*Motion*), spanning
eight active reasoning intents.

Conditioned on $`\sigma`$, the Proposer samples a tuple
$`(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{S})\sim\pi_{\theta}^{\mathrm{P}}(\cdot\mid\sigma)`$:
an executable *world program* $`\mathcal{W}`$ and an *evidence schema*
$`\mathcal{S}`$. The world program is authored in PhysDSL (a
domain-specific Python API over rigid-body simulation) specifying 3D
geometry, material properties (mass, restitution, friction), initial
states, kinematic constraints, and rendering viewpoints. The evidence
schema $`\mathcal{S}`$ declares the semantic roles of key entities
(e.g., query projectiles, barrier obstacles, potential collision
targets) required by intent $`\sigma`$. The executor validates these
role declarations against the realized simulation, rejecting any
proposal whose physical dynamics cannot support the requested reasoning
intent. Complete specifications of PhysDSL and evidence schemas are
provided in
Appendices [A](#A1 "Appendix A Complete PhysDSL and Executable World Interface ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
and [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 Reported evidence schemas ‣ Appendix A Complete PhysDSL and Executable World Interface ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds").

### 3.4 Intervention-Grounded Task Synthesis

A candidate pair $`(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{S})`$ represents only a
potential reasoning opportunity; simulation execution must establish its
physical semantics before it can constitute a valid training task. The
executor admits an item only when execution recovers unambiguous
physical evidence that determines a unique answer. This contract is
executed across three sequential stages:

World verification and freezing. The executor parses and executes the
world program in a sandboxed simulator, resolving evidence-schema roles
against simulated rigid bodies. It admits only worlds exhibiting valid
geometry, non-trivial physical events, and the causal prerequisites of
intent $`\sigma`$. A bounded deterministic repair stage corrects minor
placement or boundary syntax violations without introducing new bodies
or joints. Once admitted, the world definition is strictly *frozen* into
an immutable scene definition. Freezing guarantees that all subsequent
intervention branches share identical initial geometry, material
properties, and simulation seeds, ensuring that divergent outcomes are
strictly attributable to declared interventions.

Branch elision. For interventional reasoning intents, the executor
constructs a finite set of body removals $`\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}`$. For
each $`B\in\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}`$, it elides those bodies and their
dependent constraints from the frozen world, yielding the matched
execution pair:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bigl(\tau_{\emptyset},\tau_{B}\bigr)=\bigl(\Phi_{\xi}(\mathcal{W}),\Phi_{\xi}(\mathcal{W}\setminus B)\bigr),\qquad B\in\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}.
``` |  | (2) |

Both trajectories share the identical simulator state, random seed
$`\xi`$, and time horizon. The Solver observes only a video rendering of
the factual trajectory $`\tau_{\emptyset}`$, while the executor retains
the divergent branch trajectories $`\{\tau_{B}\}`$ as objective ground
truth.

Answer-bound question synthesis. Each reasoning intent defines a
discrete readout function, feasibility margins, and natural language
templates. The executor applies the readout across baseline and branch
trajectories. An item is suppressed if readouts are non-unique,
infeasible, or fall within sensitive physical margin thresholds (e.g.,
velocity differences below $`0.30`$ m/s). Otherwise, the executor
determines the unique factual or counterfactual outcome, binds it to
ground-truth option index $`a^{\star}\in\{A,B,C,D\}`$, and renders the
final visual question and distractors. For inverse intervention tasks
(e.g., “Which wall should be removed to make the ball land in the target
pit?”), the executor runs all candidate elisions (remove A, remove B,
remove both) and validates whether exactly one branch achieves the goal;
“none of the above” is selected only if no branch reaches the target.
Detailed contracts, margin tests, and binding rules are documented in
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Intervention-Grounded Task Contracts ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds").

### 3.5 Co-Evolution Training and Adaptive Curriculum

The synthesized answer-bound items close the self-play loop by providing
exact, discrete supervision to both policy roles.

Exact solver supervision. Each retained item defines a group of $`G`$
Solver rollouts from policy $`\pi_{\theta}^{\mathrm{S}}`$. Given the
executor-bound label $`a^{\star}`$ and predicted options
$`\{\hat{a}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}`$, the binary Solver reward and empirical
item success rate are:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{i}^{\mathrm{solve}}=\mathbf{1}[\hat{a}_{i}=a^{\star}],\qquad\bar{a}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}r_{i}^{\mathrm{solve}}.
``` |  | (3) |

In contrast to the continuous proximity score $`s`$ in
Equation ([1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Problem Formulation and Pitfalls of Naive Physical Self-Play ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")),
$`r_{i}^{\mathrm{solve}}`$ provides unambiguous binary verification
immune to proximity-induced reward hacking.

Hierarchical co-evolution. Co-evolution coordinates task selection and
world exploration across two complementary levels:

- •
  *Macro-level intent curriculum:* Across reasoning intents, the rolling
  Solver accuracy $`\bar{a}_{f}`$ for each task family $`f`$ governs
  adaptive intent sampling. After an 8-round cold start, intent sampling
  weights follow
  $`w_{f}=\rho_{f}\max(0.05,4\bar{a}_{f}(1-\bar{a}_{f}))`$, where
  $`\rho_{f}`$ denotes structural family priors. This formulation
  concentrates training on intents situated at the frontier of the
  model’s evolving competence (where $`4\bar{a}_{f}(1-\bar{a}_{f})`$
  peaks near $`\bar{a}_{f}=0.5`$). A family-difficulty multiplier
  further modulates Proposer credit according to rolling intent
  challenge.
- •
  *Micro-level world exploration:* Conditioned on the sampled intent
  $`\sigma`$, the Proposer explores the space of valid, physically rich
  world programs. Proposer rewards reward successful compilation,
  dynamic collision feasibility, and structural edit-distance diversity
  $`I`$, penalized by anti-copy and saturation controls
  (Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Optimization and Active Curriculum ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).

Shared-policy GRPO optimization. Both Proposer and Solver roles are
optimized jointly via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on the
shared parameters $`\pi_{\theta}`$, regularized against a frozen
reference policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. Proposer groups share a sampled
intent $`\sigma`$, while Solver groups share an executor-rendered item.
Joint optimization ensures that while the Solver masters causal physical
deduction, the Proposer continuously adapts to author executable
environments that remain informative and verifiable
(Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Reproducibility, Training, and Evaluation Configuration ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

#### Research questions.

We design our empirical evaluation around four central questions: (Q1)
Targeted capability transfer: Does PhysEvolve yield targeted
improvements on the specific physical capabilities it simulates during
training? (Q2) Out-of-distribution transfer: Do causal principles
learned in synthetic rigid-body environments generalize to unseen
complex media (fluid, cloth, soft bodies) and real-world video? (Q3)
Architectural necessity: How critically do paired branch simulation and
joint Proposer–Solver optimization contribute to performance? (Q4)
Failure anatomy: What exact supervisory signals are forfeited by relying
on single-trajectory execution and continuous proximity rewards?

#### Training configuration.

All experimental variants are initialized from the open-weight base
model Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct ([Bai et al., 2025a](#bib.bib42)). Training
is executed in a single run for 100 steps using one-epoch GRPO with a
learning rate of $`10^{-6}`$. At each step, the curriculum samples
$`N=4`$ reasoning intents and generates $`G=4`$ world program proposals
per intent, capping retention at 32 active Solver groups with 4 rollouts
per group. The complete hyperparameters and training loop are detailed
in
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Reproducibility, Training, and Evaluation Configuration ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
and
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ F.1 Role contracts and update loop ‣ Appendix F Reproducibility, Training, and Evaluation Configuration ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds").

#### Benchmarks and evaluation protocols.

We evaluate across three rigorous physical reasoning benchmarks: (1)
PhysBench ([Chow et al., 2025](#bib.bib8)) covers broad static and
dynamic physical QA over 9,802 items; (2) ContPhy ([Zheng et al.,
2024](#bib.bib10)) benchmarks counterfactual and goal-directed physical
reasoning across 1,950 scenarios involving complex continuous dynamics
(cloth, fluid, rope, and soft bodies); and (3) CausalPhys ([Tang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)) assesses physical anticipation and intervention in
3,057 real-world video scenarios. All evaluated models share identical
prompts, visual frame sampling, greedy decoding, and official evaluation
protocols
(Appendix [F.3](#A6.SS3 "F.3 Evaluation protocols ‣ Appendix F Reproducibility, Training, and Evaluation Configuration ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).

#### Baselines and comparative context.

Our primary, strictly matched comparison comprises: (1) the base
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model; (2) Naive self-play, which replicates
single-trajectory execution with continuous graded proximity rewards;
and (3) PhysEvolve. To contextualize performance across model scales, we
also report unaligned results for prominent open-weight models:
InternVL2.5-8B ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib11)), Llama-3.2-11B-Vision
([Meta, 2024](#bib.bib12)), Pixtral-12B ([Agrawal et al.,

the scaled Qwen3-VL-32B ([Bai et al., 2025a](#bib.bib42)).

Within the matched 8B evaluation, PhysEvolve achieves substantial gains

#### Q2: Out-of-distribution transfer to complex media and real video.

The most compelling evidence of physical generalization emerges on
ContPhy: despite being trained exclusively on synthetic rigid-body
simulations, PhysEvolve demonstrates remarkable zero-shot transfer to
continuous media (cloth, fluid, rope, and soft bodies). On
Counterfactual Removal, accuracy increases by $`7.38`$ points over Base
($`51.64\%`$ vs. $`44.26\%`$), while on Goal-Pit, accuracy surges by
$`24.44`$ points ($`44.44\%`$ vs. $`20.00\%`$, surpassing even
Qwen3-VL-32B’s $`26.7\%`$). On CausalPhys real-world videos, PhysEvolve
similarly yields consistent gains on Anticipation ($`+2.46`$) and
Intervention ($`+2.27`$). Because the non-rigid dynamics and visual
textures of ContPhy are completely absent from the training templates,
these gains confirm that PhysEvolve internalizes an abstract
*intervention operator*—the ability to deduce how physical states
diverge under structural removal—rather than memorizing superficial
simulation artifacts.

### 4.4 Auditing the Single-Trajectory Interface

#### Q4: What causes naive physical self-play to collapse?

We conduct an empirical audit of the single-trajectory verifier and task
stream in Naive self-play to validate the theoretical pitfalls
identified in
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Problem Formulation and Pitfalls of Naive Physical Self-Play ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds").

Divergence between graded reward and exact correctness.
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Q3: Necessity of branch-enabled synthesis and joint co-evolution. ‣ 4.3 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
exposes a striking pathology: continuous proximity rewards $`s`$
consistently provide deceptive reinforcement. For Forward prediction,
rollouts achieve a high mean graded reward of $`0.373`$ despite *zero*
exact matches across all 100 rounds. For Inverse and Parameter tasks,
mean rewards reach $`0.664`$ and $`0.787`$ while exact accuracy remains
below $`7\%`$. An error audit reveals severe mode collapse: the policy
learns to output stationary initial velocities (746 instances) or the
median rung of the parameter ladder (145 instances), exploiting the
graded metric to reap partial credit without solving the physical
events.

Task space starvation. Over 100 training rounds, the naive
single-trajectory verifier generated *zero* intervention questions and
*zero* counterfactual tasks. Constrained to an un-intervened factual
trajectory $`\tau`$, the system is incapable of synthesizing causal
supervision. Consequently, downstream PhysBench dynamics accuracy
stagnates
(Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4.2 Main Results: Targeted and External Transfer ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")).

## 5 Conclusion

PhysEvolve demonstrates that verifier-grounded self-evolution extends to
continuous multimodal physical reasoning by anchoring self-play in
executable 3D world programs and paired counterfactual branches.
Converting continuous physics into discrete verifier contracts
eliminates the dual traps of continuous reward hacking and causal
blindness inherent in naive physical self-play. Strong targeted gains on
Newtonian dynamics and zero-shot transfer to unseen continuous media and
real-world video show that counterfactual execution provides a scalable
foundation for generalizable physical intelligence in vision-language
models, entirely without any human-annotated physical reasoning
supervision during self-play training. Each training target is bound to
a verified physical outcome.

## Appendix A Complete PhysDSL and Executable World Interface

### A.1 Complete callable surface

A proposal is a Python module containing build_scene(api) and a literal
SCENE_MANIFEST. Every construction/setter call mutates a builder and
returns None; the proposal cannot branch on simulator state. The
executor alone calls freeze() to obtain a scene. Table
[5](#A1.T5 "Table 5 ‣ A.1 Complete callable surface ‣ Appendix A Complete PhysDSL and Executable World Interface ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
lists the complete public surface used by the current executor,
including operations not exercised by every reported world class.
Section [B](#A2 "Appendix B Auditable World-Program Gallery ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
instantiates the interface with three auditable golden-world
illustrations.

|  Capability |  Calls |  Effect |
|----|----|----|
|  Geometry |  add_box, add_sphere, add_cylinder, add_capsule, add_plane |  Register fixed or free rigid bodies and visual/material attributes. |
|  Initial state |  set_velocity, set_rotation |  Set linear/angular velocity or a normalized orientation quaternion. |
|  Constraints |  add_hinge, add_slide, add_spring |  Add one-DOF joints or a damped spatial spring between existing bodies. |
|  Environment |  set_gravity, set_duration, add_camera |  Set the execution horizon, gravity vector, and look-at camera. |
|  Finalization |  freeze |  Add default ground/camera if absent and snapshot a SceneDef. |

Table 5: Complete PhysDSL callable surface. Arguments are validated when
the call is made; freeze() returns the immutable executor input.

#### Hard validation envelope.

Table [6](#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ Hard validation envelope. ‣ A.1 Complete callable surface ‣ Appendix A Complete PhysDSL and Executable World Interface ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")

|----|----|

|  Geometric size |  Each non-plane radius/half-size/half-height in $`[0.01,1.0]`$ m; each plane half-size is at least $`0.01`$ m and has no API upper cap. |
|  Material |  Mass $`[0.01,100]`$ kg, sliding friction $`[0,2]`$, restitution $`[0,1]`$, and every RGBA channel $`[0,1]`$. |

|  Spring |  Stiffness $`[0.1,10^{4}]`$, damping $`[0,10^{3}]`$, body-local anchor components in $`[-2,2]`$ m, and optional rest length $`>0`$; at least one endpoint is dynamic and neither endpoint is a plane. |
|  Environment |  Duration $`[0.1,60]`$ s; finite three-vector gravity; camera position and target must differ. |

Table 6: PhysDSL construction-time bounds. Intervals are closed unless
an inequality is written explicitly.

### A.2 Reported evidence schemas

A reasoning intent specifies the semantic relation to probe and the
evidence needed to decide it. PhysDSL can express more worlds than the
reported run uses; the run enables exactly the three schemas in
Table [7](#A1.T7 "Table 7 ‣ A.2 Reported evidence schemas ‣ Appendix A Complete PhysDSL and Executable World Interface ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds"),
which jointly serve the eight active families in
Section [C](#A3 "Appendix C Intervention-Grounded Task Contracts ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds").
The executor resolves every declared role against the frozen world
before constructing an item.

|  World class |  Required role fields |  Served active families |
|----|----|----|
|  Throwing |  balls, three or more fixed platforms, two or more removable walls, region_kind, and three destination regions derived from platform gaps |  final destination; current-run judgment; counterfactual destination; inverse intervention |
|  Collision |  dynamic target; trigger; exactly four dynamic movers; relation_pair={a,b,expected} |  final state; final relation; motion attribution |
|  Motion |  dynamic motion_target; optional speed_pair={a,b} |  motion-state change, including forward, speed-profile, and pairwise surface forms |

A counterfactual(A) item is admitted exactly when $`r_{A}\neq\bot`$ and
$`r_{A}\neq r_{0}`$, then binds answer semantic $`r_{A}`$. For an
inverse target $`t\neq r_{0}`$, define
$`H_{t}=\{A:r_{A}=t,\ B:r_{B}=t,\ AB:r_{AB}=t\}`$. The generator
requires all three branch views and makes two fixed-order passes over
left/right/middle. The first returns the first target with $`|H_{t}|=1`$
and binds its sole lever. Only when no target has a unique achiever does
the second return the first target with $`|H_{t}|=0`$ and bind *none of
the above*; a target with $`|H_{t}|>1`$ is skipped in both passes. Thus
“none” is an executor-derived negative, not a catch-all for failed
execution, and an ambiguous target does not preclude a decidable target
elsewhere.

### C.2 Surface realization boundary

Only after an answer semantic is admitted does the task-interface
library select a compatible stem and four-option layout and bind the
semantic to an index. A canonical family layout is used whenever a
sampled surface lacks the required semantic. This ordering keeps the
physical decision rule executor-determined while allowing multiple
linguistic realizations of the same evidence.

## Appendix D Verification, Repair, and Branch Execution

#### Typed termination.

Every proposal receives exactly one status. FAILED_SYNTAX,
FAILED_SEMANTIC, and FAILED_RUNTIME cover parsing, eager DSL validation,
and build_scene execution. FAILED_COMPILE, FAILED_OVERLAP, and
FAILED_NAN cover simulator-model, initial-interpenetration, and
numerical failures. FAILED_TIMEOUT and FAILED_RENDER cover bounded
execution and rendering. FAILED_VQA means the manifest or generated item
violates its contract. TRIVIAL means maximum displacement is below
$`0.05`$ m and there is no collision. Only OK worlds with admitted items
enter Solver groups.

#### Bounded repair.

Repairs are recorded as an ordered trace. Minor repairs address
supported syntax/argument variants, manifest repairs recover fields
mechanically determined by authored geometry, and geometry repairs clamp
workspace positions or separate simple initial overlap. Repair does not
add a new body, joint, spring, or declared causal relation. The
strongest applied tier yields a proposal-credit multiplier of $`0.90`$
(minor), $`0.80`$ (manifest), or $`0.75`$ (geometry); unrepaired worlds
use $`1.0`$.

#### Frozen matched branches.

After repair, the executor calls freeze() once. A branch clones that
immutable SceneDef, elides its named body set and every incident
joint/spring, and re-executes with the baseline seed and horizon.
Identical removal sets share one cached execution. At most six distinct
removal sets are run per world; failed/capped branches are absent,
causing dependent items to be suppressed. The throwing scheduler places
the A/B/A+B inverse sets for the first two balls before compatible
counterfactual requests, so the latter can reuse the same branch views.

1: proposal module $`p`$, stage-2 DSL, simulation seed $`s`$

2: parse build_scene and literal SCENE_MANIFEST

3: execute the sandboxed builder with eager DSL checks

4: apply bounded repair and freeze one immutable world $`W`$

5: compile, execute, and render baseline
$`\tau_{0}\leftarrow\mathcal{E}(W,s)`$

6: validate manifest roles against $`W`$ and enumerate required elisions

7: for each distinct removal set $`B`$ up to the branch cap do

8:   $`W_{B}\leftarrow W`$ with $`B`$ and incident constraints elided

9:   $`\tau_{B}\leftarrow\mathcal{E}(W_{B},s)`$; cache by $`B`$

10: end for

11: for each requested active family $`f`$ do

12:   compute evidence predicates from $`\tau_{0}`$ and required
$`\tau_{B}`$

13:   if all decidability and margin conditions for $`f`$ hold then

14:    derive answer semantic; select a compatible surface layout

15:    bind semantic to one option index and emit the item

16:   end if

17: end for

## Appendix E Optimization and Active Curriculum

#### General objective.

Let $`z`$ be the terminal proposal status, $`n`$ the number of retained
Solver rollouts, $`G_{\min}=4`$, and $`I`$ the intrinsic score. Before
repair, anti-copy, saturation control, and family scaling, the
implementation defines

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{\mathrm{base}}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{\mathrm{fail}}c_{z},&z\neq\texttt{OK},\\
0,&z=\texttt{OK}\ \text{and}\ n<G_{\min},\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{learn}}^{\mathrm{eff}}r_{\mathrm{learn}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}I,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (4) |

Equation [4](#A5.E4 "In General objective. ‣ Appendix E Optimization and Active Curriculum ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds")
separates terminal failure, insufficient retained evidence, and admitted
worlds. Here
$`r_{\mathrm{learn}}=1-2|\bar{a}_{\mathrm{prop}}-\tfrac{1}{2}|`$ is the
instance-level tent statistic, where $`\bar{a}_{\mathrm{prop}}`$ denotes
the retained-rollout Solver accuracy used to score the proposal. The
Solver receives exact option-match reward. Status prices are
$`c_{z}=-1.0`$ for syntax, semantic, and VQA failure, $`-0.8`$ for
runtime failure, $`-0.5`$ for compile and overlap failure, $`-0.3`$ for
numerical, timeout, and render failure, and $`0`$ for a trivial world.

#### Reported instantiation and hierarchical co-evolution.

Co-evolution operates through a two-level curriculum. At the macro
level, family sampling tracks the Solver’s competence boundary across
reasoning intents: after an eight-step prior-weighted cold start,
selection uses $`\max(0.05,4\bar{a}_{f}(1-\bar{a}_{f}))`$ multiplied by
the structural priors in
Table [12](#A5.T12 "Table 12 ‣ Reported instantiation and hierarchical co-evolution. ‣ Appendix E Optimization and Active Curriculum ‣ PhysEvolve: Verifier-GroundedSelf-Evolution for Physical Reasoningwith Executable Worlds"),
so saturated or unsolvable families are down-weighted. The
family-difficulty multiplier further scales Proposer credit by rolling
$`\bar{a}_{f}`$, so Solver outcomes reshape both which intents are
sampled and how admitted worlds are valued. At the micro level,
conditioned on the sampled intent $`\sigma`$, the shared policy explores
the program space for valid, physically non-trivial environments.
Instance-level scoring prioritizes physical validity, collision
feasibility, and structural diversity $`I`$, with the instance-level
tent coefficient held at $`\lambda_{\mathrm{learn}}^{\mathrm{eff}}=0`$.
This hierarchy combines competence-adaptive task selection with
verifier-grounded exploration of executable worlds. The executor
supplies the verifiable answers that connect both levels. Executable
demonstrations remain available as syntactic references. Through this
dual-level loop, Solver progression reshapes the training distribution
via the adaptive intent curriculum, and shared-parameter updates of
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ align world generation with physical reasoning.

The active proposal signals are typed failure/validity, repair credit,
structural diversity, anti-copy, saturation control, and
family-difficulty scaling; the active Solver signal is exact option
match.
````
