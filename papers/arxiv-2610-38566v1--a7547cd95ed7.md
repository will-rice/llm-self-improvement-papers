###### Abstract

Rubric-based reinforcement learning (RL) offers interpretable,
fine-grained supervision for aligning large language models (LLMs) on
open-ended generation tasks. However, static rubric criteria may become
saturated or remain beyond the policy’s exploration, rendering the
reward signals insufficiently discriminative. While recent efforts seek
to guide sampling exploration or evolve rubrics, they fail to explicitly
factor in how rubric difficulty should track the evolving policy’s
capabilities, resulting in a persistent mismatch between the
optimization objective and the policy’s current capability frontier. In
this paper, we propose Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability
Awareness (CoCA), a closed-loop framework that jointly adapts the rubric
and policy through criterion-level diagnosis. Specifically, CoCA
contrasts query-only and rubric-guided rollouts to characterize each
criterion relative to the current policy, recalibrates criteria with
mismatched difficulty, and identifies guidance-revealed capability gaps
for frontier-based learning. Based on this insight, CoCA either expands
rubric-conditioned frontiers through guided optimization or transfers
qualified gains through on-policy self-distillation. As the policy
updates, CoCA improves the capability diagnosis and enables more
reliable distillation, thereby completing the co-evolution loop.
Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and LLMs demonstrate
that CoCA consistently improves performance over rubric-based RL
methods. Moreover, CoCA promotes the internalization of
guidance-revealed capabilities into query-only behavior while mitigating
capability degradation.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities
in open-ended generation, signaling a shift from functional tools toward
personified partners ([Arora et al., 2025](#bib.bib21); [Liu et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib25); [Zhang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib59)). These successes
are supported by the extension of reinforcement learning with verifiable
rewards (RLVR) ([Su et al., 2026](#bib.bib45); [Guo et al.,
2025](#bib.bib43); [Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib44)), e.g., by using
elaborate rubrics to provide discriminative reward signals for on-policy
supervision ([Gunjal et al., 2026](#bib.bib2); [Huang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib46); [Shen et al., 2026](#bib.bib47)). However, as
training progresses, predefined criteria may become saturated or remain
unexplored ([Figure 1
(a)](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")),
resulting in stalled policy improvement ([Ding et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)).

![Refer to caption](2610.38566v1/intro.png)

Figure 1: Motivation and overview of CoCA. (a) Static criteria may
become saturated or remain unmet, limiting reward discriminativeness.
(b) Query-only failures can conceal capabilities elicitable under rubric
guidance. (c) Distinct from existing rubric-based RL methods, CoCA
tracks the policy’s capability boundary to couple rubric adaptation and
policy learning in a closed loop.

To mitigate this issue, recent work adopts a rubric-evolution paradigm
that utilizes external feedback to update criteria, incorporating
desired response patterns or previously overlooked errors ([Shao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib33); [Rezaei et al., 2026b](#bib.bib6); [Wang et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib34); [Ding et al., 2026](#bib.bib8); [Li et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib1)). Despite their effectiveness, these methods overlook
whether new criteria align with the task objective and the policy’s
capability boundary ([Kim et al., 2026b](#bib.bib37)), potentially
allowing superficial attributes (e.g., response length or
format ([Rezaei et al., 2026b](#bib.bib6))) to drive discriminativeness.
Worse still, although an improved rubric provides more informative
rewards, its outcome-only supervision forces the policy to discover
desired behaviors through sample-inefficient trial and error ([Wang et
al., 2026b](#bib.bib4); [Zhang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib5); [Zhou et al.,
2026](#bib.bib3)). This motivates a critical question: Can rubric’s
evolution track the policy’s evolving capability while guiding the
policy’s exploration beyond scalar rewards?

Pursuing this objective raises significant challenges. First, how to
identify the current capability boundary? Judging capabilities from
query-only failures may confuse missing capabilities with those
elicitable under rubric guidance ([Figure 1
(b)](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"));
the latter are precisely what training should internalize in the model’s
parameters. Second, which evolved criteria should be adopted?
Indiscriminately adding new criteria risks stylistic drift ([Yang et
al., 2026b](#bib.bib48)), whereas appropriate criteria should expose
differences in how current responses satisfy the optimization objective.
Third, when does rubric guidance provide a reliable direction for
exploration? Guidance may elicit different behaviors that already fall
within the policy’s unaided capabilities.

To dismantle these barriers, we propose Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric
via Capability Awareness (CoCA), a closed-loop framework that couples
rubric evolution with policy optimization through criterion-level
capability assessment ([Figure 1
(c)](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")).
Specifically, CoCA first sample query-only and rubric-conditioned
rollouts from the same policy and contrast criterion satisfaction to
characterize its current capability boundary and the associated
supervision requirements. Subsequently, CoCA selectively recalibrates
criterion difficulty to expose differences in task fulfillment among
distinct responses while preserving the original evaluation intent.
Finally, to provide a reliable direction for exploration, CoCA treats a
conditioned response as a guided frontier only if it satisfies the
diagnosed targets, using it as privileged context for token-level
supervision through self-distillation. Otherwise, optimization of
conditioned rollouts expands the capability frontier, supplying improved
responses for capability diagnosis and subsequent self-distillation,
thereby closing the co-evolution loop.

We evaluate CoCA with Qwen3-4B/8B ([Yang et al., 2025](#bib.bib19)), and
Gemma-3-4B-it ([Team et al., 2025](#bib.bib20)) across 7 benchmarks
spanning the medical, scientific, and psychological domains. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that CoCA achieves consistent gains over
standard rubric-based RL while reaching comparable performance in
approximately $`2\times`$ fewer training steps. Moreover, CoCA surpasses
state-of-the-art counterparts while maintaining robust out-of-domain
generalization. Further analyzes trace how behaviors elicited by rubric
guidance become consolidated into query-only responses, providing
evidence of capability internalization.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

- •
  We propose CoCA, a novel policy-rubric co-evolutionary RL framework
  that persistently tracks the policy’s capability boundary to maintain
  informative supervision throughout training.
- •
  Distinct from existing isolated methods, CoCA establishes dual-group
  capability diagnosis as a shared decision signal for rubric adaptation
  and policy learning, enabling closed-loop policy–rubric co-evolution.
- •
  Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoCA outperforms representative
  rubric-based methods in open-ended generation.

## 2 Preliminaries

Rubric-based Reinforcement Learning extends verifiable rewards to
open-ended tasks by decomposing response quality into interpretable
criteria ([Gunjal et al., 2026](#bib.bib2); [Zhou et al.,
2026](#bib.bib3); [Rezaei et al., 2026b](#bib.bib6); [Rezaei et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib7)). Each query $`x`$ is paired with a rubric
$`\mathcal{R}_{x}=\{(c_{j},w_{j})\}_{j=1}^{J_{x}}`$, where $`J_{x}`$ is
the number of criteria, $`c_{j}`$ denotes the $`j`$-th criterion, and
$`w_{j}`$ is its signed importance weight. Given a policy response
$`y_{i}`$, a fixed LLM judge $`\mathcal{J}_{\phi}`$ returns a binary
verdict $`z_{i,j}=\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(x,y_{i},c_{j})\in\{0,1\}`$
indicating whether the criterion is met. Positive-weight criteria reward
desirable behaviors, while negative-weight criteria penalize undesirable
ones. The scalar reward aggregates these verdicts and normalizes by the
total positive weight:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
r_{i}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{J_{x}}w_{j}z_{i,j}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,w_{j}>0\end{subarray}}^{J_{x}}w_{j}}.
``` |  | (1) |

This normalization assumes at least one positive-weight criterion and
expresses reward relative to the rubric’s total positive weight. A
single positive-weight binary correctness criterion recovers the
standard verifiable-reward setting.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib44)) uses this score as an outcome reward. For each query,
it samples $`K`$ responses
$`y_{i}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x)`$, where
$`\theta_{\mathrm{old}}`$ is the frozen pre-update snapshot of the
trainable policy parameters $`\theta`$. Writing
$`\bar{r}=K^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}r_{i}`$ and $`\sigma_{r}`$ for the group
reward mean and standard deviation, the group-normalized advantage is
$`\widehat{A}_{i}=(r_{i}-\bar{r})/(\sigma_{r}+\epsilon)`$, with
$`\epsilon>0`$ ensuring numerical stability. The token-level importance
ratio is
$`\rho_{i,t}(\theta)=\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})/\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})`$.
The clipped policy loss is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{1}{|y_{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{i}|}\Big[&\min\!\left(\rho_{i,t}(\theta)\widehat{A}_{i},\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\rho_{i,t}(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon\right)\widehat{A}_{i}\right)\Big],\end{split}
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\varepsilon`$ controls the clipping range. Since $`r_{i}`$ is
assigned at the sequence level, the same advantage $`\widehat{A}_{i}`$
supervises every token in $`y_{i}`$, without token-level attribution for
individual rubric criteria.

![Refer to caption](2610.38566v1/methods.png)

Figure 2: Pipeline overview of our CoCA. Dual-Group rollouts contrast
query-only and rubric-conditioned behavior to diagnose criterion-level
capabilities, guiding capability-aware rubric calibration and frontier
selection. Normal GRPO remains active for every query, complemented by
Frontier-Guided OPSD when a qualified frontier is available. Otherwise,
Guided GRPO expands the rubric-conditioned frontier, supplying improved

co-evolution loop. The algorithm pseudocode of CoCA is provided in
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B CoCa Algorithm ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation").

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) converts training-time privileged
information into dense token-level supervision on trajectories sampled
by the policy itself ([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [Li et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib11); [Han et al., 2026](#bib.bib15)). Let $`\xi`$ denote
information available only during training, such as a reference
solution ([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [Liu et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib14)), external feedback ([Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib10); [He et al., 2026a](#bib.bib12)), or a rubric ([Rezaei
et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7); [Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)). For a
query-only response $`y\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$, the student and
self-teacher share the same policy but receive different contexts. At
position $`t`$, their next-token distributions are
$`p_{t}^{\mathrm{S}}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})`$ and
$`p_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,\xi,y_{<t})`$,
respectively. The forward-KL objective is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OPSD}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x,\,y\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}\left[\frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{sg}[p_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}]\,\|\,p_{t}^{\mathrm{S}}\right)\right],
``` |  | (3) |

where $`\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]`$ stops gradients through the teacher
distribution. Evaluating both distributions on student-generated
prefixes aligns supervision with the policy’s current state
distribution. Unlike the sequence-level GRPO signal,
Eq. [3](#S2.E3 "In 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")
supplies a soft target at every token, distilling behaviors elicited by
$`\xi`$ into the query-only policy used at inference time.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts

Criterion-level Capability Diagnosis. For each query $`x`$ with rubric
$`\mathcal{R}_{x}=\{(c_{j},w_{j})\}_{j=1}^{J_{x}}`$, we independently
sample $`K_{\mathrm{N}}`$ query-only responses and $`K_{\mathrm{G}}`$
rubric-conditioned responses from the same pre-update policy ([Figure 2
(a)](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")),
forming the Normal Group $`\mathrm{N}`$ and Guided Group $`\mathrm{G}`$,
respectively:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{N}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\{y_{i}^{\mathrm{N}}\}_{i=1}^{K_{\mathrm{N}}},`$ | $`\displaystyle y_{i}^{\mathrm{N}}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x),`$ |  | (4) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\{y_{i}^{\mathrm{G}}\}_{i=1}^{K_{\mathrm{G}}},`$ | $`\displaystyle y_{i}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x,\mathcal{R}_{x}).`$ |  |  |

A fixed judge $`\mathcal{J}_{\phi}`$ then evaluates each response
against each criterion. Let
$`z_{i,j}^{v}=\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(x,y_{i}^{v},c_{j})\in\{0,1\}`$ indicate
whether $`y_{i}^{v}`$ meets the criterion $`c_{j}`$, where
$`v\in\{\mathrm{N},\mathrm{G}\}`$ and $`K_{v}`$ denotes the size of
group $`v`$. Notably, target achievement depends on the sign of
$`w_{j}`$: for a positive-weight criterion, it means satisfying the
criterion, whereas for a negative-weight criterion, it means avoiding
the undesirable behavior specified by the criterion. We therefore
convert judge verdicts into a polarity-invariant target-achievement
indicator:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
s_{i,j}^{v}=\begin{cases}z_{i,j}^{v},&w_{j}>0,\\
1-z_{i,j}^{v},&w_{j}<0,\end{cases}\qquad\bar{s}_{j}^{v}=\frac{1}{K_{v}}\sum_{i=1}^{K_{v}}s_{i,j}^{v}.
``` |  | (5) |

Here, $`\bar{s}_{j}^{v}`$ measures the empirical target-achievement rate
within group $`v`$. The achievement signature
$`(\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}},\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}})`$ induces the
following diagnosis at the criterion level:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\underbrace{\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}}=1\ \land\ \bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}}=1}_{\text{{Saturated}}},\quad\underbrace{\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}}=0\ \land\ \bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}}>0}_{\text{{Learnable}}},\quad\underbrace{\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}}=0\ \land\ \bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}}=0}_{\text{{Too-Hard}}},\quad\underbrace{0<\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}}<1}_{\text{{Discriminative}}},\quad\underbrace{\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}}=1\ \land\ \bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}}<1}_{\text{{Regressive}}}.
``` |  | (6) |

The five states distinguish criteria that are saturated (Saturated),
observed only under guidance (Learnable), unmet in both sampled groups
(Too-Hard), naturally variable among Normal responses (Discriminative),
or degraded under guidance (Regressive). This policy-relative diagnosis
routes each criterion to rubric calibration, frontier-based capability
learning, or direct group-relative optimization.

### 3.2 Capability-Aware Rubric Calibration

Difficulty Calibration. Inspired by curriculum learning ([Bengio et al.,
2009](#bib.bib17); [Narvekar et al., 2020](#bib.bib18)), we instantiate
a criterion-level curriculum by adapting each decision boundary to the
current policy rather than sequencing tasks ([Figure 2
(b)](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")).
Specifically, we strengthen a Saturated criterion to contract its
target-achievement set, and weaken a Too-Hard criterion to expand this
set toward a reachable intermediate target. The other criteria remain
unchanged. Using sampled responses, a polarity-aware rewriter revises
only the criterion text while preserving its intent, polarity, weight
$`w_{j}`$, and metadata. Denoting the resulting criterion by
$`c^{\prime}_{j}`$, the adapted rubric is
$`\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{x}=\{(c^{\prime}_{j},w_{j})\}_{j=1}^{J_{x}}`$.

Selective Rejudging and Reward Reconstruction. Calibration changes only
a subset of criteria, so we rejudge the modified criterion–response
pairs in both the Normal and Guided Groups while reusing all other
verdicts. Let $`\tilde{z}_{i,j}^{v}`$ denote the verdict under
$`\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{x}`$. For $`v\in\{\mathrm{N},\mathrm{G}\}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{z}_{i,j}^{v}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(x,y_{i}^{v},c^{\prime}_{j}),&c^{\prime}_{j}\neq c_{j},\\
z_{i,j}^{v},&c^{\prime}_{j}=c_{j}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (7) |

Aggregating these verdicts into the reward in
Eq. [1](#S2.E1 "In 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")
gives the reconstructed reward for each group:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{r}_{i}^{v}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{J_{x}}w_{j}\tilde{z}_{i,j}^{v}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,w_{j}>0\end{subarray}}^{J_{x}}w_{j}},\qquad v\in\{\mathrm{N},\mathrm{G}\}.
``` |  | (8) |

Both groups are therefore evaluated under the same policy-adapted
rubric, making their rewards directly comparable for subsequent frontier
selection and policy optimization.

### 3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation

Empirical Frontier Selection and Gating. We distill only capability
gains that address the diagnosed gaps and exceed the policy’s query-only
behavior by a prescribed margin. Let
$`\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,J_{x}\}:\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}}=0\land\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}}>0\}`$
denote the indices of the Learnable criteria, and let
$`\mathcal{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}=\{(c^{\prime}_{j},w_{j}):j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\}`$
be the corresponding sub-rubric. We also obtain the post-calibration
target-achievement indicator $`\tilde{s}_{i,j}^{v}`$ by replacing
$`z_{i,j}^{v}`$ with $`\tilde{z}_{i,j}^{v}`$ in
Eq. [5](#S3.E5 "In 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation").
The highest-reward Normal response defines the Current Frontier:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
i_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}=\underset{1\leq i\leq K_{\mathrm{N}}}{\arg\max}\;\tilde{r}_{i}^{\mathrm{N}},\qquad y_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}=y_{i_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}}^{\mathrm{N}}.
``` |  | (9) |

In contrast, a Guided response is eligible only if it achieves every
Learnable criterion. We define the set of eligible Guided response
indices $`\mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}=\begin{cases}\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,K_{\mathrm{G}}\}:\tilde{s}_{i,j}^{\mathrm{G}}=1\ \forall j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\right\},&\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\neq\emptyset,\\
\emptyset,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (10) |

When $`\mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}\neq\emptyset`$, the eligible
response with the highest full-rubric reward becomes the Guided
Frontier:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
i_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}=\underset{i\in\mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}}{\arg\max}\;\tilde{r}_{i}^{\mathrm{G}},\qquad y_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}=y_{i_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}}^{\mathrm{G}}.
``` |  | (11) |

Denoting the frontier reward gap by $`\Delta_{x}`$, we define the
distillation gate as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g(x)=\mathbbm{1}\!\left[\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\neq\emptyset\ \land\ \mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}\neq\emptyset\ \land\ \Delta_{x}>\delta\right],\qquad\Delta_{x}=\tilde{r}_{i_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}}^{\mathrm{G}}-\tilde{r}_{i_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}}^{\mathrm{N}},
``` |  | (12) |

where $`\delta\geq 0`$ is a minimum improvement margin. This gate admits
only Guided Frontiers that satisfy all Learnable targets and strictly
improve the full-rubric reward.

Frontier-conditioned Token-level Distillation. For each active query
with $`g(x)=1`$, the self-teacher receives the Learnable criteria in
$`\mathcal{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}`$ together with the Guided Frontier
$`y_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$, whereas the student receives only $`x`$. Both
share the current policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ and are teacher-forced along
the Current Frontier:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{t}^{\mathrm{S}}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,y_{x,<t}^{\mathrm{C}}),\qquad p_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,\mathcal{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}},y_{x}^{\mathrm{G}},y_{x,<t}^{\mathrm{C}}).
``` |  | (13) |

We then minimize the forward KL divergence at each response position
([Figure 2
(c)](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Frontier\text{-}OPSD}}(\theta;x)=\frac{1}{|y_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}|}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{sg}[p_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}]\,\|\,p_{t}^{\mathrm{S}}\right).
``` |  | (14) |

Crucially, $`y_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ provides context rather than an
imitation target: both distributions are aligned on
$`y_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}`$, yielding local corrections to query-only
behavior. For efficiency, we approximate the full-vocabulary KL with the
teacher’s top-$`k_{\mathrm{tok}}`$ tokens and one tail-mass bin, where
$`k_{\mathrm{tok}}`$ is the retained support size.

### 3.4 Guided Frontier Expansion and Joint Optimization

Group-wise Relative Optimization and Frontier Expansion. Although
evaluated under the same adapted rubric, the two groups are generated
from different contexts and can exhibit distinct reward distributions;
we therefore compute relative advantages independently within each
group:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\overline{\tilde{r}}^{\,v}=\frac{1}{K_{v}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_{v}}\tilde{r}_{\ell}^{v},\qquad\widehat{A}_{i}^{v}=\frac{\tilde{r}_{i}^{v}-\overline{\tilde{r}}^{\,v}}{\sigma_{\tilde{r}}^{v}+\epsilon},\qquad v\in\{\mathrm{N},\mathrm{G}\},
``` |  | (15) |

where $`\sigma_{\tilde{r}}^{v}`$ is the reward standard deviation within
Group $`v`$. Applying
Eq. [2](#S2.E2 "In 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")
with $`K=K_{v}`$, using query-only context $`x`$ for $`\mathrm{N}`$ and
rubric-conditioned context $`(x,\mathcal{R}_{x})`$ for $`\mathrm{G}`$,
yields the per-query losses
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{N}}(\theta;x)`$ and
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\theta;x)`$. Normal GRPO
remains active for every query, whereas Guided GRPO is masked by
$`1-g(x)`$, so all $`K_{\mathrm{G}}`$ Guided responses contribute only
when $`g(x)=0`$. This restriction directs Guided GRPO toward queries
without a qualified frontier.

Joint Training Objective. As shown in [Figure 2
(d)](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
with coefficients $`\alpha`$ and $`\beta`$ for Frontier-OPSD and Guided
GRPO, respectively, the joint objective is defined as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{joint}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{N}}(\theta;x)+\alpha g(x)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Frontier\text{-}OPSD}}(\theta;x)+\beta\bigl(1-g(x)\bigr)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\theta;x)\right].
``` |  | (16) |

Here, $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{N}}`$ improves query-only
behavior, while the gated branches use
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ to build a qualified
frontier when $`g(x)=0`$ and
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Frontier\text{-}OPSD}}`$ to transfer it when
$`g(x)=1`$. Guided GRPO then yields stronger rubric-conditioned
rollouts, which refresh capability diagnosis, recalibrate the rubric,
and supply improved frontiers to subsequent OPSD, closing the
policy–rubric co-evolution loop.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Model Architecture and Datasets. We evaluate our CoCA on Qwen3-4B,
Qwen3-8B ([Yang et al., 2025](#bib.bib19)), and Gemma-3-4B-it ([Team et
al., 2025](#bib.bib20)). We consider training and evaluation across
three domains: medical, science, and psychology. For medical, we draw
$`4,000`$ training examples from HealthBench ([Arora et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21)), excluding HealthBench-Hard, and evaluate on
HealthBench-Hard, RaR-Medicine ([Gunjal et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)), and
LLMEval-Med ([Zhang et al., 2025](#bib.bib22)). For science, we randomly
sample $`4,000`$ ResearchQA ([Yifei et al., 2026](#bib.bib23)) examples
for training and evaluate on its validation split and the RaR-Science
test set ([Gunjal et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)). For psychology, we
construct LLM-generated rubrics for CPsyCoun ([Zhang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib24)), randomly select $`2,400`$ examples for training,
reserve 500 held-out examples for in-domain testing, and evaluate
cross-dataset generalization on HeartBench ([Liu et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib25)). We also assess Out-of-Domain (OOD)
instruction-following generalization on InfoBench ([Qin et al.,
2024](#bib.bib28)), FollowBench ([Jiang et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)), and
AdvancedIF ([He et al., 2026b](#bib.bib30)). Detailed descriptions and
metrics for each dataset are provided in
[Appendix C.1](#A3.SS1 "C.1 Open-ended Generation Datasets ‣ Appendix C Detailed experimental settings ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation").

Baseline. We compare our CoCA against five baselines covering
alternative reward construction and distinct intervention points in
rubric-based RL. LLM-Rank ([Liusie et al., 2024](#bib.bib27)) is a
rubric-free GRPO baseline that assigns relative ranks within each group,
thereby ensuring reward variation. RaR ([Gunjal et al.,
2026](#bib.bib2)) serves as the standard rubric-based GRPO baseline.
RuscaRL ([Zhou et al., 2026](#bib.bib3)), Online Rubrics ([Rezaei et
al., 2026b](#bib.bib6)), and RGSD ([Rezaei et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7))
represent guided rollout exploration, online rubric evolution, and
self-distillation, respectively.

### 4.2 Overall Performance

CoCA demonstrates robust effectiveness and generalizability across
backbones and domains. As shown in
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.4 Guided Frontier Expansion and Joint Optimization ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
CoCA achieves the highest mean score for Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, and
Gemma-3-4B-it ($`61.87\%`$, $`66.41\%`$, and $`56.08\%`$), improving
over standard rubric-based GRPO (RaR) by $`+2.51\%`$, $`+2.43\%`$, and
$`+3.10\%`$, respectively. It also outperforms RuscaRL, Online Rubrics,
and RGSD on most individual benchmarks. Together, these results indicate
that coordinating capability diagnosis, rubric calibration, guided
frontier expansion, and selective transfer is more effective than
repairing individual stages of rubric-based RL in isolation. The weaker
LLM-Rank results suggest that holistic group rankings discard useful
criterion-level structure, whereas RGSD’s weak results are consistent
with recent findings that unfiltered or standalone OPSD can become
unstable under noisy teacher signals and teacher–student competence
mismatch ([Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib13); [Kim et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib42)).

CoCA couples stronger rubric-conditioned capability with robust OOD
generalization. To examine whether rubric-guided gains transfer to
query-only instruction following beyond the training domain, we evaluate
both rubric-conditioned capability and OOD generalization. As shown in
[Table 2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
when the full ground-truth rubric is included in each HealthBench-Hard
prompt, CoCA substantially improves over the base model across all
backbones (e.g., $`+17.38\%`$ for Qwen3-4B). It also consistently
surpasses RuscaRL by $`+1.89\%\sim+4.80\%`$ Across the OOD benchmarks,
CoCA consistently outperforms RuscaRL and generally improves over the
corresponding base models, whereas RuscaRL degrades OOD performance with
drops ranging from $`-0.22\%`$ to $`-4.15\%`$. This contrast
demonstrates that CoCA enables broadly transferable rubric-guided
learning.

## 5 Analysis and Discussions

In this section, we mainly investigate: (1) What does the distribution
of rubric capability states reveal about the policy’s evolving
capability boundary; (2) Which capability-state transitions toward
internalization are more pronounced under CoCA than RaR; and (3) How
does CoCA affect training dynamics and inference-time scaling? We
further discuss rubric discrimination in
[Appendix E.4](#A5.SS4 "E.4 Rubric Discrimination and Reward Signals ‣ Appendix E Additional Analysis ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation").

Capability-state distributions reveal an evolving policy frontier.
[Figure 3](#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")
shows that, relative to the base models, CoCA shifts the proportions of
Saturated and Learnable by $`+14.9\%\sim+20.5\%`$ and
$`-8.1\%\sim-5.9\%`$, respectively, across all backbones. The Too-Hard
and Regressive proportions also decrease consistently, indicating fewer
unreachable targets and guidance-induced failures. This separation is
most pronounced on Qwen3-8B, where CoCA changes Saturated and Regressive
by $`+18.00\%`$ and $`-2.80\%`$, compared with $`+12.70\%`$ and
$`-0.70\%`$ for RaR. This redistribution suggests a narrowing gap
between elicited and default capability, but marginal state counts alone
cannot distinguish internalization from degradation, motivating a
transition-level analysis.

CoCA promotes capability internalization across backbones.
[Table 5](#S4.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")
shows that CoCA exceeds RaR on every internalization transition for all
three backbones. The gains are particularly pronounced on Qwen3-8B,
where Learnable $`{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.1992,0.1992}\Longrightarrow}`$
Saturated and Discriminative
$`{\color[rgb]{0.0977,0.6484,0.4609}\Longrightarrow}`$ Saturated
increase by $`+7.06\%`$ and $`+10.53\%`$, respectively. CoCA also
suppresses degradation: relative to RaR, Learnable
$`{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.1992,0.1992}\Longrightarrow}`$ Too-Hard changes
by $`-0.51\%`$, $`-0.07\%`$, and $`-1.11\%`$ across Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B,
and Gemma-3-4B-it, while Saturated
$`{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.1992,0.1992}\Longrightarrow}`$ Others changes by
$`-0.65\%`$ and $`-0.83\%`$ on Qwen3-4B and Gemma-3-4B-it. These
transition patterns demonstrate that CoCA more effectively internalizes
guided capabilities while promoting more stable behavior.

CoCA couples efficient learning with sustained exploration and a higher
sampling ceiling. For Qwen3-8B, as shown in
[Figure 4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
CoCA maintains higher actor entropy than RaR over most of training,
consistent with broader exploration and less premature policy
contraction. At step $`250`$, CoCA reaches $`42.19\%`$, slightly
exceeding RaR at step $`500`$ ($`41.88\%`$) with approximately
$`2.0\times`$ fewer training steps. Its Best-of-$`K`$ advantage over RaR
further remains between $`+3.85\%`$ and $`+4.45\%`$ for
$`K\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}`$. Overall, these results suggest that CoCA reaches
comparable performance in fewer training steps while sustaining
exploration and expanding the attainable capability frontier.

## 6 Related Work

Rubric as Reward in RL. Open-ended generation ([Arora et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21); [Yifei et al., 2026](#bib.bib23); [Liu et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib25)) lacks deterministic verifiers, making scalar or
pairwise rewards too coarse to capture multidimensional response
quality ([Chen et al., 2026](#bib.bib38)). Rubric-based RL instead
decomposes evaluation into interpretable criteria, with RaR ([Gunjal et
al., 2026](#bib.bib2)) establishing this formulation for policy
optimization beyond verifiable domains. Subsequent work improves
different stages of this pipeline: OpenRubrics ([Liu et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib31)) scales rubric generation from contrastive
preferences, Rubric-ARM ([Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib32)) jointly
optimizes the rubric generator and judge, and RuscaRL ([Zhou et al.,
2026](#bib.bib3)) uses rubric criteria to scaffold policy exploration.
To overcome static evaluation targets, DR Tulu ([Shao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib33)) and Online Rubrics ([Rezaei et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib6)) update criteria from policy-generated responses.
Although effective, these methods construct or update criteria from
static annotations or response contrasts without tracking the policy’s
evolving ability to fulfill the task objective, risking stylistic drift.
In contrast, CoCA adapts criterion difficulty to the policy’s evolving
capabilities while preserving the original evaluation intent (further
verified in
[Appendix E.3](#A5.SS3 "E.3 Rubric Intent Preservation ‣ Appendix E Additional Analysis ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")).

On-Policy Distillation and Self-Distillation. On-policy distillation
trains a student on its own rollouts under dense teacher supervision,
reducing the train–inference mismatch of off-policy
distillation ([Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib36); [Gu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib39); [Yang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib40)). OPSD ([Zhao et
al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [Wang et al., 2026c](#bib.bib41)) removes the
need for a separate teacher by conditioning the same model on privileged
information and matching its token distributions along student
trajectories. Recent variants instantiate the privileged information
with rich environmental feedback ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib10)),
binary rewards and self-revision ([He et al., 2026a](#bib.bib12)), or
explicit rubrics ([Rezaei et al., 2026a](#bib.bib7)). Beyond logit
matching, ROPD ([Fang et al., 2026](#bib.bib16)) induces rubrics from
teacher–student contrasts to score student rollouts, enabling black-box
on-policy distillation without teacher logits. However, sample-level
teacher signals may overlook whether criterion-level gains are
elicitable and transferable, risking weak or overly difficult
supervision. We instead transfer capability-qualified frontiers and
jointly expand their source, coupling selective distillation with
capability-aware rubric calibration. CriPO ([Xia et al.,
2026](#bib.bib13)) is a concurrent study mostly related to ours, which
combines criterion-directed self-distillation with token-level advantage
flipping in GRPO; in contrast, CoCA uses dual-group capability diagnosis
to drive closed-loop policy–rubric co-evolution. Further Comparison is
provided in
[Appendix A](#A1 "Appendix A Extended Related Work and Comparison ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation").

## 7 Conclusion

In this work, we propose CoCA, a capability-aware framework for jointly
evolving rubrics and policies in open-ended generation. In contrast to
prior methods that treat rubric adaptation, guided exploration, and
knowledge transfer in isolation, CoCA unifies these processes within a
capability-aware policy–rubric co-evolution loop. This closed loop keeps
the optimization objective aligned with the policy’s evolving capability
frontier while selectively transferring qualified guided gains into
query-only behavior. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoCA
consistently improves performance and out-of-domain generalization
across diverse tasks and models. Further analyses reveal complementary
effects among CoCA’s components and more favorable capability-state
transitions, indicating that guided gains are progressively consolidated
into stable query-only behavior.

### Ethics statement

This work studies post-training methods for improving rubric-guided
learning in open-ended domains. We use existing publicly available
research benchmarks and pretrained models, and do not collect new
human-subject data or attempt to identify individuals. Because our
experiments include medical and psychological content, model outputs may
contain inaccurate, biased, or otherwise harmful advice; the resulting
models are intended only for research and should not be used for
diagnosis, treatment, crisis intervention, or psychological counseling.
Rubric generation and LLM-based judging can inherit biases from source
data and evaluator models, while adaptive optimization may amplify
misspecified criteria or judge artifacts. Benchmark improvements
therefore do not establish real-world reliability or clinical safety.
Deployment-oriented use would require domain-expert review, demographic
and cultural bias audits, privacy assessment, and application-specific
safety evaluation.

Algorithm 1: CoCA: Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability
Awareness

 

0:  Training set $`\mathcal{D}=\{(x,\mathcal{R}_{x})\}`$ with
$`\mathcal{R}_{x}=\{(c_{j},w_{j})\}_{j=1}^{J_{x}}`$; policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$; fixed judge $`\mathcal{J}_{\phi}`$

0:  Rollout group sizes $`K_{\mathrm{N}},K_{\mathrm{G}}`$; frontier
margin $`\delta\geq 0`$; loss coefficients $`\alpha`$ (Frontier-OPSD)
and $`\beta`$ (Guided GRPO); retained-token count $`k_{\mathrm{tok}}`$

0:  Optimized query-only policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$

1:  repeat

2:   Set $`\theta_{\mathrm{old}}\leftarrow\theta`$, sample a mini-batch
$`\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{D}`$, and initialize
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}\leftarrow 0`$

3:   for $`(x,\mathcal{R}_{x})\in\mathcal{B}`$ do

4:    $`\blacktriangleright`$ Capability
Probing (Sec. [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"))

5:    Independently sample $`\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{N}}`$
($`K_{\mathrm{N}}`$ responses) and $`\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$
($`K_{\mathrm{G}}`$ responses) via
Eq. [4](#S3.E4 "In 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

6:    Evaluate $`z_{i,j}^{v}=\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(x,y_{i}^{v},c_{j})`$ for
$`v\in\{\mathrm{N},\mathrm{G}\}`$, $`i=1,\ldots,K_{v}`$, and
$`j=1,\ldots,J_{x}`$

7:    Compute $`s_{i,j}^{v}`$ and $`\bar{s}_{j}^{v}`$ via
Eq. [5](#S3.E5 "In 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

8:    Diagnose each $`c_{j}`$ from
$`(\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{N}},\bar{s}_{j}^{\mathrm{G}})`$ via
Eq. [6](#S3.E6 "In 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
yielding $`d_{j}`$

9:    $`\blacktriangleright`$ Rubric
Calibration (Sec. [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Capability-Aware Rubric Calibration ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"))

10:    for $`j=1,\ldots,J_{x}`$ do

11:     if $`d_{j}=\textit{Saturated}`$ then

12:
     $`c^{\prime}_{j}\leftarrow\operatorname{Strengthen}(x,c_{j},w_{j},\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{N}},\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}})`$

13:     else if $`d_{j}=\textit{Too-Hard}`$ then

14:
     $`c^{\prime}_{j}\leftarrow\operatorname{Weaken}(x,c_{j},w_{j},\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{N}},\mathcal{Y}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}})`$

15:     else

16:      $`c^{\prime}_{j}\leftarrow c_{j}`$

17:     end if

18:    end for

19:    Form
$`\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{x}=\{(c^{\prime}_{j},w_{j})\}_{j=1}^{J_{x}}`$;
obtain $`\tilde{z}_{i,j}^{v}`$ by selectively rejudging modified pairs
via
Eq. [7](#S3.E7 "In 3.2 Capability-Aware Rubric Calibration ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

20:    Compute $`\tilde{s}_{i,j}^{v}`$ using $`\tilde{z}_{i,j}^{v}`$ in
Eq. [5](#S3.E5 "In 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
and $`\tilde{r}_{i}^{v}`$ via
Eq. [8](#S3.E8 "In 3.2 Capability-Aware Rubric Calibration ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

21:    $`\blacktriangleright`$ Frontier Selection and
Gating (Sec. [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"))

22:    Set
$`\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\leftarrow\{j\in\{1,\ldots,J_{x}\}:d_{j}=\textit{Learnable}\}`$
and
$`\mathcal{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\leftarrow\{(c^{\prime}_{j},w_{j}):j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\}`$

23:    Select $`i_{x}^{\mathrm{C}},y_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}`$ via
Eq. [9](#S3.E9 "In 3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation");
construct $`\mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ via
Eq. [10](#S3.E10 "In 3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

24:    Initialize $`g(x)\leftarrow 0`$

25:    if $`\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\mathrm{L}}\neq\emptyset`$ and
$`\mathcal{E}_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}\neq\emptyset`$ then

26:     Select $`i_{x}^{\mathrm{G}},y_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}`$ via
Eq. [11](#S3.E11 "In 3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

27:     Set
$`\Delta_{x}\leftarrow\tilde{r}_{i_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}}^{\mathrm{G}}-\tilde{r}_{i_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}}^{\mathrm{N}}`$
and $`g(x)\leftarrow\mathbbm{1}[\Delta_{x}>\delta]`$

28:    end if

29:    $`\blacktriangleright`$ Joint
Optimization (Sec. [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Guided Frontier Expansion and Joint Optimization ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"))

Eq. [15](#S3.E15 "In 3.4 Guided Frontier Expansion and Joint Optimization ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

31:    Compute $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{N}}(\theta;x)`$
over all $`K_{\mathrm{N}}`$ Normal responses with context $`x`$

32:    if $`g(x)=1`$ then

33:     Evaluate $`p_{t}^{\mathrm{S}}`$ and detached
$`p_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}`$ on prefixes of $`y_{x}^{\mathrm{C}}`$ via
Eq. [13](#S3.E13 "In 3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")

$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Frontier\text{-}OPSD}}(\theta;x)`$ via

using the teacher’s top-$`k_{\mathrm{tok}}`$ tokens plus one tail-mass
bin

    $`\mathcal{L}_{x}\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{N}}(\theta;x)+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Frontier\text{-}OPSD}}(\theta;x)`$

36:    else

37:     Compute $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\theta;x)`$
over all $`K_{\mathrm{G}}`$ Guided responses with their original context
$`(x,\mathcal{R}_{x})`$

    $`\mathcal{L}_{x}\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{N}}(\theta;x)+\beta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\theta;x)`$

39:    end if

40:

41:   end for

Capability diagnosis as a shared control signal. EvoRubrics and
DynamicRubric co-evolve reward construction with policy learning ([Ding
et al., 2026](#bib.bib8); [Wang et al., 2026a](#bib.bib34)), and CriPO
uses criterion states to direct distillation and advantage
correction ([Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib13)). CoCA instead derives a
shared control signal by contrasting criterion achievement between
query-only and rubric-conditioned rollouts of the same policy. The
resulting diagnosis simultaneously determines which criteria to
recalibrate and which guidance-elicited targets a frontier must satisfy.
Crucially, this separates behaviors that guidance can elicit from
targets unmet in both sampled groups, rather than reading query-only
failure as evidence of absent capability. Frontier eligibility and the
full-rubric reward advantage then determine the choice between
self-distillation and guided optimization, with Normal GRPO active
throughout
(Eqs. [6](#S3.E6 "In 3.1 Capability Probing via Dual-Group Rollouts ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
[12](#S3.E12 "In 3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
and [16](#S3.E16 "In 3.4 Guided Frontier Expansion and Joint Optimization ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")).
As the policy evolves, renewed diagnosis updates both calibration
decisions and learning targets, linking reward adaptation, capability
expansion, and internalization in a closed feedback loop.

## Appendix B CoCa Algorithm

Algorithm Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for
Open-Ended Generation summarizes the complete CoCa training loop. Each
iteration uses dual-group rollouts to diagnose policy capability,
calibrates the rubric against the resulting empirical frontier, and then
routes each query to either frontier transfer or frontier expansion
while retaining Normal GRPO. Notably, the rewritten criteria serve only
to reconstruct rewards for the current rollout groups and are not
carried over to subsequent iterations; each revisit to a query starts
from its original rubric.

## Appendix E Additional Analysis

### E.1 OPSD Activation and Capability Internalization

Figure 5: Query-level OPSD activation ratio during training on Qwen3-8B.

CoCA adapts distillation to the policy’s evolving capabilities. As shown
in
[Figure 5](#A5.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ E.1 OPSD Activation and Capability Internalization ‣ Appendix E Additional Analysis ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
OPSD activation generally decreases across all three datasets during
training, with a particularly pronounced decline on CPsyCoun. Since
Frontier-Guided OPSD requires Learnable criteria and a superior guided
frontier (see
[Section 3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Frontier-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")),
this trend supports progressive internalization of rubric guidance into
query-only behavior, reducing the fraction of queries that require
distillation. The higher sustained activation on HealthBench and
ResearchQA indicates that guided rollouts continue to expose learnable
requirements, keeping supervision focused on remaining capability gaps.
Together, these dynamics illustrate how CoCA adjusts distillation
coverage to the policy’s learning progress, reducing its reliance on
privileged guidance as capabilities develop while retaining targeted
supervision where guidance remains useful.

### E.2 Training-Time Overhead

As shown in
[Figure 6](#A5.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ E.1 OPSD Activation and Capability Internalization ‣ Appendix E Additional Analysis ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation"),
reward computation accounts for a substantial share of training time in
rubric-based RL, partly due to API latency and concurrency limits.
Compared with fixed-rubric methods (e.g., RaR ([Gunjal et al.,
2026](#bib.bib2))), LLM-driven rubric evolution paradigms (e.g. Online
Rubrics ([Rezaei et al., 2026b](#bib.bib6)) and Ours) further increases
this cost through rubric generation or rewriting, validation, and
rescoring. Notably, CoCA (Ours) achieves a higher score at step $`250`$
than RaR at step $`500`$
([Figure 4(b)](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.3 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")),
while using $`-10.1\%`$ ($`22.50`$ vs. $`25.03`$ hours) fewer training
time. Besides, CoCA combines adaptive, selective rubric calibration with
finer-grained guidance for policy optimization; its training-time
estimate is $`-14.0\%`$ lower than that of Online Rubrics ($`51.37`$ vs.
$`59.71`$ hours). Nevertheless, mitigating this API bottleneck remains
an important future direction for scaling rubric-based RL.

### E.3 Rubric Intent Preservation

We examine whether CoCA’s criterion rewrites preserve the original
evaluation intent while adjusting requirements in the intended
direction. For each dataset, we sample $`200`$ adopted rewrite events
from existing training logs, stratified by original weight polarity
(i.e., positive or negative) and operation (i.e., strengthen or weaken),
and use an LLM auditor (i.e., Qwen3.7-max) to compare each
original–rewritten criterion pair in its query context, revealing the
operation only when assessing adjustment direction. Intent Preservation
Rate (IPR) measures the proportion of rewrites judged to preserve the
original evaluation target and reward/penalty direction. Difficulty
Direction Accuracy (DDA) measures the proportion judged to change the
textual requirement in the intended, polarity-aware strengthen/weaken
direction; both metrics are weighted by each stratum’s frequency in the
logs.

## Appendix F Case Study

[Figure 8](#A5.F8 "Figure 8 ‣ E.4 Rubric Discrimination and Reward Signals ‣ Appendix E Additional Analysis ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")
illustrates CoCA’s criterion-level decisions on a HealthBench query
about screening and monitoring. The location-seeking and documentation
criteria are both unmet by query-only responses, but rubric guidance
elicits advice on documenting assessments while the location-seeking
criterion remains unmet. CoCA relaxes the former from requesting the
clinician’s location to acknowledging regional differences, while
strengthening the saturated (Saturated) screening criterion to require
an explicit repeat-screening recommendation. The remaining criteria are
preserved: Learnable criteria identify potential transfer targets,
Discriminative criteria already separate responses, and failures on the
Regressive criterion under guidance highlight the need to further
strengthen the policy’s rubric-conditioned capability. Together, these
decisions illustrate how CoCA adapts supervision to the policy’s
capability boundary without indiscriminately expanding the rubric.

## Appendix G Limitation

While CoCA advances capability-aware policy–rubric co-evolution, several
limitations remain. First, capability diagnosis and frontier selection
rely on finite rollout groups and LLM judgments, making them sensitive
to sampling variability and evaluator errors. Similarly, the quality of
rubric calibration depends on the rewriter’s capabilities. Second,
dual-group sampling, rubric revision, and self-distillation add per-step
overhead, so fewer training steps need not yield proportional wall-clock
savings; API-bound reward computation remains a practical bottleneck
([Appendix E.2](#A5.SS2 "E.2 Training-Time Overhead ‣ Appendix E Additional Analysis ‣ Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation")).
Finally, this work is limited to text-generation benchmarks, and
extending the framework to multimodal generation or interactive
environments with delayed feedback requires further validation.
````
