# AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision

Yizhuo Li ^(†)^(†)thanks: This manuscript is a rewritten,
single-environment condensation of our earlier technical report
(arXiv:2608.05987v1). The ALFWorld results reported here are reproduced
from that work. Affiliation: Beijing Institute of Technology

###### Abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards ordinarily spreads a
single advantage over a whole trajectory, a granularity too coarse to
isolate the few decisions on which success in a long, multi-turn
interaction actually turns. Privileged self-distillation supplies denser
supervision, but it leaves unanswered how a set of local teacher–student
discrepancies should be converted into credit along a sequence. We
introduce AgentOPSD, a critic-free scheme that assigns credit
recursively, one interaction turn at a time. Inside a turn, the
token-level log-probability gaps between a skill-conditioned
self-teacher and the student are summed, and that sum acts as evidence
in a recursive Bayesian update carried out in log-odds space; the
resulting revisions reallocate the outcome-derived advantage across
turns through a multiplier that is bounded and preserves its sign. With
Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B as policy backbones on ALFWorld, the method
improves consistently on GRPO and on self-distillation baselines, and
attains $`89.1\%`$ success at the 7B scale. Ablations attribute the gain
to aggregating evidence at turn boundaries and to making the belief
revision depend on history, rather than to the local gap by itself.

## 1 Introduction

Teaching large language models to act as agents has become a principal
avenue for extending their capacity on difficult tasks ([Guo et al.,
2025](#bib.bib7); [Team et al., 2025](#bib.bib11); [Yang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib10); [Comanici et al., 2025](#bib.bib14); [Team et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib15)). The setting is not static single-turn reasoning: an
agent inhabits a partially observable world and keeps acting, choosing
from what it currently sees and then observing the world’s response
before deciding again ([Shen et al., 2023](#bib.bib16); [Shi et al.,
2025](#bib.bib13); [Jimenez et al., 2023](#bib.bib17)). Verification,
moreover, often becomes possible only once a trajectory has finished, so
a training procedure must infer the role of one intermediate step from a
single sparse terminal signal. Yet steps inside a trajectory are far
from interchangeable: a successful run can contain redundant, spurious
or misleading actions, and a failing run can contain sensible reasoning.

Group-relative policy optimization, with GRPO ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib8); [Yu et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)) being the canonical
instance alongside its agentic descendants ([Dong et al.,
2025](#bib.bib18); [Feng et al., 2025](#bib.bib6)), derives a single
advantage for the whole trajectory from the outcome reward and applies
it unchanged at every position. Credit that is spread this flatly cannot
separate a handful of pivotal decisions from routine operations around
them, and the distortion worsens as turns accumulate. Per-turn credit is
therefore a prerequisite both for locating decisions that actually drive
the outcome and for supervising long horizons at finer granularity.

Denser token-level supervision has been pursued along a separate line.
In on-policy distillation ([Ye et al., 2026](#bib.bib19); [Yang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib20); [Team et al., 2026a](#bib.bib22)), a student is
trained under a teacher on rollouts of its own, while the
_self_-distillation variants of this idea ([Zhao et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9); [He et al., 2026](#bib.bib21)) do away with any
separate teacher by conditioning one and the same policy on privileged
information that exists only during training ([Lu et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib3)). Reinforcement learning has likewise used such
signals as an auxiliary supervision source.

Two mismatches appear once OPSD meets agentic reinforcement learning.
The first concerns granularity: token-level signals sit awkwardly
against an interaction in which many tokens jointly constitute one
action and the environment reacts only when a turn ends. The second
concerns memory: even step-aware methods score each turn
separately ([Zhang et al., 2026](#bib.bib27)), leaving earlier evidence
unused. What must be solved, then, is how local OPSD signals become
turn-level credit that depends on history.

We begin from the position that what should govern a turn’s credit is
not its local signal taken on its own, but how far that signal moves the
estimated probability of eventual success. Precision comes from reading
the per-turn self-distillation gap as newly arrived evidence driving a
Bayesian belief update ([Åström, 1965](#bib.bib36); [Kaelbling et al.,
1998](#bib.bib35)). The belief state is then the probability of eventual
task success given the history so far.

This observation leads us to AgentOPSD (_agentic on-policy
self-distillation_), a method that assigns credit turn by turn in
long-horizon agents. Token-level teacher–student log-probability gaps
are first pooled by AgentOPSD into turn-level evidence. A Bayesian
belief state, initialized at the mean group success rate, is then
revised recursively turn after turn in log-odds space, with neither
extra rollouts nor a learned critic required. The verifier fixes the
direction of optimization globally, while the belief updates
redistribute the trajectory-level learning signal over individual turns.
AgentOPSD is evaluated on ALFWorld ([Shridhar et al., 2020](#bib.bib5))
at two model scales, and
Figure [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")
shows it to outperform GRPO and strong self-distillation baselines
consistently. The ablations point to two sources of the improvement:
pooling token-level signals at the turn boundaries that coincide with
environment transitions, and reframing a gap that stands on its own as a
revision of belief that recurs along the trajectory.

We summarize the contributions of this work below:

- •
  Credit at the level of a turn is cast as the amount by which one turn
  revises the estimated chance that the run ends in success. Carrying
  the recursion in log-odds space ties the evidence of one turn to a
  Bayesian update applied repeatedly, and makes plain that a
  self-distillation gap on its own does not amount to sequential credit.
- •
  We propose AgentOPSD, which collapses teacher–student log-probability
  gaps at the token level into turn-level evidence that lines up with
  environment transitions, then carries it recursively through the
  belief over trajectory success; neither extra rollouts nor a learned
  critic is required.
- •
  On ALFWorld, at two model scales, experiments together with ablations
  show AgentOPSD to outperform GRPO and strong self-distillation
  baselines consistently, and the ablations confirm that aggregating at
  turn boundaries and revising belief by recursion bring benefits that
  are complementary.

## 2 Methodology

### 2.1 Task, Interaction, and Notation

Each episode opens with a task $`x`$ and an initial observation
$`o_{0}`$, so that the interaction starts from the state
$`s_{1}=(x,o_{0})`$. At turn $`k`$ the policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ emits an
action conditioned on the interaction history $`s_{k}`$ that the agent
can currently see; we denote that action by $`a_{k}`$, its $`t`$-th
token by $`y_{k,t}`$, and the token count of the action by $`L_{k}`$.
The observation $`o_{k}`$ returned by the environment then extends the
history through $`s_{k+1}=(s_{k},a_{k},o_{k})`$. Chaining these steps
over $`K`$ turns produces a trajectory
$`\boldsymbol{\tau}=(s_{1},a_{1},o_{1},\ldots,s_{K},a_{K},o_{K})`$, to
which a verifier attaches a binary outcome reward
$`R(\boldsymbol{\tau})`$.

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
a_{k}=(y_{k,1},\ldots,y_{k,L_{k}})\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid s_{k}),
``` |  | (1) |

Every task is handled by drawing $`G`$ trajectories under group-relative
policy optimization, from whose rewards a sequence-level advantage is
derived. Throughout, the index $`i`$ runs over these $`G`$ samples,
$`\bar{R}`$ and $`\widehat{\sigma}_{R}`$ are the mean reward of the
group and its standard deviation, and the small positive constant
$`\epsilon_{0}`$ recurs everywhere as a guard against division by zero
and against unbounded log-odds. The resulting advantage
$`A_{\mathrm{seq}}^{(i)}`$ is handed to all tokens of trajectory $`i`$
in exactly the same form, so no distinction of credit between turns is
ever drawn.

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{\mathrm{seq}}^{(i)}=\frac{R^{(i)}-\bar{R}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{R}+\epsilon_{0}},\qquad\bar{R}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^{G}R^{(j)}.
``` |  | (2) |

### 2.2 Turning Outcome Influence into Bayesian Turn Evidence

The counterfactual influence of turn $`k`$ could be quantified head-on
only by averaging the outcome reward over all continuations that might
follow $`a_{k}`$, and for interactions spanning many turns that
marginalization is hopeless. Our stance is instead retrospective and
evidential. Let $`C`$ denote the event in which the trajectory ends
successfully. An action that advances the task should occur more often
inside successful behavior than inside unsuccessful behavior, and Bayes’
rule turns this observation into a change of belief about $`C`$,
captured by a likelihood ratio on the action side ([Åström,
1965](#bib.bib36); [Kaelbling et al., 1998](#bib.bib35)):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{logit}p(C\mid s_{k},a_{k})-\operatorname{logit}p(C\mid s_{k})`$ |  | (3) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\log\frac{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},C)}{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},\neg C)}.`$ |  |  |

The map $`\operatorname{logit}(u)=\log\frac{u}{1-u}`$ converts a
probability into log-odds. What sits on the right is the ideal Bayes
factor ([Kass and Raftery, 1995](#bib.bib33)), i.e., the ratio between
the likelihood of $`a_{k}`$ under success and its likelihood under
failure; its sign tells us at once whether the turn adds to the case for
eventual success or subtracts from it.

Neither outcome-conditional behavioral distribution can be inspected
directly, so the Bayesian evidence has to be recovered after the fact.
We obtain it from a per-turn self-distillation contrast that is actually
computable: a privileged self-teacher, associated with success, is set
against the ordinary student policy. The two share the parameters
$`\theta`$ and both score the same action, as sampled from the
student ([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). Such a contrast supplies a
tractable stand-in for a belief update that would otherwise stay out of
reach, and the quality of the stand-in, together with the assumptions
that justify it, is examined below. The two branches are told apart only
by the token context they receive:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{k,t}=(s_{k},y_{k,<t}),\qquad h_{k,t}^{+}=(s_{k},c^{+},y_{k,<t}).
``` |  | (4) |

In this notation $`y_{k,<t}`$ is the prefix of tokens already emitted
within turn $`k`$, while $`c^{+}`$ is a skill retrieved at training time
only, summarizing subgoals and action patterns of use for the task ([Xia
et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)). The skill-conditioned branch stands in for
success-associated behavior, whereas the branch that sees no skill
supplies the background likelihood.

For a generated token $`y_{k,t}`$, the detached likelihood contrast is
written as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{k,t}=\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{k,t}\mid h_{k,t}^{+})-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{k,t}\mid h_{k,t}).
``` |  | (5) |

Whenever $`\delta_{k,t}`$ is positive, the skill has made the generated
token more probable than it would otherwise be. Summing the contrast
over the $`L_{k}`$ tokens that make up the turn gives the turn-level
evidence

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
e_{k}=\sum_{t=1}^{L_{k}}\delta_{k,t}=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k},c^{+})}{\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k})}.
``` |  | (6) |

The quantity $`e_{k}`$ therefore acts as a computable, retrospective
surrogate for the ideal turn evidence of
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In 2.2 Turning Outcome Influence into Bayesian Turn Evidence ‣ 2 Methodology ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision"));
Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Self-Teacher Contrast as a Surrogate for the Bayes Factor ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Derivations ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")
states the conditions under which the two agree. Bayes’ rule also
permits a more general identity:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\log\frac{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},C)}{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k})}=\log\frac{p(C\mid s_{k},a_{k})}{p(C\mid s_{k})}.
``` |  | (7) |

Read this way, $`e_{k}`$ is an evidential score, and its sign records
the direction in which $`a_{k}`$ moves the support for eventual success.
Sign agreement of this kind is the very property that AgentOPSD draws
on, which is why $`e_{k}`$ is treated here as an evidence proxy that is
both tractable and Bayesian in spirit.

### 2.3 Recursive Evidence State

Taken on its own, a local score $`e_{k}`$ cannot tell whether the
evidence it carries is decisive or whether earlier turns have already
made it redundant. We therefore keep an accumulator of evidence that
decays over turns, and score each turn according to how far it moves the
support state maintained so far:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{0}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{clip}(\bar{R},\epsilon_{0},1-\epsilon_{0}),\qquad c_{0}=0,`$ |  | (8) |
|  | $`\displaystyle c_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\gamma\,c_{k-1}+e_{k},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\ell_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{logit}(B_{0})+c_{k}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{logit}(B_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\gamma^{\,k-j}e_{j},`$ |  |  |

with $`B_{k}=\sigma(\ell_{k})`$ and $`\sigma(u)=(1+e^{-u})^{-1}`$. The
seed value is the proportion of successful trajectories in a group of
size $`G`$, namely $`\bar{R}=S/G`$, which is exactly the group mean GRPO
already forms
(Prop. [7](#Thmproposition7 "Proposition 7 (𝐵_0 as an Estimate of the Group Success Rate). ‣ A.2 Properties of the Reshaped Advantage ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Derivations ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision"));
$`B_{0}`$ is then obtained by clipping that fraction into
$`[\epsilon_{0},1-\epsilon_{0}]`$ with $`\epsilon_{0}{=}10^{-4}`$, and
the resulting log-odds remain finite even for a group that is uniformly
correct or uniformly wrong. Evidence is gathered in $`c_{k}`$, and
$`\gamma\in(0,1]`$ discounts it geometrically as turns recede; the
discount touches $`c_{k}`$ alone, since the prior
$`\operatorname{logit}(B_{0})`$ enters every step unchanged. Setting
$`\gamma{=}1`$ gives back the plain, undiscounted sum of log-likelihood
ratios familiar from sequential testing ([Wald, 1945](#bib.bib34)),
whereas $`\gamma{<}1`$ weights recent turns more heavily, so evidence
gathered many turns ago can no longer pin the support level. Because
$`e_{k}`$ is produced by the self-teacher
(§[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Turning Outcome Influence into Bayesian Turn Evidence ‣ 2 Methodology ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")),
$`B_{k}`$ is read as a relative level of support rather than as a
calibrated probability of success.

Turn importance is then defined through the marginal revision that a
turn induces in this support:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Delta B_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle=B_{k}-B_{k-1}=\sigma(\ell_{k})-\sigma(\ell_{k-1}),`$ |  | (9) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\Delta B_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle\approx B_{k-1}(1-B_{k-1})\big(e_{k}-(1-\gamma)\,c_{k-1}\big).`$ |  |  |

What the increment $`\ell_{k}-\ell_{k-1}=e_{k}-(1-\gamma)c_{k-1}`$
contains is the newly arrived evidence once the decayed carry-over has
been removed, and the increment is multiplied by the state sensitivity
$`B_{k-1}(1-B_{k-1})`$: evidence counts for most while support is still
uncertain, and its effect fades as support approaches saturation. At
$`\gamma{=}1`$ the expression reduces to $`B_{k-1}(1-B_{k-1})\,e_{k}`$.
We apply the recursion at turn boundaries, keeping a token-level variant
purely as an ablation.

#### Credit oriented by the outcome.

The revision becomes outcome-aware once it is oriented by the terminal
update, after which both its size and its direction can be read off:

|     |                                                            |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       q_{k}=\operatorname{sign}(A_{\mathrm{seq}})\,\Delta B_{k}.
       ```                                                         |     | (10) |

The *magnitude* $`|\Delta B_{k}|=|q_{k}|`$ measures how large a revision
the turn makes, while the *sign* $`\operatorname{sign}(q_{k})`$ records
whether that revision matches the outcome signal supplied by the
verifier. After the within-trajectory standardization described below,
turns scoring above the average $`q_{k}`$ are amplified and turns
scoring below it are attenuated; the multiplier itself stays strictly
positive, so the direction of the GRPO update is never reversed.

### 2.4 Bounded Multiplicative Reshaping

What the raw credit $`q_{k}`$ is allowed to touch is the magnitude of
the advantage derived from the verifier, and nothing else. Trajectory
$`i`$ contributes $`K_{i}`$ turn-wise credits; we standardize them and
pass the result through a bounded multiplier:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mu_{q}^{(i)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=K_{i}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{i}}q_{j}^{(i)},`$ |  | (11) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\sigma_{q}^{(i)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sqrt{K_{i}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{i}}\big(q_{j}^{(i)}-\mu_{q}^{(i)}\big)^{2}},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle z_{k}^{(i)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{q_{k}^{(i)}-\mu_{q}^{(i)}}{\sigma_{q}^{(i)}+\epsilon_{0}},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle w_{k}^{(i)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(1+bz_{k}^{(i)},\,1-b,\,1+b\right),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{k}^{(i)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=A_{\mathrm{seq}}^{(i)}\big[(1-\lambda)+\lambda w_{k}^{(i)}\big],`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle b\in(0,1),\quad\lambda\in[0,1].`$ |  |  |

Here $`\mu_{q}^{(i)}`$ is the average credit inside trajectory $`i`$ and
$`\sigma_{q}^{(i)}`$ its standard deviation, $`z_{k}^{(i)}`$ is the
credit after standardization, with $`\epsilon_{0}`$ keeping the
normalization stable, the constant $`b`$ confines $`w_{k}^{(i)}`$ to
$`[1-b,1+b]`$, and $`\lambda`$ sets how much reshaping is applied.

Every token $`t`$ inherits the advantage of its own turn,
$`\widetilde{A}_{\kappa_{i}(t)}^{(i)}`$, so that the training objective
reads

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{AgentOPSD}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\sum_{t}M_{i,t}}`$ |  | (12) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{t}M_{i,t}\,\Phi_{i,t}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\beta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\Phi_{i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\min\!\Big(r_{i,t}\widetilde{A}_{\kappa_{i}(t)}^{(i)},`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{clip}(r_{i,t},1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\,\widetilde{A}_{\kappa_{i}(t)}^{(i)}\Big),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle r_{i,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t}\mid h_{i,t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid h_{i,t})}.`$ |  |  |

In this expression $`\Phi_{i,t}`$ is the clipped per-token surrogate,
the mask $`M_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}`$ selects the response tokens that count,
$`\kappa_{i}(t)`$ returns the turn to which token $`t`$ belongs,
$`r_{i,t}`$ is the importance ratio between the current policy and the
rollout policy $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$, $`\varepsilon`$ fixes
the clipping radius, and $`\beta`$ scales the final term. Nothing is
added in the way of a separate distillation loss: the detached signal
from the self-teacher reaches the objective through $`\widetilde{A}`$
and through nothing else.

## 3 Experiments

### 3.1 Experimental Setup

#### Benchmark.

Every experiment reported here is run on ALFWorld ([Shridhar et al.,
2020](#bib.bib5)), a benchmark in which embodied agents act through
text. Its task suite is organized into six families of household
activity: Pick and Place (Pick), Pick Two and Place (Pick2), Pick Clean
then Place (Clean), Pick Cool then Place (Cool), Pick Heat then Place
(Heat), and Look at Object in Light (Look).

#### Training configuration.

Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct models are trained with $`8\times`$H800 GPUs.
Privileged skills are drawn from the SkillBank released with
SkillRL ([Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)) and matched by keywords; they
are supplied during training alone, and no external skill is consulted
at inference. The prior $`B_{0}`$ is initialized to the share of
successful rollouts within each GRPO group, which coincides with the
usual group mean $`\bar{R}`$. Every remaining optimization detail is
inherited from our common protocol. Full tables of training and
AgentOPSD-specific hyperparameters are given in
Table [3](#A6.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Appendix F Hyperparameters ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")
of
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Hyperparameters ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision").

#### Compared methods.

Three families of baselines enter the comparison. (1) No training:
Vanilla, i.e. the backbone as released, and Skill-Prompt, which
concatenates retrieved skills onto the inference-time context. (2)
Group-relative RL: GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib8)); Skill-GRPO,
for which skills are added to the training prompt and evaluation is run
either with them (Skill-GRPO\*) or without. (3) Self-distillation RL:
OPSD ([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)), StepOPSD ([Zhang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib27)), and their combination GRPO+OPSD; these three differ
in the route by which the teacher–student gap reaches the policy.
Backbone, data, and training budget are held identical across every row,
and complete algorithmic descriptions are deferred to
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Training Algorithm ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision").

### 3.2 Main Results

#### Gains trace to credit construction, not to privileged information.

AgentOPSD and the privileged baselines consume an identical pool of
retrieved skills, so what separates them is chiefly the way a
skill-induced teacher–student discrepancy is converted into a learning
signal. On ALFWorld, AgentOPSD beats every baseline it is compared
against at both model scales. Holding the available information constant
in this fashion localizes the origin of the improvement: a gap measured
locally between teacher and student does not by itself amount to a
dependable credit signal. Once that gap is accumulated into a belief
state and credit is granted according to how the belief is revised, the
turns that actually alter the predicted outcome are pinned down far more
precisely.

#### The margin widens as the interaction horizon lengthens.

Because AgentOPSD is aimed at the setting in which a uniformly assigned
credit does the greatest harm, a second question follows: how quickly
does accuracy fall away when tasks need more turns? In
Figure [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")(b),
success on each sub-task is regressed against the empirical mean turn
count of successful episodes on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-7B, so that a
fitted slope reads as the number of success points surrendered for each
extra turn. GRPO, which broadcasts one advantage to all turns alike,
sheds $`-2.91`$ points per turn, whereas AgentOPSD stays markedly
flatter at $`-0.54`$. Such a gap is what turn-level credit predicts:
every added decision in a long trajectory must otherwise share a single
broadcast advantage, so a revision conditioned on history pays off more
as the horizon stretches.

Removing the outcome-signed direction or the anchor on the state prior
causes the largest drop, whereas the recursive revision of the belief
state and the turn-level granularity of credit bring smaller yet
consistent improvements.

#### Turn-level granularity and recursive revision.

Belief tracking at the level of turns preserves the full-method score;
when accumulation is instead done token by token, success drops to
$`85.9`$. Feedback from the environment is delivered for a whole action
and never for an isolated token, so splitting a decision into
token-sized units loosens the tie between the gap and the outcome. A
further decline, to $`82.8`$, follows from discarding the recursive
revision $`\Delta B_{k}`$ in favour of the raw local gap $`e_{k}`$. The
raw quantity scores every turn on its own, whereas $`\Delta B_{k}`$
records the change that the gap induces in the belief assembled from all
preceding turns; a local gap that looks decisive while the outcome is
still undecided therefore turns redundant once the accumulated state
already indicates how the episode will end. Isolating the recursion in
this controlled manner substantiates the central claim of the paper,
namely that a local gap is not yet sequential credit.

#### Sign alignment with the verifier outcome.

If the sign is dropped and only the magnitude $`|\Delta B_{k}|`$ is kept
(Eq. [10](#S2.E10 "In Credit oriented by the outcome. ‣ 2.3 Recursive Evidence State ‣ 2 Methodology ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")),
that is, if $`|\Delta B_{k}|`$ is standardized in place of the signed
$`q_{k}`$, success falls back to $`80.5`$. A magnitude reveals that the
belief state has shifted, yet it cannot say whether the shift agrees
with the outcome returned by the verifier: one and the same upward
revision is consistent with success and inconsistent with failure.
Making the direction explicit separates those two cases, so that
revisions agreeing with the outcome are rewarded more and revisions
contradicting it are rewarded less.

#### Anchoring to the state prior.

Discarding the empirical prior term
$`B_{0}=\operatorname{clip}(\bar{R},\epsilon_{0},1-\epsilon_{0})`$
brings success down to $`78.9`$. Before any trajectory-specific gap
accumulates, the group success rate $`\bar{R}`$ already supplies an
estimate of task difficulty grounded in the verifier; beyond that,
$`B_{0}`$ sets the initial log-odds and therefore the region in which
the $`B(1-B)`$ gate operates. Lacking such an anchor, the starting
uncertainty of a trajectory is arbitrary, early revisions of the belief
may be rescaled wrongly, and the early turns that look pivotal can be
misjudged. Read together, the three ablations assign distinct roles:
localizing credit is the task of belief revision, agreeing with the
final outcome is the task of the signed direction, and holding the
reference point steady is the task of prior anchoring.

### 3.4 Hyperparameter Sensitivity

The mechanism ablation has established that each component is necessary;
the question here is how far AgentOPSD depends on the continuous
hyperparameters. One knob is varied while all others stay at the
full-AgentOPSD values ($`\lambda{=}0.5`$, $`\gamma{=}0.95`$,
$`\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}}{=}0.24`$;
Appendix [F](#A6 "Appendix F Hyperparameters ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")),
and two configurations are examined in
Figure [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 Hyperparameter Sensitivity ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision"),
namely Qwen2.5-7B on ALFWorld ($`89.1`$) and Qwen2.5-3B on ALFWorld
($`84.4`$), with both checkpoints collected in the grid below.

Figure 3: Sensitivity of AgentOPSD to its hyperparameters. Rows
correspond to ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-7B and ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-3B.
Each column varies a single knob ($`\lambda`$, $`\gamma`$,
$`\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}}`$) while the other two keep our values. Each

#### Weight of the reshaping term $`\lambda`$.

The sweep $`\lambda\in\{0.5,0.25,0.1,0.01\}`$ moves the objective from
pure GRPO ($`\lambda{=}0`$) towards fully reshaped beliefs. Of all the
knobs this one matters most: $`\lambda{=}0.5`$ gives the best result,
and every smaller value costs accuracy ($`89.1`$ at $`\lambda{=}0.5`$
versus $`84.4/85.9/83.6`$), as expected when a smaller $`\lambda`$
weakens the bounded multiplier until turn-level credit is effectively
discarded. We therefore keep $`\lambda{=}0.5`$ everywhere.

#### Decay of accumulated evidence $`\gamma`$.

Turn-level evidence is accumulated geometrically,
$`c_{k}=\gamma\,c_{k-1}+e_{k}`$; in log-odds form, $`\ell_{k}`$ is the
offset $`\operatorname{logit}(B_{0})`$ plus the discounted sum
$`\sum_{j\leq k}\gamma^{\,k-j}e_{j}`$. For
$`\gamma\in\{1.0,0.95,0.9,0.8\}`$ the result moves by only a handful of
points ($`87.5/82.0/85.2`$) with no monotone ordering, which indicates
that the recursion does not hinge on the rate at which older evidence is
discounted. All main results consequently adopt the mild choice
$`\gamma{=}0.95`$.

#### Clipping of the policy ratio $`\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}}`$.

Holding $`\epsilon_{\mathrm{low}}{=}0.2`$ fixed while
$`\epsilon_{\mathrm{high}}\in\{0.2,0.24,0.28\}`$ is varied
(clip-higher ([Yu et al., 2025](#bib.bib26))), AgentOPSD barely moves
($`88.3`$ for both $`0.2`$ and $`0.28`$), suggesting that the reshaped
objective retains the trust-region robustness of GRPO. Taken as a whole,
$`\lambda`$ is the sole knob whose effect is systematic; at either
ALFWorld scale examined here the remaining knobs stay within a few
points of the full configuration, so AgentOPSD is insensitive to these
secondary hyperparameters.

## 4 Related Work

### 4.1 Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Agents

Work on RL with verifiable rewards initially targeted single-turn
reasoning ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib8); [Guo et al.,
2025](#bib.bib7); [Yu et al., 2025](#bib.bib26); [Lu et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib12)), then progressed toward agents that act across many
steps in embodied text environments ([Shridhar et al., 2020](#bib.bib5);
[Lu et al., 2025](#bib.bib37)). Only a sparse terminal reward is
available in such settings, so identifying the turns that merit credit
becomes particularly hard. Vanilla GRPO avoids this question altogether:
it spreads one trajectory-level advantage evenly across all turns.
GiGPO ([Feng et al., 2025](#bib.bib6)) improves matters on the reward
side by pairing episode-level advantages with step-level counterparts
estimated from anchor states that reappear across trajectories.
AgentOPSD, by contrast, grounds its turn-level evidence in privileged
teacher–student likelihood gaps, and apportions credit through recursive
updates of a belief state. The two approaches therefore rely on
complementary sources of information—environment rewards in the case of
GiGPO, self-distillation evidence in the case of AgentOPSD.

### 4.2 Distilling On-Policy Signals

In on-policy distillation, a policy is optimized on its own rollouts
with guidance from a teacher ([Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib23); [Gu et
al., 2026](#bib.bib24); [Wen et al., 2023](#bib.bib25)). Newer
self-distillation variants drop the separate teacher entirely,
conditioning the teacher branch instead on privileged information that
is accessible only during training ([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib9); [He
et al., 2026](#bib.bib21); [Lu et al., 2026b](#bib.bib3)). The
teacher–student log-probability gap produced in this way has lately been
brought into RLVR in the form of local supervision, where it may be
reweighted, annealed on a schedule, or folded into a denser reward ([Xu
et al., 2026](#bib.bib4); [Wang et al., 2026](#bib.bib1); [Ye et al.,
2026](#bib.bib19); [He et al., 2026](#bib.bib21)). Throughout this
literature the gap is mostly handled as a token-level or step-level
quantity. Yet such local signals align poorly with action turns, whose
contribution depends on evidence gathered across earlier interactions.
StepOPSD ([Zhang et al., 2026](#bib.bib27)) moves in this direction by
pooling the teacher–student signal across action-centered step spans,
though each span is still scored by its local log-ratio. AgentOPSD
abandons this local perspective: it pools token-level gaps within
individual turns and then recursively incorporates that evidence into a
running support state.

### 4.3 Assigning Credit over Long Horizons

Assigning credit over long horizons is a problem with a long history.
PPO trains a value function and, through GAE, converts it into per-step
temporal-difference signals ([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib28);
[Schulman et al., 2016](#bib.bib29)). Under sparse, delayed rewards, two
families of methods recover intermediate structure: return-decomposition
approaches such as RUDDER push the terminal reward back onto the steps
responsible for it ([Arjona-Medina et al., 2019](#bib.bib30)), whereas
process reward models and Monte-Carlo credit schemes like VinePPO infer
intermediate value from additional rollouts or a learned scorer ([Cui et
al., 2025](#bib.bib32); [Kazemnejad et al., 2024](#bib.bib31)). Both
regain per-step resolution at exactly the cost GRPO was meant to
avoid—an extra trained critic, reward model, or heavy rollout budget.
AgentOPSD restores a per-turn value signal inside the critic-free,
group-relative framework, and pays only for one teacher forward pass.
Here the belief state plays a role analogous to GAE’s value baseline,
and its per-turn revision parallels the TD signal, all without learning
a value network.

## 5 Conclusion

This paper tackles credit assignment for long-horizon language agents,
where a trajectory-level reward can hardly separate decisive actions
from routine or redundant ones. The central claim of this work is that a
turn’s credit should capture more than its local signal: it should
capture the way in which that signal revises an accumulated belief about
the trajectory’s eventual success. AgentOPSD realizes this idea by
aggregating token-level self-distillation gaps within turn boundaries
that follow the environment’s structure, and by recursively maintaining
a belief about trajectory success in log-odds space. The resulting
belief revisions spread the trajectory-level advantage across turns,
requiring neither additional rollouts nor a learned critic. On ALFWorld
and at two model scales, AgentOPSD consistently outperforms GRPO as well
as strong self-distillation baselines, and ablations verify that
turn-level aggregation and history-dependent belief revision both
matter. Overall, these results suggest that recursive belief updating
offers a simple yet effective route to recovering fine-grained temporal
credit for critic-free agentic RL.

## 6 Limitations

The empirical evaluation is confined to one text-based embodied
benchmark, ALFWorld, and to two checkpoints of a single model family. We
therefore make no claim that the observed gains carry over to other
environments, larger model scales, or other modalities. The formulation
presupposes that turn boundaries can be read off the environment’s
interaction structure and that a skill-conditioned self-teacher is
available during training; settings with continuous feedback or without
a usable teacher signal would call for a different construction.
Group-relative advantages still require several rollouts per prompt, so
the approach does not remove the sampling cost of GRPO-style training.
The analysis in the appendix is developed under simplifying assumptions
about the belief update and does not establish optimality or convergence
of the complete training objective. Hyperparameter sensitivity is
measured through one-dimensional sweeps around a single operating point;
interactions between the knobs and behaviour at horizons beyond those
present in ALFWorld remain unexamined. Finally, success rate is the only
reported metric, and may not reflect other desiderata such as sample
efficiency or robustness to distribution shift.

## Appendix A Theoretical Derivations

### A.1 Self-Teacher Contrast as a Surrogate for the Bayes Factor

We take the turn-wise Bayes factor as the reference quantity,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log\frac{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},C)}{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},\neg C)}`$ |  | (13) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{logit}p(C\mid s_{k},a_{k})-\operatorname{logit}p(C\mid s_{k})`$ |  |  |

while AgentOPSD substitutes the self-teacher contrast in its place,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
e_{k}\;=\;\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k},c^{+})}{\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k})},
``` |  | (14) |

Throughout, $`C`$ stands for eventual success and
$`\rho_{k}=p(C\mid s_{k})`$. Two assumptions underpin the derivation:
(A1) the skill-conditioned branch behaves as the success-conditional
policy,
$`\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k},c^{+})\approx p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},C)`$;
(A2) in the rare-success regime ($`\rho_{k}`$ small), failed rollouts
dominate the marginal,
$`\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k})\approx p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},\neg C)`$.

The marginal action distribution mixes the success- and
failure-conditioned policies,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k})=\rho_{k}\,p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},C)+(1-\rho_{k})\,p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},\neg C).
``` |  | (15) |

Substituting
([15](#A1.E15 "In A.1 Self-Teacher Contrast as a Surrogate for the Bayes Factor ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Derivations ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision"))
into
([14](#A1.E14 "In A.1 Self-Teacher Contrast as a Surrogate for the Bayes Factor ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Derivations ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision"))
under (A1) gives

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle\approx\;\mathcal{B}_{k}-\log\!\big(1-\rho_{k}+\rho_{k}\,e^{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\big)`$ |  | (16) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\;\rho_{k}\to 0\;]{}\;\mathcal{B}_{k},`$ |  |  |

Hence (A2) isolates the $`\rho_{k}\to 0`$ regime, where the contrast
coincides with the Bayes factor. Under (A1) alone, on the other hand,
$`e_{k}`$ reduces to the pointwise mutual information

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
e_{k}\;\approx\;\log\frac{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k},C)}{p(a_{k}\mid s_{k})}\;=\;\log\frac{p(C\mid s_{k},a_{k})}{p(C\mid s_{k})},
``` |  | (17) |

which is positive if and only if $`a_{k}`$ increases the posterior
probability of success. In
([16](#A1.E16 "In A.1 Self-Teacher Contrast as a Surrogate for the Bayes Factor ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Derivations ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")),
the correction is monotone in $`\mathcal{B}_{k}`$; it follows that
$`\operatorname{sign}(e_{k})=\operatorname{sign}(\mathcal{B}_{k})`$,
meaning $`e_{k}`$ preserves the ordering of turns by evidential
strength. AgentOPSD consults $`e_{k}`$ solely through this sign and
ordering.

### A.2 Properties of the Reshaped Advantage

Let $`A^{(i)}`$ denote the group-relative advantage, and let
$`\Delta B_{k}=B_{k}-B_{k-1}`$ denote the per-turn belief revision. We
further write $`z_{k}`$ for its within-trajectory standardization,
$`m_{k}=\mathrm{clip}\!\big(1+b\,\operatorname{sign}(A^{(i)})z_{k},\,1-b,\,1+b\big)`$
for the bounded multiplier with $`b\in(0,1)`$, and
$`\tilde{A}_{k}=A^{(i)}\big((1-\lambda)+\lambda m_{k}\big)`$ for the
reshaped advantage with $`\lambda\in[0,1]`$.

###### Proposition 1 (Bounded Perturbation).

$`\big|\tilde{A}_{k}-A^{(i)}\big|\leq\lambda b\,|A^{(i)}|`$, so that
$`(1-\lambda b)|A^{(i)}|\leq|\tilde{A}_{k}|\leq(1+\lambda b)|A^{(i)}|`$.

###### Proof.

$`m_{k}\in[1-b,1+b]`$ implies $`|m_{k}-1|\leq b`$, while
$`\tilde{A}_{k}-A^{(i)}=A^{(i)}\lambda(m_{k}-1)`$. ∎

###### Proposition 2 (Preservation of Sign).

$`\operatorname{sign}(\tilde{A}_{k})=\operatorname{sign}(A^{(i)})`$
holds at each turn $`k`$.

###### Proof.

$`(1-\lambda)+\lambda m_{k}\geq 1-\lambda b>0`$ because
$`\lambda\leq 1,\,b<1`$; multiplication by a strictly positive factor
leaves the sign unchanged. ∎

###### Proposition 3 (GRPO as a Special Case).

When $`\lambda=0`$, $`\tilde{A}_{k}=A^{(i)}`$ at every token, and the
AgentOPSD gradient reduces to the GRPO gradient.

###### Proof.

Setting $`\lambda=0`$ yields $`(1-\lambda)+\lambda m_{k}=1`$; hence
$`\tilde{A}_{k}=A^{(i)}`$ identically, whatever the belief signal. ∎

###### Proposition 4 (First-Order Expansion of the Belief Revision).

With $`c_{k}=\gamma c_{k-1}+e_{k}`$,
$`\ell_{k}=\operatorname{logit}(B_{0})+c_{k}`$, and
$`\Delta\ell_{k}=e_{k}-(1-\gamma)c_{k-1}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta B_{k}=B_{k-1}(1-B_{k-1})\,\Delta\ell_{k}+O\!\big((\Delta\ell_{k})^{2}\big).
``` |  | (18) |

###### Proof.

$`B_{k}=\sigma(\ell_{k})`$, where $`\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma(1-\sigma)`$;
Taylor expansion to first order about $`\ell_{k-1}`$ yields
$`B_{k}=B_{k-1}+B_{k-1}(1-B_{k-1})\Delta\ell_{k}+O((\Delta\ell_{k})^{2})`$.
The gating factor $`B(1-B)`$ peaks at $`B=\tfrac{1}{2}`$ and tends to
zero as $`B\to\{0,1\}`$. ∎

###### Proposition 5 (Telescoping Property of the Idealized Recursion).

Summing the idealized increments telescopes to the net change,
$`\sum_{k}\Delta B_{k}=B_{K}-B_{0}`$.

###### Proof.

$`\sum_{k=1}^{K}(B_{k}-B_{k-1})=B_{K}-B_{0}`$. ∎

###### Proposition 6 (Non-Identifiability of Turn-Level Contributions).

One can find two trajectories sharing the same outcome reward—and
therefore the same broadcast advantage—whose per-turn contributions
nevertheless differ; turn-level credit is thus not recoverable from the
trajectory’s return by itself.

###### Proof (by construction).

Consider $`\tau_{1},\tau_{2}`$ within a single group satisfying
$`R(\tau_{1})=R(\tau_{2})`$, whence $`A(\tau_{1})=A(\tau_{2})`$ and GRPO
broadcasts one scalar to all turns. Suppose $`\tau_{1}`$ succeeds via
one decisive turn ($`\Delta B`$ concentrated), whereas $`\tau_{2}`$
succeeds via progress spread evenly across turns. Although the returns
agree, the underlying per-turn contributions do not, so recovering them
calls for an extra per-turn signal. ∎

###### Proposition 7 ($`B_{0}`$ as an Estimate of the Group Success Rate).

Consider a task whose success probability is $`\theta_{x}`$, together
with a group of $`G`$ trajectories, of which $`S`$ succeed given a
binary reward. The maximum-likelihood estimator of $`\theta_{x}`$ equals
the group success fraction $`\bar{R}=S/G`$, i.e., the usual GRPO group
mean; accordingly, AgentOPSD initializes
$`B_{0}=\operatorname{clip}(\bar{R},\epsilon_{0},1-\epsilon_{0})`$.

###### Proof.

The MLE under a Binomial$`(G,\theta_{x})`$ likelihood equals $`S/G`$.
Clipping ($`\epsilon_{0}{=}10^{-4}`$) merely keeps
$`\operatorname{logit}(B_{0})`$ finite for groups that are entirely
correct or entirely wrong; such groups carry zero group-relative
advantage and therefore contribute nothing to the update. ∎

## Appendix B Training Algorithm

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix B Training Algorithm ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")
summarizes one AgentOPSD training iteration at the granularity of turns.
Relative to GRPO, the added components are limited to one teacher
forward pass at every turn, along with the belief-reshaping block
applied per turn; all remaining steps follow the standard group-relative
update.

1: policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$, verifier of rewards $`R`$, group size $`G`$,
plus a skill retriever; mixing coefficient $`\lambda`$, clipping range
$`b`$, evidence discount $`\gamma`$

2: for every training iteration do

3:   sample a mini-batch of tasks $`\{x\}`$

4:   for each task $`x`$ together with its retrieved skill $`c^{+}`$ do

5:    draw $`G`$ trajectories
$`\{y^{(1)},\dots,y^{(G)}\}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$; each
trajectory $`i`$ contains $`K_{i}`$ turns $`\triangleright`$ sample
on-policy rollouts

6:    for $`i=1,\dots,G`$ do

7:      obtain from the verifier the reward
$`R^{(i)}=R(x,y^{(i)})\in\{0,1\}`$

8:    end for

9:    $`A_{\mathrm{seq}}^{(i)}\leftarrow(R^{(i)}-\mu_{G})/\sigma_{G}`$
$`\triangleright`$ group-relative advantage estimate

10:    for $`i=1,\dots,G`$ do

11:
     $`B_{0}\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(\bar{R},\epsilon_{B},1-\epsilon_{B})`$; $`\ell_{0}\leftarrow\mathrm{logit}(B_{0})`$
$`\triangleright`$ initialize the prior using the empirical success
fraction $`\bar{R}{=}S/G`$
(Prop. [7](#Thmproposition7 "Proposition 7 (𝐵_0 as an Estimate of the Group Success Rate). ‣ A.2 Properties of the Reshaped Advantage ‣ Appendix A Theoretical Derivations ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision"))

12:      $`c_{0}\leftarrow 0`$

13:      for $`k=1,\dots,K_{i}`$ do $`\triangleright`$ iterate over
turns, evolving the belief state

14:
      $`e_{k}\leftarrow\sum_{t}\mathrm{sg}[\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{k,t}\mid s_{k}^{+})-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{k,t}\mid s_{k})]`$
$`\triangleright`$ one extra teacher forward pass

15:
      $`c_{k}\leftarrow\gamma\,c_{k-1}+e_{k}`$; $`\ell_{k}\leftarrow\ell_{0}+c_{k}`$; $`B_{k}\leftarrow\sigma(\ell_{k})`$; $`\Delta B_{k}\leftarrow B_{k}-B_{k-1}`$

16:      end for

17:
     $`q_{k}\leftarrow\mathrm{sign}(A_{\mathrm{seq}}^{(i)})\,\Delta B_{k}`$
for all $`k`$ $`\triangleright`$ sign credit by the trajectory outcome

18:
     $`z_{k}\leftarrow(q_{k}-\mathrm{mean}(q))/(\mathrm{std}(q)+\epsilon)`$
$`\triangleright`$ standardize per trajectory

19:      $`w_{k}\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(1+b\,z_{k},\,1-b,\,1+b)`$
$`\triangleright`$ clip into a bounded multiplier

20:
     $`\widetilde{A}_{k}^{(i)}\leftarrow A_{\mathrm{seq}}^{(i)}\big[(1-\lambda)+\lambda w_{k}\big]`$;
each token takes the $`\widetilde{A}`$ of the turn it belongs to

21:    end for

22:   end for

23:   optimize $`\theta`$ on the clipped GRPO objective
$`\mathcal{L}_{\text{AgentOPSD}}(\theta)`$ using $`\{\widetilde{A}\}`$
$`\triangleright`$ perform the policy-gradient update

24: end for

Updates

#### Token-level variant.

In the token-level variant, the same recursion runs over the
response-masked, flattened stream of tokens rather than over turns:
$`\delta_{t}`$ accumulates directly at token level, $`\Delta B_{t}`$
undergoes standardization across all tokens of a trajectory, and
$`\widetilde{A}_{t}`$ is assigned token by token. This variant appears
in
Table [2](#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.3 Ablating the Mechanism Components ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ AgentOPSD: Turn-Level Credit Assignment via Recursive Belief Revision")
as the granularity ablation.

#### Cost.

Compared with GRPO, the only added overhead is one teacher forward pass
per trajectory. Since the belief-reshaping block acts elementwise, it
introduces neither extra rollouts nor any trainable parameters.

## Appendix C Datasets

We run all experiments in ALFWorld, one multi-turn, text-based embodied
environment focused on household reasoning.

#### ALFWorld

([Shridhar et al., 2020](#bib.bib5)) contains six task categories—Pick
and Place, Look at Object in Light, Pick Clean then Place, Pick Heat
then Place, Pick Cool then Place, and Pick Two and Place. Given a
language goal together with the textual observations it receives, the
agent repeatedly chooses among the admissible actions until the goal is
achieved.

## Appendix D Baseline Details

We evaluate AgentOPSD against baselines from three families, all
assessed under a common protocol. By default the model only observes the
standard task prompt together with the interaction history the
environment returns; a method marked with $`*`$ additionally receives a
retrieved skill at validation and test time. The $`*`$ flag therefore
marks the only departure from this shared protocol.

#### Vanilla.

Here the off-the-shelf instruction-tuned backbone is evaluated directly,
with its parameters untouched by post-training.

#### Skill-Prompt^(∗).

Parameters remain frozen as in Vanilla, but a retrieved task-relevant
skill is added in front of the context during validation and testing.
This isolates the inference-time benefit of skills in the absence of any
parameter update.

#### GRPO

([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib8)). GRPO is a critic-free, group-relative
RL method: for every task it samples a group of trajectories, maps their
terminal rewards onto normalized relative advantages, and then optimizes
a clipped surrogate objective, assigning the resulting sequence-level
advantage uniformly to each token in the trajectory.

#### Skill-GRPO / Skill-GRPO^(∗).

This variant adds a retrieved skill to the GRPO training prompt.
Skill-GRPO withholds the skill at inference, testing whether the
guidance has been internalized, while Skill-GRPO^(∗) retains it.

#### OPSD

([Zhao et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). An on-policy self-distillation scheme:
a teacher branch with access to privileged context during training
re-evaluates each token sampled by the student, producing dense
token-level targets through distribution matching. The teacher’s outputs
are detached, while the privileged context is dropped at inference.

#### GRPO+OPSD.

Here the GRPO loss at trajectory level and the OPSD objective at token
level are optimized jointly, combining outcome-based RL with generic
self-distillation in a straightforward way.

#### StepOPSD

([Zhang et al., 2026](#bib.bib27)). This baseline applies the
self-distillation signal at the granularity of turns (steps) instead of
tokens, and uses the local signal of each step in isolation.

All post-training baselines use the same backbone models, the ALFWorld
environment interface, the training data, and the training budget as
AgentOPSD; what distinguishes them is chiefly the optimization objective
and whether retrieved skills are provided at training time or at
evaluation.

## Appendix E Evaluation Metrics

#### ALFWorld.

We measure the overall success rate on the evaluation tasks, i.e., the
proportion of episodes that attain the specified goal; this equals a
sample-weighted average across the six per-category success rates.
````
