# Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context

Manh Nguyen ^(†)^(†)thanks: Corresponding Author    Sunil Gupta    Hung
Le Affiliation: Applied Artificial Intelligence Initiative, Deakin
University, Australia Affiliation: {manh.nguyen, sunil.gupta,
thai.le}@deakin.edu.au

###### Abstract

Reasoning systems increasingly improve answers by incorporating
additional context into the model’s input, from multi-agent debate,
where agents read their peers’ responses, to self-refinement, where a
model revisits its own drafts. In these systems, communication consumes
context as one agent’s output becomes another’s input. We study the
regime where token reading and generation draw on one fixed token
window, so every token spent reading is a token that cannot be spent
reasoning. We develop a token-budget theory that formalizes this
trade-off, modeling accuracy as a function of the reasoning budget
remaining after reading and aggregating votes with a Condorcet jury
model that permits correlated errors. For multi-agent debate, the theory
gives a closed-form prediction for the optimal number of agents. This
quantity is estimated from single-agent measurements, allowing the
optimal group size to be determined without running debate, and the same
accounting extends to a heterogeneous set of different models. We
empirically verify our theory and show that, at the predicted group
size, our method outperforms strong baselines under the tightest
calibration spend, where debate-calibrated rules can afford only one or
two questions. Self-refinement obeys the same accounting, with its own
draft in place of a peer message. The theory further predicts that
communication pays only when the gain in the accuracy ceiling exceeds
the reasoning room spent on reading, with this condition tightening as
the window shrinks. Across five open models and four reasoning tasks,
self-consistency matches or beats deployed debate at $`93`$% of $`980`$
matched window-and-group-size settings, with the gap being largest under
tight windows and nearly disappearing at a loose one, consistent with
the theory. Under a shared window, how much room a model has left to
think matters more than what it reads.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in reasoning
pipelines that reuse model-generated text as input to subsequent
computation. Multi-agent debate exchanges answers among agents ([Du et
al., 2024](#bib.bib1); [Liang et al., 2024](#bib.bib2)), while
self-refinement feeds a model its own previous drafts ([Madaan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib32)). Such reuse can improve reasoning by providing
additional information, but under a fixed token budget, it also raises a
fundamental question: not _whether_ added feedback helps, but _how much_
it is worth in terms of the reasoning budget it consumes.

Figure 1: Overview of our method (Qwen2.5-7B, GSM8K). One window $`W`$
pays for both reading (question plus peer messages) and thinking
(generation $`g`$), and each extra agent adds $`m`$ tokens of reading.
The per-agent edge $`\delta`$ is the gap between the probability of the
correct answer and that of the strongest wrong answer: larger $`\delta`$
means a stronger correct-answer margin, while $`\delta\leq 0`$ means
that a wrong answer is at least as likely. Left: The dashed line marks
the smallest thinking room at which $`\delta>0`$; a group that reads
past it (here $`n{=}8`$) leaves too few tokens for reasoning and its
accuracy collapses. Right: The effective sample size
$`n_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ measures the independent-vote equivalent of $`n`$
correlated agents, while $`\delta`$ falls as thinking room shrinks.
Their product $`S`$, the synergy index, therefore captures the trade-off
between per-agent accuracy and voting amplification and peaks at
$`n^{\ast}=4.9`$. The shaded band marks $`\delta{\leq}0`$, where votes
amplify a wrong majority and extra agents cannot help.

Majority-voting analyses predict that adding agents amplifies a correct
majority until correlated errors cap the gain ([Ladha,
1992](#bib.bib31); [Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)). However, on multi-hop
reasoning tasks, a single long chain-of-thought ([Wei et al.,
2022](#bib.bib15)) often matches or outperforms a multi-agent system
under an equal thinking budget ([Tran and Kiela, 2026](#bib.bib5)).
Debate also does not consistently outperform self-consistency on an
accuracy-per-token-cost frontier ([Smit et al., 2024](#bib.bib20);
[Bertalanic and Fortuna, 2026](#bib.bib35)), echoing the broader
trade-off between token expenditure and accuracy in sampling and
search ([Snell et al., 2025](#bib.bib17); [Brown et al.,
2024](#bib.bib18)). The token budget deserves separate attention because
it corresponds directly to the cost incurred in practice. At current API
prices, inference can range from cents to tens of dollars per million
tokens, depending on the model and provider ([OpenAI, 2026](#bib.bib53);
[Google, 2026](#bib.bib54); [Anthropic, 2026](#bib.bib52)). For
multi-agent debate, the cost grows further with the number of agents
$`n`$ (group size): adding agents increases not only the amount of
reasoning generated, but also the amount of peer text that must be read
and processed. Existing work attacks this cost either by compressing
agent communication or by fixing the reasoning budget, but treats
communication and reasoning as separate costs, despite both consuming
the same token budget
($`\lx@sectionsign`$[2](#S2 "2 Related Work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
Once the token budget is viewed this way, a trade-off appears: with
$`n`$ small, more of the budget remains for thinking, while with $`n`$
large, most of it is spent on reading peer messages, leaving little room
to reason. The question is then how to estimate the best group size
$`n^{\ast}`$ for multi-agent debate under this trade-off. Existing
approaches measure $`n`$ by sweeping or adaptively searching the design
space, for instance, successive halving ([Jamieson and Talwalkar,
2016](#bib.bib41)). Both require debate runs to identify the operating
point, without explaining _why_ one group size is optimal or _when_
adding more agents stops improving accuracy.

We therefore introduce a token-budget theory that answers both questions
in closed form. Consider homogeneous debate in which each agent, working
under a window of $`W`$ tokens, must first pay for what it reads, namely
the question and its peers’ messages, together $`H`$ tokens, plus a
small fixed overhead $`c`$. The remainder, $`g=W-H-c`$, is the room it
has left to reason. Instead of assigning each agent a fixed chance of
voting correctly, we introduce its _ability_, which is the accuracy it
reaches when its reasoning is capped at $`g`$ tokens. Ability grows with
thinking room, too little room can prevent an agent from reaching an
answer, while beyond a sufficient room, additional tokens yield little
further gain. We let it grow with $`g`$
(Eq. [3](#S3.E3 "In 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"))
via the fittable thinking-gain function $`\phi`$, whose functional form
and parameters can be calibrated from held-out calibration data. We then
combine the resulting votes with a Condorcet jury model that allows
correlated errors, developing the per-answer margin $`\delta`$
(Eq. [6](#S3.E6 "In 3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
the group accuracy $`A(n)`$, the effective sample size
$`n_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ and the synergy index
$`S(n)=\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}`$
(Eq. [10](#S3.E10 "In 3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"))
in
Section [3](#S3 "3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
provides an overview of our method.

Our model yields a closed-form prediction of an interior optimal group
size $`n^{\ast}`$ that _moves with the budget_. It is read off
single-agent measurements: we measure the ability curve with a cap sweep
and estimate one error-correlation parameter at the _initial round_,
then predict group size without running the debate. With a sufficiently
large budget, our predictions approach the classical correlation-limited
ensemble, where the main benefit comes from aggregating independent
evidence. The practical advantage of our approach is therefore greatest
in the tight-budget regime, where choosing the group size correctly
matters most and our method achieves the best accuracy-budget trade-off.
At the predicted size, it incurs only a $`1.4\%`$ accuracy gap from a
per-cell oracle, using between $`26`$ and $`79`$ calibration questions
depending on the cell, where the debate-calibrated rules use only one or
two at matched spend. The accounting extends to a heterogeneous model
set, where the rule chooses which models to include as well as how many.

Our framework also clarifies the cost of communication relative to
self-consistency ([Wang et al., 2023](#bib.bib16)). Self-consistency
leaves the full window available for reasoning, whereas debate spends
part of that budget processing peer responses, so communication must
provide sufficient informational benefit to justify this cost. In our
experiments, self-consistency matches or beats deployed debate at
$`93\%`$ of the $`980`$ matched operating points, while a paired test
finds debate significantly ahead at only $`0.5\%`$. The gap is largest
under tight budgets and nearly disappears at a loose budget, where
communication consumes a smaller share of the available reasoning
budget. Self-refinement, which rereads only its own last draft without
voting, pays the same fixed reading cost every round with none of the
amplification that voting provides, so its accuracy collapses faster
than debate’s as rounds accumulate.

Our contributions are as follows. First, we introduce a token-budget
theory that predicts the optimal debate size $`n^{\ast}`$ in closed form
and extends to heterogeneous model sets, outperforming strong baselines
under the tightest calibration spend without running a debate. Second,
the theory reveals when debate’s voting benefits outweigh its
communication cost relative to self-consistency under the same token
budget. Third, ablations show that reading cost drives collapse and the
interior optimum, with robustness tests covering longer reasoning and
practical token pricing.

## 2 Related Work

#### Multi-agent debate in LLMs.

LLM instances critique and revise each other’s answers to improve
reasoning relative to a single pass ([Du et al., 2024](#bib.bib1);
[Liang et al., 2024](#bib.bib2)), and voting over more agents can
improve accuracy in practice ([Wang et al., 2023](#bib.bib16); [Li et
al., 2024a](#bib.bib19)). Each additional agent, however, also
introduces peer messages that other agents must process, increasing the
communication burden of the group. Existing work addresses this burden
by reducing communication through sparse speaker selection, group
partitioning, or retaining only conflicting messages ([Li et al.,
2024b](#bib.bib8); [Liu et al., 2024b](#bib.bib7); [Zeng et al.,
2025](#bib.bib6); [Nguyen et al., 2026c](#bib.bib3)). A complementary
line studies whether debate is worthwhile under a matched sampling or
thinking budget, with several works finding that self-correction or a
single long reasoning chain can match or outperform debate ([Huang et
al., 2024](#bib.bib33); [Tran and Kiela, 2026](#bib.bib5); [Bertalanic
and Fortuna, 2026](#bib.bib35)). A third line promotes diversity through
heterogeneous model sets, arguing that homogeneous evaluation overvalues
debate ([Zhang et al., 2025](#bib.bib44)), attributing vanilla debate’s
weakness to lost candidate diversity ([Zhu et al., 2026](#bib.bib50)),
and showing that mixed panels revise less harmfully than homogeneous
ones ([Nilayam et al., 2026](#bib.bib51)). Beyond debate, this idea
appears in mixtures of agents ([Wang et al., 2025](#bib.bib47)), model
routing ([Ong et al., 2025](#bib.bib48)), and cost-aware cascades ([Chen
et al., 2023](#bib.bib49)).

#### Estimating the operating point.

Existing approaches search the design space, e.g., via successive
halving ([Jamieson and Talwalkar, 2016](#bib.bib41)), or learn the
operating point from data, but both require debate runs for calibration.
[Chen et al. (2024)](#bib.bib34) make voting accuracy non-monotone in
call count through query-difficulty diversity, but their optimum is
refit separately at each budget, whereas ours transfers from a
single-agent cap sweep. [Liu et al. (2026)](#bib.bib4) give the closest
theoretical treatment, deriving an amplification-collapse threshold in
closed form from a power-law ability curve. Their ability curve is
calibrated in simulation and remains concave, so it cannot capture the
near-zero accuracy below the minimum length needed for a solution, the
regime where our collapse occurs. Their accounting also holds the
per-agent generation allowance fixed, so adding an agent costs no
reasoning room, making their collapse a consequence of correlation alone
rather than reading. We recover their setting as the $`m=0`$ case
($`\lx@sectionsign`$ [4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Ablations ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

#### Token Budgeting in LLMs.

A separate line studies how to allocate a fixed token budget across
samples or reasoning length for a single agent, including token budgets
for chain-of-thought, budget forcing at decoding time, budget-aligned
tree search, and the non-monotone effect of reasoning length on task
success ([Han et al., 2025](#bib.bib11); [Muennighoff et al.,
2025](#bib.bib45); [Miyamoto et al., 2026](#bib.bib12); [Qi,
2026](#bib.bib36)). Controlled comparisons at matched sampling or search
budgets find that voting, rather than talking, carries the gain ([Choi
et al., 2025](#bib.bib9); [Kaesberg et al., 2025](#bib.bib42); [Wang et
al., 2024a](#bib.bib43)), while [Wunderlich et al. (2026)](#bib.bib39)
report the opposite ordering under per-agent rather than shared
contexts. These methods budget within reasoning or sampling, leaving
peer text outside the account.

## 3 Method

We formalize the token-budget problem behind debate, define per-agent
ability as a function of the room left to think, and turn it into a
group-accuracy prediction. We call the resulting predictor TB-MAD, for
token-budget multi-agent debate: it reads a single-agent probe sweep and
returns the group size, with no debate run.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Consider $`n`$ LLM agents that debate a question from a task $`Q`$ over
several rounds under one token budget $`W`$, the _window_. Reading and
thinking draw on the same $`W`$, where tokens spent reading peer
messages are tokens no longer available for the agent’s own chain of
thought, and vice versa. Each peer message costs about $`m`$ tokens on
average to read once. Under last-round visibility, which we use
throughout, agent $`i`$ retains the question ($`q`$ tokens), a fixed
overhead ($`c`$), and $`n-1`$ peer messages, so its retained history and
remaining room to think are

|     |                                                              |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle H\;=\;q+(n-1)\,m,\qquad\qquad g\;=\;W-H-c.`$ |     | (1) |

Retaining the full transcript would increase $`H`$ at every $`n`$ and
can only decrease $`g`$, so the penalty we measure is a lower bound.
Self-refinement uses a different accounting. The group size is one, so
there are no peer messages, but the agent retains its own last draft.
Its history is $`H=q+m`$ and its room is $`g=W-q-m-c`$. In contrast,
Eq. [1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
with $`n=1`$ describes a debate agent with no peer messages,
corresponding to self-consistency.

Adding an agent raises $`H`$ and lowers $`g`$ by $`m`$ tokens, which
cuts both ways. When $`W`$ is generous, $`g`$ stays past the point where
extra tokens stop buying accuracy and a larger group is close to free.
When $`W`$ is tight, the agent pushes $`g`$ below the room a chain needs
to reach an answer, and the lost generation budget outweighs the extra
vote. We call this _collapse_: under a shared budget, more agents can
reduce accuracy. Given $`W`$, we want the group size that maximizes task
accuracy,

|     |                                                                                 |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\max_{n}\;A_{Q}(n,W)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad g(n)=W-H(n)-c\geq 0,`$ |     | (2) |

where $`A_{Q}(n,W)`$ is the debate group’s accuracy at size $`n`$ and
window $`W`$. Since a debate agent’s vote depends on how much of the
window it got to reason in, solving
Eq. [2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
needs a model of per-agent ability as a function of $`g`$, which we give
next.

### 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain

Rather than assuming fixed voting accuracy, we let that chance depend on
what the window leaves an agent to think. The _per-agent ability_
$`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)`$ is the accuracy of a single agent whose
generation is capped at $`g`$ tokens, measured directly with a cap sweep
and no debate. We drop the $`Q`$ subscript below, since every quantity
is on the same task.

|     |                                                                                                                                                                    |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)\;=\;p_{\max}\,\underbrace{\phi(g)}_{\text{thinking gain}},\qquad\phi(g)\;=\;\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)/p_{\max}\;\in\;[0,1],`$ |     | (3) |

where $`p_{\max}=\lim_{g\to\infty}\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)`$ is the
ceiling ability with unlimited room, so the _thinking gain_ $`\phi`$ is
the fraction of its own ceiling an agent reaches at cap $`g`$.

###### Assumption 1 (Admissible gain).

$`\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]`$ is continuously differentiable, strictly
increasing ($`\phi^{\prime}>0`$) on $`(0,\infty)`$, and saturating
($`\phi(\infty){=}1`$), and satisfies $`\phi(0){=}0`$.

More room cannot hurt, since a longer allowance can always reproduce a
shorter chain, and it eventually stops helping. This is consistent with
the diminishing return in test-time-compute scaling literature ([Snell
et al., 2025](#bib.bib17)).

#### Choice of $`\phi`$.

The cap sweeps are _sigmoidal_, near zero below the length a solution
needs, rising sharply around it, then saturating. Held-out error selects
the sigmoid _family_ over the concave power law prior work adopts ([Liu
et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)). Isotonic regression extrapolates
competitively out of range but is far worse under leave-one-out. Within
the sigmoid family, the Hill form has the lowest total error (Appendix
Table [9](#A2.T9 "Table 9 ‣ B.3 Calibration Cost ‣ Appendix B Implementation Details ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
We adopt it,

|     |                                                                                                      |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)\;=\;p_{\max}\,\frac{g^{\kappa}}{g^{\kappa}+g_{0}^{\kappa}},`$ |     | (4) |

with scale $`g_{0}`$ and shape $`\kappa>0`$, fitted on a separate
calibration set. Under this form, $`\phi(g)`$ could be interpreted as
the probability that a solution fits within a budget of $`g`$ tokens,
making $`g_{0}`$ a median solution length
(Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Implementing TB-MAD ‣ Appendix B Implementation Details ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

|                                                 |                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Form                                            | $`\phi(g)`$                                                           |
| Hill ([Hill, 1910](#bib.bib55))                 | $`\dfrac{g^{\kappa}}{g^{\kappa}+g_{0}^{\kappa}}`$                     |
| Burr XII ([Burr, 1942](#bib.bib57))             | $`1-\left(1+\left(\frac{g}{g_{0}}\right)^{\kappa}\right)^{-\lambda}`$ |
| Weibull ([Weibull, 1951](#bib.bib56))           | $`1-e^{-\left(\frac{g}{g_{0}}\right)^{\kappa}}`$                      |
| Power law ([Pareto, 1964](#bib.bib59))          | $`1-(1+\frac{g}{g_{0}})^{-\kappa}`$                                   |
| Monotone ([Robertson et al., 1988](#bib.bib58)) | isotonic step                                                         |

Table 1: Candidate forms for the thinking gain $`\phi`$, adopted form in
bold. _Right:_ all five fitted to one measured cap sweep
(Qwen2.5-7B/GSM8K).

### 3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index

To turn per-agent ability into group accuracy, we use a
correlation-aware Condorcet model ([de Condorcet, 1785](#bib.bib29);
[Boland, 1989](#bib.bib30); [Ladha, 1992](#bib.bib31)) that captures how
additional agents help until correlated errors diminish the gain. The
following three simplifications make it tractable. We read
$`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ as a per-question probability where
Eq. [4](#S3.E4 "In Choice of ϕ. ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
defines a task average ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib34)), and treat the
agents as _homogeneous_ so all share $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g(n))`$.
Moreover, we assume their errors are _equicorrelated_, with every pair
sharing a $`\rho`$ independent of $`n`$, estimated at round $`0`$ before
any agent reads a peer and so measurable without running a debate
(Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1.SSS0.Px4 "Estimating 𝜌̂, and its invariance ‣ B.1 Implementing TB-MAD ‣ Appendix B Implementation Details ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
$`\rho`$ plays the role that diversity measures play in the ensemble
literature ([Krogh and Vedelsby, 1994](#bib.bib60); [Kuncheva and
Whitaker, 2003](#bib.bib61)), capturing a similar trade-off between
individual accuracy and error independence.

For a given question, let the correct answer’s margin over the strongest
wrong answer be

|     |                                                                                                                                |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\delta(n)=\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\bigl(g(n)\bigr)-q_{\max}(n),\qquad q_{\max}=(1-\beta_{\mathrm{eff}})\,c_{w},`$ |     | (5) |

where $`q_{\max}`$ is the highest-probability wrong answer and $`c_{w}`$
is the _error concentration_, the probability of producing the strongest
wrong answer conditional on being wrong. On a $`K`$-way task, $`c_{w}`$
is bounded between $`1/(K-1)`$ and $`1`$, with the lower bound attained
when errors are spread uniformly over the wrong options and the upper
bound when every error picks the same wrong option. We hold $`c_{w}`$
fixed across $`n`$ and $`g`$, so all budget dependence enters through
$`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$. Thus

|     |                                                              |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\delta=(1+c_{w})\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}-c_{w},`$ |     | (6) |

which is positive exactly when
$`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}>\tau\equiv c_{w}/(1+c_{w})`$. Across answers,
$`c_{w}`$ is analogous to $`\rho`$ across agents: $`\rho`$ captures
dependence in whether agents are correct, while $`c_{w}`$ captures
dependence in which wrong answer they choose when they are incorrect.
Both are measured at round $`0`$ and held fixed and debiased for small
samples
(Appendix [D.4](#A4.SS4 "D.4 Is the error concentration 𝑐_𝑤 constant? ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

Score agent $`j`$ by $`X_{j}=+1`$ when it answers correctly,
$`X_{j}=-1`$ when it gives the strongest wrong answer, and $`X_{j}=0`$
otherwise, so the group prefers the correct answer exactly when
$`\bar{X}_{n}>0`$, with $`\mathbb{E}[X_{j}]=\delta`$ by homogeneity.
Write $`\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{Var}(X_{j})`$ for this per-agent
variance, common to all $`j`$ by homogeneity
(Appendix [D.5](#A4.SS5 "D.5 Does 𝜎 depend on 𝑛? ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
gives its closed form in $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ and $`c_{w}`$).
Equicorrelation with $`\rho\in(0,1)`$ gives
$`\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j})=\rho\sigma^{2}`$ and hence

|     |                                                  |                                                                                                       |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_{n})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma^{2}\bigl(1+\rho(n-1)\bigr)}{n}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)},`$ |     | (7) |
|     | $`\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)`$            | $`\displaystyle=\frac{n}{1+\rho(n-1)}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\frac{1}{\rho}.`$                      |     | (8) |

Thus $`n`$ correlated agents provide the effective sample size
$`n_{\mathrm{eff}}`$, which is bounded by $`1/\rho`$ and yields
diminishing returns ([Kish, 1965](#bib.bib37)). Approximating
$`\bar{X}_{n}`$ by a normal with the same moments,

|     |                                                                                                                       |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle A(n)=\Pr[\bar{X}_{n}>0]\simeq\Phi\!\left(\frac{\delta(n)\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)}}{\sigma}\right),`$ |     | (9) |

where $`\Phi`$ is the standard normal CDF and $`\delta(n)`$ carries
$`g(n)`$ from
Eq. [1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").
Eq. [9](#S3.E9 "In 3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
is an _aggregation head_: given a per-agent ability, it maps that
ability to the accuracy of the resulting vote. For debate, feeding it
the pre-revision ability $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g(n))`$ gives the
initial-round vote accuracy $`A(n)`$. After revision, the same head
applies to the agents’ updated ability, so revision changes the ability
entering the head instead of the aggregation mechanism itself. We
therefore use the pre-revision head to determine the optimal operating
point $`n^{\ast}`$, while treating revision as a separate accuracy lift
whose effect is evaluated on the deployed debate.

Because $`\Phi`$ is monotone and $`\sigma>0`$, we can maximize the
signed _synergy index_

|     |                                                                                                     |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\arg\max_{n}A(n)=\arg\max_{n}S(n),\qquad S(n)=\delta(n)\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)}.`$ |     | (10) |

$`\delta(n)`$ is the per-agent edge the budget supplies;
$`\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)}`$ the amplification voting supplies.
Keeping the sign of $`\delta`$ matters for the objective’s behaviour
just outside $`\mathcal{F}`$. Votes amplify a negative edge as readily
as a positive one, so $`S`$ falls once $`\delta`$ turns negative and the
rule returns $`n=1`$, where an unsigned measure would keep climbing.
Since $`n^{\ast}`$ is read off $`S(n)`$ rather than accuracy itself, it
does not depend on $`\Phi`$ being Gaussian: any strictly increasing map
from margin to accuracy yields the maximizer. As $`n`$ grows the
amplification rises while $`\delta(n)`$ falls, because each agent costs
$`m`$ tokens of room and so lowers $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$, producing
the interior optimum.
Eq. [10](#S3.E10 "In 3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
treats $`\sigma`$ as a constant fit once per cell; once the vote score
is fixed, $`\sigma`$ is instead a function of $`n`$ through
$`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)`$, and substituting that derived $`\sigma(n)`$
moves $`\hat{n}^{\ast}`$ at only $`7`$ of $`140`$ swept operating points
(Appendix [D.5](#A4.SS5 "D.5 Does 𝜎 depend on 𝑛? ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
We summarize the procedure in Appendix
Alg. [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

#### Self-refinement as the degenerate case.

Self-refinement reads its own previous draft, corresponding to $`n{=}1`$
while still paying the reading cost. Hence
$`g_{\mathrm{ref}}(W)=W-q-m-c`$, reducing $`\delta`$ through the
generation-floor effect, while $`\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}=1`$ provides no
amplification. Repeating rounds therefore does not improve $`S`$ unless
revision increases the ability of subsequent generations.

### 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime

We now analyze what happens when ability is tied to the window, using a
single model in which reading consumes reasoning budget and yields the
following results.

#### The interior best size.

Throughout, $`n`$ is continuous, $`g(n)=W-q-c-(n-1)m`$, and we restrict
to the feasible region $`\mathcal{F}=\{n:\delta(n)\geq 0\}`$. The
constraint is set by $`\delta`$, not $`g`$: $`\delta(n)`$ reaches zero
strictly before $`g(n)`$ does, so
$`\mathcal{F}={n:\delta(n)\geq 0}\subset{n:g(n)\geq 0}`$, while
$`\phi(g)\to 0`$ continuously as $`g\to 0`$.

###### Proposition 1 (Single-peaked accuracy).

Let $`\phi`$ satisfy
Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Admissible gain). ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"),
with $`m>0`$, $`p_{\max}\in(0,1]`$, $`\rho\in[0,1)`$, $`\delta(1)>0`$,
and suppose some larger group helps ($`S(n)>S(1)`$ for some
$`n\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{F})`$). Then $`\delta`$ is strictly
decreasing on $`\mathcal{F}=[1,n_{0}]`$, where $`n_{0}>1`$ is the unique
group size at which the average agent’s edge over the strongest
distractor vanishes ($`\delta(n_{0}){=}0`$), and $`S(n)^{2}`$ attains an
interior maximum $`n^{\ast}\in(1,n_{0})`$.

###### Proof.

See
Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. ) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").
∎

Every form in
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Choice of ϕ. ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
has $`\phi(0){=}0`$, so $`\delta<0`$ once the reasoning budget is
exhausted at $`n_{\mathrm{exh}}=1+(W-q-c)/m`$ (distinct from the
deployment cap $`n_{\max}`$ used elsewhere); hence $`n_{0}`$ exists and
$`\mathcal{F}`$ lies strictly inside the domain of $`\phi`$. The
mechanism is a race between two rates, one shrinking like $`1/n^{2}`$ as
an extra agent adds amplification, the other growing without bound as it
costs per-agent edge with $`\delta\to 0^{+}`$ near $`n_{0}`$. Continuity
on a compact interval and a vanishing edge at $`n_{0}`$ give existence;
uniqueness follows from log-concavity of the shifted edge
$`\delta`$(Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. ) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
The $`1/n^{2}`$ rate limits how fast $`n^{\ast}(W)`$ can grow. Each
extra agent costs $`m`$ tokens of room while its amplification gain
shrinks, keeping $`n^{\ast}`$ below the feasibility cap $`n_{\max}`$
(Appendix
Cor. [2](#Thmcorollary2 "Corollary 2 (Slow growth). ‣ A.2 The large-window limit ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
As $`W\to\infty`$, thinking room ceases to bind and the classical
correlation ceiling returns, so an interior optimum arises only when the
window is scarce
(Appendix [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 The large-window limit ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

#### Reading cannot beat voting at matched aggregation.

Self-consistency draws $`n`$ chains from one agent without exchanging
messages, so each reads only the question ($`g_{\mathrm{SC}}{=}W-q-c`$),
while a debate agent also retains $`n-1`$ peer messages and keeps only
$`g(n)\leq g_{\mathrm{SC}}`$. Thus, debate’s “synergy” is a voting
effect unless peer text raises $`\Delta p_{\max}`$, which we measure in
§[4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

Let $`A^{0}_{\mathrm{deb}}(n,W)`$ be initial-round debate vote accuracy
of

before any revision. Under

and the dominance result above is the special case

#### Baselines.

We compare our closed-form prediction against eight alternatives grouped
by calibration cost. _Debate-calibrated_, using outcomes from actual
debate runs that our method does not require: Empirical Argmax selects
the best accuracy among tested $`(n,W)`$ configurations, Successive
Halving ([Jamieson and Talwalkar, 2016](#bib.bib41)) spends the same
budget adaptively, and a Quadratic Surface in $`(n,W)`$. _No
calibration:_ naive $`n{=}1`$ and naive $`n{=}n_{\max}`$.
_Probe-calibrated, as ours is:_ Learned $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$
replaces the functional form with gradient-boosted trees; Star
Exponent ([Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)) substitutes the power-law
ability curve into our aggregator and budget model, which isolates the
effect of the functional form; and Difficulty Mixture ([Chen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib34)) estimates per-question accuracy from probe sweeps and
simulates voting, with the per-call allowance adjusted under our window
accounting as $`n`$ varies. More details are in
Appendix [B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2 Implementing Baselines ‣ Appendix B Implementation Details ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

#### Fitting And Predicting Performance.

TB-MAD provides the best regret-cost trade-off, with regret decreasing

Revision inherits the same generation-floor effect as debate, making

### 4.3 Ablations

#### The reading cost $`m`$.

$`m`$ is the token cost of reading one peer message
(Eq. [1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
determining how quickly added agents reduce the group’s shared thinking
room. Both the collapse and interior optimum arise from $`m>0`$. Setting
$`m{=}0`$ holds $`g`$ fixed regardless of group size, as in debate where
each agent has its own window. The theory then predicts the largest
feasible group at every window, with no interior optimum and no collapse
(Appendix
Figure [9](#A4.F9 "Figure 9 ‣ D.6 Sensitivity of the prediction to 𝑚. ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
More details are in
Appendix [D.6](#A4.SS6 "D.6 Sensitivity of the prediction to 𝑚. ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

#### The price of a token.

We ablate the output-to-input price ratio
$`r{=}c_{\text{out}}/c_{\text{in}}`$ to reflect practical cost models,
where reading and generating tokens can have different prices. Making
input more expensive reduces their performance accordingly and TB-MAD
keeps the lowest regret at every ratio tried on these spends (Appendix
Table [19](#A4.T19 "Table 19 ‣ D.3 Repricing at API rates ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
while cheaper input buys a larger cost-optimal group at the same spend
(Appendix
Figure [6](#A4.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ D.3 Repricing at API rates ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

#### Is the collapse a long-context artifact?

A longer transcript could hurt through position ([Liu et al.,
2024a](#bib.bib13); [Hsieh et al., 2024](#bib.bib14)) or length
alone ([Levy et al., 2024](#bib.bib46)), but these effects do not
explain the collapse. Moving the question to the end of the transcript,
after the peer messages, at fixed $`H`$ and $`g`$ changes accuracy by
less than $`1\%`$ on average (Appendix
Table [23](#A4.T23 "Table 23 ‣ D.7 Generation-floor recovery. ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
Question position moves the collapse cell without lifting it out of
collapse.

## 5 Conclusion

We develop a token-budget theory of when additional context is worth its
cost by placing reading and thinking in a shared window. The theory
predicts optimal group size and identifies a critical window below which
a single agent can outperform debate. Empirically, our method achieves
the lowest regret under tight budgets, while self-consistency often
matches or outperforms debate at lower cost. The theory offers a simple
guide for _when_ to read more, think more, or add agents. Limitations
and Future work are discussed in
Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

### AI use statement

In this work, we used generative AI tools solely to support writing,
including proofing, language polishing, and improving readability. We
did not use them to develop research ideas, conduct experiments, or
generate the substantive content of the work. All AI-assisted text was
reviewed and verified by the authors, who take full responsibility for
the final content.

### Ethics statement

TB-MAD lowers the token cost of deploying multi-agent debate by choosing
a group size before any debate is run. Its predictions are calibrated
per model and task, so a size chosen outside the regime the probe sweep
covers can lose accuracy with no signal that it has. We use only public
benchmarks and open-weight models, with no human subjects or personal
data.

## Appendix A Proofs

### A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Single-peaked accuracy). ‣ The interior best size. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"))

Treat $`n`$ as continuous, so

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
g(n)=W-q-c-(n-1)m,\qquad\delta(n)=(1+c_{w})\,p_{\max}\,\phi\bigl(g(n)\bigr)-c_{w}.
``` |  |

Since $`\phi`$ is defined on $`[0,\infty)`$, we work on
$`[1,n_{\mathrm{exh}}]`$ with $`n_{\mathrm{exh}}=1+(W-q-c)/m`$, the
(real-valued, not the deployment cap $`n_{\max}`$ used elsewhere) group
size at which the reasoning budget is exhausted
($`g(n_{\mathrm{exh}})=0`$).

#### Step 1: $`\delta`$ is strictly decreasing.

$`g`$ is strictly decreasing in $`n`$ ($`g^{\prime}(n)=-m<0`$) and
$`\phi`$ is strictly increasing
(Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Admissible gain). ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
so $`\phi(g(n))`$ is strictly decreasing and hence $`\delta(n)`$ is also
strictly decreasing. Every form in
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Choice of ϕ. ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
has $`\phi(0)=0`$, so $`\delta(n_{\mathrm{exh}})=-c_{w}<0`$. With
$`\delta(1)>0`$, $`\delta`$ continuous and strictly decreasing, there is
a unique root $`n_{0}\in(1,n_{\mathrm{exh}})`$, and
$`\mathcal{F}=[1,n_{0}]`$ lies strictly inside the domain of $`\phi`$.
(This step uses only monotonicity of $`\phi`$, not the differentiability
also assumed in
Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Admissible gain). ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").)

#### Step 2: the log-derivative decomposition.

Write $`M(n)=S(n)^{2}=n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)\delta(n)^{2}`$ for the squared
synergy index of
§[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").
Since $`\phi\in C^{1}`$ and $`g`$ is affine, $`\delta\in C^{1}`$; on
$`\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{F})`$ we have $`\delta>0`$, so
$`\log M(n)=\log n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)+2\log\delta(n)`$ is differentiable
and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}n}\log M=\underbrace{\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\prime}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}}_{\alpha(n)}-\underbrace{\Bigl(-2\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{\delta}\Bigr)}_{\mathcal{C}(n)}.
``` |  |

With $`D(n)=1+\rho(n-1)`$ and $`n_{\mathrm{eff}}=n/D`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\prime}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}n}\bigl(\log n-\log D\bigr)=\frac{1}{n}-\frac{\rho}{D}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{D-\rho n}{nD}=\frac{1-\rho}{\,n\bigl(1+\rho(n-1)\bigr)\,},`$ |  |

is the amplification term $`\alpha(n)`$. It is strictly positive for
$`\rho\in[0,1)`$, strictly decreasing in $`n`$ because the denominator
$`nD`$ is increasing. Moreover, its decay rate depends on $`\rho`$, with
$`\alpha(n)=\Theta(n^{-2})`$ as $`n\to\infty`$ for $`\rho\in(0,1)`$ and
$`\alpha(n)=1/n`$ at $`\rho=0`$. For the cost term,
$`\delta^{\prime}(n)=-(1+c_{w})p_{\max}m\,\phi^{\prime}\bigl(g(n)\bigr)`$,
and $`\phi^{\prime}`$ is continuous and strictly positive on the compact
interval $`[g(n_{0}),g(1)]`$, so $`|\delta^{\prime}|`$ is bounded below
by some $`\varepsilon>0`$ on $`\mathcal{F}`$. Since $`\delta>0`$ and
$`\delta^{\prime}<0`$ on the interior, $`\mathcal{C}(n)>0`$ there, and
as $`n\to n_{0}^{-}`$ we have $`\delta\to 0^{+}`$ with
$`|\delta^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon`$, so $`\mathcal{C}(n)\to+\infty`$.

#### Step 3: existence of an interior maximizer.

$`M=S^{2}`$ is continuous on the compact interval
$`\mathcal{F}=[1,n_{0}]`$, so it attains a maximum there. At the right
endpoint $`\delta(n_{0})=0`$ gives $`M(n_{0})=0<M(1)`$, and by
hypothesis $`M(\tilde{n})>M(1)`$ for some
$`\tilde{n}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{F})`$. The maximum therefore exceeds
both endpoint values and is attained at some $`n^{\ast}\in(1,n_{0})`$.
At any such interior maximizer $`\log M`$ is differentiable (Step 2), so
$`\alpha(n^{\ast})=\mathcal{C}(n^{\ast})`$.

#### Step 4: uniqueness under log-concavity.

$`\mathcal{C}(n)=-2(\log\delta)^{\prime}(n)`$ is non-decreasing iff
$`(\log\delta)^{\prime}`$ is non-increasing iff $`\delta`$ is
log-concave on $`\mathcal{F}`$. If $`\mathcal{C}`$ is non-decreasing
then $`\alpha-\mathcal{C}`$ is strictly decreasing (strictly decreasing
$`\alpha`$ minus non-decreasing $`\mathcal{C}`$), hence has exactly one
zero. Therefore $`\log M`$ is strictly quasi-concave on
$`\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{F})`$. The interior maximizer $`n^{*}`$ of Step
3 is a stationary point; by uniqueness, it is the only one, so $`n^{*}`$
is the unique interior maximum.

###### Lemma 1 (Uniqueness of the peak).

If $`\delta`$ is log-concave on $`\mathcal{F}`$, the stationary point of
$`\log M`$ is unique, so $`n^{\ast}`$ is the only interior maximum.

The ability $`\beta(n)=p_{\max}\phi(g)`$ is log-concave in $`g`$, and
$`g`$ is affine in $`n`$, so $`\beta`$ is log-concave in $`n`$. Since
$`\log\beta=\log p_{\max}+\log\phi`$ and for the adopted hill gain
$`\phi(g)=g^{\kappa}/(g^{\kappa}+g_{0}^{\kappa})`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
(\log\phi)^{\prime}(g)\;=\;\frac{\kappa\,g_{0}^{\kappa}}{g\bigl(g^{\kappa}+g_{0}^{\kappa}\bigr)},
``` |  | (12) |

which is decreasing in $`g`$, so $`\log\phi`$ is concave in $`g`$ and
hence in $`n`$ because $`g`$ is affine in $`n`$. The same holds for the
power law, where
$`(\log\phi)^{\prime}=\kappa/\bigl(g_{0}u(u^{\kappa}-1)\bigr)`$ with
$`u=1+g/g_{0}`$ is decreasing in $`g`$. For the bounded exponential,
$`\log(1-e^{-u})`$ has second derivative
$`-1/\bigl(4\sinh^{2}(u/2)\bigr)<0`$, and $`u=g/g_{0}`$ is affine, so
log-concavity in $`g`$ follows. The Weibull case needs the chain rule,
since $`u=(g/g_{0})^{\kappa}`$ is convex for the fitted $`\kappa>1`$
(Table [5](#A2.T5 "Table 5 ‣ Fitting 𝛽_eff. ‣ B.1 Implementing TB-MAD ‣ Appendix B Implementation Details ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

The shift also preserves log-concavity, on the whole of $`\mathcal{F}`$.
Recall $`\delta(n)=(1+c_{w})\beta(n)-c_{w}`$ from the definitions above,
and drop the argument $`n`$. Since $`c_{w}>0`$ is a constant,
$`\delta^{\prime}=(1+c_{w})\beta^{\prime}`$ and
$`\delta^{\prime\prime}=(1+c_{w})\beta^{\prime\prime}`$ share
$`\beta^{\prime\prime}`$’s sign.

- •
  If $`\beta^{\prime\prime}(n)\leq 0`$: on $`\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{F})`$
  we have $`\delta(n)>0`$ (Step 1), so
  $`\delta\delta^{\prime\prime}\leq 0\leq(\delta^{\prime})^{2}`$, giving
  $`\delta\delta^{\prime\prime}-(\delta^{\prime})^{2}\leq 0`$ directly;
  log-concavity of $`\beta`$ is not needed in this case.
- •
  If $`\beta^{\prime\prime}(n)>0`$: substituting the derivatives above,

  |  |  |  |  |  |
  |----|----|----|----|----|
  |  | $`\displaystyle\delta\delta^{\prime\prime}-(\delta^{\prime})^{2}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(1+c_{w})\Bigl[(1+c_{w})\bigl(\beta\beta^{\prime\prime}-(\beta^{\prime})^{2}\bigr)-c_{w}\beta^{\prime\prime}\Bigr]`$ |  | (13) |
  |  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq(1+c_{w})\bigl[0-c_{w}\beta^{\prime\prime}\bigr]\leq 0,`$ |  | (14) |

  using $`\beta\beta^{\prime\prime}-(\beta^{\prime})^{2}\leq 0`$
  (log-concavity of $`\beta`$, shown above) and
  $`c_{w}\beta^{\prime\prime}\geq 0`$.

The two cases exhaust every $`n\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{F})`$, so
$`\delta\delta^{\prime\prime}-(\delta^{\prime})^{2}\leq 0`$ throughout,
i.e. $`\delta`$ is log-concave on all of $`\mathcal{F}`$.
Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (Uniqueness of the peak). ‣ Step 4: uniqueness under log-concavity. ‣ A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. ) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
then applies unconditionally: $`\mathcal{C}`$ is non-decreasing on
$`\mathcal{F}`$, so $`\alpha-\mathcal{C}`$ has exactly one zero and
$`n^{\ast}`$ is unique. The only inputs are log-concavity of $`\beta`$
(established above for every candidate in
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Choice of ϕ. ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"))
and $`c_{w}>0`$.

###### Corollary 1 ($`n^{\ast}`$ decreases in $`\rho`$).

Under
Prop. [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Single-peaked accuracy). ‣ The interior best size. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
and
Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (Uniqueness of the peak). ‣ Step 4: uniqueness under log-concavity. ‣ A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. ) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"),
$`n^{\ast}`$ is strictly decreasing in $`\rho`$.

###### Proof.

$`n^{\ast}`$ solves $`\alpha(n)=\mathcal{C}(n)`$, and
$`\mathcal{C}(n)=-2\delta^{\prime}(n)/\delta(n)`$ does not depend on
$`\rho`$. From Step 2,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial\rho}(n)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\frac{1-\rho}{n(1+\rho(n-1))}=\frac{-1}{\bigl(1+\rho(n-1)\bigr)^{2}}\;<\;0
``` |  |

for every $`n>1`$, so $`\alpha`$ falls pointwise as $`\rho`$ rises while
$`\mathcal{C}`$ is fixed. $`\alpha-\mathcal{C}`$ is strictly decreasing
in $`n`$ at the crossing (Step 4), so lowering the $`\alpha`$ shifts the
crossing left and $`n^{\ast}`$ strictly decreases in $`\rho`$. ∎

More correlated errors buy less from an extra vote at every group size,
so the optimum sits at a smaller group.

### A.2 The large-window limit

Write
$`\alpha(n)=n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\prime}/n_{\mathrm{eff}}=(1-\rho)/\bigl(n(1+\rho(n-1))\bigr)`$
for the amplification term of A.1 Step 2. Since
$`\delta^{\prime}(n)=-(1+c_{w})p_{\max}m\,\phi^{\prime}\bigl(g(n)\bigr)`$,
the cost term depends on $`n`$ and $`W`$ only through the room $`g`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C(n)\;=\;-2\frac{\delta^{\prime}(n)}{\delta(n)}\;=\;\widehat{C}\bigl(g(n,W)\bigr),\qquad\widehat{C}(g)\;=\;\frac{2m(1+c_{w})p_{\max}\,\phi^{\prime}(g)}{(1+c_{w})p_{\max}\phi(g)-c_{w}}.
``` |  |

###### Corollary 2 (Slow growth).

Assume the conditions of Prop. 1 and Lemma 1, so that $`C`$ is
non-decreasing in $`n`$ on $`F`$, equivalently
$`\widehat{C}^{\prime}\leq 0`$. Then on any window range where the
maximizer stays interior, $`n^{\ast}(W)`$ is differentiable,
non-decreasing in $`W`$, and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\;\leq\;\frac{dn^{\ast}}{dW}\;=\;\frac{1}{\,m+\alpha^{\prime}(n^{\ast})/\widehat{C}^{\prime}(g)\,}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{m}\;=\;\frac{dn_{\max}}{dW}.
``` |  |

The optimal size therefore never grows faster than the feasibility cap,
and the gap between them widens as the window loosens.

###### Proof.

The interior maximizer solves
$`F(n,W)=\alpha(n)-\widehat{C}\bigl(g(n,W)\bigr)=0`$ with
$`g(n,W)=W-q-c-(n-1)m`$, so $`\partial g/\partial W=1`$ and
$`\partial g/\partial n=-m`$. Hence
$`\partial F/\partial W=-\widehat{C}^{\prime}(g)`$ and
$`\partial F/\partial n=\alpha^{\prime}(n)+m\widehat{C}^{\prime}(g)`$.
By A.1 Step 2, $`\alpha`$ is strictly decreasing, so
$`\alpha^{\prime}<0`$; by hypothesis $`\widehat{C}^{\prime}\leq 0`$, so
$`\partial F/\partial n<0`$ and the implicit function theorem applies.
Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{dn^{\ast}}{dW}=-\frac{\partial F/\partial W}{\partial F/\partial n}=\frac{\widehat{C}^{\prime}(g)}{\alpha^{\prime}(n^{\ast})+m\widehat{C}^{\prime}(g)}.
``` |  |

Numerator and denominator are both non-positive, giving
$`dn^{\ast}/dW\geq 0`$. Dividing through by $`\widehat{C}^{\prime}`$
gives the stated form, and
$`\alpha^{\prime}/\widehat{C}^{\prime}\geq 0`$ gives the upper bound
$`1/m`$. Since $`n_{\max}(W)=1+(W-q-c)/m`$, that bound is exactly
$`dn_{\max}/dW`$. ∎

###### Corollary 3 (The large-window limit).

As $`W\to\infty`$, thinking room stops binding,
$`\delta(n)\to(1+c_{w})p_{\max}-c_{w}`$ is constant in $`n`$, and
$`S(n)^{2}`$ becomes monotone increasing. For $`\rho>0`$ it saturates at
$`\bigl((1+c_{w})p_{\max}-c_{w}\bigr)^{2}/\rho`$ as
$`n_{\mathrm{eff}}\to 1/\rho`$, the classical correlation ceiling. At
$`\rho=0`$ the errors are independent, $`n_{\mathrm{eff}}=n`$ is
unbounded, and no ceiling exists. An interior best size therefore exists
only while the window is scarce, and once it is not, the classical
picture returns.

###### Proof.

$`g(n)\to\infty`$ forces $`\phi\bigl(g(n)\bigr)\to 1`$, so
$`\delta^{\prime}\to 0`$ and $`\widehat{C}\to 0`$. With $`\alpha>0`$
everywhere, $`\log M`$ has no interior stationary point and $`M`$ is
monotone increasing on $`F`$, saturating at the stated ceiling as
$`n_{\mathrm{eff}}\to 1/\rho`$ when $`\rho>0`$. ∎

### B.1 Implementing TB-MAD

#### Fitting $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$.

The three parameters $`(p_{\max},g_{0},\kappa)`$ are fitted to the
single-agent *thinking* sweep by bound-constrained nonlinear least
squares. Let $`{(g_{i},A_{i},N_{i})}`$ denote the per-level mean
accuracies $`A_{i}`$ at reasoning budget $`g_{i}`$, computed from
$`N_{i}`$ samples. We minimize the sample-weighted sum of squared
residuals:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle(\hat{p}_{\max},\hat{g}_{0},\hat{\kappa})=\arg\min_{p_{\max},g_{0},\kappa}\sum_{i}N_{i}\bigl(A_{i}-\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g_{i})\bigr)^{2},`$ |  | (15) |

where
$`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)=p_{\max}g^{\kappa}/(g^{\kappa}+g_{0}^{\kappa})`$
(Eq. [4](#S3.E4 "In Choice of ϕ. ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
with $`p_{\max}\in(0,1]`$ and $`g_{0},\kappa>0`$.

$`\mathcal{D}`$ is the calibration sample and $`g(n,W)`$ the room the

*Calibrated on measured debate cells:*

- $`\bullet`$
  Empirical argmax measures every $`(n,W)`$ cell on $`\mathcal{D}`$ and
  selects the size with the highest $`\hat{A}(n,W)`$
  (Alg. [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
- $`\bullet`$
  Successive halving ([Jamieson and Talwalkar, 2016](#bib.bib41))
  evaluates all sizes on a small sample, keeps the better half, and
  doubles the sample until one size remains or the budget is exhausted
  (Alg. [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
- $`\bullet`$
  Quadratic surface fits a least-squares quadratic in
  $`\{1,n,W,n^{2},W^{2},nW\}`$ to the measured cells and selects its
  best predicted size per window
  (Alg. [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

*Calibrated on single-agent probes only, as ours is:*

- $`\bullet`$
  Learned $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ uses our aggregator with a
  gradient-boosted-tree estimate $`\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{gbt}}`$ fitted
  to the $`|\mathcal{G}|{=}7`$ probe caps, with no parametric shape
  (Alg. [4](#alg4 "Algorithm 4 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
- $`\bullet`$
  Star exponent ([Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)) uses our aggregator,
  $`\hat{\rho}`$, and budget model, replacing $`\phi`$ with
  $`p_{\max}\bigl(1-(1+g/g_{0})^{-\kappa}\bigr)`$, the pow form of
  Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Choice of ϕ. ‣ 3.2 Per-Agent Ability and the Thinking Gain ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
  (Alg. [4](#alg4 "Algorithm 4 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
- $`\bullet`$
  Difficulty mixture ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib34)) estimates
  per-question accuracy $`p_{i}(g)`$ from the probe sweep and simulates
  votes. The original fixes the per-call allowance, so $`p_{i}`$ cannot
  vary with $`n`$; we instead evaluate $`p_{i}`$ at the remaining room
  $`g(n,W)`$, interpolated in $`\log g`$, and add our correlation
  $`\hat{\rho}`$ and tie penalty $`c_{w}`$
  (Alg. [5](#alg5 "Algorithm 5 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

*No calibration:* naive $`n{=}1`$ and naive $`n{=}n_{\max}`$ always use
one agent or the largest group; neither reads data, so neither has an
algorithm.

*Ours:* TB-MAD fits $`(p_{\max},g_{0},\kappa)`$ on the probe sweep and
evaluates $`p_{\max}\phi(g;g_{0},\kappa)`$ at the remaining room for
each size
(Alg. [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).

## Appendix C Generalization to heterogeneous settings

We extend our method to heterogeneous model settings, where agents have
different abilities $`\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}`$. To isolate the effect of
model heterogeneity, we consider a *uniform allocation* setting in which
the $`K`$ models are assigned the same number of agents, while every
agent keeps the same room $`g(n)`$ and each model $`i`$ has fixed
parameters $`(p_{\max,i},g_{0,i},\kappa_{i})`$. The goal is the optimal
number of agents across the $`K`$ models under a total budget of $`n`$
agents, with $`n/K`$ agents assigned to each model.

### C.1 Extended method

#### Per-model edges and candidate subsets.

Under uniform allocation, every agent receives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle g(n)=\mathrm{clip}\bigl(W-q-c-(n-1)m,\;g_{\min},\;g_{\mathrm{cap}}\bigr),`$ |  | (16) |

so heterogeneity enters through the model-specific edge

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{j}]=\delta_{j}(n)=(1+c_{w})\,p_{\max,j}\,\phi_{j}\bigl(g(n)\bigr)-c_{w}.`$ |  | (17) |

A negative edge indicates a model that systematically points toward the
wrong answer, so mixing it with positive-edge models can dilute the
group edge. We therefore allow the $`n`$ agents to draw either from all
$`K`$ models or from any positive-edge singleton when the fitted edges
have mixed signs:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\delta_{i}(n),`$ |  | (18) |

with candidate set

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(n)=\begin{cases}\{\{1,\dots,K\}\}\cup\{\{i\}:\delta_{i}(n)>0\},&\min_{i}\delta_{i}(n)\leq 0<\max_{i}\delta_{i}(n),\\[2.0pt]
\{\{1,\dots,K\}\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}`$ |  | (19) |

Thus $`|S|=1`$ is simply a homogeneous group from model $`i`$, whereas
the full set remains available at every $`n`$.

#### Correlation becomes block structure.

Let $`\hat{\rho}_{w}`$ and $`\hat{\rho}_{b}`$ denote round-$`0`$ error
correlations for agents backed by the same and different models,
respectively. For a subset $`S`$ with equal blocks of $`n/|S|`$ agents,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)=\frac{n}{1+\hat{\rho}_{w}(n/|S|-1)+\hat{\rho}_{b}(n-n/|S|)}.`$ |  | (20) |

This reduces to the homogeneous $`n/(1+\hat{\rho}_{w}(n-1))`$ when
$`|S|=1`$ and, as $`n\to\infty`$, approaches
$`|S|/(\hat{\rho}_{w}+(|S|-1)\hat{\rho}_{b})`$. Hence mixing raises the
asymptotic ceiling whenever $`\hat{\rho}_{b}<\hat{\rho}_{w}`$, i.e.,
cross-model errors are less correlated.

#### Accuracy and operating point.

With common variance $`\sigma^{2}`$,
$`\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_{n})=\sigma^{2}/n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)`$,
giving

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle A(n,S)=\Phi\!\left(\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)}\big/\sigma\right).`$ |  | (21) |

Since $`\Phi`$ is monotone, maximizing accuracy is equivalent to

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(n,S)=\operatorname{sign}\bigl(\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)\bigr)\,n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)\,\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)^{2}.`$ |  | (22) |

We preserve the sign of the margin to avoid amplifying a negative-edge
subset, consistent with the treatment of $`S(n)`$ in
$`\lx@sectionsign`$ [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").
The edges are obtained from single-agent probes, while only
$`\hat{\rho}_{w},\hat{\rho}_{b}`$ require round-$`0`$ measurements
(independent answers before any exchange, not the full multi-round
debate); calibrating all $`K`$ models therefore costs roughly $`K`$
times a set-blind probe rule. As in the homogeneous setting
(§[4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
we charge this round-$`0`$ probe cost against the calibration budget of
every rule that reads $`\hat{\rho}_{w},\hat{\rho}_{b}`$ from the debate
bank rather than treating them as free constants, summed once per
distinct model in the model set;
Tables [12](#A3.T12 "Table 12 ‣ C.3 Results ‣ Appendix C Generalization to heterogeneous settings ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
and [13](#A3.T13 "Table 13 ‣ C.3 Results ‣ Appendix C Generalization to heterogeneous settings ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
already reflect it.

#### The selection gate.

Algorithm [6](#alg6 "Algorithm 6 ‣ Appendix E Limitations and Future work ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
evaluates $`\mathcal{V}(n,S)`$ over the swept grid and
$`\mathcal{S}(n)`$, then selects

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle(\hat{n}^{\ast},\hat{S})\in\arg\max_{n\in\{K,2K,\dots,n_{\max}\},\;S\in\mathcal{S}(n)}\mathcal{V}(n,S).`$ |  | (23) |

The gate admits singletons only when fitted edges disagree in sign;
otherwise it reduces to the homogeneous rule. Allowing singletons at
every $`n`$ would simply favor the strongest model whenever
$`\bar{\delta}`$ dominates, with little corresponding change in
$`n_{\mathrm{eff}}`$, which explains the weaker performance of
*TB-MAD-hetero w/o gate* in
Tables [12](#A3.T12 "Table 12 ‣ C.3 Results ‣ Appendix C Generalization to heterogeneous settings ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
and [13](#A3.T13 "Table 13 ‣ C.3 Results ‣ Appendix C Generalization to heterogeneous settings ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

### C.2 Updated theory analysis

###### Proposition 3 (Heterogeneous aggregation head).

Under uniform allocation into $`|S|`$ equal blocks, a common $`\sigma`$,
within/between round-$`0`$ correlations $`(\rho_{w},\rho_{b})`$, and the
$`\pm 1`$ scoring of
§[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"),
the group accuracy is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle A(n)=\Phi\!\left(\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)\,\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)}\big/\sigma\right),`$ |  |

with $`n_{\mathrm{eff}}`$ as in
Eq. [20](#A3.E20 "In Correlation becomes block structure. ‣ C.1 Extended method ‣ Appendix C Generalization to heterogeneous settings ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").
Whenever $`\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)>0`$,
$`\arg\max_{n}A(n)=\arg\max_{n}n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)\,\bar{\delta}_{S}(n)^{2}`$,
and every step of
§[3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
that uses $`\delta`$ only through $`A(n)`$ holds verbatim with
$`\delta\to\bar{\delta}_{S}`$.

###### Proof sketch.

The covariance of $`(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})`$ has constant diagonal
$`\sigma^{2}`$, constant within-block off-diagonal
$`\rho_{w}\sigma^{2}`$, and constant cross-block off-diagonal
$`\rho_{b}\sigma^{2}`$. Summing gives
$`\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_{n})=n^{-2}\bigl[n\sigma^{2}+(n/|S|-1)n\rho_{w}\sigma^{2}+(n-n/|S|)n\rho_{b}\sigma^{2}\bigr]=\sigma^{2}/n_{\mathrm{eff}}(n,|S|)`$.
The normal approximation of $`\bar{X}_{n}`$ with mean
$`\bar{\delta}_{S}`$ and this variance gives $`A(n)`$; monotonicity of
$`\Phi`$ and $`\sigma>0`$ give the $`\arg\max`$ identity. ∎

We do not have experiments isolating the two results below, so we only
note whether each still holds and under which conditions.

#### Prop. [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Self-consistency dominates the initial-round vote). ‣ Reading cannot beat voting at matched aggregation. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context") (self-consistency dominates the initial-round vote).

Still holds on a model set when no member’s ceiling is raised by peer
text, $`\Delta p_{\max,i}\leq 0`$ for all $`i`$:
$`g_{\mathrm{SC}}\geq g(n)`$ and each $`\phi_{i}`$ increasing make
$`\bar{\delta}`$ larger under self-consistency, so the dominance carries
over with no new assumption. It can fail once some member is weaker than
a peer that sends it information its own model lacks,
$`\Delta p_{\max,i}>0`$. The crossing condition is then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{i}\Delta p_{\max,i}\,\phi_{i}\bigl(g(n)\bigr)\;>\;\sum_{i}p_{\max,i}\bigl(\phi_{i}(g_{\mathrm{SC}})-\phi_{i}(g(n))\bigr),`$ |  | (24) |

the homogeneous
Eq. [11](#S3.E11 "In Proposition 2 (Self-consistency dominates the initial-round vote). ‣ Reading cannot beat voting at matched aggregation. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
summed over members.

#### The interior optimum.

The result extends under the stated restrictions. Existence (Step 3 of
Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. ) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context"))
requires only that $`\bar{\delta}_{S}`$ be continuous, strictly
decreasing, and cross zero once, all of which continue to hold here. In
particular, even when $`g_{0,i}`$ and $`\kappa_{i}`$ vary across
members, $`\bar{\phi}=|S|^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\phi_{i}`$ remains strictly
increasing as a positive average of strictly increasing functions, so
the feasibility boundary $`\bar{\delta}_{S}{=}0`$ has a unique crossing
in $`g`$. Local uniqueness at this crossing also carries over without
assuming log-concavity of $`\bar{\phi}`$. Whenever
$`\bar{\delta}_{S}\to 0^{+}`$ with
$`\bar{\delta}_{S}^{\prime\prime}\leq 0`$, the boundary argument in
Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Single-peakedness (Prop. ) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
applies unchanged, since it relies only on positivity of
$`\bar{\delta}_{S}`$ and the sign of its second derivative. Away from
the boundary, however, log-concavity of $`\bar{\phi}`$ need not hold for
a heterogeneous average and is not assumed here.

### D.2 Voting against talking, and the single agent

Two secondary consequences follow from the same comparison:
self-consistency weakly dominates debate
(Prop. [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Self-consistency dominates the initial-round vote). ‣ Reading cannot beat voting at matched aggregation. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
and forming a group is not always worthwhile ($`\delta\leq 0`$).

#### Where the margin is largest, and how firmly.

Prop. [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Self-consistency dominates the initial-round vote). ‣ Reading cannot beat voting at matched aggregation. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
implies that the self-consistency margin is set by the per-vote ability
consumed by peer reading. It is therefore largest when the generation
window binds and shrinks as the window loosens, eventually vanishing
once both methods reach their best size at sufficiently large $`W`$
(Figures [4](#A4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ Debate against single-agent long-CoT. ‣ D.2 Voting against talking, and the single agent ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")–[5](#A4.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ Debate against single-agent long-CoT. ‣ D.2 Voting against talking, and the single agent ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
The large gap at the tightest window is partly a clipping effect: many
$`(n,W{=}1200)`$ cells collapse to the same clipped generation room,
whereas restricting to unclipped cells reduces the margin from $`+42.0`$
to $`+13.7\%`$. A question-clustered paired bootstrap with $`2000`$
resamples confirms the cell-level effect; applying the same test per
window gives the SC sig. $`W`$ column in
Table [18](#A4.T18 "Table 18 ‣ Debate against single-agent long-CoT. ‣ D.2 Voting against talking, and the single agent ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context").

#### Where the closed form gets the sign wrong.

CSQA is the one task where the closed form recovers the $`\delta`$ sign
poorly, most severely on Gemma3-12B (predicted $`24\%`$, the lowest cell
in
Table [18](#A4.T18 "Table 18 ‣ Debate against single-agent long-CoT. ‣ D.2 Voting against talking, and the single agent ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
The per-cell fit there has $`k{\approx}8.6`$, an unusually steep Hill
curve, fit to only seven calibration points; its leave-one-out RMSE is
over an order of magnitude above its in-sample RMSE, the signature of
overfitting rather than of a genuine near-step response. Combined with
$`c_{w}`$ estimates that are the least stable of any task
(Table [21](#A4.T21 "Table 21 ‣ D.4 Is the error concentration 𝑐_𝑤 constant? ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")),
the fitted $`\phi`$ crosses the break-even $`\beta`$ almost as soon as
the window opens, so the closed form predicts the group winning at every
swept size while the group in fact never does. All five models show the
same direction of error on CSQA, so this is a property of the task’s fit
stability, not an isolated bug in one cell.

#### Is $`\Delta p_{\max}`$ actually positive?

Prop. [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Self-consistency dominates the initial-round vote). ‣ Reading cannot beat voting at matched aggregation. ‣ 3.4 Theory: the collapse regime ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")’s
dominance result is the special case $`\Delta p_{\max}\leq 0`$. We test
directly whether peer text raises the per-agent ceiling, instead of
measuring it through majority vote. On Qwen2.5-7B and Falcon3-7B across
GSM8K and MMLU-Pro, we fix $`g{=}2048`$ and compare a single agent’s
round-$`1`$ accuracy without peer access ($`n{=}1`$) with the
round-$`1`$ accuracy of two agents after each reads the other’s
round-$`0`$ answer ($`n{=}2`$). This isolates the effect of peer text
from voting. $`\widehat{\Delta p_{\max}}`$ is small and sign-mixed, at
$`2`$–$`4\%`$ in magnitude, with the sign determined by model rather
than task: positive for Falcon3-7B and negative for Qwen2.5-7B on both
tasks
(Table [15](#A4.T15 "Table 15 ‣ Is Δ⁢𝑝_max actually positive? ‣ D.2 Voting against talking, and the single agent ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
Thus, peer reading does not reliably raise the ceiling in these cells,
suggesting that debate gains arise primarily from voting rather than
from $`\Delta p_{\max}`$.

### D.4 Is the error concentration $`c_{w}`$ constant?

On a $`K`$-way decision, $`c_{w}`$ is bounded by
$`\tfrac{1}{K-1}\leq c_{w}\leq 1`$, while the observed values lie well
within these bounds. Uniform errors would give $`c_{w}{=}\tfrac{1}{9}`$

### D.5 Does $`\sigma`$ depend on $`n`$?

§[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Group Accuracy and the Synergy Index ‣ 3 Method ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")

whether replacing the fitted constant with this derived, $`n`$-dependent
value changes the prediction.

## Appendix E Limitations and Future work

First, the aggregation model is a tractable approximation rather than an
exact account of group behavior. It relies on equicorrelation, a fixed
error concentration, and an unweighted, average-based decision
threshold, all of which are empirically evaluated rather than assumed to
hold exactly; consensus-weighted aggregation ([Nguyen et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib62)) is not considered. Moreover, although we investigate
heterogeneous model sets, allocation remains uniform across agent
counts; adaptive allocation ([Nguyen et al., 2026b](#bib.bib10)) is an
interesting direction for future work.

Second, the analysis focuses on self-generated computation, including
debate and self-refinement, and does not cover settings where additional
context is retrieved or supplied externally, such as retrieval-augmented
generation.

Third, all five models have at most 12B parameters and short fitted
solution lengths $`g_{0}`$, ranging from $`137`$ to $`478`$ tokens.
Models with thousand-token reasoning chains would have much larger
$`g_{0}`$, shifting the collapse regime to larger windows and
potentially beyond deployed context lengths. We expect the qualitative
shape to transfer, but not the specific operating points.

Fourth, additional revision rounds increase visibility and may shift the
trade-off if their revision benefit does not offset the added context
cost;
Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Self-refinement, full results ‣ Appendix D Additional Results ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")
explores $`R`$ up to 3 for self-refinement.

Fifth, the whole account assumes one window pays for reading and
generation. Under per-agent contexts, reading does not consume
generation room, m = 0 in our accounting, and the interior optimum
disappears
($`\lx@sectionsign`$ [4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Ablations ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Think More or Read More? A Token-Budget Theory of Reasoning with Shared Context")).
````
