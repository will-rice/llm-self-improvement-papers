###### Abstract

Self-correction is an effective technique for maintaining parallel
sampling in discrete diffusion models with minimal performance
degradation. Prior work has explored self-correction at inference time
or during post-training; however, such approaches often suffer from
limited generalization and may impair reasoning. GIDD ([von Rütte et
al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) pioneers pretraining-based self-correction via a
multi-step BERT-style uniform-absorbing objective. However, GIDD relies
on a continuous interpolation-based pipeline with opaque interactions
between uniform transitions and absorbing masks, which complicates
hyperparameter tuning and hinders practical performance. In this work,
we propose a Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion (SCDD) model to
reformulate pretrained self-correction with explicit state transitions
and learn directly in discrete time. Our framework also simplifies the
training noise schedule, eliminates a redundant remasking step, and
relies exclusively on uniform transitions to learn self-correction.
Experiments at GPT-2 scale show that our method enables more efficient
parallel decoding while preserving generation quality. Our code is
available at:
[https://github.com/laaaarrywang/Self-Correcting-Discrete-Diffusion.git](https://github.com/laaaarrywang/Self-Correcting-Discrete-Diffusion.git).

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) ([Radford et al., 2018](#bib.bib8);
[Radford et al., 2019](#bib.bib34); [Brown et al., 2020](#bib.bib7))
have revolutionized the realization of intelligence and significantly
enhanced productivity across domains, including software development
([Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib28); [Gong et al., 2025](#bib.bib27)) and
content generation ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib26)). LLMs adopt an
autoregressive (AR) generation pipeline to predict the next token
sequentially. Despite its simplicity, next-token generation becomes
quite time-consuming for long sequences. Alternatively, motivated by the
success of BERT ([Devlin et al., 2019](#bib.bib24)), masked diffusion
language models (MDLMs) ([Austin et al., 2021](#bib.bib4); [Shi et al.,
2024](#bib.bib3); [Ou et al., 2025](#bib.bib1); [Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2); [Gat et al., 2024](#bib.bib6); [Campbell et al.,
2022](#bib.bib5); [Nie et al., 2025](#bib.bib11); [Ye et al.,
2025](#bib.bib18)) propose a probabilistic, order-agnostic generation
paradigm, which potentially leads to much reduced inference latency due
to parallel generation and boosts the development of reasoning ([Song et
al., 2025](#bib.bib10); [Khanna et al., 2025](#bib.bib9)) and agentic
artificial intelligence (AI).

Despite their potential for fast parallel generation, mainstream MDLMs
typically achieve only comparable performance to AR by decoding a
limited number of tokens per generation step using non-trivial sampling
techniques. Decoding more tokens often disrupts inherent token
dependencies and degrades reasoning performance. To address this issue,
various acceleration strategies have been proposed, such as
semi-autoregressive generation with block diffusion ([Arriola et al.,
2025](#bib.bib21)), block-level KV cache ([Ma et al., 2025](#bib.bib22);
[Wu et al., 2025](#bib.bib17)), and distillation-based models ([Chen et
al., 2025a](#bib.bib19); [Chen et al., 2025b](#bib.bib30); [Qian et al.,
2026](#bib.bib20); [Zheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib25)). However, these
approaches inevitably increase model complexity, making them harder to
maintain and scale.

To avoid these complexities, we focus on the simple and effective
self-correction techniques for maximizing the parallel generation
abilities ([Jiang et al., 2025](#bib.bib12)). A notable example is the
confidence- or entropy-based remasking step used during parallel
generation to rectify erroneous tokens and revise previous mistakes
([Nie et al., 2025](#bib.bib11); [Wang et al., 2026](#bib.bib31); [Song
et al., 2025](#bib.bib10); [Chen et al., 2025b](#bib.bib30)). Rather
than relying solely on the inherent generation ability of MDLMs, [Kim et
al. (2025)](#bib.bib16) further enhances self-correction by fine-tuning
pre-trained MDLMs with minimal effort. However, due to the limited
generalization ability of post-training ([Ouyang et al.,
2022](#bib.bib36); [Sutskever and Patel, 2025](#bib.bib29); [Yue et al.,
2025](#bib.bib23)), self-correction remains underexplored.

Figure 1: Example of self-correction between two consecutive denoising
steps (127→128). Generated by SCDD ($`p_{u}=0.2`$, trained on OWT) under
128 total denoising steps. Inappropriate tokens are directly corrected
without remasking.

To improve the generalization of self-corrections, GIDD ([von Rütte et
al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) studied the pretraining-based self-correction
via a multi-step BERT-style objective that incorporates additional
uniform transitions. However, its interpolation-based pipeline
introduces opaque interactions between uniform transitions and absorbing
masks, complicating hyperparameter tuning and hindering practical
performance. Moreover, although GIDD enables token revision through
additional uniform transitions, its reverse process still retains
remasking behavior, which can make correction less direct and reduce the
effective correction capacity under few-step parallel generation. To
tackle these limitations, we propose a Self-Correcting Discrete
Diffusion (SCDD) model to reformulate pretrained self-correction with
clear and explicit state transitions in discrete time. Our framework
also simplifies the training noise schedule, eliminates a redundant
remasking step, and relies exclusively on uniform transitions to learn
self-correction. To summarize, our contributions are three-fold:

- •
  We redesign the forward process with Signal-to-Noise ratio (SNR) -
  informed parameters, thus providing separate control over different
  types of forward noising rates while maintaining clarity in marginal
  distribution representation at the same time.
- •
  Our training and inference pipeline is clean and engineering-light.
  More specifically, during training, the model is trained on
  theoretical ELBO loss without additional re-weighting; during
  inference, SCDD requires no post-hoc heuristic samplers and no
  hyperparameter-tuning. All token generation and correction are
  performed solely by running the backward process derived from Bayes’
  rule.
- •
  To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to train a diffusion
  language model that achieves self-correction completely free of
  remasking during generation. Experiments conducted at the GPT-2 scale
  indicate that our model consistently surpasses existing benchmarks
  over a few standard datasets, achieving lower generative perplexity
  without sacrificing sample diversity in parallel generation settings.

````math
## 2 Preliminaries

Masked diffusion models (MDM/MDLM) ([Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2);
[Shi et al., 2024](#bib.bib3)) are a class of generative models that
corrupt data by gradually replacing clean tokens with a special \[mask\]
token. The forward process starts from a clean token
$`\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{V}^{/\mathbf{m}}`$ and progressively increases
the probability of transitioning to $`\mathbf{m}`$. Formally, for time
points $`s,t`$ with $`0\leq s<t\leq 1`$, the forward kernel is defined
as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t|s}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}+(1-\alpha_{t|s})\,\mathbf{m}\right),
``` |  |

where $`\alpha_{t|s}=\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{s}`$ and $`\alpha_{t}\in[0,1]`$
is a noise scheduling function controlling the survival rate of the
token. The induced marginal is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t}\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha_{t})\,\mathbf{m}\right).
``` |  |

As $`t\rightarrow 1`$, the process collapses to the prior
$`q_{1}(\mathbf{z}_{1})=\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{z}_{1}=\mathbf{m}\}}`$,
meaning that the clean token is replaced by the \[mask\] token.

Given this forward process, sampling proceeds in the reverse direction
using a learned parametric kernel
$`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ that denoises \[mask\]
tokens back to clean tokens. The denoiser is trained by minimizing the
negative ELBO, which reduces to

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{z}_{t}}\left[\frac{\alpha_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\log\left(\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)^{\top}\mathbf{x}\right)\right],
``` |  |

where $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)`$ is a denoiser that
predicts the clean token distribution.

Limitations. Despite its scalable training, MDLM lacks an explicit
self-correction mechanism to revise low-quality tokens from earlier
steps, degrading the quality of parallel generation ([Jiang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib12)). Consequently, MDLM typically decodes only a few
tokens per step to achieve competitive reasoning performance ([Zhao et
al., 2025](#bib.bib44); [Rojas et al., 2026](#bib.bib43)), often making
it even slower than autoregressive (AR) alternatives with speculative
decoding.

## 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model

To maximize parallel generation potential, we reformulate the MDLM
pipeline to enhance self-correction during pre-training, a stage known
to yield strong generalization performance ([Sutskever and Patel,
2025](#bib.bib29)). Motivated by BERT’s success in semantic
understanding ([Devlin et al., 2019](#bib.bib24)), we incorporate
uniform transitions alongside the absorbing-mask in the forward process.
While related ideas have been explored in prior works, such as GIDD
([von Rütte et al., 2025](#bib.bib38))¹¹ 1 We refer readers to Appendix
Section [F](#A6 "Appendix F Related Works ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for further discussion., our approach differs by introducing clear and
explicit state transitions and eliminating the need for an additional
remasking step completely. This simplification substantially eases
hyperparameter tuning and improves parallel generation through enhanced
self-correction.

### 3.1 Forward Noising Process

For clarity, we first derive the model for the single token case before
generalizing it to the sequence case. We consider a discrete-time
forward Markov noising process $`q`$ that transforms the clean data
$`\mathbf{x}`$ into latent variables
$`\{\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{T}`$. A typical choice of time grid
$`\{t_{i}\}_{i=0}^{T}`$ is equal-spaced grid
$`\{\frac{i}{T}\}_{i=0}^{T}`$, which is also the one that we use
throughout this paper. For notation convenience, we define
$`\mathbf{z}_{t_{-1}}=\mathbf{x}`$ and interchangeably use them to refer
to the clean data. The joint distribution of clean data and latent
variables follows

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{0},...,\mathbf{z}_{1})=q_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\prod_{i=0}^{T}q(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}),`$ |  | (1) |

where $`q_{\text{data}}`$ is the unknown data distribution. Let
$`\mathbf{u}=\tfrac{1}{K}(\mathbf{1}_{K+1}-\mathbf{m})`$ denote the
probability vector for the uniform distribution over all non-\[mask\]
tokens. We use $`\mathbf{u}`$ to refer to both the vector and the
distribution itself. To elicit self-correction capability, we are
interested in forward processes whose marginal distributions of
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ takes the form

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\text{Cat}\Bigl(\mathbf{z}_{t};\gamma_{t}\bigl(\rho_{t}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\mathbf{u}\bigr)+(1-\gamma_{t})\mathbf{m}\Bigr),`$ |  | (2) |

where $`\rho_{t},\gamma_{t}:[0,1]\to[0,1]`$ with boundary conditions
$`\rho_{1}=\gamma_{1}=0`$ and $`\rho_{0},\gamma_{0}\approx 1`$.
Moreover, $`\rho_{0},\gamma_{0}\to 1^{-}`$ as $`T\to\infty`$. The
mixture of $`\mathbf{x}`$, $`\mathbf{u}`$ and $`\mathbf{m}`$ in the
probability vector ensures the diffusion model encounters both uniform
noise and \[mask\] tokens during training. At time $`t=0`$, the latent
variable $`\mathbf{z}_{0}`$ is slightly corrupted relative to
$`\mathbf{x}`$; however, this corruption probability vanishes as the
number of timesteps $`T`$ approaches infinity. At time $`t=1`$, the
clean data is completely corrupted and becomes \[mask\]. For
intermediate times $`t\in(0,1)`$, the token can be replaced by another
non-\[mask\] token or masked.

We refer to $`\rho_{t}\gamma_{t}`$, the probability mixture ratio of
$`\mathbf{x}`$, as the probability that “$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ *retains*
$`\mathbf{x}`$”, meaning that the event
$`\{\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{x}\}`$ occurs due to an explicit stay-in
mechanism rather than by coincidence from uniform sampling. In
particular, this excludes the case where $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ is sampled
from $`\mathbf{u}`$ and happens to equal $`\mathbf{x}`$. Similarly,
$`\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t})`$ is referred to as the probability that
“$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ is sampled from $`\mathbf{u}`$”. The parameters can
also be individually interpreted as two kinds of signal-to-noise ratios
(SNR):

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{t}=q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}\mid\mathbf{x}),\quad\rho_{t}=q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\text{ retains }\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\mathbf{x}),`$ |  |

where $`\gamma_{t}`$ measures the SNR of absorbing mask, and
$`\rho_{t}`$ measures the SNR of uniform transitions. For notation
convenience, we set $`\rho_{-1}=\gamma_{-1}=1`$.

There are infinite forward noising processes with marginals agree with
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
However, to eliminate the remasking behavior during token generation
(i.e., the backward process), the \[mask\] state $`\mathbf{m}`$ needs to
be an absorbing state of the forward process. Therefore, we make an
additional assumption that $`\rho_{t},\gamma_{t}`$ are monotonically
decreasing sequences. Under this mild assumption, the marginal in
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
can be realized by a Markov chain of which $`\mathbf{m}`$ is an
absorbing state, similar to masked diffusion models ([Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2); [Shi et al., 2024](#bib.bib3)).

###### Proposition 1.

When $`\rho_{t},\gamma_{t}`$ are monotonically decreasing, the following
forward Markov transition kernel induces the marginal distribution
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q`$ | $`\displaystyle(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s}):=`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\begin{cases}\gamma_{t|s}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{t}\}}\rho_{t|s}+\tfrac{1-\rho_{t|s}}{K}\right),&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m},\\
1-\gamma_{t|s},&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m},\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m},\\
1,&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m},\end{cases}`$ |  | (3) |

where $`t,s\in\{t_{-1},t_{0},...,t_{T}\}`$ are two adjacent time points
satisfying $`t>s`$, $`\rho_{t|s}:=\tfrac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}`$, and
$`\gamma_{t|s}:=\tfrac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}`$.

###### Proof.

Since $`\rho_{t}`$ and $`\gamma_{t}`$ are monotonically decreasing, the
above transition kernel is well-defined. The Markovian forward process
implied by the forward kernel
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ Proposition 1. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
can be equivalently characterized as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}Q_{t|s}\right)
``` |  |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Q_{t|s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho_{t|s}\gamma_{t|s}\mathbf{I}+\frac{(1-\rho_{t|s})\gamma_{t|s}}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}&(1-\gamma_{t|s})\mathbf{1}\\[2.0pt]
\mathbf{0}^{\top}&1\end{array}\right)
``` |  | (4) |

is the forward transition matrix, where $`\mathbf{I}`$ and
$`\mathbf{1}`$ are abbreviations of the $`K`$-dimensional identity
matrix and $`K`$-dimensional all-ones vector. Let $`i`$ be the unique
index such that $`t_{i}=t`$, the marginal distribution of
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ is therefore

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\text{Cat}(\mathbf{z}_{t};\mathbf{x}^{\top}\bar{Q}_{t}),
``` |  | (5) |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{i}Q_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{A}_{t}&(1-\prod_{j=0}^{i}\gamma_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}})\mathbf{1}\\
\mathbf{0}^{\top}&1\end{array}\right)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{A}_{t}&(1-\gamma_{t})\mathbf{1}\\
\mathbf{0}^{\top}&1\end{array}\right).`$ |  |

$`\bar{A}_{t}`$ is greatly simplified due to the idempotence ²² 2 Define
$`\mathbf{P}:=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/K`$. Since
$`\mathbf{P}^{2}=\mathbf{P}`$, for any scalars $`a,b`$, we have
$`(a\mathbf{I}+(1-a)\mathbf{P})(b\mathbf{I}+(1-b)\mathbf{P})=ab\mathbf{I}+(1-ab)\mathbf{P}`$.

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bar{A}_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{i}\Bigl(\rho_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}\gamma_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}\mathbf{I}+\tfrac{(1-\rho_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}})\gamma_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\Bigl)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{i}\rho_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}\gamma_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}\Bigr)\mathbf{I}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\frac{1}{K}\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{i}\gamma_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}-\prod_{j=0}^{i}\rho_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}\gamma_{t_{j}|t_{j-1}}\Bigr)\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\gamma_{t}\left(\rho_{t}I+\tfrac{1}{K}(1-\rho_{t})\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\right),`$ |  |

Therefore, substituting the closed-form expression of $`\bar{Q}_{t}`$
into
([5](#S3.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ Proof. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
we obtain that, for any ordinary token $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\gamma_{t}\left(\rho_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{x}\}}+\frac{1-\rho_{t}}{K}\right),
``` |  |

while

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       q(\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}\mid\mathbf{x})=1-\gamma_{t}.
       ```                                                       |     |

This is exactly the marginal distribution specified in
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
∎

#### Forward Process in Continuous Time.

The discrete-time Markov chain introduced above can be extended to
continuous time via a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) model.
Although deriving SCDD doesn’t require CTMC theory, we include this
analysis for the sake of completeness. Moreover, when we borrow the
forward transition rate concept, we obtain a better interpretation of
the noise schedules of our model. As before, we assume that
$`\gamma_{t}`$ and $`\rho_{t}`$ are differentiable, decreasing functions
of time $`t\in[0,1]`$.

Let $`s`$ and $`t`$ be two adjacent time points with $`\Delta t=t-s>0`$.
We define the infinitesimal generator $`R_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)`$ by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}):=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\right)\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{s}\\
\ -\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\Bigl(\tfrac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\tfrac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\mathbf{m}\Bigr),&\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}\\
0,&\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}`$ |  | (6) |

The following lemma proves that
([6](#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
is the forward transition rate of the process
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ Proposition 1. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
We leave the complete proof to Appendix
[A](#A1 "Appendix A Proof of Lemma ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction").

###### Lemma 2.

Let $`R_{t}`$ be defined as in
([6](#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Then, $`R_{t}`$ is a valid infinitesimal generator (rate matrix) in the
sense of [Lipman et al. (2024)](#bib.bib33); [Gat et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib6), i.e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})\geq 0,\forall\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{z}_{s},\ \sum_{\mathbf{z}_{t}}R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})=0.
``` |  |

Moreover, the discrete-time forward kernel
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ Proposition 1. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
admits the continuous-time expansion:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}}+\Delta t\,R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})+o(\Delta t).
``` |  |

Therefore, the probability evolution induced by $`R_{t}`$ is the
continuous-time limit of the discrete Markov chain
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ Proposition 1. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).

###### Remark 3.

When $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, $`\rho_{t}`$ and $`\gamma_{t}`$
govern the forward transition rates for sampling $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ from
$`\mathbf{u}`$ and setting $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ to \[mask\], respectively.
Thus, in addition to their SNR interpretation in the marginals, they act
as explicit and independent controllers of the two noise mechanisms. In
contrast, under the same marginal representation, GIDD couples the
absorbing-mask and uniform-transition components in its forward rates,
preventing independent control without compromising the clean marginal
form. SCDD preserves this marginal structure while decoupling the two
transition rates. See Table
[1](#S1.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for a generator-level comparison and Appendices
[A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Forward Rate Matrix ‣ Appendix A Proof of Lemma ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
and
[D.2](#A4.SS2 "D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for further discussion.

### 3.2 Backward Denoising Process

Follow the same notations, the true posterior
$`q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})`$ derived from Bayes’
rule is given by:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})=`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbf{\rho}_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\bigl(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\frac{1}{K}\bigr),&\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}\\[6.0pt]
(1-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m})\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}\right)\\[2.0pt]
\quad+\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m}\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}},&\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}\end{cases}`$ |  | (7) |

We refer to the Appendix
[B](#A2 "Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for the details of the derivation.

#### Efficient Parallel Self-Correction.

Since we have \[mask\] being as an absorbing state in the forward
process, it follows that there is no transition from non-\[mask\] tokens
to \[mask\], i.e., no remasking, in the backward process. By eliminating
the redundant remasking step, SCDD acquires more correction capacity for
any given number of inference steps. In particular, SCDD can be twice as
efficient as purely remasking-based self-correcting discrete diffusion
models, since it only needs one step to correct a token instead of two.
As a result, it potentially improves the generation quality, especially
in few-step generation scenarios.

Since we don’t know the true clean data $`\mathbf{x}`$, we follow
previous work ([Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015](#bib.bib35); [Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2)) to parameterize the backward process as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}):=q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t))`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbf{\rho}_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\bigl(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\frac{1}{K}\bigr),&\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\\[6.0pt]
(1-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m})\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}\right)\\
\quad+\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m}\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}},&\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m},\end{cases}`$ |  | (8) |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathcal{V}\times[0,1]`$ | $`\displaystyle\to\Delta^{K+1},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)`$ | $`\displaystyle\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t),`$ |  |

is a denoising neural network that predicts clean data $`\mathbf{x}`$
given the latent variable $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ at time $`t`$. We omit the
arguments of $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}`$ when no confusion arises. Such
parameterization indeed defines a valid density
$`p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ conditional $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$,
see Appendix
[B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2 Validity of Backward Process ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for more details. We also derive the corresponding backward transition
rate under CTMC theory in Appendix
[B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 Derivation of Continuous-time Backward Transition Rate ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for completeness.

We further impose the Zero Masking Probabilities constraint on the
denoising network $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}`$ as in MDLM ([Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2)), i,e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)^{\top}\mathbf{m}=0,\quad\forall\ \mathbf{z}_{t},t.
``` |  |

However, we release the Carry-Over Unmasking constraint in MDLM to allow
token self-correction during generation: even if
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, we still allow $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}`$
to assign nonzero probability mass to tokens other than
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ so that previously unmasked tokens can be revised in
later denoising steps.

Additionally, we set the prior distribution to be
$`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{1}):=\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{1}=\mathbf{m})`$,
indicating that we start the backward process from \[mask\]. With these
parameterizations, we define the generative model of $`\mathbf{x}`$ as:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x})`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\int p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{1})p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{T}p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{0:1}`$ |  | (9) |

### 3.3 ELBO

To train a diffusion model, we leverage variational inference and
minimize the usual negative evidence lower bound (NELBO) as in previous
works ([Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015](#bib.bib35); [Ho et al.,
2020](#bib.bib32); [Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2)). The discrete-time
NELBO for finite $`T`$ is given by:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{NELBO}}:=\underbrace{-\mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}_{0})\big]}_{\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{reconstruction}}}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{1}\mid\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{1})\big)\right]}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}}}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{T}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}},\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}})\big)\right]}_{\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}},`$ |  | (10) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}=\begin{cases}-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)\log\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right],&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\\[4.0pt]
-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K})\log\left(\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}\right)\right],&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}`$ |  | (11) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]}\mathbb{E}_{q}\Bigl[\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{m}}\left(\frac{(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right)-\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\Bigr],&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\\
\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\log(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\right],&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}`$ |  | (12) |

where $`q`$ is the abbreviation of $`q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{0:1})`$
defined in
([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
and $`\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ is given by
([11](#S3.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 3.3 ELBO ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
up to $`\theta`$-independent additive constants. A distinctive feature
of our loss function is that the latent state $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$
contributes to the gradient regardless of whether it is masked
($`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$) or unmasked
($`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$), which is similar to GIDD ([von Rütte
et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)). Note that if $`\rho_{t}\equiv 1`$, the
diffusion loss
([11](#S3.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 3.3 ELBO ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
is reduced to MDLM diffusion loss ([Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2))
since the forward noising process doesn’t involve uniform noise anymore.
We left the derivation of discrete-time ELBO to Appendix
[C](#A3 "Appendix C Analysis of Training Loss ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"),
and the discussion of the relationship with MDLM to Appendix
[D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 SCDD and MDLM ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction").

#### Continuous-time ELBO.

By taking $`T\to\infty`$, the discrete-time negative ELBO will converge
to its continuous-time extension, denoted by
$`\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{NELBO}}`$. This will give tight
approximation to ELBO ([Kingma et al., 2021](#bib.bib37); [Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2)). Among the three components in
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.3 ELBO ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
$`\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}}\equiv 0`$ by construction. When
$`T\to\infty`$,
$`\mathcal{\lim}_{T\to\infty}\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{reconstruction}}=0`$
due to the fact that $`\mathbf{z}_{0}`$ converges in distribution to
$`\mathbf{x}`$ when $`T\to\infty`$. Therefore, the only component left
in $`\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{NELBO}}`$ is
$`\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$,
which is given in
([12](#S3.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ 3.3 ELBO ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
up to $`\theta`$-independent additive constants. See Appendix
[C](#A3 "Appendix C Analysis of Training Loss ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for derivation of continuous-time ELBO.

### 3.4 Generalization to Sequences of Tokens

In practice, we operate on sequences of tokens rather than single
tokens. Our generalization follows [Austin et al. (2021)](#bib.bib4);
[Sahoo et al. (2024)](#bib.bib2); [von Rütte et al. (2025)](#bib.bib38).
Let $`\mathbf{x}^{1:L}`$ be a length-$`L`$ sequence of clean data, and
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L}`$ be a length-$`L`$ sequence of latent variables
at time $`t`$. The forward noising process is applied to each of the
$`L`$ positions in the sequence independently to corrupt the clean data
$`\mathbf{x}^{1:L}`$. The model $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}`$ now accepts a
whole sequence $`\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L}`$ as input, and outputs
$`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L},t)=(\mathbf{x}^{l}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L},t))_{l=1}^{L}`$,
where $`\mathbf{x}^{l}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L},t)`$ is the
predicted distribution of clean data at position $`l`$. The denoising
process admits a factorization over all positions, i.e.,
$`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}^{1:L}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L})=\prod_{l=1}^{L}p_{\theta}\left(\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\ell}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L}\right)`$.
The final training objective is defined as the sum of the per-token
NELBOs across all positions.

### 3.5 Sampling

We perform the usual ancestral sampling as in other works in this field
([Shi et al., 2024](#bib.bib3); [Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2); [von
Rütte et al., 2025](#bib.bib38); [Wang et al., 2026](#bib.bib31)). To
sample from the model $`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x})`$, the backward process
is initiated from a sequence with \[mask\] at all positions, and runs
iteratively according to
$`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}^{1:L}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L})`$ following
the prefixed sampling time schedule. Given the current intermediate
sample $`\mathbf{z}_{t}^{1:L}`$, the model samples in parallel across
all positions in the sequence for the next time step $`s<t`$ in the
schedule.

### 4.1 Experiment Setup

standard datasets, LM1B (One Billion Words Dataset, [Chelba et al.

### 4.4 Ablation Study

We carry out three ablation studies to investigate into self-correction
behaviors. Specifically, we want to know: 1) If a larger uniform noise
ratio would encourage more aggressive (parallel) self-correction? 2) How
does the timing of peak uniform noise affect the temporal strength of
self-correction throughout the generation process? 3) Does “higher
correction rate” imply more successful self-corrections, or only more
frequent but pointless token revisions?

#### Parallel Self-Correction.

![Refer to caption](2603.02230v2/Figures/ablation1-1.png)

Figure 2: Correction Rate per Step versus total number of denoising
steps at different maximum uniform noise ratios. Reported values are
averaged from 128 independently generated sequences.

To answer the first question, we train our model on Wikitext-103
([Merity et al., 2017](#bib.bib52)) with maximum uniform noise ratio
$`p_{u}\in\{\text{1e-6},0.05,0.1,0.2\}`$, see Appendix
[E.1](#A5.SS1 "E.1 Training Details. ‣ Appendix E Experimental Details ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for training details. Then, we define the following metric:

|     |                                                        |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \textit{Correction Rate per Step}=\frac{C}{L\times N},
       ```                                                     |     |

where $`C=\text{\# Total Corrections}`$, $`L=\text{\# Context Length}`$,
and $`N=\text{\# Denoising Steps}`$. Note that the Correction Rate per
Step is derived by applying a normalization factor $`N`$ to the standard
Correction Rate. This normalization compensates for the inherent bias
toward higher total corrections in generation with more denoising steps
as we see in Table
[5](#S4.T5 "Table 5 ‣ Correction Capacity. ‣ 4.3 Unconditional Language Generation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction").

For a fixed $`N`$, Figure
[2](#S4.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 4.4 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
shows that total corrections increases as $`p_{u}`$ increases,
consistent with the intuition that more uniform noise in the forward
process would incur more corrections in the backward process. Notably,
Correction Rate per Step is negatively correlated with the number of
total denoising steps, indicating more active parallel self-correction
at fewer-step generation scenarios. In contrast, self-correction is
amortized throughout the whole process in an extended sampling horizon,
resulting in a modest increase in total corrections.

#### Dynamics of Self-Correction.

![Refer to caption](2603.02230v2/Figures/tpeak1_512.png)

Figure 3: Cumulative correction ratio over 512 denoising steps, defined
as the fraction of total corrections completed by step $`s`$. Results
are averaged over 128 independently generated sequences.

We train the same model on Wikitext-103 data, but with a different noise
schedule that attains the maximum uniform noise ratio at a general time
point $`t_{\text{peak}}`$ (see Appendix
[E.4](#A5.SS4 "E.4 Noise Schedule of SCDD ‣ Appendix E Experimental Details ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for a detailed discussion of noise schedules). From Figure
[3](#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ Dynamics of Self-Correction. ‣ 4.4 Ablation Study ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
We see a clear pattern that the model shifts the timing of its
self-correction to align with the peak uniform noise time of the
training schedule. When the maximum noise ratio occurs later in the
forward process (higher $`t_{\text{peak}}`$), the model tends to
self-correct in the early stage during generation, as shown by the
concave trajectory of the $`t_{\text{peak}}=0.75`$ curve in the first
half. In this setting, the model completes nearly 40% of its total
correction within the first 200 steps. Conversely, when the noise peak
is shifted to the beginning of the forward process
($`t_{\text{peak}}=0.25`$), the correction is significantly delayed,
resulting in a convex curve where the majority of the corrections occur
only in the final 100 denoising steps.

#### Exact Recovery of Corrupted Tokens.

So far, we have seen that SCDD substantially increases the overall
correction rate during generation. To directly verify that these
corrections are beneficial rather than spurious revisions, we conduct a
controlled corruption–recovery experiment on clean OWT validation
sequences. For each sequence, we randomly select $`K\in\{5,10,20,50\}`$
positions, replace their tokens with uniformly sampled alternatives, and
apply one SCDD ($`p_{u}=0.2`$) denoising step at the last-step noise
level, corresponding to step 127 in a 128-step sampling schedule. We
report the *touch rate*, the fraction of corrupted tokens modified by
the model, and the *recovery rate*, the fraction of corrupted tokens
exactly restored to their original clean values. As shown in
Table [6](#S4.T6 "Table 6 ‣ 4.5 Benchmark Performance ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"),
SCDD touches nearly all corrupted tokens and exactly recovers
$`64.4\%`$–$`69.4\%`$ of them. Moreover, under the strongest corruption
level $`K=50`$, Gen PPL drops from $`154.8`$ to $`25.5`$ after one
denoising step. These results show that SCDD performs meaningful content
recovery rather than merely making frequent token edits.

## 5 Conclusion

In this work, we propose the Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion (SCDD)
model that enhances self-correction to enable more effective parallel
generation. In contrast to post-hoc self-corrective samplers, our
approach explicitly learns self-correction during pretraining, leading
to improved generalization. Compared to GIDD, our forward transition
defines clear and explicit state transitions and removes a redundant
remasking step, making the model easier to tune and more effective in
few-step parallel generation scenarios. Empirically, SCDD demonstrates
better generation performance and stronger parallel self-correction
capability on LM1B and OWT. For future work, we are interested in
scaling up SCDD to acquire more generalizable self-correction ability,
as well as exploring reinforcement learning methods to further enhance
self-correction ability in reasoning tasks.

## Appendix A Proof of Lemma [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Lemma 2. ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")

###### Proof.

Firstly, $`R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})\geq 0`$ for
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{z}_{s}`$ and the row sum condition follows
by construction, proving that $`R_{t}`$ is a valid infinitesimal
generator.

Fix $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$ and consider two adjacent time
points $`s`$ and $`t=s+\Delta t`$. From the discrete forward kernel
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ Proposition 1. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
we have the following three transition probabilities:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=1-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}},`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\text{ is sampled from }\mathbf{u}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}\left(1-\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\right),`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\text{ retains }\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\frac{\gamma_{t}\rho_{t}}{\gamma_{s}\rho_{s}}.`$ |  |

Using first-order Taylor expansions (at $`t`$) for $`\gamma`$ and
$`\rho`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}=1+\Delta t\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}=1+\Delta t\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}+o(\Delta t).`$ |  |

Substituting these gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\Delta t\left(-\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)+o(\Delta t).`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\text{ is sampled from }\mathbf{u}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\Delta t\left(-\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\right)+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\text{ retains }\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=1+\Delta t\left(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\right)+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |

These match exactly the off-diagonal entries of $`R_{t}`$ and the
diagonal term. Hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}}+\Delta t\,R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |

where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}):=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\right)\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{s}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\Bigl(\tfrac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\tfrac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\mathbf{m}\Bigr),&\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}\\
0,&\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}`$ |  |

∎

### A.1 Forward Rate Matrix

Equivalently, the above forward rate admits a compact matrix form.
Following mask diffusion models ([Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2); [Shi
et al., 2024](#bib.bib3)), we adopt the convention that columns
represent current states, and all columns sum to zero. Under this
convention, the (time-inhomogeneous) generator matrix can be written as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\,\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)}_{\text{MDLM masking structure}}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\,\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\bigr)}_{\text{uniform mixing among non-mask tokens}}.`$ |  | (13) |

The first term is the MDLM masking generator; the second is the standard
uniform generator $`\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)`$
right-multiplied by
$`\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\bigr)`$, which zeros out
the \[mask\] column so that \[mask\] stays absorbing while uniform
transitions only shuffle the $`K`$ non-mask tokens. Both terms have zero
column sums, hence so does $`\mathbf{R}_{t}`$.

###### Proposition 4 (Consistency of the matrix generator).

Let $`\mathbf{R}_{t}`$ be defined in
([13](#A1.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ A.1 Forward Rate Matrix ‣ Appendix A Proof of Lemma ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Then the forward rate induced by $`\mathbf{R}_{t}`$ via
$`R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})=\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{R}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}`$
coincides with the forward rate
([6](#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).

###### Proof.

Using the identity
$`\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}=\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\bigr)`$,
we can equivalently write
([13](#A1.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ A.1 Forward Rate Matrix ‣ Appendix A Proof of Lemma ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{t}=\left(\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\Bigr)\mathbf{I}-\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\right)\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\bigr).`$ |  |

If $`\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}`$, the trailing factor gives
$`\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\bigr)\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{0}`$,
hence $`\mathbf{R}_{t}\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{0}`$ and
$`R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{m})=0`$ for all $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$, i.e.,
\[mask\] is absorbing.

If $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, then
$`\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{s}=0`$ and
$`\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{s}=1`$, it follows that
$`\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\bigr)\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{s}`$,
$`\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{s}-\mathbf{m}`$,
and
$`\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{s}-\mathbf{u}`$.
Hence

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\bigl(\mathbf{z}_{s}-\mathbf{m}\bigr)+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\bigl(\mathbf{z}_{s}-\mathbf{u}\bigr)=\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\Bigr)\mathbf{z}_{s}-\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\mathbf{m}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}\Bigr),`$ |  |

and therefore

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{R}_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}=\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\Bigr)\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{s}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\Bigl(\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\mathbf{m}\Bigr),`$ |  |

which matches the scalar expression. ∎

###### Remark 5 (degeneration to MDLM).

We recall that the forward rate matrix in MDLM ([Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2)) can be written, under the same column-sum-zero
convention, as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{t}^{\mathrm{MDLM}}=\frac{(\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{MDLM}})^{\prime}}{\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{MDLM}}}\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)`$ |  | (14) |

When $`\rho_{t}\equiv 1`$ (thus $`\rho_{t}^{\prime}/\rho_{t}\equiv 0`$),
the uniform-mixing term in
([13](#A1.E13 "Equation 13 ‣ A.1 Forward Rate Matrix ‣ Appendix A Proof of Lemma ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
vanishes and the generator reduces to
$`\mathbf{R}_{t}=\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{m}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr)`$,
i.e., $`\mathbf{R}_{t}^{\mathrm{MDLM}}`$ under the reparameterization
$`\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{MDLM}}=\gamma_{t}`$; see
Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 SCDD and MDLM ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for a further discussion of the relation between SCDD and MDLM.

## Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process

In this section, we first derive the posterior distribution in
([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Then, we discuss validity of the backward process defined in
([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Finally, we derive the CTMC backward transition rate.

### B.1 Derivation of Posterior Distribution

Let $`s,t`$ be two adjacent time points such that $`s<t`$.

#### Case 1: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$.

When $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, we have
$`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$ since SCDD doesn’t allow “remasking”
during token generation. The posterior
$`q(\mathbf{z}_{s}|\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})`$ is given by Bayes’ rule:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{q(\mathbf{z}_{s}|\mathbf{x})}{q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{x})}q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{z}_{s})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{s}\left(\rho_{s}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}\right)+(1-\gamma_{s})1(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m})}{\gamma_{t}\left(\rho_{t}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}\right)+(1-\gamma_{t})1(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m})}\cdot\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}\left[\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{t})+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\frac{1}{K}\right]\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(1-\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}))\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\left[\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{t})+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\frac{1}{K}\right].`$ |  | (15) |

Note that when $`\rho_{s}=0`$, we have $`\rho_{t}=0`$ since $`\rho_{t}`$
is nonnegative and nonincreasing. In this case, we use convention
$`\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}=0`$ and the posterior becomes
$`q(\mathbf{z}_{s}|\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})\equiv\frac{1}{K}`$.

#### Case 2: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$.

If $`\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}`$, the posterior is given by

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{q(\mathbf{z}_{s}|\mathbf{x})}{q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{x})}q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{z}_{s})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}.`$ |  | (16) |

Similarly, if $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, the posterior is given
by

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{q(\mathbf{z}_{s}|\mathbf{x})}{q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{x})}q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{z}_{s})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{s}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K})}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}\right).`$ |  | (17) |

Combining
([16](#A2.E16 "Equation 16 ‣ Case 2: 𝐳_𝑡=𝐦. ‣ B.1 Derivation of Posterior Distribution ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
and
([17](#A2.E17 "Equation 17 ‣ Case 2: 𝐳_𝑡=𝐦. ‣ B.1 Derivation of Posterior Distribution ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
we obtain:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m})\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}+\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m})\frac{\gamma_{s}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K})}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m})\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}+\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m})\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(\rho_{s}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{x})+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}\right).`$ |  | (18) |

Since $`\mathbf{z}_{s}`$, $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$, $`\mathbf{m}`$ and
$`\mathbf{x}`$ are all one-hot vectors, we replace the indicator
functions with inner products to get
([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
This step ensures valid gradient propagation when we replace
$`\mathbf{x}`$ with $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}`$, which is not a one-hot
vector, in the backward process.

### B.2 Validity of Backward Process

In this section, we show that the model parameterization in
([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
defines a valid density $`p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ for any
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$.

###### Lemma 6.

The model parameterization in
([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
defines a valid density $`p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ for any
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$.

###### Proof.

We write $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}c_{i}e_{i}`$ where
$`e_{i}`$ is the $`i`$-th $`K+1`$-dimensional canonical basis vector,
and $`c_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{\theta}^{\top}e_{i}`$. We have
$`c=(c_{1},...,c_{K},0)\in\Delta_{K+1}`$.

#### Case 1: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$.

In this case, the backward process is given by:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})=(1-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m})\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K})+\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m}\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}`$ |  | (19) |

Summing over the support of $`p_{\theta}`$ in
([19](#A2.E19 "Equation 19 ‣ Case 1: 𝐳_𝑡=𝐦. ‣ B.2 Validity of Backward Process ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
gives:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}\left[(1-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m})\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K})+\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m}\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{=}\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(\rho_{s}\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+1-\rho_{s}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=1,`$ |  | (20) |

where $`(i)`$ is from the fact that $`\mathbf{x}_{\theta}`$ is a
probability vector over the vocabulary, and hence
$`\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}=1`$.
Therefore, $`p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ defines a probability
density in this case.

#### Case 2: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$.

In this case, we have $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$ since \[mask\] is
absorbing. Using the fact
$`\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}=\frac{b_{1}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}+\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}`$,
we obtain:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\sum_{i}c_{i}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K})}{\sum_{i}c_{i}(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\sum_{i}\underbrace{\left(\frac{c_{i}(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})}{\sum_{i}c_{i}(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})}\right)}_{\kappa_{i}}\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}e_{i}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}=1`$ |  |

Thus, $`p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ defines a probability density
in this case. ∎

### B.3 Derivation of Continuous-time Backward Transition Rate

In this section, we derive the transition rate for the backward process
defined in
([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
similarly to the forward transition rate
([6](#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
For each $`t\in(0,1]`$ and
$`\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}\in\mathcal{V}`$, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{K}\dfrac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\dfrac{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t},\ \mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{K}\dfrac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\dfrac{1-(\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{t},\ \mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}\\[10.0pt]
-\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\left(\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t)+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}\right)&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t},\ \mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}\\
0&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\ \mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}\end{cases}`$ |  | (21) |

###### Proposition 7.

Let $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ be defined as
in ([21](#A2.E21 "Equation 21 ‣ B.3 Derivation of Continuous-time Backward Transition Rate ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Then the following statements hold:

- •
  $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ is a valid infinitesimal generator in the
  sense of [Gat et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6); in particular,

  |  |  |  |
  |----|----|----|
  |  |
  ``` math
  \sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})=0,\qquad\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})\geq 0,\forall\,\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t}.
  ``` |  |
- •
  The discrete-time backward process defined in
  ([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
  coincide with $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$. In particular, for adjacent
  time points $`s<t`$ with $`\Delta t=t-s`$, we have

  |  |  |  |
  |----|----|----|
  |  |
  ``` math
  p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}}+\Delta t\,\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})+o(\Delta t).
  ``` |  |

###### Proof.

The proof follows the same argument as in
Lemma [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Lemma 2. ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction").
First, we show that $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ is a valid generator.

Fix an arbitrary $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$. Since
$`\rho_{t}^{\prime},\,\gamma_{t}^{\prime}\leq 0`$, all off-diagonal
entries of $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ are non-negative, i.e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})\geq 0,\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t}.
``` |  |

Moreover, by construction of $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ we have the
row-sum condition

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}}\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})=0.
``` |  |

Therefore, $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ is a valid infinitesimal generator
in the sense of [Gat et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6).

Next, we show that $`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}`$ is indeed the backward
transition kernel of the backward process
([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Let $`s=t-\Delta t`$ with $`\Delta t>0`$. Then by first-order Taylor
expansion we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\rho_{s}=\rho_{t}-\rho_{t}^{\prime}\Delta t+o(\Delta t),\qquad\gamma_{s}=\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{t}^{\prime}\Delta t+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (22) |

We now verify that the discrete backward
kernel ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t}}+\Delta t\,\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})+o(\Delta t)`$ |  |

case by case.

#### Case 1. $`\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m},\ \mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t}`$.

In this case, $`\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{m}=0`$ and
$`\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}=0`$. Thus, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{s}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\left(\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}-\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{K}-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta})}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Delta t+o(\Delta t)\right)\left(\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}(-\tfrac{1}{K})\Delta t+o(\Delta t)\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\Delta t\left(-\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\tfrac{1}{K}\frac{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\right)+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (23) |

#### Case 2. $`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$.

We have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{s}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{K}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta})}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Delta t+o(\Delta t)\right)\left(1+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}(1-\tfrac{1}{K})\Delta t+o(\Delta t)\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=1+\Delta t\left(\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{K}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta})}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}(1-\tfrac{1}{K})\right)+o(\Delta t)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=1+\Delta t\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\tfrac{1}{K}\frac{1-(\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (24) |

#### Case 3. $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m},\ \mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$.

We have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\bigl(\rho_{s}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}\bigr)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=-\Delta t\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\bigl(\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}+\rho_{t}^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{K}-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta})\Delta t\bigr)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=-\Delta t\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\bigl(\rho_{t}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}\bigr)+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (25) |

#### Case 4. $`\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$.

We have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}=1+\Delta t\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}}+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (26) |

#### Case 5: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, $`\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}`$.

The last case holds from the fact that SCDD doesn’t allow remasking.

In all cases, $`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ aligns
with the process implied by
$`\tilde{Q}^{\theta}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})`$
in ([21](#A2.E21 "Equation 21 ‣ B.3 Derivation of Continuous-time Backward Transition Rate ‣ Appendix B Discussion of Posterior Distribution and Backward Process ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
which completes the proof. ∎

## Appendix C Analysis of Training Loss

### C.1 Discrete-time NELBO

Following standard variational arguments on diffusion models
([Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015](#bib.bib35); [Sahoo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib2)), we start from the classical ELBO:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[-\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x})]`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[-\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{0:1})+\log q(\mathbf{z}_{0:1}\mid\mathbf{x})\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{0:1})}\big[\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}_{0})\big]}_{\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{reconstruction}}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{0:1})}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{1}\mid\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{1})\big)\right]}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}}}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{0:1})}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{T}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}},\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}})\big)\right]}_{\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}}=:\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{NELBO}}.`$ |  | (27) |

In this section, we explicitly calculate the the diffusion loss
$`\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$. As before, we use $`t,s`$ to
denote two adjacent time points with $`t>s`$. We first rewrite the
summation over $`i`$ as an expectation over all time points:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{T}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}},\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}})\big)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[TD_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}},\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t_{i}})\big)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},...,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[TD_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})\big)\right]`$ |  | (28) |

Since

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p_{\theta})=\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log q]-\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p_{\theta}],
``` |  |

the first (negative entropy) term in KL-divergence is independent of
$`\theta`$, and minimizing $`\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ is
equivalent to minimizing the expectation of second cross entropy term.
With a little abuse of notation, we drop the first term and redefine the
$`\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},...,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})\right]`$ |  | (29) |

When $`\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m}`$, we have
$`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$ and
$`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})=\frac{1-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}`$.
This case is independent of $`\theta`$ and will be ignored in
optimization. We therefore restrict to the case
$`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$.

#### Case 1: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$.

Substituting $`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\right)`$ |  |

The second term in $`\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$
is independent of $`\theta`$ and drops from the loss. Thus, up to an
additive constant,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}\mid_{\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},...,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)\log\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)\log\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right],`$ |  | (30) |

up to some $`\theta`$-independent additive constant. We replaced
$`\mathbf{z}_{s}`$ with $`\mathbf{v}`$ in the last line to make it clear
that the summation doesn’t depend on the value of $`\mathbf{z}_{s}`$.

#### Case 2: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$.

In this case,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m})=\log\Big(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)+\log\Big(\tfrac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}\Big),
``` |  |

Dropping the second term in
$`\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ and substituting
into the cross-entropy objective yields

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}\mid_{\mathbf{z}_{t}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\gamma_{s}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\frac{1}{K})}{1-\gamma_{t}}\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}\log\Big(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}\Big)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[T\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{t}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K})\log(\rho_{s}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K})\right],`$ |  | (31) |

up to some $`\theta`$-independent additive constant.

### C.2 Continuous-time NELBO

Next, we show that reconstruction loss and prior loss vanishes as
$`T\to\infty`$, and explicitly derive
$`\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}`$.

#### Reconstruction Loss

As $`T\to\infty`$, $`\rho_{0},\gamma_{0}\to 1^{-}`$ by the design of
noising schedule. Therefore, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{0}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\text{Cat}\Bigl(\cdot;\gamma_{0}\bigl(\rho_{0}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{0})\mathbf{u}\bigr)+(1-\gamma_{0})\mathbf{m}\Bigr)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{d}{\Longrightarrow}\text{Cat}\Bigl(\cdot;\mathbf{x}\Bigr),`$ |  | (32) |

where “$`\overset{d}{\Longrightarrow}`$” stands for “converge in
distribution”. Therefore, we have the reconstruction loss vanishes as
follows:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{reconstruction}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{q}[-\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}_{0})]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{=}\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[-\log\frac{\mathbf{\rho}_{-1}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{0},0)+(1-\rho_{-1})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{0}\mathbf{z}_{0}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{0},0)+(1-\rho_{0})\frac{1}{K}}\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho_{-1}}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{0}+\frac{\rho_{-1}-\rho_{0}}{\rho_{-1}}\frac{1}{K}\right)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[-\log\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},0)}{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},0)}\left(\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\right)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{q}[-\log 1]=0,`$ |  | (33) |

where $`(i)`$ is from plugging $`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{z}_{0},t=0`$
into
([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Efficient Parallel Self-Correction. ‣ 3.2 Backward Denoising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).

#### Prior Loss

At $`t=1`$, the forward marginal distribution becomes
$`q(\mathbf{z}_{1}\mid\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{1}=\mathbf{m})`$,
indicating that the forward noising process ultimately transforms clean
data $`\mathbf{x}`$ to $`\mathbf{m}`$ at $`t=1`$. We also choose the
prior to be
$`p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{1})=q(\mathbf{z}_{1}\mid\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{z}_{1}=\mathbf{m})`$
so that the backward process starts from $`\mathbf{m}`$. Therefore, we
have $`\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{prior}}\equiv 0`$.

#### Diffusion Loss

In this section, we discuss the continuous-time version of the loss
function in
([11](#S3.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 3.3 ELBO ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
We assume that $`\rho_{t}`$ and $`\gamma_{t}`$ are decreasing,
differentiable functions of time.

###### Proposition 8.

Pick adjacent time points $`s<t`$ and let $`\Delta t=t-s`$ be the time
step of the above discrete stochastic process. Then as $`\Delta t\to 0`$
or, equivalently, $`T\to\infty`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{m}}\left(\frac{(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right)-\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right],&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\\[10.0pt]
\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\log(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\right],&\text{if }\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}`$ |  | (34) |

###### Proof.

We distinguish two cases of $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ to calculate the
continuous-time loss function.

#### Case 1: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$.

If $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, using the first-order Taylor
expansion of $`\rho_{s}`$ and $`\gamma_{s}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(\rho_{t}-\rho^{\prime}_{t}\Delta t)\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t}+\rho^{\prime}_{t}\Delta t)\tfrac{1}{K}+o(\Delta t)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})+\Delta t\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+\tfrac{1}{K})+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\log\frac{\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}+\Delta t\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho_{s}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\tfrac{1}{K}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\Delta t(\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}-\tfrac{1}{K})+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (35) |

Substituting the above expansions into the discrete loss
([30](#A3.E30 "Equation 30 ‣ Case 1: 𝐳_𝑡≠𝐦. ‣ C.1 Discrete-time NELBO ‣ Appendix C Analysis of Training Loss ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}\mid_{\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}}=-`$ | $`\displaystyle T\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\Bigg[\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K}}\Bigg(\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}+\Delta t\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\Bigg)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\Delta t\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{K})\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}+(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}(\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}-\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}+o(\Delta t)\Bigg].`$ |  |

We distinguish two cases when doing summation over $`\mathbf{z}_{s}`$ to
simplify the above expansion: (i) if $`\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}_{t}`$,
all logarithmic terms vanish and the loss reduces to

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle-T\Delta t\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right]+o(1)=-\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right]+o(1);`$ |  |

\(ii\) if $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t}`$, the first term inside of
expectation and summation vanishes and we obtain

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{T}\}}\mathbb{E}_{q}\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{m}}\left[\frac{(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right]+o(1).`$ |  | (36) |

Combining the two cases, letting $`T\to\infty`$, and replacing the
$`\mathbf{z}_{s}`$ notation with $`\mathbf{v}`$ yields the desired limit
for $`\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}\mid_{\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m}}=\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{m}}\left(\frac{(\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K})\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\log\frac{\rho_{t}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right)-\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}\right].
``` |  |

#### Case 2: $`\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}`$.

Using the first-order Taylor expansions to get

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{s}}{1-\gamma_{t}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\Delta t\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}},`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})+\Delta t\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+\tfrac{1}{K})+o(\Delta t),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\log(\rho_{s}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{s})\tfrac{1}{K})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})+\Delta t\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}(-\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{K})}{\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\frac{1}{K}}+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (37) |

Substituting into the discrete loss
([31](#A3.E31 "Equation 31 ‣ Case 2: 𝐳_𝑡=𝐦. ‣ C.1 Discrete-time NELBO ‣ Appendix C Analysis of Training Loss ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}\mid_{\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}}=\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\gamma_{t}}(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})\log(\rho_{t}\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{\theta}+(1-\rho_{t})\tfrac{1}{K})\right],`$ |  | (38) |

which completes the proof.

∎

## Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models

### D.1 SCDD and MDLM

In this section, we discuss the relation between SCDD and MDLM ([Sahoo
et al., 2024](#bib.bib2)), and show SCDD can be reduced to MDLM under
specific parameter settings. Firstly, recall several facts of MDLM (see
[Sahoo et al. (2024)](#bib.bib2) for full derivations):

#### Marginal Distribution.

The marginal distribution at time $`t\in[0,1]`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{x})=\text{Cat}(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{\text{MDLM}}\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{t})\mathbf{m}),`$ |  | (39) |

where $`\alpha_{t}^{\text{MDLM}}\in[0,1]`$.

#### Forward Process.

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{t|s}\mathbf{z}_{s}+(1-\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{t|s})\mathbf{m}\right),`$ |  | (40) |

where $`\alpha_{t|s}=\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{s}`$.

#### Backward Process.

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}\left(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t}\right)\begin{cases}\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{s};\mathbf{z}_{t}\right),&\mathbf{z}_{t}\neq\mathbf{m},\\
\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{s};\frac{\left(1-\alpha_{s}\right)\mathbf{m}+\left(\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{s}-\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{t}\right)\mathbf{x}_{\theta}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t},t\right)}{1-\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{t}}\right).&\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m},\end{cases}`$ |  | (41) |

#### Training Loss.

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{T}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=-T\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\text{MDLM}}-\alpha_{t}^{\text{MDLM}}}{1-\alpha_{t}^{\text{MDLM}}}\log(\mathbf{x}_{\theta}^{\top}\mathbf{x})\right],`$ | discrete version |  | (42) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\text{diffusion}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\frac{(\alpha^{\text{MDLM}}_{t})^{\prime}}{1-\alpha_{t}^{\text{MDLM}}}\log(\mathbf{x}_{\theta}^{\top}\mathbf{x})\right],`$ | continuous version |  | (43) |

###### Proposition 9.

If we set $`\rho_{t}\equiv 1`$ and
$`\gamma_{t}\equiv\alpha_{t}^{\text{MDLM}}`$ in
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
SCDD can recover MDLM. In particular, the marginal distribution, forward
process & posterior, and loss function can recover MDLM.

###### Proof.

It’s easy to see the marginal

### D.2 SCDD and GIDD

In this section, we discuss the relation between the SCDD and GIDD ([von
Rütte et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)), and derive the noise schedule of SCDD
that recovers the marginal distribution of GIDD. Firstly, recall several
facts of GIDD (see [von Rütte et al. (2025)](#bib.bib38) for full
derivations):

#### Marginal Distribution.

The marginal distribution at time $`t\in[0,1]`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}\mathbf{x}+\beta_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi_{t}\right),
``` |  | (44) |

where $`\beta_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}=1-\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}`$ and
$`\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}\in[0,1]`$.

#### Forward Process.

Given a mixing rate $`\alpha_{t}`$ and a time-dependent mixing
distribution $`\pi_{t}`$, the forward Markov chain is defined such that
the cumulative transition from $`s`$ to $`t`$ satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t|s}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\text{Cat}\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t|s}^{\text{GIDD}}\mathbf{z}_{s}+\beta_{t|s}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi_{t|s}\right),
``` |  | (45) |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{t|s}^{\text{GIDD}}=\frac{\alpha_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}}{\alpha_{s}^{\text{GIDD}}},\qquad\beta_{t|s}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi_{t|s}=\beta_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi_{t}-\frac{\alpha_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}}{\alpha_{s}^{\text{GIDD}}}\beta_{s}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi_{s}.
``` |  | (46) |

#### Forward Transition Rate

The forward rate matrix of GIDD is given by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})=\frac{(\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t})^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}}\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t}}+\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}(\beta^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}\pi_{t}^{\prime}-\frac{(\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t})^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}}\pi_{t}),`$ |  | (47) |

and thus

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t|s}(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{z}_{s})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t}}+R^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})\Delta t+o(\Delta t),
``` |  |

where $`\Delta t=t-s`$.

#### Backward process.

The backward process is parameterized as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p^{\text{GIDD}}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t})=\frac{q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t|s}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})q^{\text{GIDD}}_{s}(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\theta})}{q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\theta})}.
``` |  | (48) |

#### Training Loss.

The continuous-time negative ELBO is given by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
-\log q(x)\leq\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{z}_{t}}\left[w_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},x)\big(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x})\|q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x}_{\theta}))+D_{\mathrm{IS}}(q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})\|q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\theta}))\big)\right]+C,
``` |  | (49) |

with weighting term

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
w_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{q^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})}\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\left(\beta_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi^{\prime}_{t}-\frac{(\alpha^{\text{GIDD}}_{t})^{\prime}}{\alpha_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}}\pi_{t}\right),
``` |  | (50) |

where $`D_{\text{IS}}`$ denotes the Itakura–Saito divergence and $`C`$
is the ELBO constant.

The next proposition shows that GIDD can be rewritten under our marginal
parameterization, but with a non-absorbing \[mask\] token and more
coupled forward transition kernel.

###### Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD).

Consider the forward transition kernel

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{z}_{s})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cat}\Bigl(\mathbf{z}_{t};\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}\mathbf{z}_{s}+\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t\mid s})\mathbf{u}+\bigl((1-\gamma_{t})-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})\bigr)\mathbf{m}\Bigr).`$ |  | (51) |

This kernel induces the marginal distribution in
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Its continuous-time transition rate is

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t}}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\left[\gamma_{t}\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\left((1-\gamma_{t})\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)\mathbf{m}\right].`$ |  | (52) |

Moreover, under the reparameterization

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{cases}\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}=\rho_{t}\gamma_{t},\\
\beta_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}=1-\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}},\\
\beta_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\pi_{t}=\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t})\mathbf{u}+(1-\gamma_{t})\mathbf{m},\end{cases}
``` |  | (53) |

the forward process induced by
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
coincides with the GIDD forward process in
([45](#A4.E45 "Equation 45 ‣ Forward Process. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Consequently, the marginal distribution and continuous-time transition
rate induced by
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
coincide with the GIDD marginal distribution and transition rate in
([44](#A4.E44 "Equation 44 ‣ Marginal Distribution. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
and
([47](#A4.E47 "Equation 47 ‣ Forward Transition Rate ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
respectively.

###### Remark 11.

Proposition [10](#Thmtheorem10 "Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
shows that the forward kernel in
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
provides a reparameterized form of the GIDD forward process under the
parameter translation in
([53](#A4.E53 "Equation 53 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
This kernel induces the same marginal distribution as
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
but unlike the SCDD forward kernel, it does not impose the
absorbing-mask condition
$`q(\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{m}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{m})=1`$.
Therefore, SCDD and GIDD can be expressed under a common marginal
parameterization, while differing in their forward transition kernels.
This distinction explains why the uniform-transition and masking
components are coupled in the GIDD transition rate, whereas they are
controlled separately in SCDD.

###### Proof.

We prove the proposition in three steps.

Step 1: Marginal distribution. We first verify that the transition
kernel in
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
induces the marginal distribution in
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Suppose that at time $`s`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{Cat}\Bigl(\mathbf{z}_{s};\gamma_{s}\bigl(\rho_{s}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\mathbf{u}\bigr)+(1-\gamma_{s})\mathbf{m}\Bigr).
``` |  |

By the forward transition kernel
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
the marginal at time $`t`$ has probability vector

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}\Bigl[\gamma_{s}\bigl(\rho_{s}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{s})\mathbf{u}\bigr)+(1-\gamma_{s})\mathbf{m}\Bigr]+\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t\mid s})\mathbf{u}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\qquad+\Bigl((1-\gamma_{t})-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})\Bigr)\mathbf{m}.`$ |  | (54) |

Using $`\gamma_{t\mid s}=\gamma_{t}/\gamma_{s}`$ and
$`\rho_{t\mid s}=\rho_{t}/\rho_{s}`$, the coefficient of $`\mathbf{x}`$
is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}\gamma_{s}\rho_{s}=\gamma_{t}\rho_{t}.
``` |  |

The coefficient of $`\mathbf{u}`$ is

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}\gamma_{s}(1-\rho_{s})+\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t\mid s})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\gamma_{t}\rho_{t}\frac{1-\rho_{s}}{\rho_{s}}+\gamma_{t}\left(1-\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t}).`$ |  | (55) |

The coefficient of $`\mathbf{m}`$ is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})+(1-\gamma_{t})-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})=1-\gamma_{t}.
``` |  |

Therefore,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{Cat}\Bigl(\mathbf{z}_{t};\gamma_{t}\bigl(\rho_{t}\mathbf{x}+(1-\rho_{t})\mathbf{u}\bigr)+(1-\gamma_{t})\mathbf{m}\Bigr),
``` |  |

which is exactly
([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).

Step 2: Forward transition rate. Let $`t=s+\Delta t`$. From
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")),
the probability that $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$ retains $`\mathbf{z}_{s}`$ is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}=\frac{\gamma_{t}\rho_{t}}{\gamma_{s}\rho_{s}}.
``` |  |

Using first-order Taylor expansions,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\gamma_{t}}{\gamma_{s}}=1+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\Delta t+o(\Delta t),\qquad\frac{\rho_{t}}{\rho_{s}}=1+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\Delta t+o(\Delta t),
``` |  |

we obtain

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}=1+\left(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\right)\Delta t+o(\Delta t).
``` |  |

Similarly, the coefficient of the uniform transition component is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t\mid s})=-\gamma_{t}\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\Delta t+o(\Delta t),
``` |  |

and the coefficient of the masking component is

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle(1-\gamma_{t})-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\left[(1-\gamma_{t})\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right]\Delta t+o(\Delta t).`$ |  | (56) |

Thus,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t}}+\Delta t\,R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})+o(\Delta t),
``` |  |

where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{z}_{s})=\left(\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)\delta_{\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t}}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\left[\gamma_{t}\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\left((1-\gamma_{t})\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)\mathbf{m}\right],
``` |  |

which proves
([52](#A4.E52 "Equation 52 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).

Step 3: Equivalence with GIDD. It remains to connect the forward process
in
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
with the original GIDD parameterization. Under the reparameterization

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}=\rho_{t}\gamma_{t},\qquad\beta_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}=1-\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}},\qquad\beta_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\pi_{t}=\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t})\mathbf{u}+(1-\gamma_{t})\mathbf{m},
``` |  |

we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{t\mid s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}=\frac{\alpha_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}}{\alpha_{s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}}=\frac{\rho_{t}\gamma_{t}}{\rho_{s}\gamma_{s}}=\rho_{t\mid s}\gamma_{t\mid s}.
``` |  |

Moreover,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\beta_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\pi_{t}-\alpha_{t\mid s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\beta_{s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\pi_{s}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t})\mathbf{u}+(1-\gamma_{t})\mathbf{m}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}\Bigl[\gamma_{s}(1-\rho_{s})\mathbf{u}+(1-\gamma_{s})\mathbf{m}\Bigr]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t\mid s})\mathbf{u}+\bigl((1-\gamma_{t})-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})\bigr)\mathbf{m}.`$ |  | (57) |

Therefore, substituting
([53](#A4.E53 "Equation 53 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
into the GIDD forward kernel
([45](#A4.E45 "Equation 45 ‣ Forward Process. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{t\mid s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\mathbf{z}_{s}+\beta_{t}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\pi_{t}-\alpha_{t\mid s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\beta_{s}^{\mathrm{GIDD}}\pi_{s}=\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}\mathbf{z}_{s}+\gamma_{t}(1-\rho_{t\mid s})\mathbf{u}+\bigl((1-\gamma_{t})-\gamma_{t\mid s}\rho_{t\mid s}(1-\gamma_{s})\bigr)\mathbf{m},
``` |  |

which is exactly the probability vector in
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")).
Hence, the forward process induced by
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
coincides with the GIDD forward process. Consequently, the marginal
distribution and continuous-time transition rate induced by
([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
coincide with the GIDD marginal distribution and transition rate in

###### Remark 12.

\[Comparison of Forward Transition Rates between SCDD and GIDD.\]

Under CTMC theory and using the same parameterization of marginal
distribution, the GIDD’s forward transition rate is given by:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R^{\text{GIDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})=\left(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\right)\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\left(\gamma_{t}\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\left((1-\gamma_{t})\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)\mathbf{m}\right),
``` |  |

and SCDD’s transition rate, when $`\mathbf{z}_{s}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, is
given by equation
([6](#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Forward Process in Continuous Time. ‣ 3.1 Forward Noising Process ‣ 3 Self-Correcting Discrete Diffusion Model ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")):

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R^{\text{SCDD}}_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{t})=\left(\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{t}}{\rho_{t}}\right)\mathbf{z}_{s}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}_{t}^{\top}\left(\frac{\rho_{t}^{\prime}}{\rho_{t}}\mathbf{u}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{t}}\mathbf{m}\right).
``` |  |

Our formulation yields a simpler forward process by making
$`\mathbf{m}`$ an absorbing state. In particular, the uniform transition
noise $`\mathbf{u}`$ and the absorbing mask noise $`\mathbf{m}`$ become
decoupled, and their rates are independently controlled by the marginal
parameters $`\rho_{t}`$ and $`\gamma_{t}`$, respectively. In contrast,
GIDD couples these two noise channels, making the parameters’ respective
effects less explicit. This decoupling makes the forward dynamics more
interpretable and substantially reduces the algebraic complexity of the
backward formulas and the resulting training loss, which in turn
simplifies implementation and lowers maintenance cost in practice.

## Appendix E Experimental Details

### E.1 Training Details.

For fair comparison, we retrained MDLM ([Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2))
and GIDD ([von Rütte et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) along with our SCDD
model. The detailed hyper-parameter setting for different datasets can
be found in Table
[7](#A5.T7 "Table 7 ‣ E.1 Training Details. ‣ Appendix E Experimental Details ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction").
We train MDLM and SCDD with a diffusion process and loss function
defined over $`T=1000`$ discrete time steps, and train GIDD using the
continuous-time loss with dynamic weighting to guarantee the best
performance, see ([von Rütte et al., 2025](#bib.bib38)) for the impact
of weighting function on GIDD training. We also align the marginal
distribution of SCDD with GIDD to guarantee two models see
distributionally equivalent samples during training. Both of the models
are trained using a noise schedule that achieves uniform transition peak
ratio of $`p_{u}\in\{0.1,0.2\}`$ at $`t=0.5`$. See Appendix
[E.4](#A5.SS4 "E.4 Noise Schedule of SCDD ‣ Appendix E Experimental Details ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for detailed discussion.

All the models use the DiT ([Peebles and Xie, 2023](#bib.bib13))
structure as the backbone of denoising networks, and GPT-2 tokenizer
([Radford et al., 2019](#bib.bib34)). Following MDLM and GIDD, we use
the small variant of DiT as the denoising network with 12 Transformer
blocks, 12 attention heads per block, and 768 hidden dimension, yielding
166M trainable parameters. Time conditioning is projected to a dimension
of 128 before being injected into the network. No dropout is applied
during training.

For LM1B ([Chelba et al., 2013](#bib.bib39)) dataset, all the model are
trained with a batch size of 512, context length of 128, for 500k steps,
yielding 33B ($`512\times 128\times 500`$k) training tokens in total.
For OWT ([Gokaslan and Cohen, 2019](#bib.bib40)) dataset, all the models
are trained with batch size of 256, context length of 512, for 1M steps,
yielding 131B ($`256\times 512\times 1`$M) training tokens in total. We
leave the last 100k docs as validation as in MDLM training. For ablation
study, we train the model on Wikitext-103 ([Merity et al.,
2017](#bib.bib52)) with a batch size of 128, context length 512, for
100k steps, yielding 7B ($`128\times 512\times 100`$k) training tokens
in total.

For optimization, we use the AdamW optimizer ([Kingma and Ba,
2014](#bib.bib41)) with hyperparameters $`\beta_{1}=0.9`$,
$`\beta_{2}=0.999`$, and $`\epsilon=1\text{e}-9`$. The learning rate
increases linearly from $`1\times 10^{-6}`$ to the peak rate
$`5\times 10^{-4}`$ over the first 10k steps, then follows a
cosine-decay schedule to $`5\times 10^{-5}`$ for the remainder of the
training. To stabilize training, we use bfloat16 mixed precision and
maintain an Exponential Moving Average of model parameters with a decay
rate of $`0.9999`$. Training are completed on a single node of 4
(Wikitext-103) or 8 (LM1B and OWT) NVIDIA H100-80GB GPUs.

### E.3 Benchmark Performance.

For each zero-shot task, we rank candidate completions by estimating the
conditional log-likelihood of the answer $`y`$ given the question $`x`$.
Because diffusion models are non-autoregressive, we score each candidate
using the ELBO of the concatenated sequence $`[x;y]`$. Since $`P(x)`$ is
identical across candidates, maximizing $`\log P(x,y)`$ is equivalent to
maximizing $`\log P(y\mid x)`$.

Concretely, for each $`[x;y]`$ pair, we compute its contribution to the
diffusion variational loss, which is stochastic due to sampling
$`t\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)`$, and the corresponding forward noising
process. We therefore use a Monte Carlo estimate, averaging the ELBO
over $`N=10`$ independent forward passes, and select the candidate with
the highest mean ELBO. At last, we calculate the accuracy of selection.
Results are reported in Table
[10](#A5.T10 "Table 10 ‣ E.3 Benchmark Performance. ‣ Appendix E Experimental Details ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction").

Since each of zero-shot tasks ranks answer candidates by their estimated
ELBO, there is no self-correction during evaluation, making these
benchmarks a measure of zero-shot likelihood rather than language
generation. Uniform-noise diffusion models (including both SCDD and
GIDD+) typically exhibit worse likelihood but stronger abilities in
self-correction, few-step sampling, and generation tasks ([Sahoo et al.,
2026](#bib.bib54)). Our generative PPL results (Table
[3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.3 Unconditional Language Generation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction"))
confirm that SCDD produces higher-quality text than MDLM/GIDD at equal
sampling steps, demonstrating that worse likelihood estimation does not
translate to weaker language generation.

### E.4 Noise Schedule of SCDD

[von Rütte et al. (2025)](#bib.bib38) define the following

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\alpha_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1-t}{C_{t}},\qquad\beta_{t}^{\text{GIDD}}\pi_{t}=\frac{t}{C_{t}}\mathbf{m}+\frac{c_{t}}{C_{t}}\mathbf{u},`$ |  | (58) |

attaining its maximum at $`t=1/2`$. In [von Rütte et al.

be expressed as

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|

       ``` math
       p_{u}=\frac{c_{1/2}}{C_{1/2}}=\frac{c_{1/2}}{1+c_{1/2}}.

If $`p_{u}`$ is fixed, we can solve for the corresponding constant $`B`$
as:

|     |                                                                   |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       B=\frac{c_{1/2}}{(1/2)^{\gamma}}=2^{\gamma}\frac{p_{u}}{1-p_{u}}.
       ```                                                                |     |

distribution of SCDD with that of GIDD in our experimental evaluation.
By applying the parameter translation in

|  |  |  |

(1-\rho_{t})\gamma_{t}&=\dfrac{c_{t}}{1+c_{t}},\\[6.0pt]

## Appendix F Related Works

#### Mask Diffusion Language Model (MDLM/MDM) ([Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2); [Shi et al., 2024](#bib.bib3))

In vanilla masked diffusion models, the forward process is defined by a
Markov chain of the form

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s})=\alpha_{t|s}\,\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha_{t|s})\,\mathbf{m},
``` |  |

which induces a marginal distribution

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{Cat}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{t};\,\alpha_{t}\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{m}\right).
``` |  |

Masked diffusion models offer significant computational advantages and
substantially improve upon earlier discrete flow-matching approaches.
However, they lack an explicit self-correction mechanism. In particular,
once a token is decoded during inference, it becomes fixed and cannot be
revised in subsequent denoising steps. As a result, early prediction
errors accumulate over the backward process and harms generation
quality. SCDD can be viewed as a generalization of MDLM to support
self-correction. We refer to Appendix
[D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 SCDD and MDLM ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for a formal comparison.

#### Remasking Diffusion Model (ReMDM) ([Wang et al., 2026](#bib.bib31))

ReMDM introduces a collection of post-doc samplers that can be readily
applied to pretrained MDLM models. To elicit self-correction, it adopts
a *remasking* mechanism in the backward (inference) process:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{s}\mid\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Cat}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s};(1-\sigma_{t})\mathbf{x}+\sigma_{t}\mathbf{m}\right),\\
\mathrm{Cat}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s};\frac{\alpha_{s}-(1-\sigma_{t})\alpha_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1-\alpha_{s}-\sigma_{t}\alpha_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{m}\right),\end{cases}
``` |  |

where $`\alpha_{t},\sigma_{t}\in[0,1]`$ are noise schedules. The
non-Markovian forward process is derived from Bayes’ rule.

While this remasking-based formulation enables token revision, it
exhibits two notable limitations. First, ReMDM performs self-correction
through a non-\[mask\] $`\rightarrow`$ \[mask\] $`\rightarrow`$
non-\[mask\] procedure, in which the intermediate masking step is
redundant: it takes 2 steps to correct a token. This may reduce the
parallel self-correction efficiency during inference. Second, the
performance of ReMDM highly depends on the hyperparameters that affect
the actual schedule of $`\sigma_{t}`$, which introduces additional
tuning cost.

#### Generalized Interpolating Discrete Diffusion (GIDD) ([von Rütte et al., 2025](#bib.bib38))

GIDD replaces the absorbing mask probability vector $`\mathbf{m}`$ in
the marginal distribution of MDLM ([Sahoo et al., 2024](#bib.bib2)) with
a more general corruption distribution $`\pi_{t}`$, defined as a mixture
of absorbing mask $`\mathbf{m}`$ and uniform transition noise
$`\mathbf{u}`$. The resulting marginal distribution takes the form

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x}):=\mathrm{Cat}(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t}\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha_{t})\pi_{t}),
``` |  |

which is induced by the following Markov transition:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q(\mathbf{z}_{t}\mid\mathbf{z}_{s}):=\mathrm{Cat}(\mathbf{z}_{t};\alpha_{t|s}\mathbf{z}_{s}+(1-\alpha_{t})\pi_{t}-\alpha_{t|s}(1-\alpha_{s})\pi_{s}).
``` |  |

SCDD and GIDD can both be viewed as generalizations of vanilla masked
diffusion language models, differing primarily in their choices of the
forward transition kernel. We refer to
Proposition [10](#Thmtheorem10 "Proposition 10 (Reparameterization of GIDD). ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
and
Remark [12](#Thmtheorem12 "Remark 12. ‣ Training Loss. ‣ D.2 SCDD and GIDD ‣ Appendix D Connections Between SCDD and Other Models ‣ Generalized Discrete Diffusion with Self-Correction")
for a detailed comparison.

Compared to SCDD, GIDD exhibits the following limitations. First, GIDD
controls uniform corruption and masking through an entangled
parameterization, whereas SCDD decouples these two sources of noise.
This decoupling leads to a substantially simpler backward process and a
more tractable training loss. Second, GIDD doesn’t eliminate the
remasking step during inference, while SCDD supports direct
token-to-token self-correction during both training and inference
without introducing an intermediate masking stage.

#### Informed Corrector ([Zhao et al., 2024](#bib.bib15))

[Zhao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib15) use continuous-time Markov chains
(CTMCs) to describe the forward and backward processes:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t|s}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{\delta}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}+R_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\Delta t+o(\Delta t),
``` |  |

where $`\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{V}^{D}`$ denote token sequences
(e.g. articles or paragraphs), and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\beta_{t}R(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{y}_{d}),\qquad R(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{y}_{d}):=\begin{cases}1&\mathbf{y}_{d}=\mathbf{m},\ \mathbf{x}_{d}\neq\mathbf{m},\\
-1&\mathbf{y}_{d}=\mathbf{x}_{d}\neq\mathbf{m},\\
0&\mathbf{x}_{d}=\mathbf{y}_{d}=\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}
``` |  |

This formulation is equivalent to the forward Markov chain used in
masked diffusion language models (MDLM), and the resulting marginal
distribution can be written as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t,d}\mid\mathbf{x}_{0,d})=\alpha_{t}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{x}_{t,d}=\mathbf{x}_{0,d})+(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{x}_{t,d}=\mathbf{m}).
``` |  |

During inference, [Zhao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib15) introduce a
*confidence score* $`c_{d}`$ for each token position $`\mathbf{x}_{d}`$,
defined for example as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
c_{d}=\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{0,d}\mid\mathbf{x}_{t}^{/d})-\max_{i\neq d}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{0,i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{t}^{/d}),
``` |  |

where
$`p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x}_{t}^{/d})\approx q_{0|t}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{x}^{/d})`$
denotes the denoising model, and $`\mathbf{x}^{/d}`$ is the sequence
whose $`d`$-th component is removed or masked.

For positions with low confidence scores, the corrector step performs
token-level resampling according to

|     |                                                              |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbf{x}_{d}\sim p_{\theta}(\cdot^{d}\mid\mathbf{x}^{/d}).
       ```                                                           |     |

Unlike GIDD and SCDD, Informed Corrector enables self-correction only at
the sampling or inference stage. The self-correction mechanism itself is
not learned during training, but instead relies on model-dependent
confidence estimates at inference time.

#### Plug-in Remasking for Inference-time Self-correction of Masked Diffusions (PRISM) ([Kim et al., 2025](#bib.bib16))

Given a pretrained masked diffusion model $`p_{\theta}`$, PRISM trains a
token-level score function $`g_{\phi}`$ via

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}(\phi):=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},i,\mathbf{y}\sim p_{\theta}^{i}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{z})}\Big[\mathrm{CE}\big(\mathbf{1}[\mathbf{x}_{i}=\mathbf{y}_{i}],g_{\phi}^{i}(\mathbf{y})\big)\Big],
``` |  |

where $`\mathrm{CE}`$ denotes the cross-entropy loss. Here
$`\mathbf{x}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}`$ denotes the original clean
sequence, $`\mathbf{z}\sim q(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})`$ is obtained by
the forward masking process in MDLM, and
$`\mathbf{y}^{i}\sim p_{\theta}^{i}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{z})`$ is a token
sampled from the pretrained MDM at a masked position $`i`$.

Intuitively, the score model $`g_{\phi}^{i}(\mathbf{y})`$ estimates the
conditional probability

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g_{\phi}^{i*}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{i}=\mathbf{y}_{i}\mid\mathbf{y}\oplus\mathbf{m}_{i}),
``` |  |

where the randomness is induced by the joint distribution over
$`\mathbf{x}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}`$,
$`\mathbf{z}\sim q(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})`$, and
$`\mathbf{y}^{i}\sim p_{\theta}^{i}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{z})`$. Here
$`\mathbf{y}\oplus\mathbf{m}_{i}`$ denotes the sequence obtained by
masking the $`i`$-th token of $`\mathbf{y}`$, indicating that
$`\mathbf{y}_{i}`$ is unobserved when evaluating $`g_{\phi}^{i}`$.

During inference, at each intermediate state $`\mathbf{z}_{t}`$, the
learned score $`g_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{t})`$ is used to identify unmasked
tokens with low estimated quality. Such tokens are remarked by setting
$`\mathbf{z}_{t,d}=\mathbf{m}`$, enabling inference-time self-correction
through iterative remaking and unmasking.

#### Path Planning Self-Planning (P2-Self) ([Peng et al., 2025](#bib.bib14))

In the training stage, the authors follow the Masked Diffusion Language
Model (MDLM) framework and train a denoising model

|     |                                                |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       D_{\theta}:\mathcal{V}^{L}\to(\Delta^{d})^{L}.
       ```                                             |     | (61) |

In inference, let

|     |                                            |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbf{y}\sim D_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}).
       ```                                         |     | (62) |

The authors introduce and train a planner

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{\phi}:\mathcal{V}^{L}\times\mathcal{V}^{L}\to[0,1]^{L},\qquad(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y})\mapsto G_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}),
``` |  | (63) |

where $`G_{\phi}^{j}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y})`$ denotes the
probability that the $`j`$-th coordinate should be (re)sampled. Given
adjacent time steps $`s<t`$, the reverse update is defined by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
i\sim\frac{G_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y})}{\sum_{j=1}^{L}G_{\phi}^{j}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y})},
``` |  | (64) |

and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t,\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{s}^{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i})=\begin{cases}\text{Cat}\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{s}^{i};\dfrac{(1-\alpha_{t-1})\mathbf{m}+(\alpha_{t-1}-\alpha_{t})D_{\theta}^{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})}{1-\alpha_{t}}\right),&\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}=\mathbf{m},\\[11.99998pt]
\text{Cat}\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{s}^{i};\dfrac{\big((\alpha_{t}-1)D_{\theta}^{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}+1-\alpha_{t-1}\big)\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}+(\alpha_{t-1}-\alpha_{t})D_{\theta}^{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})\big(1-D_{\theta}^{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}\big)}\right),&\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}\neq\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (65) |

Intuitively, $`G_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y})`$ selects a single
coordinate $`i`$ to update. If $`\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}=\mathbf{m}`$, the
transition reduces to the original unmasking rule in MDLM. If
$`\mathbf{x}_{t}^{i}\neq\mathbf{m}`$, the model remasks and resamples
from $`D_{\theta}`$ at coordinate $`i`$ with some probability.

Similar to PRISM and Informed Corrector, this method does not
incorporate self-correction in the pre-training stage. In addition,
there is inherent redundancy in the inference stage: If
$`\mathbf{x}_{t,i}`$ is masked, the resampling mechanism will not work.
````
