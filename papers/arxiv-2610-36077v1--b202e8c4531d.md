# The Trinity: Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents

The Trinity Research Team  [Project
Page](https://trinity-rsi.github.io/)   
 [GitHub](https://github.com/InfRec-Cardinal/The-Trinity)

###### Abstract

Multimodal intelligence can be viewed as human-guided co-evolution
between worlds and agents through interaction. However, scaling this
process faces two bottlenecks: constructing heterogeneous, verifiable
environments is costly, and manual Harness iteration cannot keep pace
with growing task diversity and complexity. A key insight is that tasks
with different modalities and surface forms can share the same
underlying capability requirements. We therefore introduce The Trinity,
a recursive self-improvement framework coupling world generation with
Harness evolution through three roles: Creator, Explorer, and Evolver.
Drawing on verified execution trajectories, Evolver shifts the basis of
task organization from surface categories to shared requirements,
reorganizing the original task tree into a hierarchical capability tree.
We introduce Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real: for heterogeneous tasks at
the same node, Creator abstracts their common requirements into
simplified, verifiable simulated worlds for capability learning, while
evaluation on original tasks measures transfer. Along this capability
tree, Explorer progressively evolves reusable capability modules through
selective inheritance, forming a Harness tree whose validated updates
accumulate across iterations within a fixed topology. The resulting
Harnesses specialize in different capabilities, motivating a
Capability-Aware Harness Router that uses public task information to
select compatible module configurations from the tree. Our evolved
Harnesses improve mean performance by 34 points across 28 training
benchmarks and 26 points across 18 unseen benchmarks, including two
real-world embodied tasks.

## 1 Introduction

Multimodal foundation models have rapidly advanced in understanding,
reasoning, and generation across text, images, video, and audio
\[[1](#bib.bib4), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib49), [4](#bib.bib3),
[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib40), [7](#bib.bib50), [8](#bib.bib119),
[9](#bib.bib123), [10](#bib.bib121), [11](#bib.bib124),
[12](#bib.bib125)\], enabling agents to perceive, decide, and act in
open environments \[[13](#bib.bib34), [14](#bib.bib35),
[15](#bib.bib122)\]. We view multimodal intelligence as an evolving
loop: worlds provide tasks, humans guide learning, and agents interact
with the world to produce verifiable outcomes \[[16](#bib.bib116),
[17](#bib.bib117)\]. As tasks and environments grow increasingly
complex, this loop faces two bottlenecks: (1) Constructing large-scale,
heterogeneous, and verifiable environments manually is costly,
motivating automated world generation; and (2) Agent capability is
increasingly constrained by the Harness \[[18](#bib.bib14),
[19](#bib.bib13), [20](#bib.bib15), [21](#bib.bib41), [22](#bib.bib48),
[23](#bib.bib43)\], whose manual iteration cannot keep pace with growing
task complexity and diversity.

Inspired by Harness recursive self-improvement \[[24](#bib.bib12),
[25](#bib.bib23), [26](#bib.bib22)\] and agent–environment co-evolution
\[[27](#bib.bib37), [28](#bib.bib39), [29](#bib.bib38)\], we ask whether
this manual loop can improve continuously. Generated worlds provide
adaptive curricula, while verified interaction feedback guides Harness
updates.

However, extending this process across heterogeneous text-only and
multimodal tasks \[[30](#bib.bib118), [31](#bib.bib120)\] presents a
challenge. Current Harness RSI works focus on text-modality tasks (e.g.,
coding and math \[[24](#bib.bib12), [23](#bib.bib43), [32](#bib.bib54),
[33](#bib.bib51)\]) and adopt task-specific evolution
\[[34](#bib.bib36), [22](#bib.bib48), [35](#bib.bib53),
[25](#bib.bib23)\]. This approach is suitable when tasks share similar
capability requirements, but can lead to Harness overfitting and poor
generalization across heterogeneous tasks. Conversely, a global Harness
struggles to accommodate conflicting task demands, including
performance–efficiency trade-offs in embodied tasks, limiting its
efficiency and adaptability.

A key insight is that tasks with different modalities and surface forms
can share the same underlying capability requirements. For example,
although ARC-AGI-3 \[[36](#bib.bib66)\] and Micromouse
\[[37](#bib.bib67)\] differ in task structure, both rely on accurate
perception and state tracking: the former requires recognizing grid
states and their changes, while the latter requires continuously
updating environmental information during maze exploration. Grouping
tasks by these shared capability requirements makes the underlying
mechanisms a common learning objective, enabling generalization across
tasks.

     Position. Current RSI training relies heavily on task-specific
optimization, limiting capability generalization across tasks. We
advocate a shift from task-driven to capability-driven self-improvement,
where shared capability requirements across tasks guide the learning and
optimization of multiple specialized Harnesses. This shift highlights
the role of shared capabilities in cross-task generalization and
naturally motivates a capability-aware Router that matches tasks to
suitable Harnesses based on their capability requirements.     

To operationalize this position, we introduce The Trinity, a recursive
self-improvement framework coupling environment generation with agent
evolution through Creator, Explorer, and Evolver. Using verified
trajectories, Evolver identifies task capability requirements and groups
tasks sharing them, reorganizing the original task tree into a
hierarchical capability tree. Heterogeneous tasks at the same node share
a learning objective, motivating Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real:
Creator abstracts their common requirements into simplified, verifiable
environments, while evaluation on the original tasks measures capability
transfer. Along the capability tree, Explorer evolves a Harness tree
through selective inheritance and local refinement, while Creator
evolves at corresponding nodes and consolidates validated updates into a
new Base Harness at the end of each iteration.

The Harnesses specialize in different capabilities, requiring
task-dependent selection. For Explorer, our Capability-Aware Harness
Router uses verified execution experience and public task information to
select a matching branch of Explorer’s Harness tree. For Creator,
overlap within its Harness tree motivates merging complementary modules
and removing near-duplicates to form a new Base Harness. Explorer and
Creator play distinct roles in RSI processes along the same capability
tree.

Contributions. ❶ Capability-driven multimodal RSI. We make shared
capability requirements the organizing principle of Harness evolution.
Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real turns these requirements into compact
training worlds, yielding a reusable module-based Harness tree whose
specialization motivates capability-aware routing. ❷ Capability–module
priors for Harness design. We connect task-level capability requirements
to reusable module configurations, using empirical associations and
controlled interventions to examine which module support matters for a
task. ❸ Efficient evolution and transfer. We show that targeting shared
decision logic supports effective Harness improvement at low evolution
cost, with transfer across heterogeneous tasks, unseen benchmarks, and
real-world embodied settings.

## 2 Preliminaries

### 2.1 Multimodal Agentic Tasks

Long-horizon multimodal agentic tasks span interactive games
\[[38](#bib.bib70)\], GUI manipulation \[[39](#bib.bib26)\], and
embodied interaction \[[13](#bib.bib34), [14](#bib.bib35)\]. They
combine perception, understanding, reasoning, and generation with
memory, planning, and adaptation across observation–action sequences.
Agents must retain evidence, revise hypotheses, and adjust plans as
observations arrive. Because later decisions depend on earlier actions
and feedback, misread observations or forgotten constraints can
propagate across steps. Agent performance thus increasingly depends on
effective Harness organization and coordination. Dynamically
coordinating capabilities over long interactions and adapting to
increasingly large, heterogeneous task sets remain key challenges.

### 2.2 Agent Harness

A Harness configuration $`\phi`$ coordinates a frozen model
$`f_{\theta}`$ through six control-flow protocols:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}=(\Pi_{\mathrm{er}},\mathbf{M},\mathbf{I},\mathbf{P},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{L}),\qquad\mathcal{H}_{0}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{\phi_{0}}\quad\text{(Base)}.
``` |  | (1) |

Given task $`\tau`$, observation $`o_{t}`$, public history $`\xi_{<t}`$,
controller state $`s_{t}`$, and available resources
$`\mathcal{C}_{\tau}`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle f_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\Pi_{\mathrm{er}}(o_{t},\xi_{<t}),`$ | $`\displaystyle v_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}(\xi_{<t},s_{t},f_{t}),`$ |  | (2) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{Cand}_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}(\tau,v_{t}),`$ | $`\displaystyle d_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(\tau,s_{t},v_{t},\mathrm{Cand}_{t}),`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}(\mathcal{C}_{\tau},s_{t},v_{t},d_{t}),`$ | $`\displaystyle(s_{t+1},e_{t},c_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}(s_{t},v_{t},d_{t},\mathcal{C}_{t}).`$ |  |  |

Perception extracts facts $`f_{t}`$; Memory builds a history-conditioned
view $`v_{t}`$; Inference maintains compatible candidates; Planning
ranks or prunes options into directive $`d_{t}`$; Orchestration
identifies applicable resources; and Loop resolves execution $`e_{t}`$
and commitment $`c_{t}`$, including withholding action. These roles are
independent of task categories and capability-tree nodes. The equations
describe functional roles and their data dependencies; individual module
bindings are invoked under the runtime policy described in Section
[3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Stage 3: Harness Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents").

Modules are pure functions of supplied public records, without
hidden-state access or environment actions. Only Memory may declare
explicit carry; hypothesis refutation requires observed contradiction.
All protocols may emit typed readiness signals, while Loop resolves
execution. Orchestration assesses applicability, relevance, and trust
within a fixed host-exposed tool set. Evolution updates module
configurations with $`\theta`$ fixed.

Capabilities and protocols have a many-to-many relationship: the tree
groups shared requirements, while protocols locate module updates.
Hypothesis consistency can involve Memory organizing evidence, Inference
checking compatibility, and Planning ranking informative tests; the same
Memory module can support several requirements. Explorer retains node
modules; Creator uses the same tree structure and consolidates its next
Base Harness. Evaluation-time skills are prepared separately (Section
[4.1](#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1 "Evaluation-Time Skill Preparation. ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents"));
they are not stored, evolved, inherited, or routed in the tree.

### 3.1 Framework Overview

The Trinity couples environment generation and Harness evolution with a
frozen backbone (Figure
[2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
Explorer collects trajectories; Evolver organizes requirements from
specifications and verified trajectories into a capability tree
(Sections
[3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Stage 1: Task Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")–[3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Stage 2: Capability Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
Through Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real, Creator builds simplified,
verifiable worlds; original-task evaluation measures transfer (Section
[3.3](#S3.SS3.SSS0.Px2 "World Creation. ‣ 3.3 Stage 2: Capability Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
Explorer selectively inherits and refines reusable modules, accumulating
validated updates in a fixed-topology Harness tree (Sections
[3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4 Stage 3: Harness Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")
and
[3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Recursive Self-Improvement ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
The Capability-Aware Harness Router selects a compatible branch using
public task information and verified experience (Section
[3.6](#S3.SS6 "3.6 Capability-Aware Harness Routing ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).

### 3.2 Stage 1: Task Tree Construction

#### Benchmark Organization.

Evolver normalizes public multimodal benchmarks into specifications of
goals, observations, actions, feedback, task-completion criteria, and
budgets, independently checked against their sources. A common
observation–action interface preserves information access and scoring
rules. Benchmarks are grouped into five requirement domains: rule
discovery, constrained problem solving, perception and control,
multi-agent interaction, and long-horizon environments. The
training/unseen split is fixed before capability induction.

#### World Exploration.

Explorer collects trajectories from sampled training instances using
$`\mathcal{H}_{0}`$ under fixed settings. Only training records support
capability-tree construction, world generation, module learning, and
Router training (Section
[3.6](#S3.SS6 "3.6 Capability-Aware Harness Routing ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
Unseen capability requirements are assessed only after the tree is
fixed, for evaluation. Instance selectors and initial-state hashes are
fixed in advance. Diagnostic reruns reset memory; completed runs are
retained, while infrastructure failures are marked invalid and retried.
Hash-chain checks verify execution records.

### 3.3 Stage 2: Capability Tree Construction

Evolver derives requirements from task goals, permitted observations,
action rules, and task-completion criteria, linking them to rules and
trajectory steps satisfying or violating these criteria through
rule-based checks. Host-private information supports only retrospective
verification.

#### Requirement Organization.

Using training-task specifications and trajectories, independent agents
propose capability categories $`\mathcal{K}`$ (Appendix
[B](#A2 "Appendix B Capability Tree ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents"));
a judge consolidates them, and reviewers independently check
definitions, evidence, assignments, and coverage. Each training task
$`\tau`$ receives a fixed requirement set
$`K_{\tau}\subseteq\mathcal{K}`$, including requirements already
satisfied by $`\mathcal{H}_{0}`$.

The tree is constructed from training tasks
$`\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{tr}}`$ only. A deterministic capability order
$`\prec`$, fixed before construction, defines paths and the tree:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\tau}=\operatorname{sort}_{\prec}(K_{\tau}),\qquad\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{Trie}\bigl(\{p_{\tau}:\tau\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\bigr).
``` |  | (3) |

The $`\operatorname{Trie}`$ merges common prefixes across domains. Each
node $`v`$ supports an Explorer module configuration for its prefix;
tasks retain endpoint associations, including at internal nodes. Node
requirements define local objectives, with selective parent inheritance
for Explorer; the order implies no causal dependencies. Requirements for
unseen tasks $`\tau\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{un}}`$ are identified
afterward using the fixed categories, solely for retrospective Router
evaluation, without modifying the tree.

![Refer to caption](2610.36077v1/fig/pipeline2.png)

Figure 3: Capability-driven world creation at node d.

#### World Creation.

Once the capability tree is built, each node is linked to training
benchmarks through shared requirements. Although the four tasks in
Figure
[3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ Requirement Organization. ‣ 3.3 Stage 2: Capability Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")
differ, all require evidence verification before decisions. This
motivates Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real: Evolver designs simplified
training levels from node requirements, descendants, and representative
tasks’ public specifications, excluding target-task layouts, private
proofs, and solution traces while preserving core decision processes.
Creator implements these levels using a node Harness initialized from
its Base Harness or inherited parent modules, omitting aesthetic
optimization to focus on task mechanisms. Evolver’s host-executed
verifier pool checks node binding, execution, state transitions,
rendering, and completion. Node identifiers link specifications,
trajectories, and module updates, while private verifiers and public
feedback guide revisions. Explorer tests verified worlds with a frozen
screening Harness, admitting only those within a predefined
intermediate-performance band. Otherwise, Creator adjusts difficulty
along predefined dimensions. Trial counts, performance thresholds,
action budgets, and retry limits are fixed in advance; worlds and
verifiers remain unchanged during local training. Curricula may be
reused or regenerated, while original benchmarks assess capability
transfer. Appendix
[E.2](#A5.SS2 "E.2 Node Training Levels ‣ Appendix E Visual Record ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")
details the curricula for each node.

### 3.4 Stage 3: Harness Tree Construction

For Explorer, capability-driven RSI builds a Harness tree aligned with
the capability tree. At each node, Explorer executes admitted worlds
with inherited and local modules. Evolver compares successful and failed
trajectories using public observations, actions, and host-assigned
outcomes to identify node-specific improvements. It develops reusable
modules through host-registered bindings in the six-protocol library
(Section
[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Agent Harness ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")),
refining interfaces, bounded configurations, or implementations. Updates
record node, predecessor-version, episode, and executable-example
references. Before local updates, the host validates evidence,
ownership, interfaces, lineage, and typed input–output assertions;
ancestor modules remain preserved. Bindings use public context and
permitted memory under a fixed runtime policy. Multiple bindings per
protocol may coexist, with model calls determining execution order.
Admission verifies grounding and executability; evaluation measures
performance. Creator evolves only upon verifier-detected failures. At
each iteration’s end, validated proposals are consolidated with existing
Base Harness modules, merging complementary modules and removing
near-duplicates. Section
[3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Recursive Self-Improvement ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")
details persistence, selective inheritance, and consolidation.

### 3.5 Recursive Self-Improvement

Explorer evolves along the capability tree, with Creator following at
corresponding nodes; their Harness trees share the same structure
(Appendix
[C](#A3 "Appendix C Harness Tree ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
Explorer selectively inherits node modules; at each iteration’s end,
Creator deduplicates and merges complementary updates into its next Base
Harness. Let $`V=V(\mathcal{T})`$ and $`V^{+}`$ denote all nodes and
non-root nodes, respectively.

#### Node State.

For Explorer, let
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E},v}^{(i)}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{\phi_{\mathrm{E},v}^{(i)}}`$
denote the Harness at node $`v\in V`$ after iteration $`i`$. Nodes
initialize with $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E},v}^{(0)}=\mathcal{H}_{0}`$ and
available training feedback $`\mathcal{F}_{v}^{(0)}`$. Top-down
iterations retain local modules and keep the synthetic root fixed.

#### Selective Inheritance.

At non-root node $`v\in V^{+}`$, compare the previous inherited registry
$`P_{v}^{(i)}`$ with the updated parent $`\widehat{P}_{v}^{(i+1)}`$,
holding local modules $`L_{v}^{(i)}`$ fixed. Writing the Harness’s
module configuration as
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E},v}^{(i)}=P_{v}^{(i)}\oplus L_{v}^{(i)}`$,
registries use host-registered binding IDs and right-biased union:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
(P\oplus L)[m]=\begin{cases}L[m],&m\in\operatorname{dom}(L),\\
P[m],&m\in\operatorname{dom}(P)\setminus\operatorname{dom}(L).\end{cases}
``` |  | (4) |

Parent exports are bound to parent identifiers and state digests.
Ancestor entries compose at recorded versions from root to parent, then
local modules; overrides preserve ancestor sources and local
ownership/version lineage. Under identical validation tasks, trial
count, and budget, the updated parent is retained only with strictly
higher host-verified performance, measured by task-completion counts;
otherwise, the previous registry is retained.

#### Continual Refinement.

Let $`P_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ be the retained inherited registry and
$`\mathcal{D}_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ the admitted-world curriculum conditioned on
the fixed requirement, previous feedback $`\mathcal{F}_{v}^{(i)}`$, and
prior coverage. Curricula may be renewed or reused. Explorer’s
trajectories $`\mathcal{Z}_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ guide Evolver’s local update:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{Rollout}\!\left(P_{v}^{(i+1)}\oplus L_{v}^{(i)},\mathcal{D}_{v}^{(i+1)}\right),`$ |  | (5) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\widetilde{L}_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{Evolve}\!\left(L_{v}^{(i)},\mathcal{Z}_{v}^{(i+1)},\mathcal{F}_{v}^{(i)}\right).`$ |  |  |

Trajectories record public observations, actions, host-assigned
outcomes, and executed configurations. The host checks update evidence,
ownership, interfaces, predecessor versions, and executable contracts.
Accepted updates modify local state; rejection preserves it. The
retained $`L_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ yields
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E},v}^{(i+1)}=P_{v}^{(i+1)}\oplus L_{v}^{(i+1)}`$;
local admission requires no score increase.

Subsequent training-side evaluation of the retained Harness yields
$`\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(i+1)}`$ and next-iteration feedback

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{F}_{v}^{(i+1)}=\operatorname{Feedback}\!\left(\mathcal{Z}_{v}^{(i+1)},\mathcal{E}_{v}^{(i+1)}\right),
``` |  | (6) |

where $`\operatorname{Feedback}`$ retains verified observations,
outcomes, failure evidence, and episode/module-version references for
the next iteration. Completed nodes export retained state to children;
sibling branches evolve independently. Explorer retains
$`\mathcal{H}^{(i)}=\{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E},v}^{(i)}:v\in V\}`$ for
routing.

#### Creator Base Harness Update.

Creator’s Base Harness $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{C}}^{(i)}`$ has module
registry $`R_{\mathrm{C}}^{(i)}`$, initialized with pre-RSI modules at
$`i=0`$. Creator evolves nodes in top-down order within each iteration.
Root children start from this base; other nodes inherit their parent’s
updated module configuration. Each node retains its own guidance.
Verifier-detected failures trigger local evolution, yielding validated
proposals $`\Delta R_{\mathrm{C},v}^{(i+1)}`$. At iteration end,
proposals and base modules are consolidated in learning order
$`\prec_{\mathrm{C}}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{\mathrm{C}}^{(i+1)}=\operatorname{Consolidate}_{\prec_{\mathrm{C}}}\!\left(R_{\mathrm{C}}^{(i)}\cup\bigcup_{v\in V^{+}}\Delta R_{\mathrm{C},v}^{(i+1)}\right).
``` |  | (7) |

The resulting registry configures $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{C}}^{(i+1)}`$,
the Base Harness for the next iteration. Validated revisions replace
predecessors; distinct capabilities are retained, partial overlaps are
merged to preserve complementary functionality, and near-duplicates
retain the earlier-learned module. Each binding retains one active
definition and its provenance. Guidance, construction notes, and action
budgets remain node-specific. Without accepted updates, the Base Harness
remains unchanged. Explorer’s evaluation-time skills are prepared
separately (Section
[4.1](#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1 "Evaluation-Time Skill Preparation. ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).

### 3.6 Capability-Aware Harness Routing

Routing follows a context–action–feedback loop \[[40](#bib.bib65)\]. Let
$`x_{n}`$ contain the public goal, initial observation, metadata, and
images, and $`\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{(i)}`$ be the fixed,
versioned Harness pool. Memory $`\mathcal{M}_{0}`$ is initialized by
importing verified training-side categorization trajectories into empty
memory once in file order (Section
[3.2](#S3.SS2.SSS0.Px2 "World Exploration. ‣ 3.2 Stage 1: Task Tree Construction ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
These $`\mathcal{H}_{0}`$ executions record public context, the executed
configuration, outcomes, and costs; unseen categorization records are
excluded. The agent, prompt, candidate modules, retrieval settings, and
verifier remain fixed.

For each task, candidate descriptions, retrieved experience $`R`$, and
observed performance–cost summaries $`S`$ guide selection:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{n}=\pi\!\left(x_{n},\mathcal{C},R(x_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}),S(\mathcal{M}_{n})\right).
``` |  | (8) |

The agent commits $`h_{n}`$ with an evidence-based rationale before
execution. Retrieval uses cosine similarity $`\geq 0.5`$, at most ten
records, and a 20,000-record FIFO memory. Summaries include only
matching executed configurations; untried outcomes remain unknown. Tasks
are processed once in file order. After terminal receipts are verified
against the committed decision and registry hash, outcome and cost
$`f_{n}`$ update memory for later tasks:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{M}_{n+1}=\operatorname{Append}\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{n},(x_{n},h_{n},f_{n})\right).
``` |  | (9) |

Unseen task–capability assignments remain excluded from routing context,
verification, and memory; they are accessed only for evaluation after
the complete prediction ledger is hash-locked. Technical failures remain
recorded and score zero; invalid selections receive no fallback.

     Finding 5. Capability-Driven RSI supports embodied transfer. The
evaluated Harness–skill configurations improve on both simulated and
physical tasks, linking node-level module evolution with task-specific
skill preparation.     

## 5 Related Work

#### Recursive Harness Self-Improvement.

Self-improvement spans Gödel machines \[[87](#bib.bib1)\], LLM systems
that rewrite their own agent code \[[88](#bib.bib10), [89](#bib.bib11),
[24](#bib.bib12)\], automated agent design \[[90](#bib.bib16),
[91](#bib.bib18), [92](#bib.bib17)\], prompt optimization
\[[93](#bib.bib19), [94](#bib.bib20), [95](#bib.bib21)\], and learning
from self-feedback and reflection \[[96](#bib.bib6), [97](#bib.bib7),
[98](#bib.bib8)\]. With weights fixed, improvement shifts to the
harness, which is synthesized from environment feedback
\[[33](#bib.bib51)\], repaired from failed trajectories
\[[32](#bib.bib54), [35](#bib.bib53)\], self-revised
\[[19](#bib.bib13)\], optimized as code \[[18](#bib.bib14),
[20](#bib.bib15)\], adapted at test time \[[99](#bib.bib56)\] or per
task \[[22](#bib.bib48)\], improved at the meta level
\[[100](#bib.bib45), [101](#bib.bib44)\], and shown to lift frozen MLLMs
\[[21](#bib.bib41)\]; benchmarks and surveys map this space
\[[102](#bib.bib57), [23](#bib.bib43), [103](#bib.bib58)\]. Closest to
ours are JIT-Agent’s harness generator \[[25](#bib.bib23)\], HarnessX’s
typed primitives \[[104](#bib.bib52)\], HSI’s per-task-family harnesses
\[[34](#bib.bib36)\], ModularRSI’s modules evolved on benchmark-disjoint
tasks \[[26](#bib.bib22)\], and the flat capability map of Harness
Continual Learning \[[105](#bib.bib42)\] and regime-branched harness
tree of Adaptive Auto-Harness \[[106](#bib.bib55)\], both evolved on the
task stream. Trinity instead induces a capability tree from training
trajectories, shares prefixes across 44 heterogeneous benchmarks with
selective module inheritance, evolves Harnesses in Creator-generated
worlds that exclude target-task layouts and solutions, and evaluates on
held-out benchmarks and real robots. Unlike LLM routers
\[[40](#bib.bib65)\], ours routes among capability-specific Harnesses
over one frozen backbone.

#### Co-evolution of Agents and Environments.

Open-ended co-evolution \[[107](#bib.bib28), [108](#bib.bib29),
[109](#bib.bib30)\], unsupervised environment design
\[[110](#bib.bib31), [29](#bib.bib38)\], curricula \[[111](#bib.bib32),
[112](#bib.bib33)\], and embodied co-design \[[28](#bib.bib39)\] adapt
training to the learner; Voyager grows a skill library under an
automatic curriculum \[[113](#bib.bib9)\]. Loops co-evolve language
agents with environments \[[114](#bib.bib59)\], optimizers
\[[115](#bib.bib60)\], or harnesses \[[116](#bib.bib61),
[117](#bib.bib64)\]; environment generation spans embodied
\[[118](#bib.bib25), [119](#bib.bib24)\], tool-use
\[[120](#bib.bib47)\], and terminal settings \[[121](#bib.bib62),
[122](#bib.bib63)\], language world models \[[123](#bib.bib27)\], and
game verification \[[124](#bib.bib46)\]. SPADE is closest
\[[27](#bib.bib37)\]: one LLM designs environments with verification
code and learns roles by reinforcement learning. Trinity keeps the
backbone frozen and couples world generation with Explorer and Creator
Harness evolution along a capability hierarchy, execution outcomes
refining Explorer and verifier-detected failures refining Creator.

## 6 Conclusion

We advocate Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement and introduce
The Trinity, a framework that couples world generation with Harness
evolution through Creator, Explorer, and Evolver. By organizing
heterogeneous tasks around shared capability requirements, the framework
constructs simplified, verifiable worlds whose execution feedback guides
the evolution of reusable modules. These modules form specialized
Harness configurations, with capability–module correlations and
controlled interventions providing empirical support for capability
requirements as priors for module design and composition. To apply these
evolved capabilities across tasks, capability-aware routing selects
suitable Harness configurations using public task information and
verified execution experience. Experiments across diverse training and
unseen benchmarks demonstrate improved aggregate performance and
cross-task generalization, while deployment in two real-world embodied
settings further illustrates the transfer of these capabilities to
physical environments.

## 7 Discussion

#### Limitations.

Our framework uses web search for benchmark discovery, but retrieval
coverage and result quality may vary across queries. In addition, our
current study focuses primarily on self-improvement at the Harness
level.

#### Outlook.

Capability-driven learning offers a new paradigm for generalizable agent
improvement, where shared capability requirements across tasks guide
learning and optimization. Our framework and results at the Harness
level demonstrate the promise of this perspective. Looking ahead, we
envision dynamically expanding the capability tree to capture emerging
capability requirements and extending this paradigm to model parameter
training, where capability-aware routing and RSI form a closed loop that
jointly drives capability discovery, acquisition, and refinement.

Candidate benchmarks were collected through agent web search over five
types of source: research benchmarks released with code or data, public
AI competitions, human contests and testing standards, published games
and puzzle communities, and professional practice. Because a multimodal
agent must perceive its world through whatever interface that world
exposes, the inventory spans every input modality in which agentic tasks
are posed, and each benchmark retains the observation protocol of its
source. Each modality probes a different part of an agent’s capability.
Image input (photographs, rendered scenes, puzzle images, and robot
camera views) tests perception from pixels, viewpoint selection, and the
grounding of decisions in what is seen. Video input tests evidence
acquisition over time: selecting frames within a budget, tracking events
across them, and understanding what happened. Boards, grids, and maps
rendered as text test spatial reasoning and state tracking over a
symbolic visual state; with pixel-level perception removed, a failure
can be attributed to reasoning rather than to seeing. Plain text tests
the capabilities a multimodal agent relies on downstream of perception:
rule discovery, experimental design, planning, negotiation, and
strategic interaction. Keeping all four modalities in one inventory lets
visual and text benchmarks share nodes in the capability tree (Appendix
[B](#A2 "Appendix B Capability Tree ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).

Inspection \[[60](#bib.bib84)\], Saboteur \[[73](#bib.bib78)\], The Long

We rebuilt each of them through agent web search from the published
rules and examples, preserving the mechanics, information access, and
scoring of the source; the rebuilt environments present their state to
the agent as images or symbolic text. Every unseen benchmark other than
the two real-robot versions runs on its official environment or
evaluation code, e.g., the BALROG harness \[[38](#bib.bib70)\], the
PHYRE simulator \[[70](#bib.bib71)\], the DiG-bench server

BuildArena \[[59](#bib.bib75)\].

## Appendix C Harness Tree

Explorer and Creator both start from H₀ and evolve along the same
capability tree, but they retain their learned modules differently.
Explorer keeps a Harness at every node except e: each node selectively
inherits its parent’s modules and refines its own local modules, and the
resulting Harness tree is retained for routing. Creator keeps a single
shared library: within an iteration, its node Harnesses inherit updated
parent modules, and at the end of the iteration the modules proposed at
all nodes are consolidated with the current library into the Base
Harness of the next iteration. Explorer’s trajectories drive Evolver’s
local module revisions, whereas worlds rejected by the verifiers drive
Creator’s module proposals. Every module is a versioned function behind
a prompt-facing interface and is installed only after the host checks
its evidence, interface, and version lineage.

### C.1 Explorer Harness Tree

Explorer plays every benchmark with the frozen backbone (Appendix
[D](#A4 "Appendix D Evaluation Details ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")),
and each instance uses the best-scoring compatible node Harness under
the Oracle setting. Every node Harness starts from H₀, which has no
learned module, and combines the inherited modules at their retained
versions with the node’s local modules; a benchmark at c$`\to`$d$`\to`$a
therefore uses the modules of c, c$`\to`$d, and c$`\to`$d$`\to`$a,
subject to selective inheritance (Figure
[12](#A3.F12 "Figure 12 ‣ C.1 Explorer Harness Tree ‣ Appendix C Harness Tree ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
At each node, Explorer plays the admitted training levels with these
modules, and Evolver compares successful and failed trajectories to
create or revise local modules; the host validates evidence, ownership,
interfaces, and version lineage before every update, and ancestor
modules are preserved. An updated parent is inherited only if it
achieves strictly more host-verified task completions than the
previously inherited modules under the same validation tasks, trial
count, and budget; otherwise the previous modules are kept. A local
module overrides an inherited module bound to the same identifier.
Module calls take public inputs and return structured advice without
advancing the environment, with at most two calls per step and none
forced.

### C.2 Creator Shared Harness

Creator builds the simplified training worlds of each node with a coding
harness, created per node and iteration, with a workspace for the world
and an evidence directory outside it. Its Base Harness at the first
iteration is H₀ extended with the pre-RSI modules listed below. Within
each iteration, Creator visits the nodes top-down: the children of the
root start from the Base Harness, and every other node inherits its
parent’s updated modules. Each node keeps its own guidance, one
construction note, and one public action budget per iteration.
Verifier-detected failures trigger local evolution, and at the end of
the iteration the validated proposals of all nodes are consolidated with
the current library in learning order (Equation
[7](#S3.E7 "In Creator Base Harness Update. ‣ 3.5 Recursive Self-Improvement ‣ 3 The Trinity ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")):
validated revisions replace their registered predecessors; among new
modules, distinct capabilities are retained, partial overlaps are merged
to preserve complementary functionality, and near-duplicates keep the
earlier-learned module and discard the later one. The consolidated
library becomes the Base Harness of the next iteration; it is shared by
all nodes and exposed as a single tool that runs one module per call.
The host, not the model, supplies the inputs of each call: the build
digest, the submission allowance, and, for a rule module, a measurement
of the build. Fields that the host does not supply remain unknown, and
missing evidence never passes. Before submission, the host reruns the
applicable audits and blocks any missing, stale, or conflicting result;
Creator returns errors in the frozen task design or verifier contract to
their owner instead of rewriting them.

In the module names, a witness is a recorded route run on the current
build; the commitment cost is the number of extra actions that a wrong
commitment costs over the direct route; the screening band is the
performance range into which a world must fall; and a neutralized
control is the previous version with the changed difficulty axis reset.
The final Creator module library holds 25 modules (Figure
[13](#A3.F13 "Figure 13 ‣ C.2 Creator Shared Harness ‣ Appendix C Harness Tree ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")).
Eleven are pre-RSI modules, audits that form the initial module library
of the Creator Harness and apply the host’s formulas to recorded routes,
claims, edits, or submissions:  Commitment Cost Arithmetic Audit,
 Witness and Action Ceiling Audit,  Difficulty Axis Range and
Feasibility Audit,  Screening Band Direction Diagnosis,  Layout Novelty
Ledger,  Effective Difficulty Change Audit,  Single Change Attribution
Audit,  Repair Scope Gate,  Claim and Execution Evidence Census,
 Bounded Search Declaration Audit, and  Submission Budget and Repeated
Rejection Guard. The other 14 are rule modules, predicate programs over
one kind of host measurement learned from rejected worlds, each listed
with the nodes at which it was formed:  Production and Private Witness
Isolation (d),  Accepted Entry and Terminal Consistency (d$`\to`$a),
 Reasoning Change and Direction Audit (a, c, d$`\to`$a, e),  Public
Visual State and Probe Coverage Audit (a, b, c),  Required Composition
and Negative Route Audit (b, d$`\to`$b),  Target Attributes, Classes,
and Acceptance Audit (a, b, c, d$`\to`$b),  Controlled Identity and
Frame-State Alignment Audit (b, c, d$`\to`$a),  Action Polarity and
Coordinate Basis Audit (a, b),  Interaction Coverage and Decision-Time
Visibility Audit (b, d),  Neutralized Control Initialization and

### E.2 Node Training Levels

At every capability node, Evolver designs short training levels from the
node’s requirement, and Creator implements and revises them under the
Creator Harness of Appendix
[C.2](#A3.SS2 "C.2 Creator Shared Harness ‣ Appendix C Harness Tree ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents");
each level is a simulation world with a 3D scene, public rules, a fixed
action budget, and a scorer, and one level per node and iteration gives
44 levels. Because e and e$`\to`$f form a separate path for video
understanding, Video-Index, the only training benchmark with video
input, feeds both nodes. Figures
[58](#A5.F58 "Figure 58 ‣ E.2 Node Training Levels ‣ Appendix E Visual Record ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")–[68](#A5.F68 "Figure 68 ‣ E.2 Node Training Levels ‣ Appendix E Visual Record ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")
show how each node evolves: on the left are the training benchmarks that
form the node’s input, and on the right its four levels are played in
turn, each tracing how the failure on that level is distilled into a new
or revised module and how the next run with that module completes the
level. The levels of a node keep one task family and raise the
difficulty from iteration to iteration. At c, two robots deliver crates
through gates that open only while the partner stands on a linked plate
( M07,  M08). At c$`\to`$d, a factory must meet orders due on fixed
shifts under staged shortages ( M12,  M13,  M14). At c$`\to`$d$`\to`$a,
ring-marked cells must satisfy relations set in stages ( M15,  M16). At
c$`\to`$a, similar jars must be collected in a revealed order ( M10,
 M09,  M11). At d, a claim may be submitted only once an object’s hidden
faces have been seen ( M17,  M18). At d$`\to`$a, stamps and a parcel
must be brought back while gates close on the return ( M19,  M20,  M21).
At d$`\to`$b, a hidden pattern with repeated symbols must be identified
by probes ( M22,  M23). At a, structural members are inspected for
defects beside look-alikes ( M01,  M02,  M03). At b, tokens are
allocated over three fields against a rotating formation ( M06,  M04,
 M05). At e, the segments holding a parcel’s hand-offs must be bought
within a budget ( M25,  M24,  M26). At e$`\to`$f, the parcel must be
followed across hand-offs to its endpoint ( M28,  M27).

### E.3 Embodied Benchmarks

The simulation of each embodied benchmark runs in an environment built
from its real scene, which closely reproduces the layout and furnishings
of the site, with a model of the same robot as the real runs: a Unitree
G1 for SOON and an AlohaMini2 for CaP-X. For SOON we rebuilt the office
from its floor plan and from photographs and video of the site: the side
room where the robot starts, the doorway into the office, the rows of
desks with their chairs and cabinets, and the white pedestal fan beside
the target desk. The office is a 3D scene in which the walls and
furniture are collision obstacles: each action moves the robot by the
nominal distance or angle of the real robot’s motion profile, a move
that hits an obstacle counts as a collision and ends the episode as a
failure, and after every action the agent receives the head-camera image
rendered from the robot’s new pose with the height, downward pitch, and
field of view of the real camera. On the real robot a person judges
every episode on site, and an episode in which the robot collides with
an obstacle is a failure. The G1 starts in the side room, walks through
the doorway into the office, and must stop at the desk next to the white
fan and touch it within 30 movement actions. For CaP-X we built the
table scene in the MuJoCo physics simulator \[[125](#bib.bib76)\] with
the official AlohaMini2 model, and with objects of the same size and
colour as the real ones: three paper cups, 9 cm tall, 7 cm across the
mouth, and 5 cm across the closed bottom, stand upside down on a white
table, and in most layouts a 2 cm cube, red, green, or blue, lies under
one of them. The AlohaMini2 stands at the near edge of the table with
its two six-joint arms; its mobile base and lift stay still. After every
action the agent sees the head camera, the wrist cameras on both
grippers, and a fixed side camera; it moves a gripper in small steps
while the host solves the arm’s inverse kinematics, and within 30
actions it must report whether a cube is present and, if it is, its
colour and the cup it is under. Figure
[69](#A5.F69 "Figure 69 ‣ E.3 Embodied Benchmarks ‣ Appendix E Visual Record ‣ The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents")
compares the real and simulated scenes directly.
````
