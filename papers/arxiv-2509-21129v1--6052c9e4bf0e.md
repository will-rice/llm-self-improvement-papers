###### Abstract

Modern email spam and phishing attacks have evolved far beyond keyword
blacklists or simple heuristics, now focusing on complex intent
exploitation that traditional systems struggle to understand ([Wang
et al. 2025b](#bib.bib52)). Adversaries now craft multi-modal campaigns
that combine natural-language text with obfuscated URLs, forged headers,
and malicious attachments, adapting their strategies within days to
bypass filters([Lin et al. 2021](#bib.bib26))([Wang et al.
2025a](#bib.bib50)). Traditional spam detection systems, which rely on
static rules or single-modality models, struggle to integrate
heterogeneous signals or to continuously adapt, leading to rapid
performance degradation.

We propose EvoMail, a self-evolving cognitive agent framework for robust
detection of spam and phishing, building on recent advances in
agent-based systems with memory and reflection capabilities ([Zhou
et al. 2024b](#bib.bib64))([Liang et al. 2025](#bib.bib24)). EvoMail
first constructs a unified heterogeneous email graph that fuses textual
content, metadata (headers, senders, domains), and embedded resources
(URLs, attachments). A Cognitive Graph Neural Network (CogGNN) enhanced
by a Large Language Model (LLM) performs context-aware reasoning across
these sources to identify coordinated spam campaigns. Most critically,
EvoMail engages in an adversarial self-evolution loop: a “red-team”
agent generates novel evasion tactics—such as character obfuscation or
AI-generated phishing text([Li et al. 2020](#bib.bib18))—while the
“blue-team” detector learns from failures, compresses experiences into a
memory module([Zhou et al. 2024b](#bib.bib64)), and reuses them for
future reasoning.

Extensive experiments on real-world datasets (Enron-Spam, Ling-Spam,
SpamAssassin, and TREC) and synthetic adversarial variants demonstrate
that EvoMail consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in
detection accuracy, adaptability to evolving spam tactics, and
interpretability of reasoning traces. These results highlight EvoMail’s
potential as a resilient and explainable defense framework against
next-generation spam and phishing threats.

## Introduction

Email remains a universal communication backbone but also the primary
vector for spam and phishing([Goenka, Chawla, and Tiwari
2024](#bib.bib11)). Contemporary campaigns are _multi-source and rapidly
adaptive([Rossi et al. 2020](#bib.bib38))_: natural and fluent text,
obfuscated or shortened URLs, forged headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC evasions),
and polymorphic attachments. As filters are updated, attackers leverage
powerful generative models ([Liu et al. 2025](#bib.bib27)) to mutate
content within days via prompt-engineering or template-based generation.
Traditional rule-based or single-modality classifiers([Xiong et al.
2024](#bib.bib58)) (e.g., keyword lists, Naive Bayes) perform well on
known patterns but generalize poorly under tactic drift.

Advanced neural methods have improved robustness, yet three gaps
persist: (i) Heterogeneous fusion: indicators span text, headers,
domains, and attachments; modeling subtle cross-modal correlations([Wang
et al. 2024b](#bib.bib49))([Li et al. 2023a](#bib.bib21)) remains
challenging([Wang et al. 2025a](#bib.bib50))([Xin et al.
2024b](#bib.bib56)). (ii) Adaptation: distribution shifts([Li et al.
2023c](#bib.bib23)) from obfuscation, domain rotation, and AI-generated
content quickly erode static models, a challenge addressed by recent
domain adaptation methods([Wang et al. 2024a](#bib.bib48))([Kirkpatrick
et al. 2017](#bib.bib17))([Du et al. 2024](#bib.bib6)). (iii) Contextual
memory: detectors rarely retain and reuse reasoning traces([Li et al.
2023c](#bib.bib23))([Lyu et al. 2025](#bib.bib32)), limiting recognition
of variants of previously observed attacks.

We present COG, a self-evolving cognitive agent that mimics human
analysts([Ren et al. 2024](#bib.bib36))([Li et al. 2023b](#bib.bib22)).
COG unifies multi-source signals into a heterogeneous email graph and
applies an LLM-enhanced CogGNN for semantic-structural reasoning. A
_red–blue_ adversarial loop continuously surfaces novel evasion
tactics([Li et al. 2023a](#bib.bib21)) and converts detection failures
into compressed, reusable experiences, injected back into reasoning and
fine-tuning. Our contributions:

- •
  Self-evolving agent for spam/phishing: a closed-loop framework
  combining heterogeneous email graphs([Li et al. 2024](#bib.bib20)),
  LLM-augmented GNN reasoning, adversarial generation, and experience
  compression([Li et al. 2024](#bib.bib20)).
- •
  CogGNN: an LLM-guided semantic attention and query optimizer that
  couples textual semantics with graph structure across content,
  headers, domains, and attachments.
- •
  Experience compression & reuse: distills failed traces into structured
  heuristics to stabilize adaptation under distributional and
  adversarial shifts.
- •
  Comprehensive evaluation: static, shift, and cross-modal settings on
  public corpora and synthetic adversarial variants; we report
  accuracy/F1 as well as STC (structured temporal consistency) and
  CIM (cognitive interpretability) for regulator-aligned transparency.

## Related Work

#### Email spam and phishing detection.

Classical filters (Bayes([Metsis, Androutsopoulos, and Paliouras
2006](#bib.bib33)), SVM([Fette, Sadeh, and Tomasic 2007](#bib.bib8)),
rule lists) and modern neural models (TextCNN([Kim 2014](#bib.bib16)),
Transformers) predominantly focus on content, with metadata and URLs
often auxiliary. Graph-based perspectives connect senders, recipients,
domains, and URLs but struggle to fuse semantics and structure
end-to-end. ([Wu et al. 2021](#bib.bib54)) ([Li et al.
2025](#bib.bib19))([Schlichtkrull et al. 2017](#bib.bib39))

#### LLMs for security text.

LLMs excel at contextual reasoning and pattern abstraction, and have
been successfully applied to tasks like phishing email detection([Devlin
et al. 2019](#bib.bib5))([Brown et al. 2020](#bib.bib2))([Zhao et al.
2025](#bib.bib61)); yet without persistent memory and guardrails, they
can be brittle to prompt injection or novel obfuscation([Wei, Haghtalab,
and Steinhardt 2023](#bib.bib53))([Zhou et al. 2024a](#bib.bib63)), and
their inference efficiency remains a consideration ([Wan et al.
2025b](#bib.bib47)). Integrating LLMs with structured models
(graphs([Zhao et al. 2023](#bib.bib60))([Ede et al. 2022](#bib.bib7)))
is a promising path([Wan et al. 2025a](#bib.bib46)) toward richer
evidence aggregation([Liang, Ding, and Fu 2021](#bib.bib25))([Qian
et al. 2023](#bib.bib35)).

#### Self-evolving systems.

Self-play([Silver et al. 2016](#bib.bib41)), reflective memory, and
inference-time optimization through reflection ([Zhuo et al.
2025](#bib.bib65)) have shown promise in dynamic domains, alongside
online prompt/model updates([Xin et al. 2024a](#bib.bib55)) for tasks
ranging from game playing to automated cyber defense([Palmer et al.
2024](#bib.bib34)). For email security, realizing safe, auditable, and
data-efficient self-evolution remains under-explored([Zhou et al.
2024a](#bib.bib63))([Thapa et al. 2023](#bib.bib42)).

## Problem Formulation

Let
$`\mathcal{D}=\{D^{\text{text}},D^{\text{meta}},D^{\text{url}},D^{\text{att}}\}`$
denote email content, metadata (headers/senders/receivers/domains),
URLs, and attachments. We build a heterogeneous graph $`G=(V,E,X)`$ with
node types (emails, senders, receivers, domains, URLs, attachments) and
relation types (sent*to, hosted_on, contains, linked_to, replied_to)
([Wang et al. 2021](#bib.bib51)). Each node $`v\in V`$ has features
$`x*{v}`$ (e.g., text embeddings, header signals, URL features, file
hashes/MIME).

Given an email (or email thread) $`q`$, the task is to predict
$`y\in\{0,1\}`$ (ham vs. spam/phishing):

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       \hat{y}=f_{\theta}\!\big(q,G\big)\in[0,1],
       ```                                         |     | (1) |

where $`f_{\theta}`$ integrates local content and global graph context.

## Method: COG Framework

### Overview

Figure [1](#Sx1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Introduction ‣ EvoMail: Self-Evolving Cognitive Agents for Adaptive Spam and Phishing Email Defense")
provides an overview of the COG framework. Incoming emails are
represented as nodes of different types (emails, senders, recipients,
domains, URLs, attachments), with edges capturing semantic and
structural relations such as sent-to, contains, hosted-on, and
linked-to. These nodes and edges together form a heterogeneous email
graph, over which the LLM-enhanced CogGNN performs multi-source
reasoning. Highlighted edges in the figure indicate a high-attention
_evidence path_, showing how EvoMail aggregates suspicious cues—e.g.,
forged headers, anomalous domains, or obfuscated URLs—into an
interpretable reasoning trace that explains why an email is classified
as spam or phishing. The full framework integrates four modules: (1)
heterogeneous graph construction, (2) CogGNN reasoning with LLM-guided
attention, (3) self-evolution via red–blue adversarial training and
experience compression([Khosla, Zhu, and He 2023](#bib.bib15)), and (4)
explainable evidence-path backtracing.

### Notation and Definitions

We summarize the notation used throughout this section:

- •
  $`\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{R})`$: heterogeneous
  email graph with nodes $`\mathcal{V}`$, edges $`\mathcal{E}`$, and
  relation types $`\mathcal{R}`$.
- •
  $`v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}`$: email node $`i`$ with raw feature vector
  $`x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$.
- •
  $`h_{v}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}`$: initial embedding of node $`v`$
  after feature transformation.
- •
  $`h_{v}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}`$: hidden representation of node
  $`v`$ at GNN layer $`k\in\{1,\ldots,L\}`$.
- •
  $`\mathcal{N}(v)`$: full neighborhood of node $`v`$;
  $`\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)\subseteq\mathcal{N}(v)`$: top-$`K`$ selected
  neighbors.
- •
  $`e_{uv}\in\mathcal{E}`$: edge between nodes $`u`$ and $`v`$ with
  relation type $`r_{uv}\in\mathcal{R}`$.
- •
  $`\alpha_{uv}^{(k)}\in[0,1]`$: attention weight from node $`u`$ to
  $`v`$ at layer $`k`$.
- •
  $`f_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]`$: detection model
  parameterized by $`\theta`$.
- •
  $`\mathcal{M}_{t}`$: compressed memory at iteration $`t`$, storing
  tuples $`(e,\hat{y},\text{trace})`$.
- •
  $`\mathcal{A}_{t}=\{E_{\text{adv}}^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^{M_{t}}`$: set of
  adversarial samples at iteration $`t`$.
- •
  $`\phi:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{\phi}}`$: feature
  extraction function mapping emails to embedding space.
- •
  $`P(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p}}`$: contextualized prompt embedding for
  node pair $`(u,v)`$.
- •
  Hyperparameters: $`K`$ (neighbors), $`L`$ (GNN layers), $`\tau`$
  (temperature), $`\beta`$ (balance), $`\lambda,\mu`$ (loss weights),
  $`M_{\max}`$ (memory budget).

### Heterogeneous Graph Construction

#### Multi-modal feature extraction.

For each email $`v`$, we extract features from multiple modalities and
construct the initial representation:

|                                                                                        |                                          |                                                                                                               |     |     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|                                                                                        | $`\displaystyle x_{v}^{\text{text}}`$    | $`\displaystyle=\text{TF-IDF}(\text{subject}(v))\oplus\text{TF-IDF}(\text{body}(v))\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{t}},`$   |     | (2) |
|                                                                                        | $`\displaystyle x_{v}^{\text{meta}}`$    | $`\displaystyle=\Bigl[\begin{aligned} &\text{hour}(v),\;\text{weekday}(v),\\                                  |
| &\text{length}(v),\;\text{attach_count}(v)\end{aligned}\Bigr]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\_{m}},`$ |                                          | (3)                                                                                                           |
|                                                                                        | $`\displaystyle x_{v}^{\text{network}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\Bigl[\begin{aligned} &\text{sender_rep}(v),\;\text{domain_age}(v),\\                         |
| &\text{url_count}(v)\end{aligned}\Bigr]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\_{n}},`$                       |                                          | (4)                                                                                                           |
|                                                                                        | $`\displaystyle x_{v}`$                  | $`\displaystyle=x_{v}^{\text{text}}\oplus x_{v}^{\text{meta}}\oplus x_{v}^{\text{network}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ |     | (5) |

where $`\oplus`$ denotes vector concatenation, $`d=d_{t}+d_{m}+d_{n}`$,
and each component captures different aspects of email characteristics.

#### Relation modeling.

We define multiple relation types in
$`\mathcal{R}=\{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{|\mathcal{R}|}\}`$:

|                                              |                                            |                                                                                         |                                         |                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
|                                              | $`\displaystyle r_{\text{domain}}(u,v)`$   | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}[\text{domain}(u)=\text{domain}(v)]\cdot w_{\text{domain}},`$ |                                         | (6)                     |
|                                              | $`\displaystyle r_{\text{temporal}}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\exp\!\Biggl(-\frac{                                   | \text{timestamp}(u)-\text{timestamp}(v) | }{\sigma\_{t}}\Biggr)\\ |
| &\cdot\;w\_{\text{temporal}}\end{aligned},`$ |                                            | (7)                                                                                     |
|                                              | $`\displaystyle r_{\text{semantic}}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\cos(\text{BERT}(u),\text{BERT}(v))\cdot w_{\text{semantic}},`$         |                                         | (8)                     |
|                                              | $`\displaystyle r_{\text{sender}}(u,v)`$   | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}[\text{sender}(u)=\text{sender}(v)]\cdot w_{\text{sender}}`$  |                                         | (9)                     |

where $`\mathbb{I}[\cdot]`$ is the indicator function, $`\sigma_{t}>0`$
controls temporal decay, and $`w_{\text{type}}>0`$ are learned relation
weights.

#### Edge construction.

An edge $`e_{uv}`$ exists if any relation exceeds threshold
$`\epsilon_{r}`$:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\begin{split}e_{uv}\in\mathcal{E}\iff&\;\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}}r(u,v)>\epsilon_{r},\\
&\text{with weight }W_{uv}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}r(u,v).\end{split}
``` |  | (10) |

### CogGNN: LLM-Enhanced Cognitive Graph Neural Network

#### Initial embedding transformation.

Raw features are transformed into hidden space via learnable projection:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{v}^{(0)}=\text{LayerNorm}(\sigma(W_{\text{init}}x_{v}+b_{\text{init}}))\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}
``` |  | (11) |

where $`W_{\text{init}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}\times d}`$,
$`b_{\text{init}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}`$, and $`\sigma(\cdot)`$ is the
ReLU activation.

#### Contextual prompt generation.

For each node pair $`(u,v)`$, we construct a structured prompt and
encode it:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{prompt\_str}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\textsc{Template}\Bigl(\begin{aligned} &\text{desc}(u),\;\text{desc}(v),\\
&\text{relation}(e_{uv}),\;\text{task\_context}\end{aligned}\Bigr),`$ |  | (12) |
|  | $`\displaystyle P(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{LLM}_{\text{encoder}}(\text{prompt\_str}(u,v))\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p}}`$ |  | (13) |

where $`\text{LLM}_{\text{encoder}}`$ produces contextualized embeddings
capturing semantic relationships.

#### Adaptive neighbor selection.

We compute a composite salience score integrating multiple signals:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle s_{\text{struct}}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\text{PageRank}(u)+\frac{\text{deg}(u)}{\max_{i}\text{deg}(v_{i})}\\
&+\frac{1}{1+\text{shortest\_path}(u,v)}\end{aligned},`$ |  | (14) |
|  | $`\displaystyle s_{\text{freq}}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\text{co\_occurrence}(u,v)}{\sqrt{\text{total\_count}(u)\cdot\text{total\_count}(v)}},`$ |  | (15) |
|  | $`\displaystyle s_{\text{semantic}}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{h_{u}^{(0)}\cdot h_{v}^{(0)}}{\|h_{u}^{(0)}\|_{2}\|h_{v}^{(0)}\|_{2}},`$ |  | (16) |
|  | $`\displaystyle S(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=w_{s}\cdot s_{\text{struct}}(u,v)+w_{f}\cdot s_{\text{freq}}(u,v)+`$ |  | (17) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle w_{c}\cdot s_{\text{semantic}}(u,v)`$ |  | (18) |

where $`[w_{s},w_{f},w_{c}]=\text{softmax}([a_{s},a_{f},a_{c}])`$ with
learnable parameters $`a_{s},a_{f},a_{c}\in\mathbb{R}`$. The top-$`K`$
neighbors are selected as:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{K}\}`$ |  | (19) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\text{where }S(u_{1},v)\geq S(u_{2},v)\geq\ldots\geq S(u_{K},v).`$ |  |  |

#### LLM-guided attention mechanism.

Building upon the principles of graph attention networks([Veličković
et al. 2018](#bib.bib44)), for each layer $`k`$, attention weights
integrate semantic and structural reasoning([Chen et al.
2025](#bib.bib3)):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{llm\_score}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{MLP}_{\text{attn}}(P(u,v))\in[0,1],`$ |  | (20) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{struct\_features}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} [&w_{r}^{\top}\text{onehot}(r_{uv}),\;\log(1+\text{deg}(u)),\\
&\log(1+\text{deg}(v)),\;\tfrac{1}{1+\text{spath}(u,v)}]\end{aligned},`$ |  | (21) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{struct\_score}(u,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=w_{\text{struct}}^{\top}\text{struct\_features}(u,v),`$ |  | (22) |
|  | $`\displaystyle e_{uv}^{(k)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\text{llm\_score}(u,v)+\beta\cdot\text{struct\_score}(u,v)\\
&+\gamma\cdot\frac{h_{u}^{(k-1)}\cdot h_{v}^{(k-1)}}{\|h_{u}^{(k-1)}\|_{2}\,\|h_{v}^{(k-1)}\|_{2}}\end{aligned},`$ |  | (23) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\alpha_{uv}^{(k)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(e_{uv}^{(k)}/\tau)}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)}\exp(e_{jv}^{(k)}/\tau)}`$ |  | (24) |

where $`\text{MLP}_{\text{attn}}`$ is a multi-layer perceptron,
$`w_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{R}|}`$ are relation embedding weights,
$`w_{\text{struct}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}`$, and $`\gamma\geq 0`$ controls
the influence of current representations.

#### Multi-layer message passing.

Node representations evolve through $`L`$ layers of message aggregation:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle m_{v}^{(k)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)}\alpha_{uv}^{(k)}\cdot(W_{\text{neigh}}^{(k)}h_{u}^{(k-1)}+W_{\text{edge}}^{(k)}\text{embed}(r_{uv})),`$ |  | (25) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{v}^{(k)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=W_{\text{self}}^{(k)}h_{v}^{(k-1)}+m_{v}^{(k)}+b^{(k)},`$ |  | (26) |
|  | $`\displaystyle h_{v}^{(k)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{LayerNorm}(\sigma(\tilde{h}_{v}^{(k)}))+\text{Dropout}(h_{v}^{(k-1)})`$ |  | (27) |

where
$`W_{\text{neigh}}^{(k)},W_{\text{self}}^{(k)},W_{\text{edge}}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}\times d_{h}}`$
are layer-specific transformation matrices,
$`\text{embed}(r_{uv})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}`$ embeds relation types, and
we include residual connections for training stability([He et al.
2015](#bib.bib14)).

#### Final prediction.

The detection score combines multi-layer representations:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle z_{v}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{Concat}([h_{v}^{(1)},h_{v}^{(2)},\ldots,h_{v}^{(L)}])\in\mathbb{R}^{L\cdot d_{h}},`$ |  | (28) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\hat{y}_{v}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{sigmoid}(W_{\text{out}}z_{v}+b_{\text{out}})`$ |  | (29) |

where $`W_{\text{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times L\cdot d_{h}}`$ and
$`b_{\text{out}}\in\mathbb{R}`$.

### Self-Evolution via Red–Blue Adversarial Training

#### Red team: Adversarial sample generation.

The red team generates adversarial emails through gradient-based and
semantic perturbations([Goodfellow, Shlens, and Szegedy
2015](#bib.bib12))([Ganguli et al. 2022](#bib.bib9)):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle E_{\text{grad}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=E_{\text{seed}}+\epsilon\cdot\text{sign}(\nabla_{E_{\text{seed}}}\log f_{\theta}(E_{\text{seed}})),`$ |  | (30) |
|  | $`\displaystyle E_{\text{semantic}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{SemanticMutate}(E_{\text{seed}},\mathcal{V}_{\text{vocab}},\rho_{\text{mut}}),`$ |  | (31) |
|  | $`\displaystyle E_{\text{hybrid}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{Combine}(E_{\text{grad}},E_{\text{semantic}},\lambda_{\text{hybrid}})`$ |  | (32) |

where $`\epsilon>0`$ controls perturbation magnitude,
$`\mathcal{V}_{\text{vocab}}`$ is the vocabulary for semantic mutations,
$`\rho_{\text{mut}}\in[0,1]`$ is mutation rate, and
$`\lambda_{\text{hybrid}}\in[0,1]`$ balances the combination.

The red team optimizes a multi-objective reward:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{Novelty}(E_{\text{adv}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\min_{(e_{i},\hat{y}_{i},t_{i})\in\mathcal{M}_{t}}\|\phi(E_{\text{adv}})-\phi(e_{i})\|_{2},`$ |  | (33) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{Evasion}(E_{\text{adv}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\max(0,0.5-f_{\theta}(E_{\text{adv}})),`$ |  | (34) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{Complexity}(E_{\text{adv}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\|\phi(E_{\text{adv}})-\phi(E_{\text{seed}})\|_{2}}{\|\phi(E_{\text{seed}})\|_{2}},`$ |  | (35) |
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{\text{red}}(E_{\text{adv}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &w_{n}\cdot\text{Novelty}(E_{\text{adv}})+w_{e}\cdot\text{Evasion}(E_{\text{adv}})\\
&-w_{c}\cdot\text{Complexity}(E_{\text{adv}})\end{aligned}`$ |  | (36) |

where $`w_{n}+w_{e}+w_{c}=1`$ and $`w_{n},w_{e},w_{c}\geq 0`$.

#### Blue team: Failure analysis.

The blue team detects failures and extracts detailed traces:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\{E\in\mathcal{A}_{t}:f_{\theta}(E)<\delta_{\text{fail}}\text{ and }\text{ground\_truth}(E)=1\},`$ |  | (37) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{Trace}(E_{\text{fail}})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\{h_{v}^{(k)},\{\alpha_{uv}^{(k)}\}_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)},\text{path}(v)\}_{k=1}^{L}`$ |  | (38) |

where $`\delta_{\text{fail}}=0.5`$ is the failure threshold and
$`\text{path}(v)`$ records the attention-based reasoning path.

#### Experience compression and memory management([Lopez-Paz and Ranzato 2022](#bib.bib29)).

Failed traces are clustered and compressed using $`k`$-medoids:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{dist}(E_{1},E_{2})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\|\phi(E_{1})-\phi(E_{2})\|_{2}\\
&+\alpha_{\text{trace}}\cdot\text{trace\_dist}(\text{Trace}(E_{1}),\text{Trace}(E_{2}))\end{aligned},`$ |  | (39) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{C}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\text{KMedoids}(\mathcal{F}_{t},k_{t}),\\
&k_{t}=\min(|\mathcal{F}_{t}|,\,M_{\max}-|\mathcal{M}_{t}|)\end{aligned},`$ |  | (40) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{j}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\arg\min_{e\in\mathcal{C}_{j}}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}}\text{dist}(e,e^{\prime})\end{aligned},`$ |  | (41) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{t+1}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\text{LRU}\Bigl(\mathcal{M}_{t}\cup\{(\tilde{e}_{j},f_{\theta_{t}}(\tilde{e}_{j}),\\
&\qquad\text{Trace}(\tilde{e}_{j}))\}_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{C}|},\,M_{\max}\Bigr)\end{aligned}`$ |  | (42) |

where $`\alpha_{\text{trace}}\geq 0`$ weights trace similarity, and LRU
removes oldest entries when memory exceeds capacity.

#### Adversarial training objective.

The total loss integrates task performance, memory consistency, and
adversarial robustness:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\sum_{(v,y)\in\mathcal{D}}[y\log f_{\theta}(v)+(1-y)\log(1-f_{\theta}(v))],`$ |  | (43) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\sum_{(e,\hat{y},t)\in\mathcal{M}_{t}}\begin{aligned} [&\hat{y}\log f_{\theta}(e)\\
&+(1-\hat{y})\log(1-f_{\theta}(e))]\end{aligned},`$ |  | (44) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\log f_{\theta}(E)+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\log(1-f_{\theta}(E_{\text{benign}})),`$ |  | (45) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\|\theta\|_{2}^{2},`$ |  | (46) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\theta)+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}(\theta)+\mu\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}(\theta)+\nu\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}(\theta)`$ |  | (47) |

where $`E_{\text{benign}}`$ are benign emails, and $`\nu>0`$ controls
regularization strength.

### Explainable Reasoning

To ensure transparency and auditability, a critical requirement for
security applications, we develop an explainable reasoning
module([Ribeiro, Singh, and Guestrin 2016](#bib.bib37))([Lundberg and
Lee 2017](#bib.bib31)). Our explanation approach, which extracts the
most influential evidence path, is one of several paradigms in graph
explainability([Vu and Thai 2020](#bib.bib45)). It contrasts with
counterfactual methods that identify minimal changes to flip a
prediction, and with probabilistic approaches that generate a
distribution over explanatory subgraphs. Furthermore, it differs from
decomposition-based methods like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation
(LRP)([Bach et al. 2015](#bib.bib1)), which explain predictions by
propagating relevance scores backwards through the network.

#### Evidence path extraction with confidence scoring.

Similar to methods developed for explaining GNNs([Ying et al.
2019](#bib.bib59)), we extract reasoning paths by following
high-attention edges with confidence assessment:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{confidence}(v,k)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\max_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)}\alpha_{uv}^{(k)},`$ |  | (48) |
|  | $`\displaystyle u_{i}^{*}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{K}(u_{i-1}^{*})}\alpha_{u,u_{i-1}^{*}}^{(L-i+1)},`$ |  | (49) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{Path}(v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &[(v,\text{confidence}(v,L)),\\
&\;(u_{1}^{*},\text{confidence}(u_{1}^{*},L-1)),\ldots,\\
&\;(u_{d}^{*},\text{confidence}(u_{d}^{*},L-d))]\end{aligned}`$ |  | (50) |

where path extraction stops when $`d\geq D_{\max}`$ or
$`\text{confidence}(u_{d}^{*},L-d)<\alpha_{\min}`$.

#### Feature importance scoring.

For each node in the reasoning path, we compute feature importance via
gradient analysis:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{importance}(f_{i},v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(v)}{\partial x_{v,i}}\right|\cdot|x_{v,i}|,`$ |  | (51) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{top\_features}(v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{TopK}_{i}(\text{importance}(f_{i},v),K_{\text{feat}})`$ |  | (52) |

where $`x_{v,i}`$ is the $`i`$-th feature of node $`v`$ and
$`K_{\text{feat}}`$ controls the number of important features to report.

#### Natural language explanation generation([Lucic et al. 2022](#bib.bib30)).

Explanations combine path analysis with feature importance:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | path_summary | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\textsc{Summarize}\big(\{(\text{type}(u_{i}),\\
&\;\;\text{relation}(u_{i},u_{i-1}),\text{confidence}(u_{i}))\}_{i=1}^{d}\big)\end{aligned},`$ |  | (53) |
|  | feature_summary | $`\displaystyle=\textsc{Describe}\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{d}\text{top\_features}(u_{i})\right),`$ |  | (54) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{Explanation}(v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\textsc{Template}\big(\text{path\_summary},\\
&\;\;\text{feature\_summary},f_{\theta}(v)\big)\end{aligned}`$ |  | (55) |

### Theoretical Analysis

###### Theorem 1 (Convergence Guarantee).

Assume each loss component in $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}`$ is
$`L`$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to $`\theta`$. If the learning
rate satisfies $`\eta\leq 1/(2L)`$, then gradient descent converges to
an $`\epsilon`$-stationary point in $`O(1/\epsilon^{2})`$ iterations.

###### Sketch.

The weighted sum of Lipschitz functions remains Lipschitz with constant
$`L_{\text{total}}=L+\lambda L+\mu L+\nu L=(1+\lambda+\mu+\nu)L`$.
Standard analysis of non-convex optimization ([Ghadimi and Lan
2013](#bib.bib10)) shows that
$`\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}\|^{2}]`$ decreases at
rate $`O(1/T)`$. ∎

###### Theorem 2 (Per-layer Time Complexity of CogGNN).

Let $`|\mathcal{V}|`$ be the number of nodes, $`L`$ the number of
layers, $`K`$ the Top-$`K`$ neighbors per node, $`H`$ the number of
attention heads, and $`d_{h}`$ the head (or hidden) dimension. Let
$`C_{\text{LLM}}`$ denote the cost of invoking the LLM on a
node–neighbor pair. Assuming sparse neighborhoods (max degree absorbed
into $`K`$) and standard query–key–value attention, the total time
complexity over $`L`$ layers is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{O}\!\big(L\cdot|\mathcal{V}|\cdot(HKd_{h}\;+\;d_{h}^{2}\;+\;KC_{\text{LLM}})\big),
``` |  |

where $`HKd_{h}`$ accounts for attention score/aggregation,
$`d_{h}^{2}`$ for linear projections, and $`KC_{\text{LLM}}`$ for
contextual LLM prompting per node.

###### Theorem 3 (Attention Complexity).

For a graph with $`|\mathcal{V}|`$ nodes, maximum degree $`\Delta`$, and
$`L`$ layers, the time complexity per iteration is:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
O(L\cdot|\mathcal{V}|\cdot K\cdot d_{h}^{2}+|\mathcal{V}|\cdot K\cdot C_{\text{LLM}})
``` |  | (56) |

where $`C_{\text{LLM}}`$ is the cost of LLM inference per node pair.

###### Sketch.

For each layer and node $`v`$: (i) computing attention scores over $`K`$
neighbors with $`H`$ heads costs $`\mathcal{O}(HKd_{h})`$ (dot-products
and softmax); (ii) the standard linear projections (e.g., $`Q,K,V`$ and
output) contribute $`\mathcal{O}(d_{h}^{2})`$ per node; (iii) applying
an LLM to each $`(v,u)`$ pair for contextual reasoning adds
$`\mathcal{O}(KC_{\text{LLM}})`$. Summing over $`|\mathcal{V}|`$ nodes
and $`L`$ layers yields the bound. This matches common analyses of
attention mechanisms ([Vaswani et al. 2017](#bib.bib43)) and
message-passing GNNs ([Zhou et al. 2020](#bib.bib62)). ∎

## Experimental Setup

#### Baselines.

As elabrated in
Table [2](#Sx7.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Overall Performance ‣ Results and Analysis ‣ EvoMail: Self-Evolving Cognitive Agents for Adaptive Spam and Phishing Email Defense"),
we compare against classical, neural, and graph-based methods:

- •
  NB / SVM / RF: TF-IDF + basic metadata([Fette, Sadeh, and Tomasic
  2007](#bib.bib8)).
- •
  TextCNN / BERT: content-only encoders([Kim 2014](#bib.bib16)).
- •
  GraphSAGE + MLP([Hamilton, Ying, and Leskovec 2018](#bib.bib13)):
  graph topology with shallow features (no LLM attention).
- •
  Early / Mid Fusion: concatenation-based multi-modal baselines
  (input-level vs. intermediate-layer fusion).
- •
  EvoMail (ours): heterogeneous email graph + LLM-enhanced CogGNN +
  self-evolving memory. Ablations: *w/o Context* (remove LLM attention),
  *w/o Query Optimizer* (static neighbor sampling), *w/o Memory* (no
  failure-trace memory).

#### Evaluation Scenarios.

Static: 80/20 train–test split.   Shift: train on P1; incremental
updates on P2–P3 (no revisits), with 10% novel P3 attacks.  
Cross-modal: (1) text-only, (2) text+metadata, (3) full graph
(text+metadata+URLs/attachments).

#### Metrics.

Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, and $`\mathrm{Precision@K}`$. We
further report STC (temporal alignment of attention-based evidence
traces) and CIM (expert-rated explanation
coherence/relevance/auditability; 0–1 scale). In dynamic settings, we
track update latency and memory compression efficiency.

#### Implementation Details.

Results are averaged over 5 runs (different seeds). The LLM attention
head is tuned via LoRA([Xin et al. 2024c](#bib.bib57))([Liu et al.
2024](#bib.bib28)). The top-$`K`$ optimizer selects
$`K\in\{8,16,24,32\}`$ on validation. Experiments use NVIDIA A100
(40GB). Additional hyperparameters are in the supplement([Shchur et al.
2019](#bib.bib40)).

validating EvoMail’s joint design.
