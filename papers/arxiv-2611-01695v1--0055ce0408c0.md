###### Abstract

B2B outbound sales involves high transaction values and
relationship-based trust, so closing a deal typically requires multiple
rounds of outreach building on prior context. Existing LLM-based sales
agents operate within single sessions and cannot carry forward context
or refine strategies from experience. Methods that do learn from
experience store lessons as unstructured text and treat all outcomes as
equally informative, with no mechanism to distinguish a flawed strategy
from an external factor. We propose SPAR, a self-evolving framework that
models selling strategies as testable hypotheses and refines them
without parameter updates. A double-loop architecture lets an inner loop
adapt to individual merchants through episodic memory while an outer
loop evolves a shared strategy playbook through causal attribution and
hypothesis-driven evolution. In a live A/B test on the 1688 platform
with over 200,000 merchants, SPAR achieves +15.8% conversion lift and
+35.8% high-intent merchant growth. Human evaluation by domain experts
rates SPAR at 4.62 out of 5 on overall sales quality, the highest among
all methods.

## 1 Introduction

In B2B outbound sales, high transaction values, multi-stakeholder
decisions, and relationship-based trust mean that closing a deal
typically requires multiple rounds of persistent, targeted outreach.
Experienced salespeople handle this by carrying forward context, judging
which approaches work, and refining their playbook across merchants.
Large language models have driven interest in automating this process
([Chiu et al., 2022](#bib.bib8); [Chang and Chen, 2024](#bib.bib6); [Kim
et al., 2025](#bib.bib9)), yet existing systems remain limited to
single-session interactions ([He et al., 2018](#bib.bib4); [Shea et al.,
2024](#bib.bib5)), leaving three closely linked challenges unaddressed.

Current systems treat each session as an isolated event, a
single-session limitation that caps conversion at first-contact
performance ([Chang and Chen, 2024](#bib.bib6)), as
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")
illustrates. Even with multi-session capability, attribution ambiguity
persists, as it remains unclear whether a failed follow-up reflects a
flawed strategy or an external factor. This ambiguity entrenches manual
dependence on hand-crafted scripts, since automating strategy updates
without reliable attribution would amplify rather than correct errors
([Deng et al., 2024](#bib.bib7)). These three barriers operate at
successive levels, and each must be resolved for the next to become
effective.

![Refer to caption](2611.01695v1/introduction.png)

Figure 1: Single-session agents repeat generic pitches across isolated
calls without addressing the merchant’s core objection, leading to
repeated rejections. Multi-session human salespeople follow up on
specific concerns, build on prior context, and achieve conversion.
$`S_{1},S_{2},\ldots`$ denote session 1, session 2, and so on.

While recent LLM-based agents have explored experience-driven
self-improvement ([Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib1); [Zhao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib23); [Park et al., 2023](#bib.bib2)), they treat all
outcomes as equally informative and store experience as unstructured
text, offering no mechanism to filter external noise or to
systematically validate and retire strategies.

We propose SPAR (Strategy Playbook via Attributed Refinement), a
self-evolving framework that models selling strategies as _testable
hypotheses_ and iteratively refines them through sequential merchant
interactions without parameter updates. Learning is organized as a
double-loop architecture, where an inner loop adapts to individual
merchants through multi-session memory, while an outer loop
systematically evolves the shared strategy playbook across merchants
through causal attribution and hypothesis-driven evolution.

Our contributions are as follows.

- •
  We formulate multi-session B2B sales as a double-loop learning problem
  and propose SPAR, a self-evolving framework that separates
  within-merchant adaptation from cross-merchant strategy refinement,
  requiring no parameter updates.
- •
  We model selling strategies as testable hypotheses with an
  evidence-based lifecycle of proposal, testing, validation, and
  retirement, and introduce causal attribution to isolate genuinely
  strategy-driven outcomes for reliable playbook updates.
- •
  We evaluate SPAR in a live A/B test on the 1688 platform with over
  200,000 merchants, achieving +15.8% conversion lift and +35.8%
  high-intent merchant growth. Simulation analysis also shows SPAR
  outperforms the strongest baseline by 9.6% on objection recognition
  and 5.5% on evidence support.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Sales Dialogue Agents

Conversational agents for sales and negotiation ([Zhan et al.,
2024](#bib.bib18)) have progressed from scripted pipelines ([Chiu et
al., 2022](#bib.bib8)) to LLM-powered approaches that inject strategies
through fine-tuning ([Chang and Chen, 2024](#bib.bib6)) and learn
adaptive policies ([Deng et al., 2024](#bib.bib7)). Complementary
efforts evaluate negotiation capabilities ([Bianchi et al.,
2024](#bib.bib11)) and profile users for personalization ([Kim et al.,
2025](#bib.bib9); [Gou et al., 2025](#bib.bib10)). Despite this
progress, all existing systems operate within a single interaction
session, with no mechanism to revisit merchants or refine strategies
from ongoing experience.

Multi-session dialogue has been studied in open-domain settings ([Xu et
al., 2022](#bib.bib14); [Maharana et al., 2024](#bib.bib15); [Li et al.,
2025](#bib.bib16)). Memory systems and benchmarks built for
multi-session agents follow the same framing, where MemMachine ([Wang et
al., 2026](#bib.bib24)) preserves one user’s conversational record at
the episode level for cheap personalized retrieval and MemoryArena ([He
et al., 2026](#bib.bib25)) tests whether an agent uses what earlier
sessions gave it rather than recalling it on demand. This line of work
is organized around the record of a single user and improves what an
agent can recall, but these systems maintain conversational consistency
rather than adapt strategically toward conversion.

### 2.2 Outcome Attribution

Determining which action caused an observed outcome is fundamental to
learning from sequential interactions. Counterfactual credit assignment
in reinforcement learning ([Mesnard et al., 2021](#bib.bib3))
distinguishes the agent’s contribution from external factors. Despite
this importance, LLM agent learning frameworks treat all results as
equally informative. SPAR bridges this gap by separating strategy-driven
outcomes from externally-determined ones before updating the playbook.

### 2.3 Experience-Driven Self-Improvement

Building on foundational agent frameworks ([Yao et al.,
2022](#bib.bib19)), recent work enables LLM agents to learn from
experience without parameter updates through self-reflection ([Shinn et
al., 2023](#bib.bib1); [Madaan et al., 2023](#bib.bib13); [Park et al.,
2023](#bib.bib2)), cross-episode transfer ([Zhao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib23); [Fu et al., 2024](#bib.bib21); [Wang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib12)), self-generated examples ([Sarukkai et al.,
2026](#bib.bib22)), and evolving context ([Zhang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib17)). However, these methods store experience as
unstructured text or flat rules, with no lifecycle to promote validated
strategies and retire failing ones.

## 3 Method

### 3.1 Task Formulation

The agent contacts $`N`$ merchants sequentially, each with behavioral
profile $`p_{i}`$. For merchant $`i`$, the agent conducts up to $`K`$
dialogue sessions. Session $`k`$ produces outcome
$`o_{i,k}\in\{1,0,-1\}`$, representing success, ongoing, and failure
respectively. The agent maintains two evolving knowledge stores. An
episodic memory $`M_{i}`$ for each merchant records structured
session-level notes across follow-ups. A shared strategy playbook
$`\mathcal{P}=\{h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\}`$ accumulates selling strategies
refined through experience across all merchants. The objective is to
maximize the cumulative conversion rate

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
R=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\max_{k\leq K}\,\mathbf{1}[o_{i,k}=1]
``` |  | (1) |

while simultaneously improving $`\mathcal{P}`$ such that later merchants
benefit from validated lessons learned from earlier ones.

### 3.2 Framework Overview

SPAR organizes learning as a double-loop architecture, illustrated in
Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3.2 Framework Overview ‣ 3 Method ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales").
The inner loop adapts to individual merchants across multiple sessions
using episodic memory $`M_{i}`$. The outer loop evolves $`\mathcal{P}`$
across merchants by treating each interaction as a natural experiment
that tests and refines strategy hypotheses.
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix B Algorithm ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")
summarizes the complete pipeline, and the following subsections detail
each component.

### 3.3 Strategy as Testable Hypothesis

We model each strategy as a testable hypothesis with accumulated
evidence that drives lifecycle transitions. The strategy playbook
$`\mathcal{P}`$ is a collection of such hypotheses, each evolved through
reinforcement, refinement, addition, merging, and deprecation
(§[3.5](#S3.SS5 "3.5 Cross-Merchant Strategy Evolution ‣ 3 Method ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")).
Each hypothesis $`h\in\mathcal{P}`$ carries an evidence set $`E(h)`$,
where each entry $`e\in E(h)`$ records the merchant context and
interaction outcome. A causal attribution function $`a(e)\in\{s,x,u\}`$
then labels the outcome as strategy-driven, externally-determined, or
uncertain. Let $`E_{s}(h)=\{e\in E(h)\mid a(e)=s\}`$ denote the
strategy-attributed evidence and $`E_{s}^{\mathrm{succ}}(h)`$ the subset
with successful outcomes. The confidence of $`h`$ is computed as the
posterior mean of a Beta-Bernoulli model with uniform prior

|     |                                                        |     |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       c(h)=\frac{1+|E_{s}^{\mathrm{succ}}(h)|}{2+|E_{s}(h)|}
       ```                                                     |     | (2) |

which starts at $`c(h)=0.5`$, avoids overconfidence from small samples,
and converges to the empirical success rate as evidence accumulates,
isolating the causal effect of the strategy by excluding
externally-attributed evidence. Each hypothesis has status
$`\sigma(h)\in\{0,+1,-1\}`$ for tentative, active, and deprecated,
transitioning by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma(h)\leftarrow\begin{cases}+1&|E_{s}(h)|\geq\tau_{p}\;\land\;c(h)\geq\theta_{p}\\
-1&|E_{s}(h)|\geq\tau_{d}\;\land\;c(h)<\theta_{d}\\
\sigma(h)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
``` |  | (3) |

where $`(\tau_{p},\theta_{p})`$ and $`(\tau_{d},\theta_{d})`$ are
promotion and deprecation thresholds.
$`\mathcal{P}^{+}=\{h\in\mathcal{P}\mid\sigma(h)\geq 0\}`$ denotes the
non-deprecated hypotheses. To balance exploitation and exploration, each
hypothesis carries an exploration priority combining Bernoulli outcome
variance with a sample-size exploration bonus

|     |                                                |     |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       I(h)=c(h)\cdot\bigl(1-c(h)\bigr)+\frac{1}{1+n}
       ```                                             |     | (4) |

where $`n=|E_{s}(h)|`$.

### 3.4 Within-Merchant Adaptation

The inner loop maintains an episodic memory $`M_{i}`$ for each merchant.
After each session, the agent generates a structured reflection
summarizing what happened, why the outcome occurred, and what to try
next, then stores it in $`M_{i}`$ alongside the outcome and strategy
used. This reflection provides actionable context for follow-up sessions
and supplies the qualitative signal needed for causal attribution in the
outer loop.

At the start of each session, the agent concatenates the merchant
profile $`p_{i}`$, interaction history $`M_{i}`$, and the non-deprecated
hypothesis set $`\mathcal{P}^{+}`$ as selection context. An LLM-based
selector implements the scoring function $`f`$ in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix B Algorithm ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales"),
choosing $`h^{*}\in\mathcal{P}^{+}`$ by evaluating precondition
alignment, consistency with prior outcomes, and exploration priority
$`I(h)`$. In follow-up sessions, recorded failure modes steer the
selector away from repeating unsuccessful strategies.

### 3.5 Cross-Merchant Strategy Evolution

After each merchant interaction, the outer loop transforms raw
experience into systematic playbook improvements through causal
attribution, hypothesis evolution, and quality curation.

#### Causal Attribution.

The LLM examines the dialogue, merchant profile, outcome, and strategy
used, then assigns an attribution label $`a(e)\in\{s,x,u\}`$. This
counterfactual credit assignment ([Mesnard et al., 2021](#bib.bib3))
gates playbook updates. Only outcomes with $`a(e)=s`$ enter
$`E_{s}(h^{*})`$ and trigger evolution. External outcomes are recorded
but do not affect $`c(h)`$.

#### Hypothesis Evolution.

For outcomes causally attributed to the strategy, the agent applies
structured delta updates to the relevant hypothesis

|     |                                                                 |     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{P}\leftarrow\Phi\!\left(\mathcal{P},\;h^{*},\;e\right)
       ```                                                              |     | (5) |

where $`e`$ is the newly constructed evidence. The LLM implements
$`\Phi`$ by selecting from seven evolution actions, designed around a
minimal revision principle that always prefers the smallest targeted
change sufficient to incorporate new evidence. *Reinforce*, *Update*,
*Condition*, and *None* preserve existing hypotheses with increasing
degrees of modification. *Add*, *Merge*, and *Deprecate* restructure the
playbook more aggressively when incremental updates are insufficient.
Together these complementary actions cover evidence accumulation,
hypothesis refinement, and structural reorganization.

#### Exploration and Curation.

The agent performs fork discovery after each interaction, identifying
unexplored strategy directions from the dialogue as exploration
candidates. The playbook is deduplicated at regular intervals, and the
deprecation rule in
Eq. [3](#S3.E3 "In 3.3 Strategy as Testable Hypothesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")
is applied after every interaction.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

#### Dataset.

We construct a multi-session B2B sales evaluation dataset from 1,642
real conversations between an experienced human sales team and merchants
on 1688, a major B2B online platform. Each conversation is manually
annotated with detailed merchant profiles, behavioral archetypes,
session-level outcomes, and strategy labels.

#### Merchant Simulator.

We use GPT-4o as the simulated merchant, conditioned on a detailed
profile with target prices, behavioral traits, and session-specific
goals. We validate fidelity by replaying 300 real conversations and
conducting a human evaluation with the sales team. The simulator is
conditioned on the full conversation context from real human merchant
interactions, yielding 98.7% outcome agreement as shown in
Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Merchant Simulator. ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales").
The 87% paired comparison accuracy further confirms that the simulator
responds differently to strong and weak strategies. Expert approval
reaches 89%, validating its use as a reliable evaluation proxy.

#### Baselines.

We compare SPAR against three baselines. Direct Prompting uses the same
backbone LLM with only the item description and no memory or evolving
playbook. Reflexion ([Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib1)) generates a
natural-language lesson after each conversation and stores it per
merchant as self-reflective feedback for future interactions. ExpeL
([Zhao et al., 2024](#bib.bib23)) extracts reusable insights from past
interactions and retrieves them based on task similarity to guide
decision-making.

#### Metrics.

We evaluate agents along three dimensions of sales dialogue quality.

- •
  Objection handling measures whether the agent recognizes the
  merchant’s core objection, responds to it directly, and supports its
  response with concrete evidence.
- •
  Dialogue quality is assessed through *monologue rate*, the fraction of
  sessions dominated by lengthy seller utterances, and *repetition
  rate*, the fraction of follow-up sessions that duplicate earlier
  questions or arguments.
- •
  Memory recall measures whether the agent proactively references
  specific facts from prior sessions before the merchant raises them,
  evaluated only after the first session.

For LLM-based evaluation, all metrics except repetition rate are scored
by an LLM evaluator ([Zheng et al., 2023](#bib.bib20)) at the session
level across all real conversations. Repetition rate is computed via
text similarity between consecutive sessions.

#### Hypothesis Design.

The hypothesis schema is derived from LLM-assisted analysis of 51,603
session notes from a top-performing B2B sales team on 1688 and validated
against 3,637 sales cases. Each hypothesis contains six fields:
*description*, *preconditions*, *action steps*, *rationale*, *success
and failure patterns*, and an *evidence* set with causal attribution
labels. Confidence scoring and lifecycle thresholds are similarly
grounded in observed patterns of how experienced salespeople adopt and
retire strategies. The session notes used for schema design have no
overlap with the 1,642 evaluation conversations.

#### Implementation.

All agents use GPT-4o as the backbone LLM. The playbook is initialized
with a small set of domain-specific tentative seed strategies and capped
at 50 entries.

### 4.2 Results and Ablation

#### LLM-based Evaluation.

Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Implementation. ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")
presents results across baselines and ablation variants under two judge
models, where SPAR leads on most metrics under both. Under GPT Eval, the
advantage is most pronounced on evidence support, where SPAR achieves
65.6% higher than Direct Prompting and 5.5% higher than the strongest
baseline, showing that the double-loop architecture yields more
substantive selling arguments. SPAR also achieves the lowest repetition
rate at 0.002 and the highest memory recall at 0.071. ExpeL and
Reflexion improve through experience accumulation, yet both lack the
structured hypothesis lifecycle that allows SPAR to validate effective
strategies and retire failing ones. The recognition gain over the
strongest baseline is 9.6%. Under Claude Eval, SPAR leads the strongest
baseline by 16.6% on response, cuts the repetition rate to 0.003 from
0.006, and raises memory recall to 0.064 from 0.042.

#### Human Evaluation.

Three domain experts annotate dialogues with 300+ real merchants under
blind conditions
(Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Implementation. ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")).
SPAR achieves the highest scores across all seven metrics, with a Sales
Quality of 4.62 out of 5 and evidence support at 0.926. The advantage is
most visible on repetition at 0.132, an 18.5% reduction over the
next-best method, confirming that playbook evolution produces
non-repetitive follow-up interactions. Direct Prompting scores
competitively on response at 0.884, reflecting the strong zero-shot
capability of the backbone LLM, but falls behind on recognition at 0.762
and evidence at 0.741, where structured learning provides the largest
gains. The performance gaps are narrower than in LLM-based evaluation
under both judge models, yet the ranking across methods remains
consistent, confirming that the automated metrics reliably capture
quality differences perceived by experienced salespeople.

#### Ablation Study.

The ablation variants isolate each component’s contribution. Under GPT
Eval, removing the inner loop causes the largest degradation, with
recognition dropping 17.8% and memory recall falling to zero, showing
that per-merchant memory is essential for cross-session continuity.
Removing the outer loop retains strong recognition at only 3.8% below
SPAR but lags 7.8% on response and 5.2% on evidence, confirming that the
evolving playbook drives deeper objection handling. Removing evolution
freezes the playbook after seeding, falling 9.3% behind on response and
11.6% on evidence, as the agent cannot refine strategies from new
experience. Under Claude Eval, recognition falls 20.4% without the inner
loop and evidence falls 13.3% without evolution. These results confirm
that all three components are necessary and mutually reinforcing.

### 4.3 Online A/B Test

To validate that simulation improvements transfer to the real world, we
deploy all methods on the 1688 platform in a live A/B test with over
30,000 merchants per group. Conversion is defined as completing a target
transaction within 7 days of the final outreach session. The M1
high-intent rate measures how often merchants reach high-intent status
during the sales process.
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Ablation Study. ‣ 4.2 Results and Ablation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")
reports the relative change over the Direct Prompting baseline on three
business metrics. Statistical significance is assessed via a
two-proportion z-test with 95% CIs from 1,000-iteration bootstrap, and
all SPAR lifts are significant at $`p<0.001`$.

SPAR achieves the highest conversion lift at +15.8% and the highest M1
achievement lift at +35.8%, while reducing outreach sessions by 9.2%.
The conversion advantage over the next-best variant is 25.4%, confirming
that the full double-loop architecture delivers the strongest end-to-end
selling capability in production. At scale, this yields operational
savings while easing merchant fatigue from repeated contact.

The ablation pattern mirrors the simulation results. SPAR w/o Outer Loop
achieves the second-highest conversion at +12.6% and the largest session
reduction at $`-`$11.2%, while SPAR w/o Inner Loop yields +10.1%
conversion with the highest M1 lift among ablation variants at +34.5%.
SPAR w/o Evolution drops M1 to +18.7%, confirming that strategy
evolution nearly doubles the M1 gain. ExpeL shows minimal improvement at
+1.1% conversion, suggesting that unstructured insight accumulation
provides limited benefit in multi-session B2B sales. We provide a
qualitative analysis of playbook evolution in
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Playbook Evolution Analysis ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales").

## 5 Conclusion

We presented SPAR, a self-evolving framework that models selling
strategies as testable hypotheses and refines them through a double-loop
architecture with causal attribution. In a live A/B test on the 1688
platform with over 200,000 merchants, SPAR achieves +15.8% conversion
lift and +35.8% high-intent merchant growth. Simulation analysis shows
SPAR outperforms the strongest baseline by 9.6% on objection recognition
and 5.5% on evidence support. These results are validated in real-world
B2B commercial outreach where merchants make purchasing decisions.

## Limitations

SPAR requires multiple interactions before the playbook meaningfully
evolves, so early merchants do not benefit from accumulated experience.
This cold-start cost is inherent to any online learning approach, and
investigating warm-start techniques such as transferring playbooks
across domains is a promising direction for future work.

The strategy evolution process relies on LLM reasoning, which inherently
introduces a degree of randomness and unpredictability in playbook
updates. While the confidence mechanism and lifecycle thresholds provide
gradual self-correction over time, individual evolution decisions may be
suboptimal. Incorporating an auxiliary verification agent to validate
proposed strategy changes before they enter the playbook is a promising
direction for improving reliability.

Merchants are processed sequentially in our current experiments. In
practice, sales teams handle multiple merchants concurrently, where
parallel interactions may produce fundamentally different learning
dynamics. Evaluating SPAR in concurrent settings remains future work.

## Appendix A Playbook Evolution Analysis

We examine how the strategy playbook evolves during the online A/B test
to illustrate the hypothesis lifecycle. The playbook is initialized with
six tentative seed strategies. As larger-scale manual analysis is
infeasible, we qualitatively examine its evolution on a sample of 200+
real merchants drawn from the A/B test.

#### Lifecycle Progression.

Two of the six seed strategies are promoted to *active* status through
accumulated evidence. Strategy H002, which instructs the agent to
reference prior conversations with returning merchants, accumulates 108
strategy-attributed successes and is promoted after reaching the
evidence threshold. Strategy H001, which recommends offering trial
programs when merchants hesitate, follows a similar trajectory with 89
attributed successes. Two other seed strategies are deprecated after
repeated failures, illustrating that the lifecycle correctly retires
ineffective approaches. The evolution process also discovers and
validates five new strategies beyond the initial seeds, demonstrating
that the playbook grows organically from interaction experience.

#### Fork Discovery.

The evolution process identifies 9 unexplored strategy directions from
dialogue analysis. These directions capture recurring patterns that
existing strategies do not address, such as offering hands-on onboarding
support and providing industry-specific case studies to build
credibility. Each direction is logged with the number of times it
appears across interactions, creating a prioritized queue for future
hypothesis generation.

#### Attribution Filtering.

The causal attribution mechanism labels a subset of outcomes as
externally driven, preventing them from distorting strategy confidence
scores. When a merchant walks away due to a recently reduced internal
budget rather than a flaw in the agent’s approach, the attribution
module assigns label $`x`$ and the corresponding evidence does not
affect $`c(h)`$. This filtering ensures that the playbook reflects
genuine strategy effectiveness rather than environmental noise.

## Appendix B Algorithm

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix B Algorithm ‣ SPAR: Experience-Driven Strategy Evolution in Multi-Session B2B Sales")
describes the complete SPAR pipeline. The outer loop iterates over
merchants sequentially. For each merchant, the inner loop runs up to
$`K`$ sessions. At each session, the agent selects a strategy hypothesis
$`h^{*}`$ from the non-deprecated set $`\mathcal{P}^{+}`$ using a
scoring function that considers precondition alignment, prior
interaction history, and exploration priority $`I(h)`$. After dialogue
execution, a reflection is stored in episodic memory $`M_{i}`$. The
outer loop then constructs evidence from the interaction, assigns a
causal attribution label, and triggers playbook evolution $`\Phi`$ only
for strategy-attributed outcomes. The session loop terminates early on
deal closure ($`o=1`$) or merchant decline with no follow-up opportunity
($`o=-1`$).

## Appendix C Sales Quality Rubric

Three domain experts rate each dialogue on a 1–5 scale based on the
following rubric. Each level describes both overall dialogue quality and
selling-strategy execution along four dimensions, namely *strategic
fit*, *objection handling*, *rapport building*, and *closing
effectiveness*.

- •
  5 – Excellent. The agent demonstrates outstanding professional
  handling and effectively guides toward conversion. Strategic fit
  matches the merchant’s profile and stage precisely, objections are
  addressed with concrete evidence, rapport is consistently built across
  sessions, and closing is clear and well-timed. Suitable as a reference
  example for sales training.
- •
  4 – Good. The agent handles the conversation professionally. Strategic
  fit is generally appropriate, objections are addressed with relevant
  supporting points, rapport is established across sessions, and closing
  is clear, with only minor opportunities for improvement.
- •
  3 – Acceptable. The agent completes the basic sales flow. Strategic
  fit is partial, objections are acknowledged but not always supported
  with concrete evidence, rapport is limited, and closing lacks
  decisiveness.
- •
  2 – Poor. The agent makes noticeable errors. Strategic fit is weak or
  mismatched, key objections are missed or inadequately handled, rapport
  is minimal, and no clear closing is offered, reducing the likelihood
  of conversion.
- •
  1 – Unacceptable. The agent provides irrelevant or inaccurate
  responses. Strategic fit is broken, objections are ignored or
  aggravated, no rapport is established, and the conversation may harm
  the merchant relationship.
````
