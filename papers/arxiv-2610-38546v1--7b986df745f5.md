# Implicit Minor Detection from Multi-Turn Dialogue in High-Risk AI Interactions: A Constrained Self-Evolving LLM HarnessThanks: Corresponding authors.

Xiaohan Zhang    Ying Zhao    Ruilin Guo    Xuanyuan Yu
Affiliation: Kaibo Huang, Yukun Wei, Zhongliang Yang, Linna Zhou^(\*)

###### Abstract

In high-risk text interactions, a system may need to estimate whether
the current speaker is likely to be a minor before applying
age-conditioned safeguards. Many text-based dialogue systems lack strong
identity signals such as account information, verified identity, facial
data, or device data, and must rely on weak cues that emerge across
multiple turns. We formulate implicit minor detection under weak
identity information as a high-risk user-attribute inference task and
propose a constrained framework comprising a large language model, a
Harness, and a Maker-Checker protocol. The framework controls when
inference is invoked, requires structured dialogue-linked evidence, and
subjects validation-selected Harness revisions to version-level review.
On a controlled Chinese benchmark comprising 8,328 multi-turn dialogues,
our method improves average precision and minor-class F1 by 7.60 and
6.37 percentage points, respectively, over direct inference using the
same LLM family.

## Introduction

Generative AI systems for companionship, learning support, and
mental-health assistance increasingly operate in settings where minors
may participate. Applying suitable safeguards often requires an initial
judgment about whether the current speaker is likely to be a minor.
Platform-level age assurance can use account, identity, facial, or
parental-control signals ([Livingstone et al. 2024](#bib.bib16)).
Text-based services embedded in external applications often have access
only to the current conversation. In these settings, life-stage
information appears gradually through references to school schedules,
parental restrictions, examinations, work, housing, and other contextual
details.

Prior work covers three adjacent problems. Platform age assurance relies
on account, identity, biometric, or parental-control signals
([Livingstone et al. 2024](#bib.bib16)). Textual author profiling infers
demographic attributes from linguistic patterns, but usually treats the
available text as a static profile ([Rosso 2017](#bib.bib20)).
Youth-safety and content-safety research instead focuses on harmful
content, moderation, and safeguards ([Yu 2025](#bib.bib31); [Yu et al.
2025a](#bib.bib32); [Yu et al. 2025b](#bib.bib33); [Han et al.
2024](#bib.bib10); [Inan et al. 2023](#bib.bib11)).
Figure [1](#Sx1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Introduction ‣ Implicit Minor Detection from Multi-Turn Dialogue in High-Risk AI Interactions: A Constrained Self-Evolving LLM Harness")
locates the gap between these lines of work.

Implicit minor detection poses four technical difficulties. First,
individually weak cues may become informative only when combined across
turns. Second, the system must bind each cue to the current speaker
rather than to a parent, child, quoted source, or past self. Third, age
inference should be invoked only when the request and dialogue justify
it; a broad trigger risks unnecessary profiling, while a narrow trigger
misses cases that need protection. Fourth, revisions are prone to
regression: an edit that fixes one failure type can damage previously
correct or boundary-sensitive cases.

![Refer to caption](2610.38546v1/problem_overview_v2_outlined.png)

Figure 1: Adjacent approaches leave a weak-identity gap. Platform age
assurance depends on strong identity signals; static single-turn
profiling loses cross-turn and speaker-binding context; content-safety
safeguards assess content risk rather than the speaker’s likely age.

We address these difficulties with a fixed LLM, a versioned Harness, and
a Maker–Checker protocol. The Harness controls invocation, contextual
support, evidence representation, and output validation. Its task-layer
resources evolve from validation errors and recent revision history,
while the promotion gate protects required behavior and rejects
candidates that violate the admission constraints. Maker–Checker
materials record version diffs and support inspection before the
selected Harness is frozen.

To evaluate the task and framework, we construct a main benchmark and a
separate trigger-boundary set. The main benchmark measures speaker-level
recognition and component effects; the trigger set measures when minor
detection should be invoked. Relative to direct inference with the same
LLM family, our method improves average precision (AP) by 7.60
percentage points and minor-class F1 by 6.37 points. Trigger evolution
raises trigger F1 by 7.23 points. A 50-participant human challenge-set
study provides complementary evidence about item difficulty and
annotator disagreement.

Our contributions are fourfold. First, we introduce a constrained
self-evolving framework composed of a fixed LLM, a versioned Harness,
and a Maker–Checker protocol. Second, we formulate implicit minor
detection as a speaker-bound, multi-turn inference task distinct from
static profiling, content moderation, and platform age assurance. Third,
we construct an 8,328-dialogue benchmark and a separate 480-example
trigger-boundary set for evaluating recognition and invocation. Fourth,
experiments on recognition, trigger routing, component ablations, and
version evolution show consistent gains over direct inference with the
same LLM family; the human challenge set further identifies difficult
and disputed cases.

## Related Work

#### Dialogue attribute inference.

Dialogue contains personal information that may be stated directly or
implied across several utterances. Author-profiling benchmarks
traditionally infer age, gender, or language variety from stable textual
patterns ([Rosso 2017](#bib.bib20)). Conversation-focused methods infer
open-ended personal facts or extract persona attributes from interaction
histories ([Tigunova et al. 2020](#bib.bib26); [Wang et al.
2022](#bib.bib27); [Zhu et al. 2023](#bib.bib35)). Dialogue
representations can also reveal a speaker’s private persona ([Li, Song,
and Fan 2022](#bib.bib15)), while modern LLMs can recover sensitive
properties from indirect cues ([Staab et al. 2024](#bib.bib23)). Minor
status adds ambiguity because apparent evidence may be hypothetical,
retrospective, or attributed to another person. Evidence-grounded NLP
benchmarks evaluate predictions together with their supporting text
([DeYoung et al. 2020](#bib.bib3)), and MT-Bench-101 shows that
turn-aware analysis can reveal behavior obscured by aggregate dialogue
scores ([Bai et al. 2024](#bib.bib1)). We formulate minor detection as
an evidence-grounded dialogue-level decision and evaluate difficult,
adversarial, and boundary cases.

#### Age assurance and youth safety.

Age-assurance research studies how platforms combine verification
technologies, governance, children’s rights, privacy, and public
acceptance ([Livingstone et al. 2024](#bib.bib16); [Jarvie and Renaud
2024](#bib.bib12)). Youth-focused generative-AI research instead
characterizes risks and parental controls ([Yu 2025](#bib.bib31); [Yu
et al. 2025a](#bib.bib32)); YouthSafe advances this line with a
benchmark and safeguard model ([Yu et al. 2025b](#bib.bib33)). Their
central question is whether content or model behavior is safe for youth.
Our setting studies an earlier decision in the policy chain: whether
age-conditioned protection is relevant when the speaker’s age is
implicit. A moderation model may identify harmful content without
resolving who is speaking or whether a youth-specific policy should be
activated.

Content-safety classifiers and safeguard models assess whether text or
model responses are harmful ([Han et al. 2024](#bib.bib10); [Inan et al.
2023](#bib.bib11)). Their labels concern content risk rather than the
identity of the speaker. XSTest shows that safety systems can
overgeneralize from superficial cues ([Röttger et al.
2024](#bib.bib21)). In minor detection, the analogous error is assigning
minor status to an adult because a dialogue contains school, family, or
emotional vocabulary. We therefore include adult controls, hard
negatives, adversarial cases, and explicit boundary examples.

#### Harnesses, self-refinement, and version governance.

Existing methods improve individual responses or task programs through
iterative critique, feedback memory, tool use, and metric-driven prompt
optimization ([Madaan et al. 2023](#bib.bib17); [Shinn et al.
2023](#bib.bib22); [Yao et al. 2023](#bib.bib30); [Khattab et al.
2024](#bib.bib13); [Yüksekgönül et al. 2024](#bib.bib34); [Yang et al.
2024](#bib.bib29); [Gou et al. 2024](#bib.bib9)). Promptbreeder evolves
prompt populations through mutation and selection ([Fernando et al.
2024](#bib.bib5)). These methods establish the value of external
feedback, but preserving previously correct behavior is not their
central evaluation contract.

Our Harness treats optimization as a governed transition between
external, versioned task resources. Candidate revisions are evaluated on
frozen validation and regression cases, promoted only when predefined
constraints are satisfied, and otherwise rejected with the incumbent
retained. The next proposal receives both current errors and the
recorded consequences of recent rejected or accepted edits. Behavioral
testing motivates preserving targeted controls beyond aggregate accuracy
([Ribeiro et al. 2020](#bib.bib19)). Proposal, evaluation, and decision
artifacts remain separate. Chain-of-Verification shows the value of an
explicit verification stage for generated answers ([Dhuliawala et al.
2024](#bib.bib4)); our framework applies verification to source-linked
evidence at inference time and to candidate versions during development.

## Method

### Overall Framework

We operationalize minor status as age below 18; speakers aged 18 or
older belong to the adult class. Given a multi-turn dialogue window, the
system has no access to account verification, facial signals, device
metadata, or a trusted age attribute. We distinguish two decisions:
whether minor detection should be invoked and, once invoked, whether the
current speaker is likely to be a minor. Recognition also returns a
confidence score and dialogue-grounded evidence.

Let $`x`$ denote a dialogue window. The task first predicts whether
minor-status inference should be invoked, $`g_{d_{t}}(x)\in\{0,1\}`$,
where $`d_{t}`$ is the current routing description. When
$`g_{d_{t}}(x)=1`$, recognition returns a speaker label
$`\hat{y}\in\{0,1\}`$, confidence $`s\in[0,1]`$, and dialogue-grounded
evidence $`e`$, where $`\hat{y}=1`$ denotes minor and $`s`$ is the
returned minor-confidence score. Our implementation additionally records
a supported profile, ICBO fields, a conflict-resolution policy, and a
follow-up action under the Harness contract. We write the system at
iteration $`t`$ as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       S_{t}=(L,H_{t},\mathcal{M}),
       ```                           |     | (1) |

where $`L`$ is the fixed base LLM, $`H_{t}`$ is the versioned task
Harness, and $`\mathcal{M}`$ denotes the Maker–Checker review protocol.
Only $`H_{t}`$ is updated; the parameters of $`L`$ remain fixed.

### Harness and Maker–Checker Protocol

The Harness converts an open-ended model call into a constrained
inference procedure with five components:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       H_{t}=(\tau_{t},C_{t},\Phi_{t},E_{t},G_{t}),
       ```                                           |     | (2) |

where $`\tau_{t}`$ controls the invocation boundary; $`C_{t}`$ organizes
auxiliary context such as temporal features, task rules, and similarity
retrieval; $`\Phi_{t}`$ defines the structured input–output contract;
$`E_{t}`$ constrains decisive claims to traceable evidence; and
$`G_{t}`$ proposes revisions, checks them for regression, and either
promotes the candidate or retains the incumbent. The first four
components govern a single inference. The final component governs
transitions between Harness versions.

Contextual support combines temporal normalization, task rules, and
similarity retrieval. Retrieval-augmented generation provides a standard
mechanism for conditioning an LLM on selected external records ([Lewis
et al. 2020](#bib.bib14)). Our retriever uses text-embedding-3-small to
select up to three cases from a case base built only from the
5,830-example training split. Retrieved labels, profiles, and utterances
cannot be copied into the current user’s profile or direct evidence.
Retrieval cannot establish an age direction when the dialogue is
insufficient or reverse a direction supported by current-dialogue
evidence. We use it only for consistency checking.

The Maker–Checker protocol is separate from runtime inference. After
validation-based selection, the implementation generates a version-diff
review and an eight-item checklist that prioritizes changed predictions,
low-confidence cases, and boundary-sensitive cases. The checklist covers
label credibility, practical acceptability, boundary drift, explanation
risk, the evaluation record, and the edited prompt text. These materials
support manual version-level inspection; they do not alter runtime
predictions or test metrics.

### Trigger Boundary

School-related words, emotional language, or discussion of young people
should not by themselves trigger minor detection. Over-routing would
apply demographic inference to inputs for which it was neither requested
nor justified. A description that requires an explicit age request would
also miss legitimate implicit speaker-profiling tasks. We therefore
model routing as a separate binary problem.

The routing description $`d_{t}`$ defines when the calling agent invokes
$`g_{d_{t}}`$. A positive route requires that the request concern the
age tendency, student stage, or minor-related risk of the current
speaker or another explicitly designated dialogue participant.
Topic-only educational discussion and incidental mentions of third
parties are negative. Several mutually consistent weak signals may
justify invocation, while a single school term, emotional expression, or
family reference does not. The recognition benchmark itself remains
current-speaker-only.

We optimize this boundary independently from downstream recognition.
Description evolution changes only $`d_{t}`$ and is evaluated on a
dedicated trigger dataset. Each example contains a user request, a gold
invocation decision, and a routing scenario. Evaluation records whether
the agent invokes the skill, whether invocation completes, and whether
the routing output satisfies its contract. The recognition pipeline
remains fixed so routing and recognition errors can be analyzed
separately.

### Structured Evidence and ICBO Representation

After invocation, the system binds each candidate cue to its subject.
Mentions of another person, retrospective school memories, quoted
material, and assistant-generated speculation cannot support a claim
about the current speaker. Strong current-user anchors, such as an
explicit K–12 task or a stable adult responsibility, receive priority.
Emotion, dependence, family conflict, communication style, and similar
weak cues contribute only when several of them support a consistent
account of the speaker’s current circumstances and no stronger cue
contradicts it.

ICBO is a task-specific scaffold for organizing this evidence. Intention
records what the speaker is trying to accomplish. Cognition records
self-descriptions and identity-relevant mental context supported by the
dialogue. Behavior summarizes relevant activity patterns.
Opportunity/time represents temporal and situational constraints,
including school schedules, work shifts, examinations, and recurring
session timing. ICBO is an explicit intermediate record, not a learned
psychological representation.

The evidence record separates direct dialogue evidence, temporal
evidence, historical context, retrieved cases, and conflicting signals.
Every direct-evidence item must be a short quotation or near-verbatim
span locatable in the current dialogue. Summaries and inferences occupy
separate fields, preventing a retrieved case or generated explanation
from being presented as a user statement. The user profile follows the
same rule and leaves fields unspecified when no dialogue-grounded
identity anchor exists.

The decision policy records minor cues, adult cues, evidence
sufficiency, conflict resolution, and the final decision basis. It
combines subject-bound, typed evidence with the ICBO record to produce
the binary label and minor-confidence score. If evidence is marked
insufficient, the frozen binary protocol maps the output to the negative
class. This means that the dialogue does not support minor status; it
does not verify adulthood. The benchmark does not evaluate a separate
abstention class. A deployed system could instead request clarification
or apply a conservative policy. The classifier receives the evidence
package and output schema in one constrained call and returns one JSON
object. A validator checks required fields, types, enumerated values,
confidence ranges, and cross-field consistency. Schema repair is invoked
only when this contract is violated.

### Gated Harness Evolution

We improve the Harness through error-driven, validation-only evolution.
Let $`J(H_{t};D_{\mathrm{val}})`$ be the current validation report and
let $`U(H_{t})`$ denote the admission metrics: F1, invocation success,
and step compliance. The report also contains contract statistics and
indexed error artifacts. At each round, DeepSeek-V3 receives four forms
of feedback: the incumbent’s current errors and their failure types;
currently correct validation cases that the revision should preserve;
new errors introduced by the preceding candidate; and the diffs, metric
changes, and acceptance or rejection reasons from the three most recent
rounds. Thus, a proposal is informed by both unresolved errors and the
observed consequences of earlier edits. The optimizer does not modify
the base-model weights, output schema, controller, or evaluation code.

Here, self-evolution refers to validation-guided revision of Harness
resources; the base model and evaluation code remain fixed.

The inner loop combines utility comparison with an explicit admission
gate. $`G_{\mathrm{core+}}=1`$ when at least one component of
$`U(H^{\prime}_{t})`$ improves over $`U(H_{t})`$. For the main
recognition task, F1 must also remain non-decreasing, as must invocation
success, step compliance, and schema validity; no new redline failure
may be introduced:

changes only $`d_{t}`$ and optimizes routing quality.

All candidate selection uses development data. The held-out recognition

repeat the procedure with seeds 42, 43, and 44, which control sampling,
ordering, and baseline randomness; external API execution may still
vary. The archived artifacts preserve accepted and rejected
trajectories, allowing reported changes to be traced to concrete Harness
versions.

## Experiments

### Benchmark Construction and Quality Control

The main benchmark contains 8,328 Chinese multi-turn dialogues from
social-support and knowledge-seeking settings. The social-support
portion contains 4,338 dialogues synthesized from publicly
redistributed, anonymized PsyQA help-seeking seeds ([Sun et al.
2021](#bib.bib24)). The knowledge-seeking portion contains 3,990
dialogues generated from the topic structures of 24 K–12 textbooks and
15 adult curricula or examinations. The evaluation unit is a complete
dialogue. Life-stage evidence may be distributed across turns, delayed
until later in the exchange, or mixed with cues pointing in the opposite
direction.

Adult examples are deliberately difficult confounders. Social-domain
negatives include young-adult help-seeking situations that resemble
adolescent concerns in topic and style. Knowledge-domain negatives
include university study, professional qualification, and adult
examination settings that overlap lexically with school-related
conversations. Systems must therefore bind evidence to the current
speaker and distinguish educational content from the speaker’s likely
life stage.

Construction includes seed-level manual curation and generated-dialogue
automated checks. Social-domain seeds undergo rule-based filtering
followed by single-review manual curation before generation.
Knowledge-domain labels are derived programmatically from curriculum
stage and examination type. After generation, automated checks verify
required fields, age–stage consistency, temporal formats, and stratum
counts. This separation of generation, assessment, and downstream use
follows established accounts of LLM-based data annotation and synthesis
([Tan et al. 2024](#bib.bib25)). We retain construction and split
records following the broader rationale of transparent dataset
documentation ([Pushkarna, Zaldivar, and Kjartansson 2022](#bib.bib18)).

The final split contains 5,830 training, 833 validation, and 1,665 test
dialogues. The protocol introduces a deliberate evidence shift: all
training and validation dialogues use explicit life-stage anchors,
whereas all test dialogues require implicit inference without a direct
age or stage statement. The test set contains 833 minor and 832 adult
examples. Its scenario families and coarse families are disjoint from
both training and validation, so the test set evaluates performance
under a family-disjoint explicit-to-implicit shift within this
controlled benchmark. The training split supports parameter estimation
and forms the retrieval case base. Neither validation nor test examples
enter retrieval. Validation data selects prompts, contracts, and Harness
versions; the test split is evaluated after these artifacts are frozen.

Trigger-Eval is constructed separately because invocation and downstream
classification answer different questions. It contains 480 requests
balanced between positive and negative labels and covers direct requests
and window-scanning conditions. We use a 288-example development split
to revise and select the invocation description. The remaining 192
examples form a held-out trigger test split and are not used during
description selection. The human study uses a 40-item challenge subset
from the main test split: 10 Easy, 15 Hard, 10 Adversarial, and 5
Boundary items. Each of 50 non-expert participants labels all 40
dialogues and reports confidence on a five-point scale.

### Comparison Protocol

All methods use the same minor/adult label space and partition.
Trainable encoders and safeguards use the training set for parameter
estimation and validation data for checkpoint selection. Prompt-based
systems fix prompts, output contracts, and parsing rules on validation
data. Minor-class average precision (AP) is computed from frozen
per-example scores with tied scores evaluated jointly. Discriminative
models use their positive-class posterior, while LLM systems use the
structured minor_confidence field; thresholded metrics use the returned
Boolean label. We report minor-class precision, recall, and F1 over
seeds 42, 43, and 44. The sample standard deviations summarize variation
across these archived runs, including stochastic optimization and remote
API execution, rather than sampling uncertainty over new dialogue
populations.

The baselines cover three paradigms. Chinese-RoBERTa-wwm-ext represents
supervised encoder fine-tuning ([Cui et al. 2020](#bib.bib2)).
Aegis-Guard FT adapts the Aegis 1.0 defensive safeguard checkpoint
([Ghosh et al. 2024](#bib.bib8); [Inan et al. 2023](#bib.bib11)) to the
binary task. Gemini Direct uses the same gemini-2.5-flash-lite
classifier, dialogue window, JSON response mode, and confidence
semantics as our method ([Gemini Team 2023](#bib.bib7)). Its task-aware
prompt covers speaker binding and minor/adult evidence, but it does not
receive the Harness evidence package, retrieved cases, temporal
features, schema-repair path, or version-evolution procedure. It is
therefore a strong direct-prompt comparison for the additional system
structure. We also evaluate the fine-tuned Aegis backbone with the full
Harness. Because the Aegis and Gemini settings use different training
and runtime protocols, the portability result is secondary to the
shared-model comparison.

## Results

### Main Task Performance

Table [1](#Sx4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Comparison Protocol ‣ Experiments ‣ Implicit Minor Detection from Multi-Turn Dialogue in High-Risk AI Interactions: A Constrained Self-Evolving LLM Harness")
reports the held-out main-task results. Our method obtains an AP of
$`0.9574\pm 0.0062`$ and a minor-class F1 of $`0.9198\pm 0.0015`$, the
highest values among the evaluated methods. Relative to Gemini Direct,
AP increases by 7.60 percentage points and F1 by 6.37 points. Precision
rises by 10.43 points, from 0.8317 to 0.9360, while recall rises by 2.24
points, from 0.8820 to 0.9044. The larger precision gain indicates that
the framework mainly reduces adult false positives without sacrificing
minor coverage. For each seed, paired item-bootstrap 95% intervals
exclude zero for both AP and F1 gains; exact McNemar tests on
correctness give $`p<6\times 10^{-12}`$ in all three runs (supplement).

The safeguard comparison shows a smaller but substantial gain.
Aegis-Guard FT is precise but misses many positive cases, with recall
0.5278 and F1 0.6640. Adding the Harness raises recall to 0.7271 and F1
to 0.7851, while precision decreases from 0.8963 to 0.8541. The same
pattern appears with a separately fine-tuned safeguard backbone,
providing secondary evidence that the Harness is useful beyond the
Gemini runtime. The different protocols preclude a broad cross-family
ranking.

### Human Challenge-Set Reference

On the difficulty-enriched 40-item subset, 50 non-expert adult readers

## Discussion

The results support a task design that separates invocation from
recognition while retaining speaker-bound evidence across the full
dialogue. Contextual support and evidence traceability affect different
parts of the precision–recall trade-off. The held-out
initial-to-selected comparison also shows that validation-guided Harness
revision can improve the initial version under the stated admission
rules. Trigger routing remains a separate source of error: a recognition
model cannot help when age inference is invoked for the wrong request or
not invoked when it is needed.

### Limitations

The current evidence focuses on implicit minor detection and a
controlled dialogue-level synthetic benchmark. It therefore supports the
effectiveness of the proposed method under the reported conditions,
without directly establishing performance in real platform deployments.
Structured constraints, retrieval support, and Maker–Checker review
introduce additional system cost while providing auditability, boundary
stability, and version governance. Evaluation under more realistic
deployment conditions, cross-lingual validation, and performance
retention in lower-cost configurations remain directions for future
work.

## Conclusion

We introduced implicit minor detection as a speaker-bound multi-turn
task and constructed an 8,328-dialogue benchmark with a separate
trigger-boundary evaluation. Our framework combines a fixed LLM, a
versioned Harness, and Maker–Checker review. Harness evolution uses
current errors together with protected correct cases and the recorded
outcomes of recent accepted and rejected updates. On the held-out main
task, the framework improves AP by 7.60 percentage points and
minor-class F1 by 6.37 points over direct inference with the same LLM
family. Trigger evolution adds 7.23 F1 points, showing that invocation
boundaries can be improved separately from recognition.

## Ethical Statement

This work treats minor status as a risk assessment grounded in
multi-turn dialogue evidence. The assessment is designed to support
protective decisions and does not verify a user’s legal age. Inferring a
sensitive attribute from indirect language creates privacy and misuse
risks ([Staab et al. 2024](#bib.bib23); [Weidinger et al.
2022](#bib.bib28)). The output should therefore not be used alone to
trigger high-stakes automated actions. High-uncertainty and boundary
cases should retain access to human review and a correction or appeal
process. We position the framework as an auxiliary risk-control layer
that can operate alongside platform-level age assurance, human judgment,
and formal identity-verification mechanisms.

The human challenge-set study used a voluntary anonymous questionnaire
completed by 50 adults aged 20–30. Participants were informed that some
items contain psychological distress or self-harm expressions and
consented to research use upon submission. No direct identifiers were
collected, and the paper reports only aggregate and item-level
statistics.

## Appendix A Benchmark and Evaluation Protocol

### A.1 Main Recognition Benchmark

The main benchmark contains 8,328 Chinese multi-turn dialogues: 4,338
social-support dialogues and 3,990 knowledge-seeking dialogues. The
frozen split contains 5,830 training, 833 validation, and 1,665 test
examples. The test set contains 833 minor and 832 adult examples. The
retrieval case base is identical to the training split; validation and
test examples are excluded from retrieval.

Social-support seeds pass rule-based filtering and single-review manual
curation before dialogue generation. Knowledge-domain labels follow
curriculum stage and examination type. Adult controls cover university
study, professional examinations, employment, renting, and other
responsibility structures that overlap lexically with school-related
dialogue. These controls require the classifier to resolve the speaker
and life stage instead of relying on isolated school or family terms.

Construction records include generation templates, automated field and
label checks, stratum counts, split manifests, and duplicate audits.
Generated-dialogue checks cover required fields, label and age–stage
consistency, temporal formats, duplicate detection, stratum counts, and
split isolation. Manual review applies to the pre-generation social
seeds.

### A.2 Family-Disjoint Split Audit

The split audit uses *family* and *coarse-family* keys. For social
examples, family pairs the source label with the originating seed
identifier; the coarse key is identical. For positive knowledge
examples, family uses subject and chapter when chapter metadata are
available, while the coarse key uses subject only. For negative
knowledge examples, family uses the source file when available and
otherwise the subject; the coarse key applies the same
source-file-or-subject grouping. The frozen manifest reports zero family
and coarse-family overlap between test and either training or
validation.

### A.3 Trigger and Human Evaluation Sets

Trigger-Eval contains 480 balanced requests covering direct requests and
window-scanning conditions. Description selection uses 288 development
examples; the remaining 192 requests form the held-out trigger test
split. Trigger development changes only the routing description and does
not update the recognition pipeline.

The human study uses a difficulty-enriched 40-item subset of the
recognition test split: 10 Easy, 15 Hard, 10 Adversarial, and 5 Boundary
items. The subset contains 22 minor and 18 adult labels. It is used only
for the human reference and paired challenge-set analysis.

## Appendix B Reproducibility and Statistical Protocol

### B.1 Recognition Systems and Baselines

The recognition classifier and Gemini Direct both use the
gemini-2.5-flash-lite alias through the same OpenAI-compatible endpoint
with JSON-object response mode. The endpoint did not return an immutable
provider-side checkpoint identifier. Harness inference retrieves the top
three cases with text-embedding-3-small. Gemini Direct uses a task-aware
prompt covering speaker binding, the under-18 decision boundary,
conflicting evidence, and confidence semantics. Its archived prompt hash
is 5e0494c2...3971ff; the full SHA-256 value is stored in the artifact
manifest. Gemini Direct omits the Harness evidence package, retrieval,
temporal features, schema repair, and development loop.

Chinese-RoBERTa uses hfl/chinese-roberta-wwm-ext, a maximum sequence
length of 512, three epochs, learning rate $`2\times 10^{-5}`$,
train/evaluation batch sizes 16/32, weight decay 0.01, and warmup ratio
0.06. Aegis-Guard FT uses
nvidia/Aegis-AI-Content-Safety-LlamaGuard-Defensive-1.0, a maximum
sequence length of 1024, one epoch, learning rate $`2\times 10^{-5}`$,
batch size 1 with gradient accumulation 8, and warmup ratio 0.03. The
archived records do not contain an immutable revision for the locally
cached Aegis backbone.

### B.2 Harness Evolution and Version Review

The Harness optimizer uses DeepSeek-V3, a maximum search budget of ten
rounds, and patience three. At each round, it receives the incumbent’s
errors and failure types, correct validation cases to protect, new
errors introduced by the preceding candidate, and the diffs, metric
changes, and acceptance or rejection reasons from the three most recent
rounds. The base-model weights, output schema, controller, and
evaluation code remain fixed.

After validation selection, the implementation produces a version-diff
report and an eight-item Maker–Checker checklist for each run. The
checklist covers changed predictions, low-confidence and
boundary-sensitive cases, label credibility, practical acceptability,
boundary drift, explanation risk, the evaluation record, and edited
prompt text. The selected versions and review materials underwent manual
inspection before freezing. This review is a version-governance
procedure, not an additional performance measurement.

Main results use seeds 42, 43, and 44. The seeds control sampling,
ordering, and trainable-baseline initialization. External API execution
is not bitwise deterministic, so the reported sample standard deviation
describes the three archived executions rather than uncertainty over a
new dialogue population. Frozen artifacts retain schemas, prompt
resources, parsing rules, version decisions, prediction files, and
metric reports.

## Appendix E Human Challenge-Set Study

### E.1 Protocol and Tie Handling

Fifty non-expert adults aged 20–30 complete the same 40-item
questionnaire. Each item asks for a minor/adult decision and a
five-point confidence rating. Participants are informed that some
dialogues contain psychological distress or self-harm expressions,
participate voluntarily, consent to research use upon submission, and
provide no direct identifiers.

Individual metrics use all 40 items. Strict-majority consensus resolves
38 items; human-028 and human-033 receive 25–25 votes and remain
unresolved. The primary majority analysis therefore has 95% coverage.
This rule is fixed from the votes and does not use the gold labels.
