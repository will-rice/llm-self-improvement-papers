###### Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly asked to judge whether a
news article is true, and their stated confidence is often taken at face
value. We test what happens to that confidence when an article’s writing
style, but not its facts, is manipulated, as in recent _style attacks_
on fake-news detectors. Tracing how the prediction and its confidence
form layer by layer inside the network, we study three model families
(LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B) on three fake-news benchmarks, and
find the same three patterns in every model–dataset pairing. First, on
the original articles the models are competent and largely honest
judges: they settle on an answer, briefly become overconfident, and then
correct their confidence in the final layers. Second, style-rewritten
articles break this self-correction: the models become far less
accurate, under the strongest attack, worse than random guessing, yet
remain just as confident, so they are _confidently wrong_ precisely when
a warning is most needed. Third, nearly all of the damage comes from
rewriting into sensational or emotionally charged styles, while sober,
neutral rewrites leave the models’ honesty largely intact. The practical
implication is that an LLM’s confidence about veracity cannot be trusted
as a safety signal when an article’s style may have been manipulated.

###### keywords

calibration; large language models; fake news detection; adversarial
robustness; mechanistic interpretability; logit lens

## 1 Introduction

Automatic fake-news detectors learn to separate real from fake partly
through _style_: fabricated articles often differ stylistically from
genuine reporting. [Wu et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6) show that this is also
a vulnerability: rewriting a fake article into the neutral,
authoritative register of a credible outlet—preserving its false
claims—degrades detector F1 by up to roughly $`38\%`$. Such rewrites
cost cents per article, making the attack cheap and realistic.

Prior analyses characterize _what_ breaks (classifier accuracy) or _how_
representations move geometrically under fine-tuning, but they do not
address the model’s _confidence_. This matters for deployment: a
detector that is wrong but uncertain invites human review, whereas one
that is wrong and _certain_ does not. The dangerous regime is therefore
overconfidence on attacked inputs, not merely lost accuracy.

We study this regime directly. Taking three off-the-shelf,
instruction-tuned LLMs and prompting them _zero-shot_ to classify
articles as real or fake, we use the logit lens ([nostalgebraist,
2020](#bib.bib3)) to read both the prediction and the confidence after
_every_ transformer layer, on original articles and on style-rewritten
ones. The zero-shot setting is deliberate: with no task-specific
training, any calibration behavior reflects the model’s intrinsic
veracity judgment rather than an artifact of fine-tuning.

#### Contributions.

(i) We bring layer-wise calibration analysis into the fake-news
adversarial-robustness setting, zero-shot. (ii) We show a original-data
_confidence-correction phase_ exists for this task and replicates across
three model families and three datasets. (iii) We show style attacks
induce _upper-layer calibration fragility_: the model becomes
confidently wrong, and under the strongest attack accuracy drops
_significantly below chance_ (systematic inversion), with the failure
co-located with the layers that normally perform the correction. (iv) We
reproduce, in calibration space and zero-shot, the
reliable-vs-unreliable style asymmetry previously reported for
fine-tuned BERT embeddings, with non-overlapping effect sizes across all
nine cells and under two confidence definitions.

## 2 Related Work

#### Calibration of LLMs.

Modern neural networks are frequently miscalibrated ([Guo et al.,
2017](#bib.bib1)), yet large LLMs often exhibit surprisingly faithful
token-level confidence ([Kadavath et al., 2022](#bib.bib11)). Confidence
can also be elicited by direct verbalization ([Tian et al.,
2023](#bib.bib12); [Xiong et al., 2024](#bib.bib13)), and calibration is
known to degrade under distribution shift ([Ovadia et al.,
2019](#bib.bib14)); our attacks are a targeted, label-preserving shift.
Recent mechanistic work localizes confidence regulation to final-layer
structures such as entropy neurons that write into the null space of the
unembedding ([Stolfo et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)). [Joshi et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib2) show, using the logit lens, that calibration _evolves
across depth_, with a late-layer correction phase on multiple-choice
benchmarks. We extend this layer-wise view from standard multiple-choice
questions to a fake-news setting under adversarial style attacks.

#### Interpretability via the logit lens and residual stream.

Decoder-only transformers maintain a residual stream updated additively
by each block ([Elhage et al., 2021](#bib.bib4)). The logit lens
([nostalgebraist, 2020](#bib.bib3)) projects intermediate residual
states through the unembedding to inspect the model’s evolving
next-token distribution without training auxiliary probes, which is the
instrument we use throughout.

#### Style attacks on fake-news detectors.

[Wu et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6) introduce SheepDog and a style-attack
benchmark in which LLM-generated rewrites preserve veracity but alter
tone; they propose a training-side defense. Their analysis targets
fine-tuned classifiers and their accuracy. We provide a layer-wise
account in _calibration_ space and in the _zero-shot_ regime, where no
fine-tuning is involved.

## 3 Method

### 3.1 Task formulation

We cast binary veracity detection as a two-option multiple-choice
question so that the answer is a single token amenable to the logit
lens:

> Article: \<text\> $`\;\ldots\;`$ Is the article above real or fake
> news? A. \<option\>   B. \<option\>   Answer:

Placing the article before the question puts the final prompt token at
the answer position. We randomize the mapping of Real/Fake to A/B per
article (with a fixed per-article rule, so an article and all its
rewrites share the same layout), which prevents the well-known
positional “A” prior from dominating the calibration measurement. The
model is never fine-tuned; all evaluations are zero-shot.

### 3.2 Calibration metrics

For predictions partitioned into $`M`$ equal-width confidence bins
$`\{B_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}`$, with per-bin mean confidence
$`\mathrm{conf}(B_{m})`$ and accuracy $`\mathrm{acc}(B_{m})`$, the
Expected and Maximum Calibration Errors are

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{|B_{m}|}{n}\bigl|\mathrm{acc}(B_{m})-\mathrm{conf}(B_{m})\bigr|,\qquad\mathrm{MCE}=\max_{m}\bigl|\mathrm{acc}(B_{m})-\mathrm{conf}(B_{m})\bigr|.
``` |  | (1) |

Both vanish for a perfectly calibrated model; we use $`M=10`$.

### 3.3 Layer-wise read-out via the logit lens

Let $`h_{\ell}`$ denote the residual stream at layer $`\ell`$ for the
final prompt token, $`\mathrm{LN}(\cdot)`$ the model’s final
normalization, and $`W_{U}`$ the unembedding. The logit lens read-out
and its distribution are

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
z_{\ell}=W_{U}\,\mathrm{LN}(h_{\ell}),\qquad p_{\ell}=\mathrm{softmax}(z_{\ell}).
``` |  | (2) |

A single forward pass yields $`h_{\ell}`$ for all $`\ell`$; the top
read-out reproduces the model’s true output logits. We report two
confidence definitions: *paper-faithful* (argmax over the full
vocabulary and its probability, matching [Joshi et al.,
2025](#bib.bib2)) and *restricted* (probability over $`\{`$A, B$`\}`$,
renormalized). The two measures agree closely on all large
effects—final-layer original-test ECE, test-level $`\Delta`$ECE, and
unreliable-style $`\Delta`$ECE differ by at most $`{\sim}0.05`$ across
cells, and the reliable/unreliable non-overlap holds under both
(Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Paper-faithful vs. restricted confidence ‣ A Layer-wise Study of How Style Rewriting Breaks the Calibration of Zero-Shot LLM Fake-News Detectors"))—and
differ only on the small reliable-style effect, whose sign is not
robust; we therefore never rest a claim on it. We present paper-faithful
and note that, in the *middle* layers, its top token is often an
ordinary word rather than an answer letter, so the informative signal
lies in the upper, answer-predicting layers.

### 3.4 Robustness quantities

At the final layer we report, relative to the original condition: the
*Accuracy Collapse Ratio*
$`\mathrm{AccCR}=\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{att}}/\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{orig}}`$
($`1.0`$ means no damage), and the calibration shift
$`\Delta\mathrm{ECE}=\mathrm{ECE}_{\text{att}}-\mathrm{ECE}_{\text{orig}}`$
(large positive values indicate the model became overconfident). The
*correction magnitude* is the upper-layer rise-then-fall in ECE on
original data. We average these separately over the two reliable styles
(objective, neutral) and the two unreliable styles (sensational,
emotionally triggering).

### 3.5 Statistical reliability

We report Wilson $`95\%`$ confidence intervals for accuracy and, via the
Katz log method, for the accuracy collapse ratio; these are exact
functions of the reported proportions and sample sizes. The four
adversarial sets restyle the *same* test articles and are therefore not
independent, so we treat the effective sample size as the test-set size
and report per-set intervals rather than pooling. Aggregated “Adv”
figures average per-set metrics (i.e. averaged ECE, not the ECE of a
pooled set).

### 3.6 Models and data

We evaluate three instruction-tuned decoder-only models from different
families: LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct ($`32`$ layers; [Dubey et al.,
2024](#bib.bib9)), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ($`28`$ layers; [Qwen Team,
2024](#bib.bib10)), and Mistral-7B-Instruct ($`32`$ layers; [Jiang et
al., 2023](#bib.bib15)). We use the three SheepDog benchmarks ([Wu et
al., 2024](#bib.bib6)): PolitiFact and GossipCop from FakeNewsNet ([Shu
et al., 2020](#bib.bib8)), and LUN ([Rashkin et al., 2017](#bib.bib7)).
Each ships original articles, four GPT-3.5 style reframings of each
training article (objective, neutral, sensational, emotionally
triggering), and four adversarial test sets (adv A to D).
([Wu et al., 2024](#bib.bib6)) establishes a set of four general
style-oriented adjectives for the prompt: “objective and professional”
and “neutral” to emulate reliable sources, and “emotionally triggering”
and “sensational” for unreliable sources.
Rewrite the following article in a / an \[specified\] tone: \[news
article\]

### 4.1 A confidence-correction phase on original inputs

On original articles, accuracy is near zero until the answer emerges in
the upper layers (around layer $`22`$ of $`32`$ for the $`32`$-layer
models, proportionally similar for Qwen’s $`28`$), then saturates;
confidence transiently overshoots accuracy (raising ECE); and the final
layers recalibrate confidence downward. The completeness of this
correction tracks task difficulty: on LUN, LLaMA ends at $`0.869`$
accuracy with ECE $`0.016`$ (near-perfect), whereas on the harder
GossipCop set it ends less calibrated (ECE $`0.173`$ for LLaMA,
$`0.289`$ for Qwen, $`0.176`$ for Mistral).

### 4.2 Style attacks induce confident error in the upper layers

Averaged over the four adversarial sets, final-layer accuracy collapses
to $`0.56`$–$`0.69`$ of the original value

### 4.3 The damage is driven by unreliable styles

Comparing each article to its four style-matched rewrites isolates the
effect of style. Reliable rewrites (objective, neutral) leave
calibration essentially intact: across all nine cases their
$`\Delta\mathrm{ECE}\in[-0.026,\,+0.100]`$, and on GossipCop they even
slightly raise accuracy ($`\mathrm{AccCR}\approx 1.02`$). Unreliable
rewrites (sensational, emotionally triggering) are damaging:
$`\Delta\mathrm{ECE}\in[+0.216,\,+0.404]`$, with accuracy falling to
$`0.58`$–$`0.83`$ of the original value. The two ranges *do not
overlap*—the worst reliable-style damage ($`+0.10`$) is milder than the
gentlest unreliable-style damage ($`+0.22`$)—so the effect is closer to
a switch than a gradient.

## 5 Discussion

The results cohere into one account. On original texts these LLMs are
competent veracity judges with faithful confidence, achieved through a
late-layer correction. That judgment, however, appears to depend on
stylistic cues, so an emotionally charged rewrite is associated with a
confident, incorrect verdict forming in the upper layers. We emphasize
that the zero-shot setting matters here: an encoder with no task
training never forms a veracity judgment at all, whereas our zero-shot
decoders do, and that judgment is fragile. One important alternative we
cannot yet exclude is that unreliable rewrites are simply more
out-of-distribution and would degrade the model on *any* task, not
veracity specifically; separating general OOD degradation from
veracity-specific fragility requires a same-articles control task (e.g.
topic classification on the identical rewrites) and is the single most
important experiment we leave to future work. Subject to that caveat,
the fragility seems intrinsic to how a capable model judges whether text
“sounds true,” rather than a byproduct of task-specific training.
Practically, an LLM’s confidence on potentially restyled content should
not be used as a decision gate; emotionally styled inputs warrant
additional scrutiny.

## 6 Conclusion

Reading per-layer prediction and confidence from three zero-shot LLMs
across three fake-news benchmarks, we find an original-data
confidence-correction phase that style rewriting suppresses: accuracy
collapses while confidence stays high,under the strongest attack, past
chance into worse-than-random behavior, and the damage is associated
almost entirely with sensational and emotional rewriting. All patterns
hold in all nine model$`\times`$dataset cells, with reliable and
unreliable calibration effects that do not overlap under either
confidence measure. The practical implication is clear: an LLM’s
confidence about veracity is unreliable when an article’s style may have
been manipulated.

## Appendix A Reproducibility details

All models are evaluated in bfloat16. Confidence is read at the final
prompt token; intermediate read-outs apply the model’s final
normalization to the raw block output, except the top read-out, which
uses the already-normalized final hidden state (so it reproduces the
model’s true output logits). We use $`M{=}10`$ calibration bins, a fixed
random seed for the per-article Real/Fake option assignment, and
truncate article bodies (preserving the lead) so the question and answer
slot are never removed. For the article-matched contrast, reframing
files are row-aligned to the original training articles and inherit
their labels; for the test-level contrast, adversarial sets are
row-aligned to the original test set.

#### Confidence intervals.

Accuracy intervals are Wilson score intervals; AccCR intervals use the
Katz log method. Both use the test-set size as the effective $`n`$;
because the four adversarial sets restyle the same articles, we do not
pool them.
````
