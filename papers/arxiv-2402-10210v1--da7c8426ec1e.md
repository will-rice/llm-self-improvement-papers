###### Abstract

Fine-tuning Diffusion Models remains an underexplored frontier in
generative artificial intelligence (GenAI), especially when compared
with the remarkable progress made in fine-tuning Large Language Models
(LLMs). While cutting-edge diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion
(SD) and SDXL rely on supervised fine-tuning, their performance
inevitably plateaus after seeing a certain volume of data. Recently,
reinforcement learning (RL) has been employed to fine-tune diffusion
models with human preference data, but it requires at least two images
(“winner” and “loser” images) for each text prompt. In this paper, we
introduce an innovative technique called self-play fine-tuning for
diffusion models (SPIN-Diffusion), where the diffusion model engages in
competition with its earlier versions, facilitating an iterative
self-improvement process. Our approach offers an alternative to
conventional supervised fine-tuning and RL strategies, significantly
improving both model performance and alignment. Our experiments on the
Pick-a-Pic dataset reveal that SPIN-Diffusion outperforms the existing
supervised fine-tuning method in aspects of human preference alignment
and visual appeal right from its first iteration. By the second
iteration, it exceeds the performance of RLHF-based methods across all
metrics, achieving these results with less data.

## 1 Introduction

Diffusion models ([Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib15); [Peebles and Xie,
2023](#bib.bib24); [Podell et al., 2023](#bib.bib25); [Nichol et al.,
2021](#bib.bib23); [Rombach et al., 2022a](#bib.bib29); [Song et al.,
2020a](#bib.bib39)) have rapidly emerged as critical entities within the
realm of generative AIs ([Creswell et al., 2018](#bib.bib10); [Kingma
and Welling, 2013](#bib.bib17)), demonstrating exceptional capabilities
in generating high-fidelity outputs. Their versatility spans a diverse
area of applications, ranging from image generation ([Rombach et al.,
2022a](#bib.bib29); [Podell et al., 2023](#bib.bib25); [Ramesh et al.,
2022](#bib.bib28)) to more complex tasks like structure-based drug
design ([Corso et al., 2022](#bib.bib9); [Guan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib14)), protein structure prediction ([Watson et al.,
2021](#bib.bib44)), text generation ([Austin et al., 2021](#bib.bib1);
[Zheng et al., 2023](#bib.bib49); [Chen et al., 2023](#bib.bib7)), and
more. Prominent diffusion models in image generation, including
DALL-E ([Ramesh et al., 2022](#bib.bib28)), Stable Diffusion ([Rombach
et al., 2022b](#bib.bib30)), SDXL ([Podell et al., 2023](#bib.bib25)),
and Dreamlike, etc., typically undergo a fine-tuning process following
their initial pre-training phase.

Recently, using Reinforcement Learning (RL) for fine-tuning diffusion
models has received increasing attention.  [Lee et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib19) first studied the alignment of text-image diffusion
models to human preferences using reward-weighted likelihood
maximization with a reward function trained on human preference data.
[Black et al. (2023)](#bib.bib3) formulated the fine-tuning of diffusion
models as a RL problem solved by policy gradient optimization. In a
concurrent work, [Fan et al. (2023)](#bib.bib12) studied a similar
formulation but with a KL regularization. Very recently, [Wallace et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib43) have bypassed the need for training reward functions
by using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) ([Rafailov et al.,
2023](#bib.bib27)) for fine-tuning diffusion models. Similar approach
was proposed in [Yang et al. (2023)](#bib.bib47) as well.

While RL fine-tuning of diffusion methods has been proven effective, its
dependency on human preference data, often necessitating multiple images
per prompt, poses a significant challenge. In many datasets including
the community-sourced ones featuring custom content, it is often the
case to have only one image associated with each prompt. This makes RL
fine-tuning infeasible.

In this paper, drawing inspiration from the recently proposed self-play
fine-tuning (SPIN) technique ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib6)) for large
language models (LLM), we introduce a new supervised fine-tuning (SFT)
method for diffusion models, eliminating the necessity for human
preference data in the fine-tuning process. Central to our method is a
general-sum minimax game, where both the participating players, namely
the main player and the opponent player, are diffusion models. The main
player’s goal is to discern between samples drawn from the target data
distribution and those generated by the opponent player. The opponent
player’s goal is to garner the highest score possible, as assessed by
the main player. A self-play mechanism can be made possible, if and only
if the main player and the opponent player have the same structure, and
therefore the opponent player can be designed to be previous copies of
the main player ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib6)).

When applying the self-play fine-tuning technique ([Chen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib6)) to diffusion models, there are two challenges: (a) an
exponential or even infinite number of possible trajectories can lead to
the same image. The generator in a diffusion model operates through a
sequence of intermediate steps, but the performance of the generator is
only determined by the quality of the image in the last step; and (b)
diffusion models are parameterized by a sequence of score functions,
which are the gradient of the probabilities rather than probabilities in
LLMs. Our algorithm design effectively surmounts these challenges by (a)
designing an objective function that considers all intermediate images
generated during the reverse sampling process; and (b) decomposing and
approximating the probability function step-by-step into products
related to the score function. We also employ the Gaussian
reparameterization technique in DDIM ([Song et al., 2020a](#bib.bib39))
to support the advanced sampling method. All these techniques together
lead to an unbiased objective function that can be effectively
calculated based on intermediate samples. For computational efficiency,
we further propose an approximate objective function, which eliminates
the need for intermediate images used in our model. We call our
algorithm SPIN-Diffusion.

Contributions. Our contributions are summarized below:

- •
  We propose a novel fine-tuning method for diffusion models based on
  the self-play mechanism, called SPIN-Diffusion. The proposed algorithm
  iteratively improves upon a diffusion model until converging to the
  target distribution. Theoretically, we prove that the model obtained
  by SPIN-Diffusion cannot be further improved via standard SFT.
  Moreover, the stationary point of our self-play mechanism is achieved
  when the diffusion model aligns with the target distribution.
- •
  Empirically, we evaluate the performance of SPIN-Diffusion on
  text-to-image generation tasks ([Ramesh et al., 2022](#bib.bib28);
  [Rombach et al., 2022a](#bib.bib29); [Saharia et al.,
  2022a](#bib.bib32)). Our experiments on the Pick-a-Pic
  dataset ([Kirstain et al., 2023](#bib.bib18)), with base model being
  Stable Diffusion-1.5 ([Rombach et al., 2022b](#bib.bib30)),
  demonstrate that SPIN-Diffusion surpasses SFT from the very first
  iteration. Notably, by the second iteration, SPIN-Diffusion
  outperforms Diffusion-DPO ([Wallace et al., 2023](#bib.bib43)) that
  utilizes additional data from ‘loser’ samples. By the third iteration,
  the images produced by SPIN-Diffusion achieve a higher
  PickScore ([Kirstain et al., 2023](#bib.bib18)) than the base model
  SD-1.5 $`79.8\%`$ of the times, and a superior Aesthetic score
  $`88.4\%`$ of the times.

SPIN-Diffusion exhibits a remarkable performance improvement over
current state-of-the-art fine-tuning algorithms, retaining this
advantage even against models trained with more extensive data usage.
This highlights its exceptional efficiency in dataset utilization. It is
beneficial for the general public, particularly those with restricted
access to datasets containing multiple images per prompt.

## 2 Related Work

Diffusion Models. Diffusion-based generative models ([Sohl-Dickstein
et al., 2015](#bib.bib38)) have recently gained prominence, attributed
to their ability to produce high-quality and diverse samples. A popular
diffusion model is denoising diffusion probabilistic modeling (DDPM)
([Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib15)). [Song et al. (2020a)](#bib.bib39)
proposed a denoising diffusion implicit model (DDIM), which extended
DDPM to a non-Markov diffusion process, enabling a deterministic
sampling process and the accelerated generation of high-quality samples.
In addition to DDPM and DDIM, diffusion models have also been studied
with a score-matching probabilistic model using Langevin dynamics ([Song
and Ermon, 2019](#bib.bib40); [Song et al., 2020b](#bib.bib41)).
Diffusion models evolved to encompass guided diffusion models, which are
designed to generate conditional distributions. When the conditioning
input is text and the output is image, these models transform into
text-to-image diffusion models ([Rombach et al., 2022a](#bib.bib29);
[Ramesh et al., 2022](#bib.bib28); [Ho et al., 2022](#bib.bib16);
[Saharia et al., 2022b](#bib.bib33)). They bridge the gap between
textual descriptions and image synthesis, offering exciting
possibilities for content generation. A significant advancement in
text-to-image generation is the introduction of Stable Diffusion (SD)
([Rombach et al., 2022a](#bib.bib29)). SD has expanded the potential of
diffusion models by integrating latent variables into the generation
process. This innovation in latent diffusion models enables the
exploration of latent spaces and improves the diversity of generated
content. Despite the introduction of latent spaces, generating images
with desired content from text prompts remains a significant challenge
([Gal et al., 2022](#bib.bib13); [Ruiz et al., 2023](#bib.bib31)). This
is due to the difficulty in learning the semantic properties of text
prompts with limited high-quality data.

Fine-Tuning Diffusion Models. Efforts to improve diffusion models have
focused on aligning them more closely with human preferences. [Rombach
et al. (2022a)](#bib.bib29) fine-tuned a pre-trained model using the
COCO dataset ([Caesar et al., 2018](#bib.bib5)), demonstrating superior
performance compared to a generative model directly trained on the same
dataset. [Podell et al. (2023)](#bib.bib25) expanded the model size of
Stable Diffusion (SD) to create the SDXL model, which was fine-tuned on
a high-quality but private dataset, leading to a significant improvement
in the aesthetics of the generated images. [Dai et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib11) further demonstrated the effectiveness of
fine-tuning and highlighted the importance of the supervised fine-tuning
(SFT) dataset. In addition to using datasets with high-quality images,
[Betker et al. (2023)](#bib.bib2); [Segalis et al. (2023)](#bib.bib35)
found that SFT on a data set with high text fidelity can also improve
the performance of the diffusion model. The aforementioned methods only
requires a high-quality SFT dataset. Recently, preference datasets have
been studied in finetuing diffusion models ([Lee et al.,
2023](#bib.bib19)). Concurrently, DDPO ([Black et al., 2023](#bib.bib3))
and DPOK ([Fan et al., 2023](#bib.bib12)) proposed to use the preference
dataset to train a reward model and then fine-tune diffusion models
using reinforcement learning. Drawing inspiration from the recent Direct
Preference Optimization (DPO) ([Rafailov et al., 2023](#bib.bib27)),
Diffusion-DPO ([Wallace et al., 2023](#bib.bib43)) and D3PO ([Yang
et al., 2023](#bib.bib47)) used the implicit reward to fine-tune
diffusion models directly on the preference dataset. Furthermore, when a
differentiable reward model is available, [Clark et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib8); [Prabhudesai et al. (2023)](#bib.bib26) applied
reward backpropagation for fine-tuning diffusion models. Our
SPIN-Diffusion is most related to the SFT method, as it only assumes
access to high-quality image-text pairs. However, the high-quality
image-text dataset can be obtained from various sources, including
selecting the winner from a preference dataset or identifying
high-reward image-text pairs through a reward model.

## 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce basic settings for text-to-image
generation by diffusion models and the self-play fine-tuning (SPIN)
method.

### 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model

Denoising diffusion implicit models (DDIM) ([Song et al.,
2020a](#bib.bib39)) is a generalized framework of denoising diffusion
probabilistic models (DDPM) ([Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015](#bib.bib38);
[Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib15)). DDIM enables the fast generation of
high-quality samples and has been widely used in text-to-image diffusion
models such as Stable Diffusion ([Rombach et al., 2022a](#bib.bib29)).
We formulate our method building upon DDIM, which makes it more general.

Forwrd Process. Following [Saharia et al. (2022b)](#bib.bib33), the
problem of text-to-image generation can be formulated as conditional
diffusion models. We use $`\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ to denote
the value of image pixels where $`d`$ is the dimension and use
$`\mathbf{c}`$ to denote the text prompt. Given a prompt $`\mathbf{c}`$,
image $`\mathbf{x}_{0}`$ is drawn from a target data distribution
$`p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$. The diffusion process is
characterized by the following dynamic parameterized by a positive
decreasing sequence $`\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$ with $`\alpha_{0}=1`$,

|     |                                     |                               |                                                   |
| --- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{x}\_{1:T} | \bm{x}_{0}):=q(\mathbf{x}_{T} | \mathbf{x}_{0})\prod_{t=2}^{T}q(\mathbf{x}\_{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{0}),`$ |     | (3.1) |

where $`q(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}|\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{0})`$ represents a
Gaussian distribution
$`\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t},\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathbf{I})`$. Here,
$`\bm{\mu}_{t}`$ is the mean of Gaussian defined as

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                       |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{t}:=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\mathbf{x}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}}.`$ |     |

It can be derived from
([3.1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
that
$`q(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0},(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{I})`$
for all $`t`$ ([Song et al., 2020a](#bib.bib39)). As a generalized
diffusion process of DDPM,
([3.1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
reduces to DDPM ([Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib15)) with a special choice of
$`\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{(1-\alpha_{t-1})/(1-\alpha_{t})}\sqrt{(1-\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{t-1})}`$.

Generative Process. Given the sequence of $`\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$
and $`\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$, examples from the generative model
follows

|     |                                                  |                                                              |                                                                 |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{0:T} | \mathbf{c})=\prod*{t=1}^{T}p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c})\cdot p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{T} | \mathbf{c}),\qquad p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c})=\mathcal{N}\big(\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t),\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathbf{I}\big).`$ |     | (3.2) |

Here $`\bm{\theta}`$ belongs to the parameter space $`\bm{\Theta}`$ and
$`\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ is the estimator
of mean $`\bm{\mu}_{t}`$ that can be reparameterized ([Ho et al.,
2020](#bib.bib15); [Song et al., 2020a](#bib.bib39)) as the combination
of $`\mathbf{x}_{t}`$ and a neural network
$`\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ named score
function. Please see
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
for more details.

Training Objective. The score function
$`\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ is trained
by minimizing the evidence lower bound (ELBO) associated with the
diffusion models in
([3.1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
which is equivalent to minimizing the following denoising score matching
objective function $`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$:

|     |                                                                                                                                                                               |     |       |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{DSM}}(\bm{\theta})=\mathbb{E}\big[\gamma_{t}\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)-\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\big\|_{2}^{2}\big],`$ |     | (3.3) |

where
$`\mathbf{x}_{t}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}`$
and the expectation is computed over the distribution
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0}\sim q_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c}),\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$,
$`t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}`$. In addition,
$`\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$ are pre-specified weights that depends on
the sequences $`\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$ and
$`\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$.

### 3.2 Self-Play Fine-Tuning

Self-Play mechanism, originating from TD-Gammon ([Tesauro et al.,
1995](#bib.bib42)), has achieved great sucesses in various fields,
particularly in strategic games ([Silver et al., 2017b](#bib.bib37);
[Silver et al., 2017a](#bib.bib36)). Central to Self-Play is the idea of
progressively improving a model by competing against its previous
iteration. This approach has recently been adapted to fine-tuning Large
Language Models (LLMs) ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib6)), called
self-play fine-tuning (SPIN). Considering an LLM where $`\mathbf{c}`$ is
the input prompt and $`\mathbf{x}_{0}`$ is the response, the goal of
SPIN is to fine-tune an LLM agent, denoted by
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$, based on an SFT dataset.  [Chen
et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6) assumed access to a main player and an
opponent player at each iteration and takes the following steps
iteratively:

1.  1.  The main player maximizes the expected value gap between the target
        data distribution $`p_{\mathrm{data}}`$ and the opponent player’s
        distribution $`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}`$:
2.  2.  The opponent player generates responses that are indistinguishable
        from $`p_{\mathrm{data}}`$ by the main player.

Instead of alternating optimization, SPIN directly utilizes a
closed-form solution of the opponent player, which results in the
opponent player at iteration $`k+1`$ to copy parameters
$`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}`$, and forming an end-to-end training objective:

|     |                                                                                                              |                                                  |                                                                       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle L*{\texttt{SPIN}}=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell\bigg(\lambda\log\frac{p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0} | \mathbf{c})}{p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0} | \mathbf{c})}-\lambda\log\frac{p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{0}^{\prime} | \mathbf{c})}{p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0}^{\prime} | \mathbf{c})}\bigg)\bigg].`$ |     | (3.4) |

Here the expectation is taken over the distribution
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|\mathbf{c})`$,
$`\ell(\cdot)`$ is a loss function that is both monotonically decreasing
and convex, and $`\lambda>0`$ is a hyperparameter.
Notably, ([3.4](#S3.E4 "In 3.2 Self-Play Fine-Tuning ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
only requires the knowledge of demonstration/SFT data, i.e.,
prompt-response pairs.

## 4 Method

In this section, we are going to present a method for fine-tuning
diffusion models with self-play mechanisam.

Consider a setting where we are training on a high-quality dataset
containing image-text pairs
$`(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x}_{0})\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c})q(\mathbf{c})`$
where $`\mathbf{c}`$ is the text prompt and $`\mathbf{x}_{0}`$ is the
image. Our goal is to fine-tune a pretrained diffusion model, denoted by
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}`$, to align with the distribution
$`p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c})`$. Instead of directly
minimizing the denoising score matching objective function
$`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$ in
([3.3](#S3.E3 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
we adapt SPIN to diffusion models. However, applying SPIN to fine-tuning
diffusion models presents unique challenges. Specifically, the objective
of SPIN
([3.4](#S3.E4 "In 3.2 Self-Play Fine-Tuning ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
necessitates access to the marginal probability
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c})`$. While obtaining
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c})`$ is straightforward in
LLMs, this is not the case with diffusion models. Given the
parameterization of the diffusion model as
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$, computing the marginal
probability $`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c})`$ requires
integration over all potential trajectories
$`\int_{\mathbf{x}_{1:T}}p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})d\mathbf{x}_{1:T}`$,
which is computationally intractable.

In the following, we propose a novel SPIN-Diffusion method with a
decomposed objective function that only requires the estimation of score
function $`\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}`$. This is achieved by employing
the DDIM formulation discussed in Section
[3](#S3 "3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation").
The key technique is self-play mechanism with a focus on the joint
distributions of the entire diffusion process, i.e.,
$`p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})=q(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}|\mathbf{x}_{0})p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c})`$
and $`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$, instead of
marginal distributions.

 Input: $`\{(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{c})\}_{i\in[N]}`$: SFT Dataset,
$`p_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}`$: Diffusion Model with parameter
$`\bm{\theta}_{0}`$, $`K`$: Number of iterations.

 for $`k=0,\ldots,K-1`$ do

  for $`i=1,\ldots N`$ do

   Generate real diffusion trajectories
$`\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\sim q(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}|\mathbf{x}_{0})`$.

   Generate synthetic diffusion trajectories
$`\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$.

  end for

  Update
$`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Theta}}\widehat{L}_{\texttt{SPIN}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$,
which is the empirical version of
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
or
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
.

 end for

 Output: $`\bm{\theta}_{K}`$.

Algorithm 1 Self-Play Diffusion (SPIN-Diffusion)

### 4.1 Differentiating Diffusion Processes

In iteration $`k+1`$, we focus on training a function $`f_{k+1}`$ to
differentiate between the diffusion trajectory $`\mathbf{x}_{0:T}`$
generated by the diffusion model parameterized by
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$, and the diffusion
process $`p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$ from the
data. Specifically, the training of $`f_{k+1}`$ involves minimizing a
generalized Integral Probability Metric (IPM) ([Müller,
1997](#bib.bib22)):

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                   |     |       |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle f_{k+1}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}\mathbb{E}\big[\ell\big(f(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x}_{0:T})-f(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T})\big)\big].`$ |     | (4.1) |

Here, the expectation is taken over the distributions
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$,
and
$`\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$.
$`\mathcal{F}_{k}`$ denotes the class of functions under consideration
and $`\ell(\cdot)`$ is a monotonically decreasing and convex function
that helps stabilize training. The value of $`f`$ reflects the degree of
belief that the diffusion process $`\mathbf{x}_{0:T}`$ given context
$`\mathbf{c}`$ originates from the target diffusion process
$`p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$ rather than the
diffusion model $`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$. We
name $`f`$ the test function.

### 4.2 Deceiving the Test Function

The opponent player wants to maximize the expected value
$`\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p(\cdot|\mathbf{c})}[f_{k+1}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})]`$.
In addition, to prevent excessive deviation of $`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}}`$
from $`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}`$ and stabilize the self-play fine-tuning, we
incorporate a Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization term. Putting these
together gives rise to the following optimization problem:

|     |                                               |                                                                                 |                                                                                                                        |             |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | --- | ------------------------- | ------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{p}`$ | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p(\cdot | \mathbf{c})}[f_{k+1}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x}_{0:T})]-\lambda\mathbb{E}\_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot)}\mathrm{KL}\big(p(\cdot | \mathbf{c}) |     | p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\cdot | \mathbf{c})\big),`$ |     | (4.2) |

where $`\lambda>0`$ is the regularization parameter. Notably,
([4.2](#S4.E2 "In 4.2 Deceiving the Test Function ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
has a closed-form solution $`\widehat{p}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$:

|     |                                              |                                                          |                                                                              |
| --- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\widehat{p}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T} | \mathbf{c})\propto p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T} | \mathbf{c})\exp\big(\lambda^{-1}f*{k+1}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x}*{0:T})\big).`$ |     | (4.3) |

To ensure that $`\widehat{p}`$ lies in the diffusion process space
$`\{p_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})|\bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Theta}\}`$, we
utilize the following test function class ([Chen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib6)):

|     |                                                                                               |                                                             |                   |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}=\bigg\{\lambda\cdot\log\frac{p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{1:T} | \mathbf{c})}{p*{\mathrm{\bm{\theta}*{k}}}(\mathbf{x}\_{1:T} | \mathbf{c})}\bigg | \bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Theta}\bigg\}.`$ |     | (4.4) |

Given the choice of $`\mathcal{F}_{k}`$ in
([4.4](#S4.E4 "In 4.2 Deceiving the Test Function ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
optimizing ([4.1](#S4.E1 "In 4.1 Differentiating Diffusion Processes ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
gives $`f_{k+1}`$ parameterized by $`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}`$ in the
following form:

|     |                                                                                                                    |                                                             |                 |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle f*{k+1}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x}*{0:T})=\lambda\cdot\log\frac{p*{\bm{\theta}*{k+1}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T} | \mathbf{c})}{p*{\mathrm{\bm{\theta}*{k}}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T} | \mathbf{c})}.`$ |     | (4.5) |

Substituting
([4.5](#S4.E5 "In 4.2 Deceiving the Test Function ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
into
([4.3](#S4.E3 "In 4.2 Deceiving the Test Function ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
yields
$`\widehat{p}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})=p_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$.
In other words, $`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}`$ learned from
([4.1](#S4.E1 "In 4.1 Differentiating Diffusion Processes ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
is exactly the diffusion parameter for the ideal choice of opponent.

### 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective

The above two steps provide a training scheme depending on the full
trajectory of $`\mathbf{x}_{0:T}`$. Specifically,
substituting ([4.4](#S4.E4 "In 4.2 Deceiving the Test Function ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
into ([4.1](#S4.E1 "In 4.1 Differentiating Diffusion Processes ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
yields the update rule
$`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Theta}}L_{\texttt{SPIN}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$,
where $`L_{\texttt{SPIN}}`$ is defined as:

|     |                                                                                                                |                                                    |                                                                         |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle L*{\texttt{SPIN}}=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell\bigg(\lambda\log\frac{p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T} | \mathbf{c})}{p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T} | \mathbf{c})}-\lambda\log\frac{p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{0:T}^{\prime} | \mathbf{c})}{p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\mathbf{x}\_{0:T}^{\prime} | \mathbf{c})}\bigg)\bigg].`$ |     | (4.6) |

Here the expectation is taken over the distributions
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$.
To formulate a computationally feasible objective, we decompose
$`\log p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}|\mathbf{c})`$ using the backward
process of diffusion models. Substituting
([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
into ([4.6](#S4.E6 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
we have that

|     |                                                      |                                                                                                                                                                    |                                                                            |                                                                 |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{0:T} | \mathbf{c})`$                                                                                                                                                      | $`\displaystyle=\log\bigg(\prod*{t=1}^{T}p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c})\cdot p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{T} | \mathbf{c})\bigg)`$ |     |     |
|     |                                                      | $`\displaystyle=\log p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{T}                                                                                                                | \mathbf{c})+\sum*{t=1}^{T}\log\big(p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{t-1}       | \mathbf{x}\_{t},\mathbf{c})\big)`$                              |                     |     |
|     |                                                      | $`\displaystyle=\text{Constant}-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}.`$ |                                                                            | (4.7)                                                           |

where the last equality holds since
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}|\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c})`$ is a
Gaussian distribution
$`\mathcal{N}\big(\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t),\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathbf{I}\big)`$
according to
([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
and $`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{T}|\mathbf{c})`$ is approximately a
Gaussian independent of $`\bm{\theta}`$. By substituting
([4.7](#S4.E7 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
into
([4.6](#S4.E6 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and introducing a reparameterization
$`\sigma_{t}^{2}=\lambda T/(2\beta_{t})`$, where $`\beta_{t}`$ is a
fixed positive value, we obtain

|     |                                                                  |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |     |       |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle L_{\texttt{SPIN}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell\bigg(-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\beta_{t}}{T}\big[\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$ |     |       |
|     |                                                                  | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\big]\bigg)\bigg].`$      |     | (4.8) |

Here the expectation is taken over the distributions
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$.
The detailed algorithm is presented in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation").
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
naturally provides an objective function for DDIM with $`\sigma_{t}>0`$,
where $`\sigma_{t}`$ controls the determinism of the reverse process
([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")).
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
remains valid for deterministic generation processes as
$`\sigma_{t}\rightarrow 0`$.

### 4.4 Approximate Training Objective

While
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
is the exact ELBO, optimizing it requires storing all intermediate
images during the reverse sampling, which is not memory-efficient. To
address this limitation, we propose an approximate objective function.
By applying Jensen’s inequality and the convexity of the loss function
$`\ell`$, we can give an upper bound of
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and thus move the average over $`t`$ outside the loss function $`\ell`$:

|     |                                                                                    |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |     |       |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell\Big(-\beta_{t}\Big[\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$                    |     |       |
|     |                                                                                    | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\Big)\bigg],`$ |     | (4.9) |

where the expectation is taken over the distributions
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\mathbf{c}),(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}),(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t})\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{c})`$,
$`t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}`$.

The following lemma shows that $`L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}`$
is an upper bound of $`L_{\texttt{SPIN}}`$.

###### Lemma 4.1.

Fix $`\bm{\theta}_{k}\in\bm{\Theta}`$ which serves as the starting point
of
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
for iteration $`k+1`$. It holds that
$`L_{\texttt{SPIN}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})\leq L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$
for all $`\bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Theta}`$.

$`L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}`$ eliminates the need to store all
intermediate steps, as it only involves two consecutive sampling steps
$`t-1`$ and $`t`$. Since the reverse process
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1:T}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0},\mathbf{c})`$
approximates the forward process
$`q(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1:T}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0})`$, we use the per
step forward process
$`q(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0})`$
to approximate
$`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0},\mathbf{c})`$.
We can further approximate
$`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{c})=\int p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0},\mathbf{c})p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0}|\mathbf{c})d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0}`$
with
$`\int q(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0})p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0}|\mathbf{c})d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0}`$.
Substituting the corresponding terms in
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
with the above approximation allows us to only compute the expectation
of
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
over the distribution $`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\mathbf{c})`$,
$`(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c})`$,
$`(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^{\prime},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t})\sim\int p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0}|\mathbf{c})q(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0})d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0}`$,
$`t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}`$. Furthermore, by incorporating
the reparameterization of $`\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}`$ into
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
we can express
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
in terms of
$`\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$. Detailed
derivations of
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
are provided in Appendix
[B](#A2 "Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation").

## 5 Main Theory

In this section, we provide a theoretical analysis of
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation").
Section [4](#S4 "4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
introduces two distinct objective functions, as defined in
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
both of which use the loss function $`\ell`$. Since
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
is an exact objective function, its analysis closely follows the
framework established by [Chen et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6). Consequently,
we instead focus on the approximate objective function
$`L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}`$ defined
in ([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
which is more efficient to optimize and is the algorithm we use in our
experiments. However, its behavior is more difficult to analyze. We
begin with a formal assumption regarding the loss function $`\ell`$ as
follows.

###### Assumption 5.1.

The loss function $`\ell(t):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}`$ is
monotonically decreasing, i.e., $`\forall t,\ell^{\prime}(t)\leq 0`$ and
satisfies $`\ell^{\prime}(0)<0`$. In addition, $`\ell(t)`$ is a convex
function.

Assumption [5.1](#S5.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 5.1. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
can be satisfied by various commonly used loss functions in machine
learning. This includes the correlation loss $`\ell(t)=1-t`$, the hinge
loss $`\ell(t)=\max(0,1-t)`$, and the logistic loss
$`\ell(t)=\log(1+\exp(-t))`$.

To understand the behavior of SPIN-Diffusion, let us first analyze the
gradient of the objective
function ([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |     |       |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\nabla L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}=\mathbb{E}\Big[\underbrace{(-\beta_{t}\ell^{\prime}_{t})}_{\mathrm{Reweighting}}\cdot\big(\underbrace{\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}}_{\mathrm{Matching}}-\underbrace{\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}}_{\mathrm{Pushing}}\big)\Big],`$ |     | (5.1) |

where the expectation is taken over the distributions
$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\mathbf{c}),(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}),(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t})\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{c})`$. ([5.1](#S5.E1 "In 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
can be divided into three parts:

- •
  Reweighting: $`\ell^{\prime}(\cdot)`$ in the “Reweighting” term is
  negative and increasing because $`\ell()`$ is monotonically decreasing
  and convex according to
  Assumption [5.1](#S5.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 5.1. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation").
  Therefore,
  $`-\beta_{t}\ell^{\prime}_{t}=-\beta_{t}\ell^{\prime}\big(-\beta_{t}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\|_{2}^{2}-\ldots+\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\|_{2}^{2}\big]\big)`$
  is always non-negative. Furthermore, $`-\beta_{t}\ell^{\prime}_{t}`$
  decreases as the argument inside $`\ell()`$ increases.
- •
  Matching: The “Matching” term matches
  $`\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ to
  $`\mathbf{x}_{t-1}`$ coming from pairs
  $`(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})`$, that are sampled from the
  target distribution. This increases the likelihood of
  $`(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})`$
  following the generative process
  ([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")).
- •
  Pushing: Contrary to the “Matching” term, the “Pushing” term pushes
  $`\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\prime},\mathbf{c},t)`$ away
  from $`\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^{\prime}`$ coming from pairs
  $`(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t})`$ drawn from the
  synthetic distribution
  $`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t})`$.
  Therefore, the “Pushing” term decreases the likelihood of these
  samples following the process in the generative
  process ([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")).

The “Matching” term aligns conceptually with the $`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$ in
SFT, as both aim to maximize the likelihood that the target trajectory
$`\mathbf{x}_{0:T}`$ follows the generative process described in
([3.2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")).
The following theorem shows a formal connection, which is pivotal for
understanding the optimization dynamics of our method.

###### Theorem 5.2.

Under
Assumption [5.1](#S5.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 5.1. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"),
if $`\bm{\theta}_{k}`$ is not the global optimum of $`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$
in
([3.3](#S3.E3 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
there exists an appropriately chosen $`\beta_{t}`$, such that
$`\bm{\theta}_{k}`$ is not the global minimum of
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and thus $`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}\not=\bm{\theta}_{k}`$.

Theorem [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
suggests that the optimization process stops only when $`\bm{\theta}`$
reaches global optimality of $`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$. Consequently, the
optimal diffusion model $`\bm{\theta}^{*}`$ found by
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
cannot be further improved using $`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$. This
theoretically supports that SFT with
([3.3](#S3.E3 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
cannot improve over SPIN-Diffusion. It is also worth noting that
Theorem [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
does not assert that every global minimum of $`L_{\mathrm{DSM}}`$ meets
the convergence criterion (i.e., $`\bm{\theta}_{k+1}=\bm{\theta}_{k}`$),
particularly due to the influence of the “Pushing” term in
([5.1](#S5.E1 "In 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")).
The following theorem provides additional insight into the conditions
under which
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
converges.

###### Theorem 5.3.

Under
Assumption [5.1](#S5.Thmtheorem1 "Assumption 5.1. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"),
if
$`p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})=p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})`$,
then $`\bm{\theta}_{k}`$ is the global minimum
of ([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
for any $`\lambda\geq 0`$.

Theorem [5.3](#S5.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 5.3. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
shows that
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
converges when
$`p_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})=p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})`$,
indicating the efficacy of SPIN-Diffusion in aligning with the target
data distribution. In addition, while
Theorems [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
and [5.3](#S5.Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 5.3. ‣ 5 Main Theory ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
are directly applicable to
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
the analogous conclusion can be drawn for
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
as well (see
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Proof of Theorems in Section ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")
for a detailed discussion).

## 6 Experiments

In this section, we conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate the
effectiveness of SPIN-Diffusion. Our results show that SPIN-Diffusion
outperforms other baseline fine-tuning methods including SFT and
Diffusion-DPO.

### 6.1 Experiment Setup

Models, Datasets and Baselines. We use the stable diffusion v1.5
(SD-1.5) ([Rombach et al., 2022a](#bib.bib29)) as our base model. While
adopting the original network structure, we use its Huggingface
pretrained version¹¹ 1
https://huggingface.co/runwayml/stable-diffusion-v1-5, which is trained
on LAION-5B ([Schuhmann et al., 2022](#bib.bib34)) dataset, a text-image
pair dataset containing approximately 5.85 billion CLIP-filtered
image-text pairs. We use the Pick-a-Pic dataset ([Kirstain et al.,
2023](#bib.bib18)) for fine-tuning. Pick-a-Pic is a dataset with pairs
of images generated by Dreamlike²² 2 https://dreamlike.art/ (a
fine-tuned version of SD-1.5) and SDXL-beta ([Podell et al.,
2023](#bib.bib25)), where each pair corresponds to a human preference
label. We also train SD-1.5 with SFT and Diffusion-DPO ([Wallace et al.,
2023](#bib.bib43)) as the baselines. For SFT, we train the model to fit
the winner images in the Pick-a-Pic ([Kirstain et al.,
2023](#bib.bib18)) trainset. In addition to the Diffusion-DPO checkpoint
provided by [Wallace et al. (2023)](#bib.bib43)³³ 3
https://huggingface.co/mhdang/dpo-sd1.5-text2image-v1 (denoted by
Diffusion-DPO), we also fine-tune an SD-1.5 using Diffusion-DPO and
denote it by “Diffusion-DPO (ours)”.

Evaluation. We use the Pick-a-Pic test set, PartiPrompts ([Yu et al.,
2022](#bib.bib48)) and HPSv2 ([Wu et al., 2023](#bib.bib45)) as our
evaluation benchmarks. All of these datasets are collections of prompts

## 7 Conclusion

This paper presents SPIN-Diffusion, an innovative fine-tuning approach
tailored for diffusion models, particularly effective in scenarios where
only a single image is available per text prompt. By employing a
self-play mechanism, SPIN-Diffusion iteratively refines the model’s
performance, converging towards the target data distribution.
Theoretical evidence underpins the superiority of SPIN-Diffusion,
demonstrating that traditional supervised fine-tuning cannot surpass its
stationary point, achievable at the target data distribution. Empirical
evaluations highlight SPIN-Diffusion’s remarkable success in
text-to-image generation tasks, surpassing the state-of-the-art
fine-tuning methods even without the need for additional data. This
underscores SPIN-Diffusion’s potential to revolutionize the practice of
diffusion model fine-tuning, leveraging solely demonstration data to
achieve unprecedented performance levels.

### A.1 Hyperparameters

We train the SPIN-Diffusion on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 80G memory. In
training the SPIN-Diffusion, we use the AdamW optimizer with a weight
decay factor of $`1e-2`$. The images are processed at a
$`512\times 512`$ resolution. The batch size is set to 8 locally,
alongside a gradient accumulation of 32. For the learning rate, we use a
schedule starting with $`200`$ warm-up steps, followed by linear decay.
We set the learning rate at $`2.0e-5`$ for the initial two iterations,
reducing it to $`5.0e-8`$ for the third iteration. The coefficient
$`\beta_{t}`$ is chosen as $`2000`$ for the first iteration, increasing
to $`5000`$ for the subsequent second and third iterations. Training
steps are $`50`$ for the first iteration, $`500`$ for the second, and
$`200`$ for the third. In training the DPO model, we employ the same
AdamW optimizer and maintain a batch size of 8 and a gradient
accumulation of 32. The learning rate is set to $`2.0e-5`$, and
$`\beta_{t}`$ is set to $`2000`$. The total number of training steps for
DPO is $`350`$. In SFT training, we use 4 NVIDIA A6000 GPUs. We use the
AdamW optimizer with a weight decay of $`0.01`$. The local batch size is
set to 32 and the global batch size is set to 512. Our learning rate is
1e-5, with linear warmup for 500 steps with no learning rate decay. We
save checkpoints every 500 steps and evaluate the checkpoints on
Pick-a-Pic validation. We select the best checkpoint, trained after 2000
steps as our SFT checkpoint.

During generation, we use a guidance scale of $`7.5`$, and fixed the
random seed as $`5775709`$.

iteration 1 checkpoint. The results shown in

observe that in terms of PickScore, HPS, and average score, continual
training on iteration 1 even results in a performance decay. Even in
terms of Aesthetic score, continual training cannot guarantee a
consistent improvement. Compared to training for more epochs in
iteration 1, iteration 2 has a much more ideal performance. These
results show the key role in updating the opponent.

## Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion

### B.1 Additional Details of DDIM.

Given a prompt $`\mathbf{c}`$, image $`\mathbf{x}_{0}`$, sequence
$`\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}\subseteq(0,1]`$ and
$`\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}\subseteq[0,+\infty)`$, the forward diffusion
process defined in
([3.1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Text-to-Image Diffusion Model ‣ 3 Problem Setting and Preliminaries ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
is

|     |                                     |                               |
| --- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{x}\_{1:T} | \bm{x}_{0}):=q(\mathbf{x}_{T} | \mathbf{x}_{0})\prod_{t=2}^{T}q(\mathbf{x}\_{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{0}),`$ |     |

where
$`q(\mathbf{x}_{T}|\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}\mathbf{x}_{0},(1-\alpha_{T})\mathbf{I})`$
and $`q(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}|\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{0})`$ admits the
following distribution,

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |     |       |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\mathbf{x}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}},\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathbf{I}\bigg).`$ |     | (B.1) |

Here $`\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$ is a decreasing sequence with
$`\alpha_{0}=1`$ and $`\alpha_{T}`$ approximately zero. By Bayesian
rule, we can show that this diffusion process ensures that
$`q(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0},(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{I})`$
for all $`t`$ and reduces to DDPM ([Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib15)) with a
special choice of
$`\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{(1-\alpha_{t-1})/(1-\alpha_{t})}\sqrt{(1-\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{t-1})}`$.

Given noise schedule $`\alpha_{t}`$ and $`\sigma_{t}`$, examples from
the generative model follows

|     |                                                  |                               |                                                                                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{0:T} | \mathbf{c})`$                 | $`\displaystyle=\prod*{t=1}^{T}p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{t-1}                                                     | \mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c})\cdot p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}\_{T} | \mathbf{c}),`$ |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle p*{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}*{t-1} | \mathbf{x}\_{t},\mathbf{c})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\big(\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t),\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathbf{I}\big).`$ |                                                                 |

Here $`\bm{\theta}`$ belongs to the parameter space $`\bm{\Theta}`$ and
$`\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ is the mean of
the Gaussian that can be parameterized ([Ho et al., 2020](#bib.bib15);
[Song et al., 2020a](#bib.bib39)) as

|     |                                                                      |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |     |       |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\bigg)+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t),`$ |     | (B.2) |

where
$`\{\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\}_{t=1}^{T}`$
are score functions that approximate noise. Compare
([B.2](#A2.E2 "In B.1 Additional Details of DDIM. ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([B.1](#A2.E1 "In B.1 Additional Details of DDIM. ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")),
we can see that
$`\big(\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\big)`$
approximates $`\mathbf{x}_{0}`$, and $`\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}`$
approximates the noise
$`\bm{\epsilon}_{t}:=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$.

### B.2 Decoupling Technique

In
Section [4](#S4 "4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"),
we demonstrate that the objective function defined in
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
can be simplified to the form in
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation")).
This reformulation only requires considering two consecutive sampling
steps, $`t-1`$ and $`t`$, rather than involving all intermediate steps.
Now, we provide a detailed derivation.

###### Proof of Lemma [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma 4.1. ‣ 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation").

|     |     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |                                                                                                                                           |
| --- | --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle L_{\texttt{SPIN}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |                                                                                                                                           |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p\_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | \mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}\_{k}}(\cdot                                                                      | \mathbf{c})}\bigg[\ell\bigg(-\sum*{t=1}^{T}\frac{\beta*{t}}{T}\Big[\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|\_{2}^{2}`$  |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\bigg)\bigg]`$                                                                                              |                                                                                                                                           |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p\_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | \mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}\_{k}}(\cdot                                                                      | \mathbf{c})}\bigg[\frac{1}{T}\sum*{t=1}^{T}\ell\bigg(-\beta*{t}\Big[\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|\_{2}^{2}`$ |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\bigg)\bigg]`$                                                                                              |                                                                                                                                           |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\cdot),\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p\_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | \mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}\_{k}}(\cdot                                                                      | \mathbf{c}),t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}}\bigg[\ell\bigg(-\beta*{t}\Big[\big\|\mathbf{x}*{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|\_{2}^{2}`$                                                                                   |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\bigg)\bigg]`$ |                                                                                                                                           |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{c}\sim q(\mathbf{c}),(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t})\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}_{t}                                                                                                                                                                                                                | \mathbf{c}),(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t})\sim p*{\bm{\theta}*{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t} | \mathbf{c}),t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}.}\bigg[\ell\bigg(-\beta*{t}\Big[\big\|\mathbf{x}*{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|\_{2}^{2}`$                                                                                  |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\bigg)\bigg]`$ |                                                                                                                                           |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle=L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k}),`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |                                                                                                                                           |

where the first inequality is by Jensen’s inequality and the convexity
of the function $`\ell`$, the second equality is by integrating the
average $`\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}`$ into the expectation via
$`t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}`$, and the third inequality holds
because the argument inside the expectation is only depend of sampling
step $`t-1`$ and $`t`$. ∎

### B.3 Objective Function of SPIN-Diffusion

We look deep into the term
$`\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$
and
$`\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$
of
([4.8](#S4.E8 "In 4.3 Decomposed Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
in this section.

When $`\mathbf{x}_{0:T}`$ Follows Forward Process. We have that
$`\mathbf{x}_{0:T}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$ and by
([B.1](#A2.E1 "In B.1 Additional Details of DDIM. ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([B.2](#A2.E2 "In B.1 Additional Details of DDIM. ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
we have that

|     |                                                                      |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |     |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t-1}`$                                    | $`\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\mathbf{x}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}}+\sigma_{t}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}`$                                                            |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\bigg)+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t),`$ |     |

where $`\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$.
Therefore,
$`\big\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$
can be simplified to

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |     |       |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle h_{t}^{2}\bigg\|\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}}-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)+(\sigma_{t}/h_{t})\cdot\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}\bigg\|_{2}^{2},`$ |     | (B.3) |

where
$`h_{t}=\big[\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}/\alpha_{t}}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}\big]`$
and
$`\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$
following a Gaussian distribution. When $`\sigma_{t}\rightarrow 0`$,
([B.3](#A2.E3 "In B.3 Objective Function of SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
becomes
$`h_{t}^{2}\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$
with
$`h_{t}=\big[\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}/\alpha_{t}}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}\big]`$
and
$`\bm{\epsilon}_{t}:=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$.

#### When $`\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}`$ Follows the Backward Process.

We have that
$`\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{0:T}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|\mathbf{c})`$
and

|     |                                                                               |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}`$                                    | $`\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)+\sigma_{t}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{\prime}`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |     |
|     |                                                                               | $`\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\bigg)+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)+\sigma_{t}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{\prime}`$ |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\bigg)+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t),`$                                                       |     |

where $`\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$.
Therefore,
$`\big\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$
can be simplified to

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |     |       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle h_{t}^{2}\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)+(\sigma_{t}/h_{t})\cdot\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{\prime}\big\|_{2}^{2},`$ |     | (B.4) |

where
$`h_{t}=\big[\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}/\alpha_{t}}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}\big]`$.
When $`\sigma_{t}\rightarrow 0`$,
([B.4](#A2.E4 "In When 𝐱^′_{0:𝑇} Follows the Backward Process. ‣ B.3 Objective Function of SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
becomes
$`h_{t}^{2}\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$
with
$`h_{t}=\big[\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}/\alpha_{t}}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}\big]`$.

#### Simple Decoupled SPIN-Diffusion Objective Function.

Substituting
([B.3](#A2.E3 "In B.3 Objective Function of SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and
([B.4](#A2.E4 "In When 𝐱^′_{0:𝑇} Follows the Backward Process. ‣ B.3 Objective Function of SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
into
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
and applying $`\sigma_{t}\rightarrow 0`$ yields,

|     |                                                                                    |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |     |       |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle L_{\texttt{SPIN}}^{\mathrm{approx}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}_{k})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell\bigg(-\beta_{t}h_{t}^{2}\Big[\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}-\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}`$ |     |       |
|     |                                                                                    | $`\displaystyle\qquad-\big\|\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)-\bm{\epsilon}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t},\mathbf{c},t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\bigg)\bigg],`$                                                                 |     | (B.5) |

where
$`h_{t}=\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}-\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}/\alpha_{t}}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}`$,
$`\mathbf{x}_{t}=\alpha_{t}\mathbf{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{t})\bm{\epsilon}_{t}`$,
and the expectation is computed over the
distribution,$`\mathbf{c}\sim q(\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}\sim p_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}|\mathbf{c})`$,
$`\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})`$ and
$`t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}`$.
([B.5](#A2.E5 "In Simple Decoupled SPIN-Diffusion Objective Function. ‣ B.3 Objective Function of SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Appendix B Additional Details for SPIN-Diffusion ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
still need the intermediate steps $`\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\prime}`$, as
discussed below
([4.9](#S4.E9 "In 4.4 Approximate Training Objective ‣ 4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"))
in
Section [4](#S4 "4 Method ‣ Self-Play Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models for Text-to-Image Generation"),
we can approximate the backward process with the forward process and
obtain
