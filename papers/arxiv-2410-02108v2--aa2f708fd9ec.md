###### Abstract

Post-training Large Language Models (LLMs) with explicit reasoning
trajectories can enhance their reasoning abilities. However, acquiring
such high-quality trajectory data typically demands meticulous
supervision from humans or superior models, which can be either
expensive or license-constrained. In this paper, we explore how far an
LLM can improve its reasoning by self-synthesizing reasoning paths as
training data without any additional supervision. Existing
self-synthesizing methods, such as STaR, suffer from poor generalization
to out-of-domain (OOD) reasoning tasks. We hypothesize it is due to that
their self-synthesized reasoning paths are too task-specific, lacking
general task-agnostic reasoning guidance. To address this, we propose
Reasoning Generalist via Self-Improvement (ReGenesis ¹¹ 1 The code is
available at:
[https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/ReGenesis](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/ReGenesis))
, a method to self-synthesize reasoning paths as post-training data by
progressing from abstract to concrete. More specifically,
ReGenesis self-synthesizes reasoning paths by converting general
reasoning guidelines into task-specific ones, generating reasoning
structures, and subsequently transforming these structures into
reasoning paths, without the need for human-designed task-specific
examples used in existing methods. We show that ReGenesis achieves
superior performance on all in-domain and OOD settings tested compared
to existing methods. For six OOD tasks specifically, while previous
methods exhibited an average performance decrease of approximately
$`4.6\%`$ after post training, ReGenesis delivers around $`6.1\%`$
performance improvement. We also conduct in-depth analysis of our
framework and show ReGenesis is effective across various LLMs and design
choices.

## 1 Introduction

Recent research has demonstrated that post-training with explicit
intermediate reasoning trajectories can improve the performance of large
language models (LLMs) ([Yuan et al., 2024](#bib.bib32); [Mukherjee
et al., 2023](#bib.bib21); [Lightman et al., 2023](#bib.bib13)) across a
wide range of complicated reasoning tasks, such as mathematical
reasoning, commonsense reasoning, etc. However, their acquisition of
high-quality reasoning trajectory data in the post-training phase
demands meticulous supervision for each reasoning step, either from
humans or superior models. Therefore, although high-quality explicit
reasoning paths can help post training, they are either expensive and
time-consuming due to the additional substantial human supervision, or
constrained by the license of superior models. It motivates us to
explore: how far an LLM can improve its reasoning by self-generating
reasoning paths as training data, without any additional supervision
beyond final answers?

Existing approaches towards this direction, STaR([Zelikman et al.,
2022](#bib.bib33)) and LMSI([Huang et al., 2023](#bib.bib9)), prompt the
given LLM to generate a chain-of-thought (CoT) ([Wei et al.,
2022](#bib.bib28)) reasoning steps and filter them with final ground
truth labels or self consistency ([Wang et al., 2022](#bib.bib27)). They
also use a small fixed set of human-designed reasoning examples and
incorporate them directly into models’ prompt for in-context learning.
After self-generating reasoning paths for given data sets, these
approaches use these data sets with reasoning paths to further fine-tune
the given model. These self-improvement approaches can improve the
model’s in-domain reasoning capabilities without the need of additional
external supervision. However, through our extensive experiments, we
have found that they struggle to improve the given LLMs on out-of-domain
(OOD) tasks that are different from the fine-tuning data that they
generate reasoning paths and further train the model on. Specifically,
we evaluate them on six OOD tasks including math, logic, common sense,
and natural language inference. Our results indicate that further
post-training using data produced by these methods leads to a $`4.6\%`$
decline on the given LLM’s performance on such OOD tasks. In other
words, existing methods don’t generalize well on OOD tasks and cannot
make given LLMs reasoning generalists.

To fill this gap, we explore how to self-synthesize reasoning paths as
post training data to make LLMs generalize well across various OOD
reasoning tasks. We hypothesize that existing methods fail to generalize
to OOD tasks because their synthesized reasoning paths are overly
tailored to specific training tasks and lack diversity. These methods
predominantly utilize a chain-of-thought approach, which leads to
uniform and narrow reasoning patterns, rather than employing a range of
cognitive strategies that foster diverse thinking steps. To resolve
these shortcomings, we introduce ReGenesis, a reasoning generalist
framework designed to generate general and diverse reasoning paths
guided by various reasoning principles. ReGenesis adopts a
methodological approach that transitions from abstract and general
reasoning guidelines to concrete and specific reasoning solutions, as
shown in Figure
[1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
More specifically, we first create multiple general task-agnostic
reasoning guidelines in the beginning of the reasoning path generation.
Then, given each general reasoning guideline, we prompt the LLM to
augment such general guideline into a task-specific reasoning guideline.
Note that in this step we fill in task-specific details into general
guidelines while still maintaining their task-agnostic reasoning
structure, as shown in Figure
[1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
Then we feed each task-specific reasoning guideline into the given LLM
to generate a reasoning structure without directly solving the tasks.
Each reasoning structure is subsequently used to create one candidate
reasoning path. Finally, all the candidate the reasoning paths are
filtered using ground truth answers and, along with the instructions and
answers, are used to fine-tune the same LLM, enhancing its reasoning
capabilities.

To demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed framework, we conducted
evaluations of ReGenesis across various in-domain and out-of-domain
(OOD) settings. In all scenarios tested, ReGenesis consistently
outperformed existing methods, showcasing its superior performance in
both in-domain and OOD contexts. For in-domain settings, across five
in-domain tasks involving math, logic, and common sense, ReGenesis
outperforms all $`5`$ baselines by $`7.1\%`$ to $`18.9\%`$. Moreover, we
evaluate ReGenesis against baseline methods on $`6`$ OOD tasks, which
include math, logic, common sense, and natural language inference. Our
results indicate that ReGenesis generalize well on OOD reasoning tasks,
achieving a $`6.1\%`$ improvement compared to the model without
fine-tuning. In contrast, existing methods such as STaR, exhibited an
average performance decrease of approximately $`4.6\%`$ on OOD tasks
when compared to the given model’s respective performances prior to
fine-tuning. These findings underscore our method’s effectiveness in not
only enhancing the reasoning abilities of LLMs but also in transforming
these LLMs into reasoning generalists through self-improvement.

## 2 Related Work

Learning from reasoning paths with supervision. Recent research shows
that employing explicit reasoning paths with extensive supervision from
either humans or superior models, can lead to better reasoning
capabilities after post-training a LLM ([Lightman et al.,
2023](#bib.bib13); [Ye et al., 2023](#bib.bib29); [Yuan et al.,
2024](#bib.bib32); [Mukherjee et al., 2023](#bib.bib21); [Achiam et al.,
2023](#bib.bib1); [Gou et al., 2023](#bib.bib7); [Mitra et al.,
2023](#bib.bib20); [Qu et al., 2024](#bib.bib23); [Sun et al.,
2024](#bib.bib24)). For example, [Mukherjee et al. (2023)](#bib.bib21)
and [Mitra et al. (2023)](#bib.bib20) illustrate how smaller models can
benefit from detailed explanations and step-by-step reasoning processes
provided by GPT-4 ([Achiam et al., 2023](#bib.bib1)). TRIPOST ([Yu
et al., 2023](#bib.bib31)) further illustrates this by effectively
training smaller models to learn from mistakes and enhance their
performance on mathematical and reasoning tasks with guidance from a
more powerful language model like Codex ([Chen et al.,
2021](#bib.bib3)). [Yuan et al. (2024)](#bib.bib32) also demonstrates
that incorporating high-quality multi-turn reasoning generated by a
superior model during training can improve the overall capabilities of
LLMs. Moreover, process supervision methods ([Lightman et al.,
2023](#bib.bib13); [Luo et al., 2023](#bib.bib15); [Wang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib26)) uses extensive finer-grained human annotations on
step-by-step reasoning paths as post-training data. These approaches all
rely on more advanced teacher models or human experts for extensive
supervision to obtain high-quality, explicit intermediate reasoning
paths. When more advanced models or human supervision are not available
due to license and cost issues, we are curious to see how well LLMs can
leveraging itself on generating reasoning paths to enhance its own
reasoning capabilities.

Learning from self-synthesized reasoning paths. Contrary to the large
number of methods above which rely on external supervision to create
reasoning paths for post-training, there are limited existing work that
employs self-synthesized reasoning paths from a LLM to improve its own
reasoning capacity. Self-Taught Reasoner (STaR) ([Zelikman et al.,
2022](#bib.bib33)) improves the reasoning capabilities of language
models using self-generated, ground-truth-filtered reasoning paths which
are prompted with a few human-written CoT examples. [Huang et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib9) trains language models with self-generated reasoning
paths filtered by self-consistent answers, also aided by human-written
examples. However, we observed that they fail to improve the given LLMs
on OOD tasks through our experiments while our framework improves model
performance across both in domain and OOD reasoning tasks.

Diverse prompting methods. Recent research has greatly improved the
reasoning capability of LLMs through more effective prompting techniques
([Zhou et al., 2022](#bib.bib36); [Kojima et al., 2022](#bib.bib11);
[Zheng et al., 2023](#bib.bib34)). For example, [Wei et al.
(2022)](#bib.bib28) introduced CoT prompting, where the model generates
a series of intermediate reasoning steps, making it easier to solve
complex, multi-step reasoning tasks. [Wang et al. (2022)](#bib.bib27)
enhanced this approach by sampling multiple different reasoning paths
and then using majority voting to identify the most consistent answers,
referred as Self-Consistency. [Zhou et al. (2024)](#bib.bib37) further
developed these techniques by enabling LLMs to select and combine simple
reasoning modules—like critical thinking and step-by-step analysis—into
a clear reasoning structure during decoding. These prompting methods,
particularly those described by [Zhou et al. (2024)](#bib.bib37), have
inspired us to develop a reasoning generalist that can generate a more
diverse and general self-synthesized reasoning paths for a given task.

## 3 ReGenesis: Reasoning Generalists via Self-Improvement

Existing methods have demonstrated that we can self-improve LLM’s
reasoning performance in in-domain settings by self-synthesizing
reasoning paths, as also verified by our experiments in Section
[4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2 In-Domain Experiments ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
However, when applying existing methods in out-of-domain (OOD) settings,
we have found that they lead to non-neglectable performance drop, shown
in Section
[4.3](#S4.SS3 "4.3 Out-of-domain Experiments ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
We hypothesize that the main reason for previous methods being unable to
generalize to OOD tasks is that their self-synthesized reasoning paths
are too task-specific, lacking general task-agnostic reasoning guidance
and diversity.

Therefore, to verify this hypothesis and to provide a self-improving
method that can help LLMs to generalize well on diverse reasoning tasks
after training LLMs on limited reasoning tasks, we propose ReGenesis.
ReGenesis adopts a strategy to self-synthesize reasoning paths by
progressing from abstract to concrete, from general reasoning structures
to task-specific reasoning paths. Building on this idea, ReGenesis can
automatically generate and integrate reasoning paths into post-training
datasets that originally contain only instructions and ground-truth
answers.
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement")
shows how we create self-synthesized reasoning paths for a task. We
start with a trained LLM $`\mathbf{M}`$ and a training dataset
$`\mathbf{D}^{\rm train}=\{q_{i},a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\mathbf{|D|}}`$, where
$`q_{i}`$ is a text instruction and $`a_{i}`$ is the corresponding
ground-truth solution. All system prompts we used during this process
are shown in
Appendix [A.4](#A1.SS4 "A.4 Details of Reasoning Generalist ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
The goal of ReGenesis is to use $`\mathbf{M}`$ to generate reasoning
paths as post-training data to fine-tune $`\mathbf{M}`$, to further
improve $`\mathbf{M}`$’s performance on both in-domain and OOD reasoning
tasks.

### 3.1 self-synthesized reasoning paths

Guidance Adaption. We first create a set of the seed general reasoning
guidelines
$`\textbf{P}^{\text{raw}}=\{p^{\rm raw}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\mathbf{|P|}}`$ ,
which provide general descriptions on problem-solving strategies which
aim at activating the language model’s reasoning capabilities. These
prompts are designed to be broad and applicable to a wide range of
different tasks. For example, “How can I break down this problem into
smaller, more manageable parts?”. The full list of seed guidelines can
be found in
Appendix [A.4.1](#A1.SS4.SSS1 "A.4.1 Seed Prompts ‣ A.4 Details of Reasoning Generalist ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
For each instruction $`q_{i}`$, we use $`\mathbf{M}`$ to edit and expand
each seed guideline, tailoring it more specifically to the task, thereby
generating an adapted task-specific reasoning guidelines
$`\textbf{P}^{\rm adp}_{i,j}=\{p^{\rm adp}_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^{\mathbf{|P|}}`$.

As demonstrated in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement"),
“Make a list of ideas for solving this problem” in one of the seed
guidelines can be tailored for a salary calculation task to: “Make a
list of ideas for understanding hourly wage and overtime rules, and
systematically apply each idea to accurately calculate wages.” In this
step, for each instruction $`q_{i}`$, we generate an adapted guidance
for every corresponding seed guidance, resulting in a total of
$`\mathbf{|P|}`$ adapted prompts per instruction.

Reasoning Structure Generation. We prompt the same language model
$`\mathbf{M}`$ to develop a set of detailed reasoning structures
$`\textbf{R}^{\rm s}_{i}=\{r^{\rm s}_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^{\mathbf{|P|}}`$ for
each instruction, $`q_{i}`$, utilizing the adapted guidance
$`\textbf{P}^{\rm adp}_{i}`$ accordingly. Specifically, for each adapted
prompt $`p^{\rm adp}_{i,j}`$, the model $`\mathbf{M}`$ generates one
corresponding reasoning structure $`r^{\rm s}_{i,j}`$. Instead of
developing a detailed reasoning path directly, the reasoning structures
convert the adapted prompts into a more detailed framework without
solving the problem.
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement")
illustrates how the adapted prompt for the salary calculation task can
be transformed into a practical reasoning structure, without directly
solving the entire task. For example, “1) Understand the hourly wage and
overtime rules, 2) Determine the number of shifts, …” This reasoning
structure serves as a thinking principle, bridging the gap between the
general seed prompts and the detailed reasoning paths necessary to
complete the task.

Reasoning Paths Generation. Each reasoning structure $`r_{i,j}^{\rm s}`$
is used to generate a potential reasoning path and predicted answer
$`(r^{path}_{i,j},\hat{a}_{i,j})`$ for a given instruction $`q_{i}`$.
The reasoning path addresses instruction $`q_{i}`$ step by step with the
reasoning structure, thereby providing robust data for subsequent
fine-tuning. For example, as shown in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement"),
the reasoning structure for calculating salary is developed into a
detailed reasoning path, “1) Tina’s regular hourly wage is \$18.00 per
hour, 2) Tina works 10 hours every day, …”, with the predicted answer
\$990.00. After this process, we obtain a total of $`|\mathbf{P}|`$
reasoning solutions per instruction. The ablation study is presented in
Section [5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Choices of Design ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

### 3.2 Filtering Reasoning Paths

Filtering with the Ground-truth. Same as previous methods such as
[Zelikman et al. (2022)](#bib.bib33) that generate reasoning paths given
prompts and ground-truth answers of existing data sets, we use the given
ground-truth answers to filter our self-synthesized reasoning paths. Due
to the limitations in the reasoning capabilities of language models,
there is no guarantee that the predicted solution $`\hat{a}_{i,j}`$
using the reasoning path is the same as the given answer $`a_{i}`$.
Therefore, we use $`a_{i}`$ to filter out those reasoning solutions that
are incorrect, assuming that only reasoning paths leading to the correct
answer are valid. Additionally, we explore self-consistency as an
alternative method for selecting valid reasoning paths in
Section [5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2 Filtering Methods ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

directly in the final reasoning path generation process, we only add
hints during “Guidance Adaption” step and “Reasoning Structure
Generation” step. By not having ground-truth answers in the final
reasoning generation stage, we reduce the risk of the final reasoning
solutions producing a correct answer but having an incorrect reasoning

Additionally, incorporating ground truth as a hint significantly

Finally, for each dataset $`\mathbf{D}^{\rm train}`$, we create a new
dataset incorporating reasoning paths, denoted as
$`\mathbf{D}^{\rm train\prime}=\left\{\left(q_{i},r^{\text{path}}_{i,j},a_{i}\right)\right\}_{i=1,j=1}^{|\mathbf{D}|,|<\mathbf{P}|}`$
, where $`r^{\text{path}}_{i,j}`$ represents the correct reasoning path
after filtering. We randomly select up to $`p`$ correct reasoning paths
for each instruction $`q_{i}`$. The language model $`\mathbf{M}`$ is
then fine-tuned using the input $`q_{i}`$ and the target outputs
$`\{r^{\text{path}}_{i,j},a_{i}\}`$.

## 4 Experiments

In this section, we assess the effectiveness of ReGenesis and existing
methods in improving LLM’s reasoning capabilities for both in-domain
tasks and OOD tasks.

### 4.1 Experiment Details

#### 4.1.1 Tasks and Datasets

We selected the following five datasets for training, including
mathematic, logical and common sense reasoning tasks. The training sets
of these datasets are used to generate self-synthetic reasoning paths
and to fine-tune the language models. The test sets are employed to
evaluate the in-domain performance of the fine-tuned models.

- •
  Mathematic reasoning: GSM8K math problem dataset ([Cobbe et al.,
  2021](#bib.bib5)) and NumGLUE dataset ([Mishra et al.,
  2022](#bib.bib19)). In this experiment, we only use the numerical
  answers as the ground truth in the GSM8K dataset, excluding the
  human-provided reasoning paths. This approach enables us to
  independently evaluate the contribution of reasoning to the overall
  performance.
- •
  Logical reasoning: We use logical reasoning dataset ReClor ([Yu
  et al., 2020](#bib.bib30)).
- •
  Commonsense reasoning: AI2 Reasoning Challenge (ARC) ([Clark et al.,
  2018](#bib.bib4)) and StrategyQA ([Geva et al., 2021](#bib.bib6))
  dataset. For ARC, we specifically use only the Challenge subset
  (ARC-c).

#### 4.1.2 Training Details

We conduct our main experiments using the instruction fine-tuned version
of the Mistral-7B-v0.3 ([Jiang et al., 2023](#bib.bib10)) model, known
as Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3. We also evaluate our method on other LLMs
such as Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct ([AI@Meta, 2024](#bib.bib2)) and
present results in Section
[5](#S5 "5 Analysis ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
We first perform self-synthesized reasoning path generation as
illustrated in
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 self-synthesized reasoning paths ‣ 3 ReGenesis: Reasoning Generalists via Self-Improvement ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
Using the Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 with a temperature setting of
$`0.85`$, we first generate $`25`$ diverse reasoning paths for every
instruction within the training set. Subsequently, we employ an exact
match method to filter out incorrect paths by comparing the answer in
the reasoning path with the ground truth. For instructions that fail to
generate any correct reasoning paths in the preceding steps, we
incorporate the ground truth as a hint for reasoning path generation
using the same model and temperature, as detailed in
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Filtering Reasoning Paths ‣ 3 ReGenesis: Reasoning Generalists via Self-Improvement ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
Finally, we randomly select up to $`p=5`$ reasoning paths per question
as target outputs. Therefore final training samples are up to the size
of $`5\times|D^{\rm train}|`$, where $`|D^{\rm train}|`$ denotes the
size of the training set. The final step involves fine-tuning the
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 model using the generated reasoning paths, with
a learning rate of $`1e^{-6}`$ and training for 3 epochs and a batch
size of $`16`$, utilizing an 8-GPU node of $`\text{A}100`$ GPUs, each
with $`40\text{GB}`$ of memory. More details can be found in
Appendix [A.5.4](#A1.SS5.SSS4 "A.5.4 ReGenesis ‣ A.5 Details of Training ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

#### 4.1.3 Baselines

Base model without further fine-tuning (w/o FT). For this baseline we
just report the performance of the base LLM, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3
([Jiang et al., 2023](#bib.bib10)). No further fine-tuning is performed.

Fine-Tuning with Ground Truth Only (FT w/ GT). In this approach, we
fine-tune the base LLM using the original instructions and ground-truth
answers in the training sets, without incorporating any self-synthesized
reasoning paths. More details in
Appendix [A.5.1](#A1.SS5.SSS1 "A.5.1 Baseline: Fine-Tuning with Ground Truth (FT w/ GT) ‣ A.5 Details of Training ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

Fine-Tuning with Self-Improvement approach (LMSI). LMSI ([Huang et al.,
2023](#bib.bib9)) first samples self-synthesized reasoning paths using
few-shot CoT prompting. It then filters these reasoning paths by
selecting the one with the majority vote answer. Finally for this
baseline, we fine-tune the base LLM using these self-generated solutions
as the target outputs. More details in
Appendix [A.5.2](#A1.SS5.SSS2 "A.5.2 Baseline: Fine-Tuning with Self-Improvement approach (LMSI) ‣ A.5 Details of Training ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

Fine-Tuning with Self-Improvement approach and ground truth (LMSI w/
GT). For this baseline, we follow the same reasoning generation process
as LMSI. However, instead of using a majority vote to filter out
incorrect reasoning paths as above, we utilize ground truth answers. The
base LLM is then fine-tuned using these self-generated solutions as the
target outputs.

Fine-Tuning with Self-Taught Reasoner approach (STaR). STaR ([Zelikman
et al., 2022](#bib.bib33)) begins by sampling self-synthesized reasoning
paths directly using the few-shot CoT prompting outlined in their paper.
In contrast, our framework progresses from abstract to concrete
reasoning without relying on CoT examples. Then, it filters these paths
based on ground truth and incorporate the ground truth as hints directly
in the instructions to generate reasoning paths for instructions lacking
correct solutions. Subsequently, fine-tune the base LLM using these
self-synthesized solutions as target outputs. More details in
Appendix [A.5.3](#A1.SS5.SSS3 "A.5.3 Baseline: Fine-Tuning with Self-Taught Reasoner approach (STaR) ‣ A.5 Details of Training ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

#### 4.1.4 Evaluation Methods

At inference, for a fair and accurate comparison, we use exact match
accuracy on all experiments. We have also tested multiple prompting
methods for ReGenesis and each baseline at inference. In the “CoT
Prompting” method ([Wei et al., 2022](#bib.bib28)), we prompt the LM
models with:“Solve the following problem step-by-step. Question: ” +
{question} + “Answer:”, using a temperature setting of $`0.8`$. In
“Self-Consistency” ([Wang et al., 2022](#bib.bib27)), we use the same
prompt but generate 15 responses at a temperature of 0.8, then select
the final answer based on majority voting.

filtering these paths through self-consistency checks. However, the lack

adjustment raised the improvement from $`1.02\%`$ to $`6.26\%`$.

ground-truth-verified reasoning paths but adopts a slightly different
prompting and reasoning path format during post-training compared to
LMSI, leading to a $`9.7\%`$ test set improvement, which remains below
our performance significantly.

ground-truth answers (FT w/ GT) and excluding reasoning paths does not
consistently improve the performance of the base large language model

tasks, relying exclusively on ground-truth answers led to performance
declines of 44.8% and 3.4%, respectively. This observation underscores
the importance of incorporating reasoning paths during the fine-tuning
stage to enhance the LLM’s reasoning capabilities. We also conduct an
analysis to identify the reasons behind the significant performance on
GSM8K in the
Appendix [A.8.1](#A1.SS8.SSS1 "A.8.1 ReGenesis Boosts Performance on GSM8K Through Diverse Answering Strategies ‣ A.8 More Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").

diverse answering strategies, which are perfectly aligned with the
specific needs of GSM8K.

#### 4.3.2 Results

As illustrated in Table 3, ReGenesis surpasses all baseline models in
OOD tasks. Specifically, ReGenesis shows a remarkable 6.1% improvement
compared to the original model without fine-tuning. Conversely, existing
methods display an average performance drop of roughly 4.6% on OOD
tasks. These findings underscore the effectiveness of ReGenesis in
enhancing the general reasoning capabilities of LLMs, enabling them to
evolve into reasoning generalists through self-improvement.

Firstly, as shown in
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.2 In-Domain Experiments ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement"),
fine-tuning the language model solely on ground-truth solutions (FT w/
GT) significantly decrease its performance on all OOD tasks, indicating
that employing reasoning paths for fine-tuning is even more important in
OOD settings. Secondly, other baselines that fine-tune the language
model using CoT prompted task-specific reasoning paths (LMSI w/ GT,
STaR) do not improve their performance across all OOD domains.
Specifically, the performance fluctuations range from a decrease of
$`20.9\%`$ to an increase of $`8.6\%`$ on these OOD tasks. We further
observe that the increase in OOD performance for such baseline methods
almost only happen on OOD tasks closely relevant to its training data.
For example, training on NumGLUE consistently boosts performance on
SVAMP across all models that incorporate self-synthesized reasoning
paths, with an average improvement of $`2.5\%`$. However, when the tasks
are more OOD, performance tends to drop significantly. For example,
training on NumGLUE or GSM8K leads to a consistent performance drop of
approximately $`3.1\%`$ on OpenBookQA and $`4.6\%`$ on BBH when using
the two baselines that incorporate reasoning paths, namely LMSI w/
GT and STaR. In contrast, ReGenesis does not restrict the
self-synthesized reasoning paths with task-specific patterns or
human-designed CoT examples, leading to performance improvement in
almost all OOD scenarios tested.

performance, we compared three combinations: “A+S”, which utilizes only

out-of-domain evaluations when either “Reasoning Structure Generation”
(A+P) or “Guidance Adaptation” (S+P) is removed, demonstrating the

### 5.2 Filtering Methods

In
Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Filtering Reasoning Paths ‣ 3 ReGenesis: Reasoning Generalists via Self-Improvement ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement"),
we utilize the ground truth answers to filter out incorrect reasoning
paths. For datasets lacking ground truth answers, [Huang et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib9) proposed a method that relies on self-consistency in
answers. Specifically, this approach filters self-synthesized reasoning
paths by choosing the answer that matches the majority vote among the
answers from these paths. We also applied this filtering technique to
our framework on the GSM8K ([Cobbe et al., 2021](#bib.bib5)) and
StrategyQA ([Geva et al., 2021](#bib.bib6)) tasks. The results, shown in
Table [5](#S5.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 5.1 Choices of Design ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement"),
demonstrate that our framework remains effective even in the absence of
ground truth answers, leading to a significant improvement in accuracy.
Compared to LMSI ([Huang et al., 2023](#bib.bib9)), our framework
achieves better performance due to the greater diversity in reasoning
paths, which enhances the fine-tuned model’s reasoning capabilities. In
conclusion, our framework is effective even when ground truth answers
are not available.

### 5.4 Diverse Preferences for Reasoning Guidelines

ReGenesis converts $`25`$ general seed reasoning guidelines into $`25`$
different reasoning paths for each instruction within the dataset. We
are curious to see if the effectiveness of these guidelines vary across
different base models. Therefore, for two different models, the
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct models, we apply
ReGenesis to the 25 guidelines for each instruction in the NumGLUE

success rate ($`x_{i}`$) for each guideline across the entire dataset.

perform z-score normalization ($`z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}`$, where $`\mu`$
and $`\sigma`$ are mean and standard deviation of the success rate

### 5.5 Preservation of Task-Agnostic Reasoning in Task-Specific Paths

In this experiment, we demonstrated that although ReGenesis uses the
final detailed and task-specific reasoning paths as training data, the
diverse task-agnostic reasoning strategies that ReGenesis adopts to
generate such final task-specific paths, are effectively preserved in
the final task-specific paths such that it is easy for LLMs, such as
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, to detect them and learn them from the final
task-specific paths. To evaluate this, we randomly select $`500`$
reasoning paths from ReGenesis and STaR’s generated data of NumGLUE
training data set. We then prompt Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 to label each
of the total 1000 reasoning paths the task-agnostic reasoning strategy
behind the task-specific path. Next, we task Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 to
cluster the task-agnostic reasoning strategies generated by itself and
suggest cluster names for both ReGenesis and STaR. For STaR, we obtain a
small number of clusters with limited diversity, such as “Direct
Calculation and Simplification” and “Problem Decomposition and
Step-by-Step Calculation”. In contrast, ReGenesis produces a
significantly more diverse set of clusters, such as “Questioning
Assumptions and Critical Thinking”, “Systematic Thinking”, and
“Reflective Thinking”. More detailed results can be found in
Appendix [A.8.2](#A1.SS8.SSS2 "A.8.2 More details about Preservation of Task-Agnostic Reasoning in Task-Specific Paths ‣ A.8 More Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
Results show that it is quite apparent for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 that
the final task-specific paths generated by ReGenesis, have more diverse
task-agnostic reasoning strategies behind them, compared to STaR. We
have also run the same analysis at OOD inference on OpenBookQA, on
models fine-tuned with ReGenesis and STaR separately on NumGLUE dataset.
Similar results can be found in
Appendix [A.8.2](#A1.SS8.SSS2 "A.8.2 More details about Preservation of Task-Agnostic Reasoning in Task-Specific Paths ‣ A.8 More Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement").
Our findings indicate that models trained with ReGenesis, actually
learned to adopt more diverse task-agnostic reasoning strategies at
inference in OOD settings.

## 6 Conclusion

We have introduced ReGenesis, a novel framework designed to
self-synthesize reasoning paths as post- training data to self-improve
their general reasoning capacities without requiring additional
supervision beyond final answers and human-designed reasoning examples.
This framework effectively self-synthesizes reasoning paths of any
datasets, regardless of whether they include ground-truth answers.
Fine-tuning the language model with such self-synthesized dataset leads
to significant improvements in performance on both in-domain and
out-of-domain tasks. We also investigated how various design choices,
language model selection, and filtering methods, affect the performance
of ReGenesis. In future work, we plan to explore the potential of
leveraging filtered-out reasoning paths to further enhance language
model performance and to develop strategies for intelligently selecting
the most effective reasoning paths for model improvement.

suggest that training models with multiple reasoning paths for a given
instruction enhances their capability to capture and utilize relevant
reasoning pattern, thereby improving overall performance.

We further examined the effect of varying the proportion of training
data on the performance of our methods. As shown in
Figure [4(b)](#A1.F4.sf2 "In Figure 4 ‣ A.3 Hyperparameters of the self-synthesized dataset creation ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ ReGenesis: LLMs can grow into Reasoning generalists via Self-Improvement"),
increasing the proportion of training data from $`25\%`$ to $`100\%`$
resulted in a significant improvement in accuracy for both the GSM8K and
StrategyQA tasks. This finding highlights the critical role of
sufficient and diverse training data in enhancing model performance, as
it allows the models to capture and utilize more relevant reasoning,
leading to better overall performance.

#### A.8.1 ReGenesis  Boosts Performance on GSM8K Through Diverse Answering Strategies

1.  1.  ReGenesis answers questions by systematically breaking down the
        problem into smaller parts: gathering data, calculating potential
        increases, and comparing profits. This approach demonstrates
        critical thinking and analytical reasoning, effectively leading to
        the correct conclusion.
    - •
      Example question: A merchant wants to make a choice of purchase
      between 2 purchase plans: jewelry worth \$5,000 or electronic
      gadgets worth \$8,000. His financial advisor speculates that the
      jewelry market will go up 2.5% while the electronic gadgets market
      will rise 1.2% within the same month. If the merchant is looking
      to maximize profit at the end of this month by making a choice,
      how much profit would this be?
    - •
      STaR answer: Let’s calculate the future value of both options: 1.
      If the merchant chooses jewelry worth \$5,000, the future value
      will be: 5,000 + (0.025 \* 5,000) = \$5,250

      2\. If the merchant chooses electronic gadgets worth \$8,000, the
      future value will be: 8,000 + (0.012 \* 8,000) = \$8,080

      Comparing both values, the merchant should choose electronic
      gadgets worth \$8,000 since it has a higher future value of
      \$8,080 compared to jewelry worth \$5,250. The profit the merchant
      would make is \$8,080 - \$8,000 = \$80.

    - •
      ReGenesis Answer: 1. Gather data: - Current prices: \$5,000
      (jewelry) and \$8,000 (electronic gadgets) - Expected market
      growth rates: 2.5% for jewelry and 1.2% for electronic gadgets 2.
      Calculate the potential increase in value: - For jewelry: 2.5% of
      \$5,000 = \$125 - For electronic gadgets: 1.2% of \$8,000 =
      \$96 3. Calculate the potential profit for each option: - Profit
      for jewelry: \$125 (increased value) - Profit for electronic
      gadgets: \$96 (increased value) 4. Compare the potential
      profits: - The merchant will make a profit of \$125 if they choose
      jewelry, or \$96 if they choose electronic gadgets. 5. Evaluate
      other factors: - The merchant should consider additional factors
      such as personal preferences, budget, and other financial
      obligations when making the final decision. 6. Make a decision: -
      Based on the calculated profits, the merchant will make a higher
      profit by choosing jewelry. However, they should weigh this with
      their personal financial situation and other factors before making
      the final decision. The merchant will make a profit of \$125 if
      they choose jewelry.

#### A.8.2 More details about Preservation of Task-Agnostic Reasoning in Task-Specific Paths

Although ReGenesis uses the final detailed and task-specific reasoning
paths as training data, the diverse task-agnostic reasoning strategies
that Regenesis adopts to generate such final task-specific paths, are
effectively preserved in the final task-specific paths such that it is
easy for LLMs, such as mistral-7B-instruct, to detect them and learn
them from the final task-specific paths. We run the following analysis
to verify this hypothesis.

We randomly select 500 reasoning paths from ReGenesis and STaR’s
generated data of NumGLUE training data set. Note that the 500 reasoning
paths of each method are all final task-specific reasoning paths. Then,
we prompt Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 to label each of the total 1000
reasoning paths the task-agnostic reasoning strategy behind the
task-specific path. The goal of this experiment is to see whether from
the task-specific path only, LLMs can already tell the task-agnostic
strategy behind it and that ReGenesis’s task-specific paths have more
diverse task-agnostic strategies. If so, it indicates that LLMs are
likely to be able to capture the task-agnostic strategies behind the
task-specific paths in the training data, even if they are trained only
with task-specific paths.

Specifically, after Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 labeling the task-agnostic
reasoning strategies, we task Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 to cluster the
task-agnostic reasoning strategies generated by itself and suggest
cluster names for both ReGenesis and STaR. We present the results as
follows. Results show that it is quite apparent for
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 that the final task-specific paths generated by
ReGenesis, have more diverse task-agnostic reasoning strategies behind
them, compared to STaR (More strategy clusters for ReGenesis and less
for STaR). Note that throughout this experiment, we don’t give
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 any knowledge on how ReGenesis generates the
final task-specific paths and what are the general reasoning strategies
used by ReGenesis in the first two data generation steps. On this
condition, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 can still back-engineer and mine the
diverse task-agnostic reasoning paths behind them, suggesting that
diverse task-agnostic reasoning strategies used by ReGenesis are
effectively preserved in the final task-specific reasoning paths, and
that Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 can detect them easily.
