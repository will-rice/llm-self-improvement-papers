###### Abstract.

As interest in using Large Language Models for interactive and
emotionally rich experiences grows, virtual pet companionship emerges as
a novel yet underexplored application. Existing approaches focus on
basic pet role-playing interactions without systematically benchmarking
LLMs for comprehensive companionship. In this paper, we introduce
Pet-Bench, a dedicated benchmark that evaluates LLMs across both
self-interaction and human-interaction dimensions. Unlike prior work,
Pet-Bench emphasizes self-evolution and developmental behaviors
alongside interactive engagement, offering a more realistic reflection
of pet companionship. It features diverse tasks such as intelligent
scheduling, memory-based dialogues, and psychological conversations,
with over 7,500 interaction instances designed to simulate pet
behaviors. Evaluation of 28 LLMs reveals significant performance
variations linked to model size and inherent capabilities, underscoring
the need for specialized optimization in this domain. Pet-Bench serves
as a foundational resource for benchmarking pet-related LLM abilities
and advancing emotionally immersive human-pet interactions. ¹¹ 1 Code
and dataset are available at
[https://github.com/HC-Guo/Act-as-Pet](https://github.com/HC-Guo/Act-as-Pet).

## 1. Introduction

![Refer to caption](2506.03761v3/pet_figures.png)

Figure 1. Overview of Pet-Bench. The framework consists of four key
components: PetChat, PetNote, PetMemory, and PetRoutine.

With the increasing interest in utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs)
for interactive and emotionally enriched experiences ([Wang et al.,
2024a](#bib.bib28); [Shao et al., 2023](#bib.bib32); [Qiu and Lan,
2024](#bib.bib18)), the application of LLMs to pet companionship
scenarios on social network services (SNS) has attracted significant
attention. However, current practices ([Xu et al., 2024b](#bib.bib20);
[Treder et al., 2024](#bib.bib21); [Zhang et al., 2024](#bib.bib23);
[Maharana et al., 2024](#bib.bib24); [Zhong et al., 2024](#bib.bib25))
predominantly rely on simple, role-playing dialogues where LLMs directly
mimic pet behaviors without internal reflection or adaptation ([Wang et
al., 2024b](#bib.bib33); [Xu et al., 2024a](#bib.bib35)). Such modes
result in static interaction patterns, lacking long-term memory,
developmental progression, and adaptive emotional responses crucial to
authentic companion. Thus, this area requires a systematic benchmark to
better assess the advancement of LLM capabilities.

To bridge these gaps, we introduce Pet-Bench, a pioneering benchmark
specifically designed to assess performance of LLMs as E-pet on social
network services (SNS). Unlike prior work confined to momentary
alignment between persona and actions ([Wang et al., 2024a](#bib.bib28);
[Shao et al., 2023](#bib.bib32); [Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib37)),
Pet-Bench emphasizes self-interaction, highlighting the importance of
internal reflection, self-evolution, and adaptive emotional behaviors in
addition to interactive engagement. This approach mirrors authentic
virtual pet companionship, where pets continuously evolve through
interactions, adapt to emotions of users, and gradually develop unique
traits over time.

Pet-Bench features four key tasks: PetChat: Facilitates general
conversations with both pets and humans, including psychological
interactions and dialogues based on the routine of pets. PetNote:
Focuses on tasks related to social media notes, such as information
extraction, intent recognition, retrieval-augmented dialogue. PetMemory:
Handles memory-related tasks such as summarization, rewriting, and
initiating human interactions based on stored experiences. PetRoutine:
Simulates the daily schedule of pets at three levels of detail, with
higher levels providing more granular plans.

Pet-Bench comprises over 7,500 instances designed to simulate lifelike
pet interactions. We conduct experiments on Pet-Bench using more than 20
prominent LLMs, leading to the following main contributions:

- •
  We propose Pet-Bench, the first benchmark designed to evaluate
  LLM-driven pet companionship capabilities across multiple nuanced
  tasks on social network services.
- •
  The comprehensive evaluation is conducted spanning different sizes and
  series. Generally, closed-source models outperform open-source ones.
  Nevertheless, the performance gap between the leading closed-source
  model and the top open-source model is quite small, only about 1%.
- •
  Our findings show that models perform more poorly in tasks that
  involve understanding complex emotions or long memories, compared with
  more straightforward tasks.

## 2. Related Work

##### Role-Playing with LLM

LLMs have exhibited remarkable language understanding and generation
proficiency, catalyzing significant role-playing application
advancements ([Tseng et al., 2024](#bib.bib36); [Chen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib37)). With the continuous evolution of research paths, the
academic community has shifted towards utilizing and optimizing large
language models to accurately reproduce the multidimensional
characteristics of roles. Domain-specific knowledge repositories ([Li et
al., 2023](#bib.bib30); [Chen et al., 2023b](#bib.bib31); [Wang et al.,
2024a](#bib.bib28)), distinctive linguistic expression patterns ([Wang
et al., 2024a](#bib.bib28); [Zhou et al., 2023](#bib.bib29)),
sophisticated cognitive decision frameworks ([Zhao et al.,
2023](#bib.bib34); [Xu et al., 2024a](#bib.bib35)), and nuanced
personality dimensions ([Shao et al., 2023](#bib.bib32); [Wang et al.,
2024b](#bib.bib33)) are all encompassed within these.

##### From Traditional to LLM Pet Companion

The human-pet bond enhances mental well-being by reducing loneliness and
stress ([, 2021](#bib.bib2); [Ellis et al., 2024](#bib.bib1); [Flynn et
al., 2020](#bib.bib3); [Abat-Roy, 2021](#bib.bib4)), yet constraints
like space, allergies, and pet abandonment ([Costa et al.,
2022](#bib.bib5); [Horecka and Neal, 2022](#bib.bib6)) drive demand for
alternatives. While robotic ([Coghlan et al., 2021](#bib.bib7); [Hwang
et al., 2024](#bib.bib9); [Jeong et al., 2023](#bib.bib8)) and virtual
pets ([Zhou et al., 2024](#bib.bib10)) exist, their rule-based designs
([Baudier et al., 2023](#bib.bib11); [Bengani, 2024](#bib.bib12)) lack
emotional depth, and early NLP chatbots struggle with semantic
understanding and connection ([Rasool et al., 2024](#bib.bib13); [Wei et
al., 2024](#bib.bib14)). LLMs enable context-aware, personalized
interactions ([Yu and McGuinness, 2024](#bib.bib15); [Guo et al.,
2024](#bib.bib16); [Stade et al., 2024](#bib.bib17); [Qiu and Lan,
2024](#bib.bib18)), with multimodal integration enhancing realism ([Xie
et al., 2024](#bib.bib19)). However, LLM-based virtual pets ([Xu et al.,
2024b](#bib.bib20); [Treder et al., 2024](#bib.bib21)) still face
persona inconsistency ([Sun et al., 2024](#bib.bib22)), latency,
multimodal challenges, limited episodic memory ([Zhang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib23); [Maharana et al., 2024](#bib.bib24); [Zhong et al.,
2024](#bib.bib25)), and ethical concerns ([Ayyamperumal and Ge,
2024](#bib.bib27); [Jiao et al., 2024](#bib.bib26)). We address these
gaps with a comprehensive evaluation framework supported by an expanded
dataset.

### 3.1. Definition

Pet-Bench is designed around two fundamental interaction modes for
LLM-driven pet companionship: human-interaction and self-interaction.
Human-interaction enables meaningful engagement with users through
dialogue and responsive behaviors, while self-interaction allows the
model to autonomously simulate pet-like behaviors, routines, and
memories. These modes are systematically organized into four core
dimensions: PetChat, PetNote, PetMemory, and PetRoutine, where PetChat
and PetNote focus on interactions with the user, and PetMemory and
PetRoutine pertain to the model’s autonomous internal processes.

$`\bullet`$ PetChat. This capability assesses conversational
interactions between virtual pets and humans, including: 1) General
Dialogue – Basic interactions and conversations initiated by humans. 2)
Psychological Dialogue – Understanding and responding to emotional cues
during interactions. 3) Contextual Routine Dialogue – Engaging in
conversations informed by previous daily routines.

$`\bullet`$ PetNote. Understanding and engaging with pet-related social
media content involves: 4) Information Extraction – Deriving essential
details from social media notes. 5) Note-share Conversation – Engaging
in discussions according to user-shared notes. 6) Intent Recognition –
Determining whether external retrieval is needed based on user intent. 7) Note-based Conversation – Responding to user questions according to
note content. 8) Note-based Greeting – Initiating interactions inspired
by pet-relevant social media notes.

$`\bullet`$ PetMemory. Effective pet companionship involves
memory-driven interactions, including: 9) Memory Summarization –
Summarizing interactions or events. 10) Memory Rewriting –
Reconstructing pet experiences into coherent narratives. 11)
Memory-based Dialogue – Proactively engaging with humans based on
remembered experiences.

$`\bullet`$ PetRoutine. Creating engaging virtual pets necessitates
realistic routine implementation, including: 12) Routine Level I/II/III
Generation – Generating diverse pet routines across three different
complexity levels. 13) Routine-based Greet – Initiating interactions
with humans based on routines. 14) Routine-based Feedback – Generating
responses based on users’ perspectives on a pet’s routines.

### 3.2. Construction

In this section, we outline the process of constructing Pet-Bench, which
comprises three main phases: (1) Data Collection, (2) Filtering, and (3)
Refinement, as shown in Figure
[2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ From Traditional to LLM Pet Companion ‣ 2. Related Work ‣ Pet-Bench: Benchmarking the Abilities of Large Language Models as E-Pets in Social Network Services").

#### 3.2.1. Collection

To evaluate large language models in pet companionship simulation, we
use data generated from detailed simulations reflecting realistic
pet-owner interactions.

We collect over 7,500 interaction dialogues and routines covering
pet-owner conversations, emotional expressions, routine activities,
memory summaries, and intent annotations. To ensure diversity in our
evaluation process, we incorporate multiple dimensions: (1) Interaction
Diversity. Scenarios encompass dialogues, emotional support, routine
care (feeding, exercise, grooming), and memory-based tasks. (2) Pet Type
Diversity. Simulated species include dogs, cats, rabbits, birds, and
reptiles, capturing a range of behavioral and emotional characteristics.
(3) Complexity Levels. Tasks range from basic routines to complex
adaptive behaviors, enabling evaluation across varying difficulty
levels. Based on these considerations, we systematically collect
comprehensive data for further processing.

#### 3.2.2. Filtering

After data collection, the dataset undergoes preliminary filtering to
ensure quality: (1) User identifiers and personal information are
removed to protect privacy; (2) Politically sensitive, inappropriate, or
offensive content is excluded; (3) Irrelevant or low-quality simulation
data is eliminated using task-agnostic rules to maintain relevance and
reliability.

#### 3.2.3. Refinement

To obtain higher-quality and more reliable data, we incorporated
multiple refinement steps, as detailed below: (1) Data Annotation.
During the data annotation phase, every data entry is carefully checked
and refined by human annotators to ensure consistency with our
evaluation goals. (2) Quality Control. To ensure high data quality, we
introduce an additional LLM-based quality evaluation step. Specifically,
all items are scored using GPT-4 ([OpenAI et al., 2024](#bib.bib38)),
and instances identified as low quality are then reviewed manually to
enable efficient filtering and quality control. (3) Expert Review. As a
final validation step, the dataset is rigorously reviewed by 10 expert
annotators, with each entry independently assessed by at least three
reviewers for coherence and domain relevance. Disagreements are resolved
by majority vote.

## 4. Experiments

### 4.1. Evaluation Settings

Experiments are carried out using 128 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, where 28 models,
comprising both open-source and closed-source models, are tested on the
Pet-Bench benchmark, as illustrated in Table
[2](#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.2.3. Refinement ‣ 3.2. Construction ‣ 3. Pet-Bench ‣ Pet-Bench: Benchmarking the Abilities of Large Language Models as E-Pets in Social Network Services").

### 4.2. Evaluation Protocol

For all tasks except PetNote Intent Recognition and PetRoutine Level
I/II/III, The metric is the average of seven metrics:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (1) |     |

````math
S=\frac{1}{7}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{4}\scriptsize{\text{BLEU-}}n+\scriptsize{\text{ROUGE-1}}+\scriptsize{\text{ROUGE-L}}+\scriptsize{\text{S}}\right)
``` |  |

where BLEU-n ([Papineni et al., 2002](#bib.bib40)) and ROUGE ([Lin,
2004](#bib.bib41)) are common metrics for generation evaluation, and
$`S`$ denotes the semantic similarity between the predicted and target
sentences, calculated using BGE ([Chen et al., 2023a](#bib.bib39)).

For the PetNote Intent Recognition task, accuracy is included among the
evaluation metrics, while for PetRoutine Levels I, II, and III, GPT-4o
scores are used as supplementary evaluation measures.

### 4.3. Main Results

#### 4.3.1. Overall Evaluation

The benchmark results highlight key insights into LLM performance on
diverse tasks. Closed-Source Models Excel: Proprietary models
consistently outperform open-source counterparts, benefiting from
superior training resources and optimization techniques. GPT-4o leads
with an average score of 41.20, closely followed by Glm-4-Plus (39.84).
Task Complexity Varies: Tasks like PetChat-General and PetMemory-Summary
show strong results across models. In contrast, tasks like
PetMemory-Rewrite pose significant challenges, with even top models
struggling. Scale Enhances Performance: Among open-source models, larger
architectures like Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct achieve the best average score
of 40.87, underscoring the importance of model size in tackling diverse
Pet-Bench tasks, as shown in Table
[2](#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.2.3. Refinement ‣ 3.2. Construction ‣ 3. Pet-Bench ‣ Pet-Bench: Benchmarking the Abilities of Large Language Models as E-Pets in Social Network Services").

#### 4.3.2. Task-Specific Evaluation

Multi-task evaluation reveals three critical insights about LLM
performance. (1) Specialized Task Dominance. Current models exhibit
strong task specialization but lack cross-scenario generalization.
Phi-4-14B and Deepseek-V3 dominate structured tasks, while
Qwen-72B-Instruct excels in contextual interactions. (2)
Complexity-Driven Performance Gap. Complex interaction tasks in PetChat
achieve low average accuracy, whereas structured tasks like routine
generation maintain high accuracy levels. (3) Stability-Specificity
Trade-off. Deepseek-V3 and Phi-4-14B demonstrate robust stability,
particularly in memory-related tasks. Qwen variants show polarized
performance through instruction tuning, yet limited memory rewriting
improvements. GPT-4o emerges as the most balanced closed-source model.

## 5. Conclusion

We introduce Pet-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating LLMs in pet
companionship across self-interaction and human-interaction. Pet-Bench
emphasizes self-evolution and developmental behaviors, offering a more
comprehensive assessment. With diverse tasks and over 7,500 instances,
our evaluation of 28 LLMs reveals performance gaps, highlighting the
need for further optimization.
````
