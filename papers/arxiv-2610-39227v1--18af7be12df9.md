# XLaw-JP-27B: A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law

Andrew Shin Affiliation: Keio University    X-Law    Inc
Email: [andrew@x-law.jp](mailto:)
Affiliation: [https://x-law.jp/](https://x-law.jp/)

###### Abstract

We present XLaw-JP-27B, an open-weight model specialized for Japanese
law that runs on a single 48 GB GPU under the Apache 2.0 licence. It
adapts Qwen3.8-27B with hybrid retrieval over all current statutes and
31k case summaries, a light QLoRA fine-tune on its own verified answers
to past bar-exam questions (self-distillation), a contract-specific LoRA
adapter, and four-sample voting. We evaluate it on eight Japanese legal
tasks—the multiple-choice section of the bar examination in 2024, 2025
and 2026, statement-level bar questions (JBE-QA), statute questions
(LawQA-JP), the patent attorney exam, and contract review and drafting
built from government model contracts—against open models of 12–33B
under identical prompts and retrieval. XLaw-JP-27B ranks first on six
tasks, ties on one and is third on one (mean rank 1.3 against 2.8–3.1
for the baselines). It is the only system that passes the bar exam in
all three years (129, 120 and 130 of 175, meeting every subject minimum;
no test-year material was used in training), while open Japanese 32–33B
models fail at least one year. A controlled study of each component
shows that retrieval, voting and self-distillation help, and that
thinking mode, retrieval-grounded synthetic tuning, self-verification,
per-statement decomposition and more self-distillation do not. The
largest remaining gaps to frontier models are in contract review and
patent law. The model is available as a free web service at
[https://x-law.jp/](https://x-law.jp/) with the weights available at
[https://huggingface.co/X-LawJapan/xlaw-jp-27b](https://huggingface.co/X-LawJapan/xlaw-jp-27b)
under Apache 2.0.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models now pass professional legal examinations in
several jurisdictions, but most strong Japanese results come from closed
models or closed retrieval products whose components are not disclosed
([Legalscape, 2025](#bib.bib5); [innovatopia, 2025](#bib.bib6)). Law
firms, courts, companies and public bodies that cannot send client
documents to an external API need something else: a model that runs on
hardware they control, under a licence that permits commercial use and
modification, and that is good at the range of things Japanese legal
work involves—not one exam, but statutes, case law, patents and
contracts.

We build such a model and evaluate it broadly. XLaw-JP-27B is a
27B-parameter model (Qwen3.8-27B, Apache 2.0) specialized for Japanese
law through retrieval over the current text of every statute and 31k
court-decision summaries, a light fine-tune on its own verified
reasoning, a contract adapter, and voting
(§[4](#S4 "4 Method ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
We compare it with the strongest open _lightweight_ models we could run
on the same hardware—Gemma 4 12B, LLM-jp-4.1 33B and Qwen3-Swallow 32B,
the latter two Japanese-specialized—on eight tasks, with identical
prompts and retrieval for every model
(§[5](#S5 "5 Japanese legal tasks at a glance ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
Because one 77-question exam paper moves by up to 16 points between
random seeds of the same system
(§[3](#S3.SS0.SSS0.Px4 "Variance. ‣ 3 Setup ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")),
we report every seed and paired bootstrap intervals, and we build the
recipe one component at a time on two clean exam years.

#### Contributions.

- •
  A lightweight open model for Japanese law. Among the open models of
  12–33B we evaluated, XLaw-JP-27B is best overall across eight tasks
  (first on six, tied on one, third on one) and the only one to pass the
  bar exam’s multiple-choice section in 2024, 2025 and 2026 (129, 120,
  130;
  §[5](#S5 "5 Japanese legal tasks at a glance ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"),
  §[6](#S6 "6 The bar exam in depth ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
- •
  A Japanese legal evaluation suite covering the bar exam,
  statement-level and statute questions, the patent attorney exam
  (Benrishi-MC) and two new tasks, OI-Contract Review and OI-Contract
  Draft, built from the Japan Patent Office’s model contracts with
  checklist grading
  (§[3](#S3 "3 Setup ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"),
  §[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Contract review and drafting ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
- •
  What works at this scale. Retrieval contributes most (+17 to +25
  points per exam), voting 10–15 and self-distillation 3–12; on bar
  statements retrieval already closes $`\sim`$87% of the gap to gold
  articles, so the remaining errors are reasoning errors
  (§[7](#S7.SS0.SSS0.Px3 "Oracle retrieval. ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
- •
  What does not. Five negative results, each with a mechanism: thinking
  mode, retrieval-grounded synthetic SFT, self-verification,
  per-statement decomposition and scaled self-distillation
  (§[8](#S8 "8 What did not help ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).

## 2 Related Work

#### LLMs on the Japanese bar exam.

[チェ et al. (2023)](#bib.bib4) evaluated GPT-3/4 on five years of the
短答式 (30–40% accuracy). In our prior work, [Shin (2026)](#bib.bib3)
fine-tuned GPT-4.1 on the original exam format with self-verification
and reached 94.7/175 on 2024 (pass mark 93). Legal retrieval products
report 160 on 2024 ([Legalscape, 2025](#bib.bib5)) and 169 on 2025
([innovatopia, 2025](#bib.bib6)), with undisclosed models and
self-reported scoring. We are not aware of a published open-weight
result on the full section, nor of any LLM result on the 2026 exam.

#### Japanese legal benchmarks.

JBE-QA ([Cao et al., 2026](#bib.bib1)) turns 2015–2024 bar-exam
statements into 3,464 true/false items and evaluates 26 models
closed-book; the best open model, Swallow-3.3-70B, reaches 0.731 F1.
LawQA-JP ([植松 and 大杉, 2026](#bib.bib2)) has 140 four-choice statute
questions with gold articles. The COLIEE statute task ([Nguyen et al.,
2025](#bib.bib7)) uses Civil Code statements with gold articles; we use
its data for evaluation only. LegalRikai ([Fujita et al.,
2026](#bib.bib8)) grades contract revision by experts but evaluates
closed models only.

#### Domain adaptation for law.

Legal LLMs are commonly built by continued pre-training or instruction
tuning on legal text. Our negative result with 15k retrieval-grounded
synthetic examples
(§[8](#S8 "8 What did not help ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"))
shows that such tuning can erode a strong instruction model’s reasoning
style; we instead keep the base model’s own reasoning and only reinforce
its verified answers.

#### Techniques.

We build on hybrid lexical–dense retrieval ([Robertson and Zaragoza,
2009](#bib.bib11); [Tsukagoshi and Sasano, 2024](#bib.bib10)),
self-consistency ([Wang et al., 2023](#bib.bib12)), learning from one’s
own correct rationales ([Zelikman et al., 2022](#bib.bib13)), and QLoRA
([Dettmers et al., 2023](#bib.bib14)). Japanese open models include
LLM-jp ([LLM-jp, 2024](#bib.bib16)) and Swallow ([Fujii et al.,
2024](#bib.bib17)).

## 3 Setup

#### Base model.

Qwen3.8-27B ([Qwen Team, 2026](#bib.bib9)) (Apache 2.0), run in FP8 with
vLLM ([Kwon et al., 2023](#bib.bib15)) on one 48 GB NVIDIA L40S.
Thinking mode is off and we use the model card’s non-thinking sampling
(temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, presence penalty 1.5) with fixed
seeds; greedy decoding fell into repetition loops on a third of
truncated answers. Exam answers get an 8,192-token budget: at 4,096, a
quarter of answers were cut off mid-reasoning and scored zero.

#### Bar exam (短答式).

The 2019–2024 papers (憲法, 民法, 刑法; 77 questions, 175 points per
year), the 2025 paper and the 2026 paper, scored with the Ministry of
Justice’s rules, including partial credit for multi-part answers and the
2026 順不同 questions. A run passes if it reaches the year’s pass mark
(93 in 2024, 81 in 2025, 99 in 2026) _and_ 40% in every subject. 2024,
2025 and 2026 are test years: no training data comes from them. On the
2026 exam human takers averaged 118.1 and successful candidates 126.2.

#### Other benchmarks.

JBE-QA (3,464 items; we report MCC, which is not inflated by a “true”
bias, and F1 for comparison with [Cao et al., 2026](#bib.bib1));
LawQA-JP (140); the 弁理士 (patent attorney) multiple-choice exam
2024–2025 (120 questions, pass $`\approx`$65%); 判例抽出, generating the
holdings summary and cited articles of 93 2022–24 decisions (ROUGE-L,
set F1); OI-Contract Review and OI-Contract Draft
(§[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Contract review and drafting ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"));
and JCommonsenseQA, JSQuAD and JEMHopQA to detect regressions in general
Japanese.

#### Variance.

One exam paper is small: across four seeds the same system spans 10–16
points. We therefore report all four seeds, their mean, the vote over
them, and 95% paired bootstrap intervals over questions (10,000
resamples) for every comparison we draw a conclusion from.

#### Contamination check.

The 2019–2024 papers and their answer keys have been public for years,
the 2026 paper since mid-2026; the base model’s pretraining data is not
disclosed. If memorisation drove the scores, older papers should score
higher. They do not
(Table [2](#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Contamination check. ‣ 3 Setup ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")):
closed-book, the untuned model scores 69–94 on 2019–2023 (mean 83.4) and
88 on 2026; with retrieval 104–131 and 104. Year-to-year differences are
of the same size as seed noise. This does not prove the absence of
contamination, but it shows no advantage on the oldest material.

## 4 Method

#### Retrieval (rag5).

The corpus holds the current text of 8,141 laws (392,422 articles, from
e-Gov) and 30,884 court-decision summaries. Candidates from BM25 and
Ruri-v3-310m dense retrieval ([Tsukagoshi and Sasano, 2024](#bib.bib10))
are pooled and reranked by ruri-v3-reranker-310m, restricted to the laws
of the question’s subject. On top of this hybrid retriever (rag4), rag5
(i) looks up every article named in the question directly, (ii)
retrieves for each lettered statement (ア．イ．…) separately as well as
for the whole question, and (iii) allows up to 10 articles and 5 case
summaries (5,500 characters). Against a silver set of articles cited in
fully correct answers, statute recall rises from 0.395 (rag4) to 0.502
(rag5); on COLIEE statements with gold articles, hybrid retrieval has
hit@3 0.890. Named-article lookup matters because exam statements often
cite an article by number (e.g. “民法第94条第2項”); the per-statement
queries matter because the five statements of one question typically
concern different articles (e.g. 事務管理: 697, 702 and 703 in the
example of
Figure [3](#S7.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 7.1 Contract review and drafting ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
For contract review the same retriever is restricted to 20
contract-relevant laws (Civil Code, the Subcontract Act and its 2026
successor, the Freelance Act, Antimonopoly Act, consumer-contract, IP
and personal-information statutes, conflict of laws and civil
procedure).
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Retrieval (rag5). ‣ 4 Method ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")
compares the two retrievers with everything else fixed.

| Statutes in context per question        | 3.0              | 4.9               |
| 2026: mean of 4 runs / worst / vote@4   | 103.8 / 94 / 126 | 108.3 / 104 / 118 |

#### Self-distillation (E8).

We sample answers from the untuned model on the 2019–2023 papers (with
rag5) and keep 150 whose final answer matches the official key exactly.
These are mixed with 91 contract reviews
(§[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Contract review and drafting ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"))
and used for QLoRA ([Dettmers et al., 2023](#bib.bib14)) (rank 32, 2
epochs, 29 steps of 16 examples, learning rate $`10^{-4}`$, max length
8,192), then merged into the base weights. Training never sees 2024,
2025 or 2026. We call this model E8 in the ablations; the released
XLaw-JP-27B is E8 plus the contract adapter of
§[7.1](#S7.SS1 "7.1 Contract review and drafting ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"),
which is applied only to contract requests (exam numbers are E8’s). In
one rag5 run (seed 0) the untuned model answers 238 of the 383 questions
of 2019–2023 exactly right; E8 uses a random 150 of these answers
verbatim (E10 pools further runs: 327 questions with rag5 and 215
closed-book), with the exam prompt and retrieved context they were
produced with, so that training input matches test input exactly.
Keeping the model’s own long, statement-by-statement reasoning (median
$`\approx`$2,300 tokens) is what distinguishes this data from the
retrieval-grounded SFT set of
§[8](#S8 "8 What did not help ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law"),
whose short answers taught the model to stop reasoning. The 91 contract
reviews were written by the same model with the official commentary as a
private reference; a first version leaked that reference in 229 of 300
answers (「解説にあるように」) and was discarded, the second filters and
rewrites such answers.

#### Voting.

Four samples with different seeds; the most frequent parsed answer wins
(ties: lowest seed; order-free answers vote on the sorted digits).

builds the system up one component at a time. Decoding matters first:

an 8k budget lifts closed-book scores by 19–23 points. Retrieval adds
the most (+17 to +25 with rag4); rag5 makes single runs pass reliably

Self-distillation adds a further 3–8 points per run and voting 10–15.

#### Is the fine-tune real?

Paired by seed, E8 beats the untuned model in 7 of 8 exam runs (one
tie). With voting on both sides the gain is +12 on 2026 (95% CI
$`[-1,+26]`$, one-sided $`p=0.047`$) and +6 on 2024 ($`[-8,+22]`$); on
the 3,460 JBE-QA items it is significant: MCC +0.028
$`[+0.012,+0.045]`$, $`p=0.001`$
(Table [6](#S7.T6 "Table 6 ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
The 150 training answers come from earlier papers, and nothing else in
the training mix concerns the exam, so we attribute the gain to
self-distillation; we test the dose in
§[8](#S8 "8 What did not help ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law").

#### Comparison with prior work.

129 on 2024 is 34 points above the fine-tuned GPT-4.1 of [Shin
(2026)](#bib.bib3) (94.7) and above o3 and Gemini 2.5 Pro as reported by
[Legalscape (2025)](#bib.bib5) ($`\approx`$115–118, retrieval not
disclosed). Closed retrieval products remain far ahead (160–169).

#### A third test year.

On the 2025 paper (pass mark 81; human candidates averaged 102.1 and
successful ones 110.6 according to prep-school reports), the final
system scores 120 with vote@4; with the same retrieval, one run each of
Gemma 4 12B, LLM-jp-4.1 33B and Qwen3-Swallow 32B scores 87, 88 and 85
(Swallow misses a subject minimum and leaves 14% of answers unparsed);
the closed product of [innovatopia (2025)](#bib.bib6) reports 169.
Single runs score 98–113, and two of the four miss the
constitutional-law minimum (18 and 19 of 50) despite totals far above
81: subject minimums, not totals, are the binding constraint for single
runs, and voting removes the risk (26/50).

Figure 1: Four single runs (dots) and their vote (stars) per system.
Seed spread is 10–16 points; voting adds 3–22 points over the mean run,
least for systems whose runs already agree (E17).

#### Where the points come from.

Pooled over both test years
(Figure [2](#S6.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Where the points come from. ‣ 6 The bar exam in depth ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")),
the final system scores 87% of the civil-law points and 80% of criminal
law but only 49% of constitutional law—no better than the untuned model
and close to the 40% subject minimum (22/50 on 2026). Constitutional
questions turn on case-law reasoning about rights and standards of
review, which the statute-heavy retrieval supports least; most models we
tested are weakest there. By question format, five-way “choose the
correct pair” questions are answered best (0.86 of points) and
true/false combination tables (one mark per statement) worst (0.33): a
table option is right only if all four or five judgements are right, so
one error in five statements costs the whole question. Self-distillation
helps most on pairs (+0.08) and tables (+0.17) and not on per-statement
digit answers, where the untuned model is already as good.

systems with rag5; baselines one run).

#### Open baselines.

With the same retrieval, the two Japanese 32–33B thinking models fall
short of the pass mark on 2026 (92 and 82) although they lead on single
true/false statements (JBE-QA, +0.06–0.12 MCC on a shared 200-item
sample). Long reasoning helps a single statement but hurts
five-statement questions: Swallow produced no parseable answer for 12%
of exam questions, mirroring our own thinking ablation
(§[8](#S8 "8 What did not help ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")).
Gemma 4 12B with rag5 is a strong cheap baseline: one greedy run passes
2026 (101) and ties us on LawQA-JP.

#### The patent attorney exam.

Benrishi-MC is the hardest benchmark in our suite. With vote@4 the final
system answers 62 of 120 questions (pass mark 78), ahead of the
baselines (42–46), but the subject profile is uneven: copyright and
unfair competition 16/20, designs 14/20, trademarks 11/20, treaties
9/20, and patents and utility models only 12/40—barely above the 20%
expected from guessing among five options. Patent questions hinge on
procedural details (deadlines, divisional and converted applications,
priority claims) spread over the Patent Act, its enforcement orders and
the PCT implementation law; we suspect that retrieval reaches the right
statute more often than the right provision (we have no gold articles to
check this), and the treaty texts are not in the corpus.

Table [6](#S7.T6 "Table 6 ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")
shows that the fine-tune transfers to statement-level bar questions
(JBE-QA) and does not hurt general Japanese reading comprehension or the
judgement summarisation task. It does not close the large gap on
intellectual-property law: the patent attorney exam remains far below
its pass mark even with retrieval over the IP statutes (the corpus lacks
the treaty texts that make up a sixth of the exam).

#### Oracle retrieval.

On COLIEE Civil Code statements (511, 2019–23; evaluation only),
closed-book MCC is 0.314, rag5 0.598 and gold articles 0.641 (accuracy
0.648 / 0.795 / 0.806): retrieval closes $`\sim`$87% of the gap, and
even with the right article the model misjudges about one statement in
five. On LawQA-JP’s specialized statutes rag4 retrieval closes only
$`\sim`$53% of the gap (accuracy 0.580 / 0.770 / 0.936). The bar exam is
therefore limited by legal reasoning, which is why training on the
model’s own correct reasoning helps there.

### 7.1 Contract review and drafting

We built two tasks from the twelve OI model contracts of the Japan
Patent Office and METI (OIモデル契約書; NDA, PoC, joint research,
licence, AI and data contracts; public data licence). _OI-Contract
Review_: given a scenario and one clause presented by the counterparty,
list risks for the client, negotiation points and a revised clause;
graded by Claude Opus 5.5 against 3–6 checklist points written from the
official commentary. _OI-Contract Draft_: write the whole contract from
a term sheet; each term-sheet row is checked for implementation and
consistency. Contracts are split in halves by document (93 review items,
6 drafts held out).

_Construction._ Each review item is one clause as it appears in the
model contract, one of the official alternative wordings
(【オプション】), or a variant proposed in the official commentary
(【変更オプション条項】)—so every clause and every gold point was
written by the lawyer-supervised drafting committee, not by us. Claude
Opus 5.5 reduced each clause’s commentary to 3–6 atomic, checkable
points under three constraints (use only what the commentary says, no
paraphrases of the clause or generic advice, keep the parties’ roles
straight); items whose commentary yielded fewer than two reviewable
points were dropped, leaving 189 items, and we spot-checked the result.
The same model judges answers point by point. Calibration: Opus itself,
answering, covers 0.478 of the points on all items; any system that
reproduced the commentary would score 1.0, so the scale has room above
frontier models.

the model covers about half of what Opus covers and is weakest on

training targets whose correctness does not rest on the teacher, we
planted one of 26 one-sided problems (uncapped liability for one side,
change-of-control termination, assignment of all IP, payment terms
beyond 60 days, unilateral price cuts, …; each with three required
review points and the governing statutes) into 322 sound clauses from
government model contracts (MAFF, METI, MLIT) and the training half of

reviewed the altered clause with retrieved statutes and a private note

consequences (M&A, listing review, IP strategy). A second, larger round

## 8 What did not help

| Technique                     | Measured on              | without       | with      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------- | --------- |
| Thinking mode (16k)           | 2026 + rag4, one run     | 88            | 58        |
|                               | JBE-QA + rag4 (400), MCC | 0.606         | 0.710     |
| Retrieval-grounded SFT (15k)  | 2026 + rag4, one run     | 105           | 70        |
| Self-verification             | vote@4, 2026 / 2024      | 126 / 108     | 126 / 102 |
| Statement decomposition       | E8, 2026 / 2024          | 130 / 129^(∗) | 116 / 120 |
| 6$`\times`$ self-distillation | vote@4, 2026 / 2024      | 130 / 129     | 123 / 123 |
| Contract SFT, full model      | vote@4, 2026 / 2024      | 130 / 129     | 118 / 118 |

Table 8: Negative results. Points /175 unless noted. ^(∗)vote@4;
decomposition uses five samples per statement, comparable compute.

#### Thinking mode.

With a 16k budget, thinking raises JBE-QA+retrieval MCC from 0.606 to
0.710 on a 400-item subset, but loses on exams (2026 + rag4: 58 vs 88):
it spends 14–15k tokens per multi-statement question and 27% of answers
are cut off, at 5–10$`\times`$ the cost.

#### Retrieval-grounded SFT.

15,000 synthetic examples (statute true/false, statute QA, consultation,
answer format) from the same model as teacher made every metric worse
(2026 + rag4: 70 vs 105). The short training answers (median 155
characters) taught the model to stop reasoning: exam answers shrank from
$`\sim`$2,300 to 260–350 tokens.

#### Citation-checked self-verification.

A second turn shows the e-Gov text of every article the draft cites and
asks for a re-check. Of 1,232 answers, 173 changed: 38
wrong$`\to`$right, 44 right$`\to`$wrong; vote@4 is unchanged on 2026 and
6 points worse on 2024. With mostly valid citations (91–96%),
verification mainly induces second-guessing, unlike the GPT-4.1 setting
of [Shin (2026)](#bib.bib3).

#### Per-statement decomposition.

Judging each statement alone (five samples, own retrieval) and
assembling the answer in code covers 70–76% of the points and reproduces
every official key from perfect verdicts. It scores like a single run
(E8: 116 and 120) and 3–14 points below whole-question voting at similar
compute: the model uses constraints between statements that
decomposition hides.

#### More self-distillation.

Six times more self-distilled answers (877; closed-book and rag5) did
not beat E8 (vote@4 123/123 vs 130/129), although it gave the best
constitutional-law scores. The gain from self-distillation saturates
early at this scale.

## 9 Cost

All runs use one NVIDIA L40S (48 GB; AWS g6e.xlarge, US\$1.86/hour on
demand). An exam answer with rag5 averages about 2,500 output tokens;
one 77-question paper takes $`\approx`$21 minutes, so the four runs of a
vote cost $`\approx`$US\$2.6 per paper, or about 3.5 cents per question.
The thinking baselines generate 2,200 (LLM-jp) to 6,700 (Swallow) tokens
per exam question and leave 12% of Swallow’s answers unparsed at a 12k
budget. Fine-tuning E8 takes about one GPU-hour and the whole study,
including every negative result, about US\$300 of GPU time plus
$`\approx`$US\$70 of judge and reference API calls. A single-GPU
deployment serving one model around the clock costs about US\$1,360 per
month on demand.

## 10 Release and deployment

The weights of XLaw-JP-27B (the merged E8 model and the contract
adapter, with checksums of every file, the base-model revision and the
training configurations) are publicly released under Apache 2.0. The
same weights serve a free public beta, _X-Law JP Agent_, at
[https://x-law.jp/](https://x-law.jp/). One vLLM process on a single
L40S hosts the merged model and applies the contract adapter per
request; a gateway performs the retrieval of
§[4](#S4 "4 Method ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")
for every query (statutes and case summaries; the contract statute set
for contract requests), streams answers, and offers a thinking-mode
toggle. Beyond chat, the interface provides clause-by-clause contract
review with Word and Excel reports, contract drafting from a term sheet,
and case and statute search. Uploaded Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel
files are converted to text, and scanned PDFs and images are read with
Japanese OCR (Tesseract); uploads are kept in memory only and deleted
after two hours. A typical answer starts streaming within about a second
and completes in 40–55 seconds; a review of a short contract takes one
to two minutes.

## 11 Discussion

A 27B open model, specialized with retrieval and a few hundred examples
of its own verified reasoning, is the strongest of the lightweight open
models we tested across eight Japanese legal tasks and passes the bar
exam in every test year. Three lessons generalise beyond this model.
First, measure variance before measuring gains: on a 77-question paper,
seed noise is as large as most of the effects in the literature. Second,
at 27B the model’s own verified reasoning is better training data than
larger volumes of synthetic data from the same model, and a few hundred
examples suffice. Third, the components that help are cheap: hybrid
retrieval and four-sample voting run on the same single GPU.

For practitioners, the result means that a model that fits on one
workstation-class GPU and can be run on premises answers bar-exam
questions at the level of successful human candidates (126.2 on 2026),
provided it retrieves the statutes. It does not mean the model is
reliable: it still misjudges about one statement in five even with the
right article in context, it can attach unverifiable citations to
correct conclusions
(Figure [3](#S7.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 7.1 Contract review and drafting ‣ 7 Further tasks in detail ‣ XLaw-JP-27B:A Lightweight Open Model Specialized for Japanese Law")a),
and on contract review it captures half of what a frontier model does.
Review of its output by a lawyer remains necessary. The gaps that
remain—constitutional law, intellectual-property law and
statute-specific contract risks—are those where the relevant knowledge
sits in case law and specialized commentary rather than in statute text,
which suggests that the next gains will come from retrieval over case
law and expert-written training data rather than from more synthetic
data generated by the model itself.

## Limitations

Three clean exam papers give wide confidence intervals, and component
ablations use only two; the 2026 exam was held before the base model’s
release, so contamination cannot be fully excluded, although no 2026
material is in our data. The exam scoring is our implementation of the
official rules. Contract grading relies on an LLM judge that is also the
reference system. All baselines run one seed, and open baselines use our
prompts and retrieval, which may not suit them. COLIEE results are
pooled over years and are not comparable to official test sets. The
system does not give legal advice.

## Ethics Statement

Exam questions, answer keys, statutes and government model contracts are
public-sector information reused under their terms; COLIEE data was used
for evaluation only, under its agreement. The model can produce
confident but wrong legal statements and should be used with
professional oversight.

## Appendix A Prompts and hyper-parameters

All prompts are in Japanese; the system prompt is
「あなたは日本法の専門家です。」 unless noted. With retrieval, the
context block is prepended to the user prompt:

> 【参考資料】（条文・判例要旨を検索により取得。関係しないものが含まれる場合があります）  
> ◆law title article text, $`\leq`$600 characters  … ◆判例 summary,
> $`\leq`$450 characters  
> —

Bar exam.
「次の司法試験短答式問題に解答してください。簡潔に検討した上で、最後の行に「解答：」に続けて、
問題文の指示に従った解答番号を数字のみで出力してください。ア・イ・ウ…の各記述についてそれぞれ番号を選ぶ問題では、
記述の順に番号を連続して記載してください（例：アが1、イが1、ウが2なら「解答：112」）。」
followed by the question. The answer is the digit string after the last
解答：; anything else scores zero.

#### JBE-QA.

A system prompt asking for 1 if the statement is correct and 0 otherwise
(digits only) and the statement as the user turn. Non-binary outputs
score as wrong (MCC and F1 treat them as 0).

#### OI-Contract Review.

The model acts as counsel for the startup (X社) and must give (1) risks
for the client, (2) negotiation points, (3) a revised clause, given an
excerpt of the official scenario and the clause. The judge (Claude Opus
5.5) receives the clause, the numbered checklist and the answer and
returns a JSON list of covered/not-covered verdicts plus whether a
revised clause is given; verdicts are cached. _OI-Contract Draft_ asks
for the full contract from the term sheet; the judge checks every
term-sheet row for implementation and for consistency with the term
sheet and the official model contract.

#### Decoding.

vLLM 0.30, FP8 weights, thinking off; temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k
20, min-p 0, presence penalty 1.5; seeds 0–3; 8,192 new tokens for
exams, 4,096 for contract review, 8,192 for drafting, 1,024 otherwise.

#### QLoRA (E8, E10, E17).

4-bit base, LoRA rank 32, $`\alpha=64`$ on all linear layers of the
language model, 8-bit AdamW ($`\beta=(0.9,0.95)`$), learning rate
$`10^{-4}`$ with 5 warm-up steps and decay to 10%, effective batch 16
sequences, maximum length 8,192, 2 epochs, seed 0. Adapters are merged
into the bf16 base by streaming one tensor at a time and served in FP8.
One L40S (48 GB); E8 trains in about an hour, E17 in 2.3 hours.
