###### Abstract.

Managing multiple chronic conditions through diet requires satisfying
disease- and medication-specific requirements jointly across meals and
days while preserving established eating habits. These requirements are
public in clinical guidelines and drug labels, yet turning them into a
feasible plan for one person remains difficult for language-model
agents. In our dietitian-approved weekly planning evaluation built from
NHANES, thirteen models, from medically fine-tuned 8B models to GPT-6.1
Sol, pass fewer than 3% of complete plans through direct generation.
Explicit rules, higher reasoning effort, or model-directed tools leave
GPT-6.1 Sol at no more than 8%. We present Noura, which pairs LLM
planning with executable personal specifications instantiated from
public clinical guidance and individual health and dietary profiles.
During planning, the specification grounds foods, optimizes portions,
checks the complete multi-day plan, and guides revision. Our key insight
is that a specification that rejects a plan can also determine what to
learn from it. Noura turns executed plans and contextually credited
revisions into supervision for compact models, recovering targets even
from trajectories that never achieve complete success. The same
specification supplies supervised targets, preferences, and rewards for
post-training. On 4,993 held-out participants, Noura-SFT raises
Qwen3-14B’s complete-plan pass rate from 24.0% to 72.5% within the same
execution loop, cuts rule violations by 97.5% with no observed critical
failures, and improves dietary quality. The locally hosted 14B planner
incurs fewer rule violations than its GPT-6 Luna teacher. In controlled
evaluation, it passes more plans than a 32B base model at 41% of its
amortized batch time. A matched comparison shows that executed targets
reduce rule violations by 41% over model-written proposals. Admitting
improved plans from unsuccessful trajectories expands Qwen3-14B’s
candidate full-plan target pool 4.7-fold. Across four backbones from the
Qwen, Phi, and Llama families, self-training alone reduces rule
violations by 41–61% on a 1,249-person population subset.

## 1. Introduction

Chronic diseases impose a substantial burden of death and disability,
and their frequent coexistence complicates long-term care. In 2023,
51.4% of US adults reported two or more chronic conditions ([Watson
et al., 2025](#bib.bib53)). Managing these conditions together requires
coordinating disease-specific needs, medication-related restrictions,
and everyday behaviors. Diet is a major modifiable factor in this
burden: dietary risks accounted for an estimated 11 million deaths
worldwide in 2017 ([GBD 2017 Diet Collaborators, 2019](#bib.bib10)).
Dietary modification is foundational to cardiometabolic care,
recommended from the outset and continued alongside medication when
indicated ([Jones et al., 2025](#bib.bib22); [American Diabetes
Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes,
2026](#bib.bib2)). Personalized dietary planning translates these
clinical requirements into daily meals that jointly account for health
conditions, medications, nutritional targets, and established eating
habits across days and weeks.

Clinical guidelines and drug labels are publicly accessible on the Web,
providing extensive guidance for dietary management ([American Diabetes
Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes,
2026](#bib.bib2); [National Library of Medicine, nd](#bib.bib38);
[National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases,
nd](#bib.bib37)). Personalized agents using this Web guidance need user
models that express individual health requirements and habits as jointly
enforceable planning constraints across days. The United States has
roughly one dietitian or nutritionist per 4,000 residents ([U.S. Bureau
of Labor Statistics, 2026](#bib.bib48); [U.S. Census Bureau,
2026](#bib.bib49)), while preparing a three-day meal plan took
dietitians an average of 44 minutes in a feasibility study ([Mundi
et al., 2026](#bib.bib31)). In healthcare settings, sensitive patient
information and institutional control requirements further motivate
locally hosted models ([Zhang et al., 2026b](#bib.bib57)). Many
hospitals lack the GPU resources needed to host large models locally,
motivating compact models that fit existing infrastructure ([Xu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib56)).

Large language model (LLM) agents can translate natural-language goals
and heterogeneous information into personalized plans ([Xie et al.,
2024](#bib.bib55)). However, their strength in open-ended generation
does not ensure joint feasibility when decisions must satisfy hard
constraints over a long horizon ([Bui et al., 2026](#bib.bib7)). For
dietary planning, this requires coordinating disease-specific
nutritional targets and medication-related restrictions through food
choices, portions, and their distribution over time. In our weekly
planning evaluation, governed by a dietitian-approved specification,
direct generation across thirteen general-purpose and medically
fine-tuned models yields complete-plan pass rates below 3%. Our tool-use
comparisons likewise show that access to checking and portion-solving
tools does not ensure complete-plan feasibility. More extensive
reasoning and search can improve planning at additional inference cost,
motivating learning from execution feedback to make more effective
decisions within that budget ([Singh et al., 2024](#bib.bib45); [Gehring
et al., 2025](#bib.bib11)). How can personal requirements guide both
reliable planning and learning to plan more effectively under limited
computational resources?

To address this question, we introduce Noura, a framework that combines
LLM planning with executable personal specifications instantiated from
online public clinical guidance and individual health profiles. The
specifications connect food choices and quantitative execution to
whole-plan checking and revision, coordinating disease- and
medication-related requirements across days. Our key insight is that the
same specification can determine what to learn from execution: executed
plans and revisions assessed within the complete plan supply supervision
even when a trajectory never achieves complete success. Noura thereby
connects planning with post-training, drawing on both self-generated and
stronger-model experience and using the specifications to construct
supervised targets, preference comparisons, and reward signals.

We evaluate Noura at population scale and through controlled comparisons
using public NHANES health and dietary records and FNDDS food identities
and nutrient-composition data ([National Center for Health Statistics,
nd](#bib.bib36); [U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research
Service, nd](#bib.bib50)). We study open-weight and API-hosted planners
and post-train Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Phi-4, and Llama-3.1-8B to support
local hosting. Noura-SFT increases Qwen3-14B’s complete-plan pass rate
from 24.0% to 72.5% within the same execution loop at population scale,
while improving HEI-2020 dietary quality. Under the same population
execution protocol, the trained 14B planner incurs fewer rule violations
than its GPT-6 Luna teacher. Matched serving comparisons also show
higher complete-plan success in substantially less batch time than the
base model with native reasoning enabled. A matched target-content
comparison shows that executed plans provide more effective supervision
for rule compliance and dietary quality than the corresponding
model-written proposals. Learning exclusively from self-generated
experience also reduces rule violations and generated output tokens
across all three model families.

Our main contributions are:

- •
  Problem formulation and systematic evaluation. We formulate
  personalized dietary planning under interacting health, medication,
  temporal, and habit requirements, and establish a grounded evaluation
  protocol for complete-plan feasibility and personal specificity using
  public data and a dietitian-approved specification.
- •
  A unified framework for planning and post-training. Noura uses
  executable personal specifications to guide planning and construct
  supervised targets, preference comparisons, and rewards, recovering
  supervision even from trajectories that never achieve complete
  success.
- •
  Reliable, efficient, and personalized planning. Noura-SFT jointly
  improves complete-plan success, dietary quality, and batch inference
  efficiency at population scale. Controlled analyses establish the
  value of executed training targets, while complementary evaluations
  demonstrate recipient-specific plans, repeatable training gains, and
  effective self-training across model families.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1. Personalized Dietary Intervention and Disease Management

Trials support using individual physiological responses and health
histories to personalize dietary care ([Rein et al., 2022](#bib.bib43);
[Bermingham et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)). Making such requirements
computable has a long line of work, from linear programs that meet
public nutrient recommendations while staying close to observed intake
([Maillot et al., 2010](#bib.bib29)), through rule-based meal planning
for health conditions ([Amiri et al., 2023](#bib.bib3)), to medical
agents that bring guidelines, medications, and longitudinal patient
context into language-model reasoning ([Wu et al., 2026](#bib.bib54);
[Liévin et al., 2026](#bib.bib27)). These strands address different
parts of the problem. The quantitative systems neither plan with
language models nor learn, while the agents reason over guidance without
quantitatively verifying a complete multi-day plan. Neither compiles
personal requirements into executable specifications that construct
training targets. Noura starts from the requirements themselves,
compiling a person’s profile and public guidance, observed intake, and
cross-day consistency into one executable specification.

### 2.2. LLM Planning with Executable Constraints

Executing such a specification is a planning problem that language
models handle poorly on their own. Agents violate constraints when
assembling complete plans from retrieved information ([Xie et al.,
2024](#bib.bib55)). Classical planners and solvers handle search ([Liu
et al., 2023](#bib.bib28); [Hao et al., 2025](#bib.bib14)), while
verifiers steer candidate revision ([Kambhampati et al.,
2024](#bib.bib23)). Tool feedback drives iterative correction ([Gou
et al., 2024](#bib.bib12)), and execution feedback refines whole
trajectories under a monotonic acceptance rule ([Zhang et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib58)). Hierarchical schemes coordinate day-level planners
under a global monitor while training a shared policy by reinforcement
learning ([Bui et al., 2026](#bib.bib7)). These approaches coordinate
generation with explicit constraint handling. Noura adopts this division
of responsibility and turns grounded food choices and solved portions
into supervised targets.

### 2.3. Learning from Domain Knowledge and Execution Feedback

How execution results can become supervision is the question of a third
line of work. Domain post-training builds reasoning data from textbooks
and multi-model generation ([Kim et al., 2025](#bib.bib26); [Sun et al.,
2025](#bib.bib47)) or lets domain verifiers guide data construction and
reinforcement learning ([Chen et al., 2025a](#bib.bib8); [Narayanan
et al., 2025](#bib.bib32)). Learning from generated experience builds on
several approaches. Expert Iteration trains a network to imitate the
decisions that search improved ([Anthony et al., 2017](#bib.bib4)),
Dreamer learns from imagined trajectories ([Hafner et al.,
2019](#bib.bib13)), and ReST-EM filters sampled solutions with feedback
before supervised updates ([Singh et al., 2024](#bib.bib45)). Agent
tuning also learns from failure, contrasting failed rollouts with expert
trajectories through preference optimization or fine-tuning on failed
trajectories under a negative prompt ([Song et al., 2024](#bib.bib46);
[Wang et al., 2025a](#bib.bib52)). Constraint verifiers can likewise
rank sampled responses into preference labels ([Wang et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib51)). Execution-aware methods supervise test-validated
repairs ([Ni et al., 2024](#bib.bib39); [Jiang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib19)), train multi-attempt use of execution feedback
([Gehring et al., 2025](#bib.bib11)), and use solver artifacts as
rewards or as privileged context for self-distillation ([Chen et al.,
2025b](#bib.bib9); [Zhu et al., 2026](#bib.bib60)). These
execution-based methods use feedback to condition model-generated
repairs, select targets, assign preferences or rewards, and re-score
responses. Noura applies the Expert Iteration principle to planning
under a personal specification. Its executor grounds proposed foods and
solves their portions, and the resulting executed plan becomes the
target. Because the specification both checks and improves each attempt,
the planner can learn from its own attempts alone. Revisions receive
credit within the complete plan. A trajectory that never passes
completely can still supply improved plans and credited revisions as
targets. The same specification supplies supervised, preference, and
reward signals, turning personal requirements into supervision.

## 3. Method

Noura connects personalized plan execution to the learning of a more
effective planning policy. It instantiates an executable specification
from structured clinical and nutritional knowledge and an individual’s
health and dietary context. That specification coordinates clinical
requirements, intake references, dietary plausibility, habit continuity,
and constraints across days. During planning, it governs food grounding,
portion optimization, evaluation, and revision. During learning, it
converts the resulting decisions into supervision: the model learns to
propose complete plans and contextual revisions with the food choices
and quantities achieved through execution.

The shared specification provides three forms of learning signal
(Figure [1](#acmlabel1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3. Method ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
Executed plans and credited revisions provide supervised targets.
Preferences compare executed alternatives, and rewards evaluate fresh
drafts. Thus, Noura learns from the outcomes of its own proposals as
well as stronger-model trajectories, using the same personal
requirements to determine what should be learned. The trained policy
continues to operate with the execution and checking interface. We
describe the specification, its execution loop, target construction, and
post-training objectives in turn.

### 3.1. Problem Definition

A personalized planning task has input $`x=(p,O,H)`$: health context
$`p`$, observed dietary records $`O`$, and a horizon of $`H`$ days.
Health context includes demographics, conditions, medications,
anthropometrics, and available laboratory measurements. Dietary records
contain foods, quantities, and eating occasions. Health context
determines applicable requirements, while observed intake supplies
quantitative references and eating habits.

A language-model policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ proposes food-and-portion plans
for $`x`$. We denote the grounded proposal by $`Z`$ and its executed
state by $`X=E_{x}(Z)`$, where $`E_{x}`$ adjusts portions under the
personal specification $`\mathcal{S}_{x}`$. Both organize food
identifiers and gram quantities by day and meal: $`Z`$ retains proposed
portions, and $`X`$ records executed portions. The policy revises
proposals using execution feedback, and the system returns the retained
plan’s executed state.

#### Clinical rules and personal references

The clinical component of $`\mathcal{S}_{x}`$ instantiates rules from
five clinical guidelines and FDA labels for 20 drug products, covering
31 medications, four conditions, and two laboratory measures. Each rule
records applicability, required inputs, individualized bounds or
exclusions, severity, and provenance.
Table [4](#A1.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Independent specification review ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
lists the scored requirements. Mean recorded intake supplies separate
references for daily and weekly energy and, under warfarin, vitamin-K
consistency ([National Library of Medicine, nd](#bib.bib38)). Following
individual diet modeling ([Maillot et al., 2010](#bib.bib29)), this
keeps energy near usual intake while changing dietary composition. A
clinician-set energy target can replace the reference ([Jensen et al.,
2014](#bib.bib18)).

#### Dietary structure and continuity

Operational planning criteria complement the clinical rules: daily
checks constrain macronutrient balance, food concentration, and item
counts, while cross-day checks constrain repetition, overlap, variety,
and intake stability. Habit continuity requires a minimum energy share
from observed foods or same-category substitutes, while allowing the
full food catalog. An independent registered dietitian reviewed and
approved the clinical rules and operational thresholds
(Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1 "Independent specification review ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

#### Success and learning objective

Complete-plan success requires passing all evaluable clinical,
plausibility, cross-day, and grounding checks on the food choices and
executed quantities. Requirements needing unavailable inputs or clinical
resolution are recorded separately. We learn a policy that reaches
passing plans efficiently, using execution under $`\mathcal{S}_{x}`$ to
construct supervision, and assess dietary quality separately with
HEI-2020.

gives thresholds and conflict handling.

### 3.2. Executing and revising dietary plans

The execution loop converts a food-level proposal into a quantitatively
evaluated plan and uses the personal specification to direct subsequent
revisions.

#### From food proposals to quantitative plans

We ground proposed foods in the USDA Food and Nutrient Database for
Dietary Studies ([U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research
Service, nd](#bib.bib50)), checking supplied identifiers against their
descriptions and resolving ambiguous entries through catalog candidates.
Grounding establishes the nutrient composition needed to evaluate
quantities in the proposal $`Z`$ and its executed state $`X`$. For food
identifier $`c_{dmi}`$ and executed grams $`g_{dmi}`$, nutrient intake
on day $`d`$ is

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (1) |     |

             ``` math
             \mathbf{n}_{d}=\sum_{m,i}g_{dmi}\,\mathbf{a}(c_{dmi}),
             ```                                                     |     |

where $`\mathbf{a}(c)`$ is the catalog nutrient vector per gram.

For each day, a linear program adjusts portions within food-specific
bounds, balancing deviation from the proposed quantities against
severity-weighted constraint residuals. Forbidden food classes receive
zero quantity bounds. The evaluator then checks the executed intake and
assesses the complete plan across days. Food repetition, variety, and
day overlap are computed from the grounded proposal. Nutrient intake and
the energy share contributed by habitual foods use executed quantities.
Keeping both representations makes the relationship between food
choices, quantitative execution, and feedback explicit.

#### Revision under a shared specification

Feedback identifies failed checks, relevant quantities and thresholds,
nutrient contributors, and grounding problems. Candidates are ranked
lexicographically by

|     |     |                                             |                                                                                                      |     |
| --- | --- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
| (2) |     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{x}(Z,X)=\bigl(`$ | $`\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{critical}},v_{\mathrm{major}},v_{\mathrm{moderate}},v_{\mathrm{minor}},`$ |     |
|     |     |                                             | $`\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{plaus}},v_{\mathrm{cross}},v_{\mathrm{ground}}\bigr),`$                   |     |

where the entries count clinical rule failures by severity, daily
plausibility findings, cross-day findings, and ungrounded items. An
all-zero vector denotes complete-plan success.

The planner receives the preceding proposal and its execution feedback
and generates candidate meal replacements. Each candidate is merged into
that proposal, grounded, executed, and evaluated under the same
specification. The best candidate advances the rollout. The
lowest-scoring retained state across all rounds determines the returned
plan, with earlier rounds preferred on ties. Planning stops when the
current state passes or the revision budget is exhausted. When
collecting training trajectories, we record every candidate, its
generating context, and its execution results. These records provide
supervision for both producing a plan and revising one.

### 3.3. Learning targets from execution

The learning object is a planning decision conditioned on the person
and, for revision, the surrounding plan. Execution transforms a proposed
decision into a quantitative outcome, while the specification determines
whether that outcome supplies an admissible target. Full-plan targets
pair the original request with the best admitted executed plan. Revision
targets pair a recorded planning state and its feedback with executed
changes credited in that context. Food identifiers make these targets
computable, while food selection, quantities, and the scope of the
changes carry the planning supervision. Replaying recorded trajectories
constructs these targets without additional language-model generations
or expert-written reference plans.

#### Executed targets for planning and revision

Let $`Z^{0}`$ be an initial proposal and $`Z^{\star}`$ the best retained
proposal in its trajectory. For an admitted initial-planning example,
the input is the original planning context $`u^{0}`$, and the target
serializes the executed plan:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (3) |     |

````math
(u^{0},y^{0}),\qquad y^{0}=\operatorname{Serialize}\!\left(E_{x}(Z^{\star})\right).
``` |  |

The target contains grounded food identities and executed portions,
rounded to whole grams, with positive rounded quantities retained. This
pairing makes the outcome of quantitative execution and iterative
revision directly available as supervision for initial generation.
Revision examples use the same executed representation for changed
meals, paired with the recorded context that generated the candidate.
Their content is selected by the contextual credit test below.

#### Target admission

Let $`F_{x}(Z)`$ be a counter of findings obtained by evaluating
$`(Z,E_{x}(Z))`$. Each coordinate identifies a failed check and its
context: a rule or plausibility check on a day, a cross-day check with
its associated food or day pair, or an ungrounded item. For counters
$`\mathbf{a}`$ and $`\mathbf{b}`$, define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (4) |  |
``` math
\mathbf{a}\prec\mathbf{b}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bigl(\forall k,\ a_{k}\leq b_{k}\bigr)\ \land\ \|\mathbf{a}\|_{1}<\|\mathbf{b}\|_{1}.
``` |  |

This relation requires fewer findings overall and no increase in any
finding coordinate, preserving the identity of the affected
requirements. We admit an initial-planning example when the trajectory
achieves complete-plan success, or when $`Z^{\star}`$ occurs in a later
round and $`F_{x}(Z^{\star})\prec F_{x}(Z^{0})`$. The latter condition
makes strictly improved executed plans available for learning before
complete feasibility is reached.

#### Contextual credit for revision targets

A candidate can modify several days, whose contributions must be
assessed in the surrounding plan. Let $`B`$ be the preceding retained
proposal, $`Q_{j}`$ a candidate, and $`D_{j}`$ its changed days. Write
$`B\oplus_{S}Q_{j}`$ for the plan formed by inserting candidate days
$`S`$ into $`B`$. A construction evaluator $`C_{x,v}(B,Q)`$ assembles
executed daily states and recomputes cross-day and grounding findings
under construction version $`v`$. Candidate $`j`$ receives credit on
days

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (5) |  |
``` math
S_{j}=\bigl\{d\in D_{j}:C_{x,v}(B,B\oplus_{\{d\}}Q_{j})\prec F_{x}(B)\bigr\}.
``` |  |

Each test retains the other days as context. Candidates with
$`S_{j}=\varnothing`$ are discarded. We combine the credited days and
require $`C_{x,v}(B,B\oplus_{S_{j}}Q_{j})\prec F_{x}(B)`$ again to
assess their joint effect. Among eligible candidates, we select the one
with the greatest reduction in total findings, breaking ties by
candidate order. Its target contains the changed meals on credited days,
serialized with executed food identities and portions. Its input is the
recorded context that generated that candidate.

Suppose a food with a four-day repetition limit already appears on three
days. Two proposed replacements each resolve a local violation by
introducing it on another day, without adding other findings.
Individually, each can receive credit. Together, they introduce a fifth
appearance. Rechecking the combined edits prevents these individually
useful changes from becoming a target that adds a weekly violation.

The construction version $`v`$ is fixed for each training corpus.
Initial-plan admission and inference use the full finding counter.
Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px4 "Versioned revision credit ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
specifies the candidate-side checks in each version, the corresponding
checkpoints, and the controlled retraining analysis.

Admission therefore operates on learning targets within a trajectory. An
unsuccessful trajectory can contribute an improved executed plan, and a
candidate that did not advance the rollout can contribute credited
revisions. Together, these targets teach the model to anticipate
quantitative execution and to make changes that improve a plan in its
personal context. Their admission is determined by the specification,
independently of which model generated the trajectory.

### 3.4. Post-training compact planners

Executed-target SFT learns the planning policy from
specification-assessed decisions. The same specification supports
further learning through preferences over contextual revisions and
rewards for fresh plans. We use standard SFT, DPO, and GRPO objectives.
The task-specific construction lies in the targets, comparisons, and
rewards supplied by execution.

#### Supervised planning and revision

We combine admitted full-plan and revision examples into
$`\mathcal{D}`$. For recorded context $`u`$ and constructed completion
$`y`$, Noura-SFT optimizes

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (6) |  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta)=-\sum_{(u,y)\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid u,y_{<t}).
``` |  |

The loss masks the input context and covers all completion tokens. Noura
constructs supervision from planner-generated experience through the
execution-target procedure above. Noura-SFT (self) uses trajectories
generated by the corresponding base planner. The primary Qwen3-14B
Noura-SFT configuration combines Qwen3-14B and GPT-6 Luna trajectories
collected for the same training participants. Both sources undergo the
same execution, target admission, and serialization to produce full-plan
and revision targets. Trajectory source determines the available
experience, while the personal specification determines its
transformation into supervision.

#### Preference learning for contextual revisions

Noura-DPO (self) starts from Noura-SFT (self) and uses self-generated
alternatives sharing a revision context, day, and changed-meal scope.
The construction evaluator assesses each alternative after substitution
into the surrounding plan. An alternative credited with reducing
findings supplies the preferred executed response. Alternatives
introducing findings, followed by those leaving findings unchanged,
supply rejected responses. Matching the scope anchors the comparison to
the same revision decision. We optimize the DPO loss ([Rafailov et al.,
2023](#bib.bib42)) against a frozen reference equal to the starting SFT
checkpoint, together with a chosen-response negative-log-likelihood
term. The specification supplies the preference labels, while the
reference model supplies the likelihood ratios.

#### Reward learning for initial plans

Noura-GRPO (self) extends the self-generated training route with
group-relative optimization ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib44)),
initialized from the DPO checkpoint. The specification scores fresh,
grounded plans at their model-written quantities, rewarding fewer
clinical, plausibility, cross-day, and grounding findings before portion
optimization or iterative repair.
Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px5 "Preference and reward learning ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
gives the reward, sampling, and optimization settings.

We use low-rank adaptation ([Hu et al., 2022](#bib.bib17)), merge
adapters for evaluation, and maintain matching training and serving
templates. All reported planners are evaluated through the same
execution interface. Training participants are disjoint from development
and test participants.
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
gives corpus sizes, objective coefficients, construction versions,
optimization settings, and checkpoint lineage.

## 4. Experiments

We address four questions through population evaluation and controlled
comparisons:

RQ1: Reliability. Can Noura produce reliable plans across a multimorbid
population
(Section [5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1. Reliable planning at population scale (RQ1) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?"))?

RQ2: Execution-based supervision. How do executed targets and
specification-guided target construction contribute to learning
(Section [5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2. Learning from executed decisions (RQ2) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?"))?

RQ3: Quality and efficiency. How do training objectives and model
backbones affect planning quality and efficiency
(Section [5.3](#S5.SS3 "5.3. Planning quality and efficiency (RQ3) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?"))?

RQ4: Personalization and robustness. How specific are plans to their
recipients, how stable are training gains, and how does post-training
affect external capabilities
(Section [5.4](#S5.SS4 "5.4. Personal specificity and robustness (RQ4) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?"))?

#### Participants and tasks

Tasks combine NHANES health information and dietary recalls ([National
Center for Health Statistics, nd](#bib.bib36)) with FNDDS food
composition ([U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research
Service, nd](#bib.bib50)), requesting a seven-day plan from each
participant’s health context and observed intake. The population cohort
contains 4,993 held-out participants with at least two of hypertension,
diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and a laboratory-defined CKD
phenotype. Controlled analyses use a separate 300-person test, including
a fixed 150-person API-comparison subset. Qwen3-14B and GPT-6 Luna are
evaluated on the full population. Cross-family replication uses a fixed
1,249-person subset. Configuration development uses another 50
participants. Cohort definitions and output completeness appear in
Appendices [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1. Cohorts, metrics, and evaluation protocol ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
and [B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4. Cross-family replication and robustness ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?").

#### Comparisons and training

We compare direct generation, which grounds foods without optimizing
portions; model-directed tool use, where the model chooses when to
retrieve, check, and solve portions; and specification-guided execution
(Section [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2. Executing and revising dietary plans ‣ 3. Method ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
Qwen3-14B is the principal platform, with Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B
providing scale comparisons. Self-training is evaluated on Qwen3-14B,
Qwen3-8B, Phi-4, and Llama-3.1-8B. The self-generated corpora contain up
to 1,500 trajectories from training participants disjoint from
evaluation. Noura-SFT additionally uses GPT-6 Luna trajectories on the
same participants. All systems use the same frozen evaluation
specification and fixed participant manifests. The principal Noura-SFT
checkpoint must meet a prespecified HEI-2020 criterion on the controlled
set before assessment on the disjoint population cohort.
Appendices [A](#A1 "Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
and [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1. Cohorts, metrics, and evaluation protocol ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")
give training and execution settings.

#### Metrics

The primary endpoints are complete-plan pass rate (CPR), the percentage
of participants whose plan has no evaluable clinical, plausibility,
cross-day, or grounding failure, and rule violations (RV), the number of
failed rule–participant–day checks. Rule-violation rate (RVR) divides RV
by applicable rule-day checks. Daily pass rate (DPR) is the percentage
of passing days, excluding cross-day checks. Critical-violation
prevalence (CVP) is the percentage of participants with any critical
failure. HEI-2020 measures dietary quality by pooling each participant’s
seven-day intake and averaging participant scores. HEI reflects
alignment with general dietary guidelines and does not assess
individualized dietary requirements ([National Cancer Institute,
2026](#bib.bib35)). We therefore interpret it alongside specification
satisfaction and habit fit rather than as a standalone measure of
clinical appropriateness. Habit fit is the share of executed energy from
observed foods or suggested substitutes. Efficiency uses output tokens
across all planning calls and amortized batch time (ABT), batch wall
time divided by participant count. Paired intervals resample
participants; denominator, unresolved-check, and bootstrap conventions
appear in
Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1. Cohorts, metrics, and evaluation protocol ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?").
External evaluation reports position-debiased QA accuracy and strict
prompt-level IFEval accuracy
(Appendix [B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4. Cross-family replication and robustness ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

## 5. Results

### 5.1. Reliable planning at population scale (RQ1)

Post-training improves complete-plan feasibility within a fixed
execution protocol. Using Qwen3-14B and GPT-6 Luna trajectories,
Noura-SFT raises complete-plan passing from 24.0% to 72.5% on 4,993
held-out participants

optimization, and revision. Rule failures decrease and HEI increases,

Portion optimization adjusts quantities within a food set, while guided
revision changes food choices and their arrangement across days.

introduces another trade-off, improving HEI by 3.2 points (95% CI

(Appendix [B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2. Planning conditions and controls ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

### 5.3. Planning quality and efficiency (RQ3)

Preference and reward learning build on the initial gains from self-SFT,
but change which aspects of a plan improve. On the 300-person test, DPO
adds 28 passing plans over SFT (95% CI $`[11,44]`$), and 400-update GRPO
reaches 50.0% complete-plan passing
(Figure [3](#acmlabel3 "Figure 3 ‣ 5.3. Planning quality and efficiency (RQ3) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

The progression in passing rates does not extend to every quality
dimension. Starting from DPO, GRPO raises habit fit from 63.0% to 75.4%
but lowers HEI from 74.0 to 68.9. Plans better retain familiar foods or
substitutes while scoring lower against general dietary guidelines,
under a reward that excludes HEI. With additional teacher experience,
Noura-SFT combines 62.3% complete-plan passing with HEI 81.4. The
training routes therefore offer different quality trade-offs
(Appendix [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3. Supervision controls and post-training ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

These gains let a compact planner reach higher feasibility at lower
serving cost. The 14B Noura-SFT produces 187 passing plans versus 119
for the 32B base model, with fewer rule failures and ABT of 21.2 versus
51.3 seconds. Both use one H200 and 16 workers, so this comparison
measures amortized serving time. The trained planner also outperforms
the thinking-enabled 14B base (ABT 77.2 seconds) in passing plans and
rule failures under the same serving setting
(Figure [3](#acmlabel3 "Figure 3 ‣ 5.3. Planning quality and efficiency (RQ3) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

Earlier success in the execution loop accompanies these savings. On the
4,993-person population cohort, Noura-SFT passes 37.8% of plans after
one revision, versus 7.9% for base. Earlier passing is consistent with
learning reducing the correction needed for a new participant. Feedback
from training trajectories can inform subsequent proposals, while the
same grounding, portion optimization, and checking procedures remain in
place to evaluate and refine each generated plan. Within this cohort’s
separate 24-worker serving setting, ABT falls from 27.5 to 15.0 seconds
(Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1.SSS0.Px4 "Cost and uncertainty ‣ B.1. Cohorts, metrics, and evaluation protocol ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

Reduced model-directed tool use also accompanies the savings, but
disabling retrieval alone does not reproduce the training gains.
Noura-SFT (self) produces 24 more passing plans (95% CI $`[7,42]`$) and
58 fewer rule failures ($`[-110,-11]`$) than retrieval-disabled
Qwen3-14B
(Table [6](#A2.T6 "Table 6 ‣ B.2. Planning conditions and controls ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
Automatic grounding, portion optimization, and checking remain active in
both configurations, so this comparison still evaluates planning with
quantitative execution.

Training each backbone on its own trajectories reproduces the compliance
and efficiency gains across three families, while exposing a distinction
between daily and weekly feasibility. Qwen3-14B, Phi-4, Qwen3-8B, and
Llama-3.1-8B all improve daily passing on the 1,249-person subset, with
41–61% fewer rule failures and 29–55% fewer output tokens. The
controlled test shows the same pattern
(Figure [4](#acmlabel4 "Figure 4 ‣ 5.3. Planning quality and efficiency (RQ3) ‣ 5. Results ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
Complete-plan passing improves for three backbones but remains largely
unchanged for Qwen3-8B ($`+0.9`$ points, 95% CI $`[-1.9,3.7]`$). Food
repetition persists in 50% of its plans and day overlap in 43%. These
weekly constraints remain obstacles to complete passing even as daily
compliance improves
(Appendix [B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4. Cross-family replication and robustness ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

### 5.4. Personal specificity and robustness (RQ4)

Cross-person transfer exposes two distinct demands of personalization.
Of 55,913 recipient pairs formed from 187 passing Noura-SFT plans on the
300-person test, only 25 pass (0.045%). Clinical failures occur in 59.5%
of pairs and critical failures in 20.5%. Re-optimizing portions raises
clinical compliance to 69.6% across 5,610 sampled pairs, showing that
quantities can address many clinical mismatches. Complete passing
remains rare after portion adaptation. Habit-continuity failures
dominate direct transfer
(Appendix [B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4. Cross-family replication and robustness ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
The available foods determine which parts of the recipient’s habitual
diet a menu can preserve. Food proposals therefore need to reflect the
recipient’s dietary context, while portion optimization aligns
quantities with their clinical targets.

This continuity remains visible when plans are generated for their
intended recipients. In the population cohort, Noura-SFT allocates a
2.5-percentage-point higher share of energy to observed foods or
suggested substitutes than GPT-6 Luna (95% CI $`[2.2,2.9]`$). Together
with the lower rule-failure count, this shows that stronger
clinical-rule compliance can coexist with greater dietary continuity in
the learned policy.

Repeated training runs preserve the planning advantage, while external
evaluations distinguish QA retention from instruction following. On the
300-person test, three Qwen3-14B self-SFT runs pass 91–98 plans versus
51 for base, and three Noura-SFT runs pass 187–197. All six reduce rule
failures (seeds in
Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px3 "Supervision corpora and optimization ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
Across six evaluated checkpoints, position-debiased accuracy on five
medical and general QA benchmarks declines by at most 1.1 percentage
points. Strict IFEval changes range from $`-5.7`$ to $`+4.4`$ points,
showing that instruction-following retention is less uniform than QA
retention
(Appendix [B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4. Cross-family replication and robustness ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).

## 6. Conclusion

Noura connects personalized dietary planning and model learning through
executable personal specifications. The same requirements coordinate
food choices and quantities across a complete plan, guide revision, and
provide executed targets, contextual preferences, and rewards for
post-training. This construction draws supervision from useful decisions
in both successful and unsuccessful trajectories, supporting learning
from self-generated and stronger-model experience. A matched
target-content comparison shows that executed decisions improve rule
compliance and HEI-2020 relative to the corresponding model-written
proposals. In an offline evaluation of 4,993 held-out NHANES
participants, Noura-SFT raises the 14B planner’s complete-plan pass rate
from 24.0% to 72.5% within the same execution loop while improving
HEI-2020. Self-training reduces rule violations and generated output
tokens across four compact models from three families. These results
show how personal requirements can serve both as criteria for execution
and as supervision for learning more effective planning policies.

Future work can extend Noura to additional conditions by adding
applicable rules and resolving interactions with existing requirements.
For populations with different dietary practices, extensions can adapt
food catalogs, habit references, and planning criteria. These extensions
would test whether the same execution and learning procedure remains
effective as personal requirements change.

## Appendix A Implementation Details

Controlled seven-day runs allow one draft, three repair rounds of four
candidates (first greedy, others temperature 0.7), and eight
information-tool calls. Information-tool and edit-suggestion interfaces
use version 1 for training and version 2 for testing.

#### Independent specification review

An independent, non-author registered dietitian approved the scored
rules, sources, overrides, and planning thresholds without changes, with
per-rule provenance logged.

#### Thresholds and portion solving

Missing inputs remain unresolved. Conflicting bounds retain the upper
bound and defer the lower to clinical judgment. Daily energy must lie
within \[0.6, 1.4\] and the seven-day mean within \[0.8, 1.2\] of mean
recalled intake
(Appendix [B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4. Cross-family replication and robustness ‣ Appendix B Additional Experiments ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")).
Except for designated staples and free beverages, foods may appear on at
most four days. Plans need at least 21 distinct non-free-beverage foods,
day-level Jaccard similarity at most 0.8, and at least 30% of energy
from observed foods or substitutes. These bounds are operational
planning criteria. Per day, the solver minimizes
$`\sum_{i}|g_{i}-t_{i}|/\max(t_{i},1)`$ plus residual penalties
$`\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}s_{j}`$ subject to
$`\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}\leq\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{s}`$,
$`\mathbf{l}\leq\mathbf{g}\leq\mathbf{u}`$, and $`\mathbf{s}\geq 0`$,
where $`\mathbf{t}`$ holds proposed grams, $`\mathbf{A}`$ encodes
numerical rules, bounds are normally $`[0.5,2]\,\mathbf{t}`$, and
$`\lambda_{j}`$ ranges from $`10^{5}`$ (critical) to $`10^{2}`$ (minor).

#### Supervision corpora and optimization

Pre-filter initial-plan/post-filter training counts are 994/4,167 for
Qwen3-14B, 1,183/4,097 for Phi-4, 1,015/3,568 for Qwen3-8B, and
937/3,914 for Llama-3.1-8B, from 1,500 self-generated trajectories each
(Phi-4: 1,499). Noura-SFT has 2,362/7,730 targets from 3,000 Qwen3-14B
and GPT-6 Luna trajectories on the same 1,500 participants. SFT uses
LoRA (rank 32, $`\alpha=64`$, dropout 0.05) on all attention and MLP
projections, two epochs, learning rate $`10^{-4}`$ with cosine decay and
3% warmup, effective batch size 16, and bfloat16, discarding records
over 16,384 tokens. Training and serving share chat templates.
Completion-only loss covers initial-plan and revision targets without
tool-call targets or evaluation participants. Data-order seeds are 42
(primary) and 1 and 2 (repeats).

#### Versioned revision credit

The Qwen3-14B, Noura-SFT, and Phi-4 corpora use original credit, zeroing
candidate-side weekly energy and vitamin-K findings but retaining
reference findings. Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B use complete credit,
checking every finding. Retraining Qwen3-14B with complete credit gains
41 passing plans over base (95% CI \[24, 58\]). Differences from
original credit (10,000 resamples, seed 0) are passing plans $`-6`$
(\[$`-20`$, 8\]), execution-loop RV $`-4`$ (\[$`-36`$, 28\]), cross-day
findings $`+114`$ (\[28, 204\]), and direct-generation RV $`+216`$
(\[56, 382\]).

#### Preference and reward learning

Within matched revision scopes
(Section [3.4](#S3.SS4 "3.4. Post-training compact planners ‣ 3. Method ‣ Can Personal Requirements Become Supervision for Planning?")),
the first credited alternative is preferred. Negatives prioritize added
findings. DPO ([Rafailov et al., 2023](#bib.bib42)) uses the frozen SFT
model as reference, coefficient 0.1, and chosen-response NLL weight 1.
GRPO ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib44)) scores sampled first drafts
without portion optimization or repair, with
$`r=-[(R+P)/H+0.25W+0.1L+U]`$: $`R`$ weights daily rule findings
3/2/1/0.5 by severity; $`P`$, $`W`$, $`L`$, and $`U`$ count
plausibility, cross-day, linking, and residual grounding findings; $`H`$
is the horizon. Empty plans or scoring failures receive $`-10`$. Each
stage trains new LoRA parameters (SFT$`\to`$DPO$`\to`$GRPO, all on
Qwen3-14B trajectories only). GRPO uses 400 updates over 1,600 prompts,
16 completions per update (four per prompt), learning rate
$`5\times 10^{-6}`$, temperature 0.8, a 5,120-token completion limit,
group-standardized rewards, clipping 0.2, and no reference KL.

## Appendix B Additional Experiments

### B.1. Cohorts, metrics, and evaluation protocol

#### Cohorts

Eligibility requires at least four foods and 500 kcal on the first
recall day. The controlled test has 300 participants selected for rule
coverage. The population cohort (4,993 participants, frozen 2026-10-12)
excludes training, test, and development participants. Prevalences are
89.9% hypertension, 52.4% diabetes, 42.9% cardiovascular disease, and
52.3% CKD (eGFR below 60 or albumin-to-creatinine ratio at least
30 mg/g). Participants have two (3,398), three (1,316), or four (279)
domains and a median of four named prescriptions (49.3% have five or
more). The principal Noura-SFT checkpoint required a prespecified lower
paired 95% HEI bound of at least $`-3`$ points against base execution on
the controlled test, a selection tolerance rather than clinical
equivalence.

#### Models

Qwen3-14B uses its 32,768-token context ([Qwen Team, 2025](#bib.bib41));
Phi-4, Med42-8B, and OpenBioLLM-8B use 16,384-token contexts, with no
fixed output cap for direct generation or specification-guided
execution. OpenBioLLM returns 299 of 300 plans. All population runs
return every requested plan. Llama-3.1-8B’s principal planning outcomes
use an updated self-SFT checkpoint (same corpus and hyperparameters).
External tasks and the profile’s violations-fixed and cross-day entries
use the earlier one.

#### Metrics

CPR/CVP denominators are intended participants, and DPR uses seven days
each. Missing outputs are nonpassing. Unresolved (missing inputs or
deferred conflicting bounds) and advisory statuses do not block CPR, but
every evaluable failure does. RVR includes unresolved and advisory
rule-day checks in its denominator (13,916 controlled; 164,927
population), so it does not complement verified passing. HEI-2020 uses
all 13 components ([National Cancer Institute, 2024](#bib.bib34)),
averages returned plans without population weights, and is absent from
target admission, preference construction, and reward.

#### Cost and uncertainty

Tokens include all planning calls and discarded candidates. Tool
attempts include invalid names and exclude automatic grounding, solving,
and checking. Timing uses one H200 with 16 workers (controlled) or 24
workers (population, four batches of about 1,249 participants). Paired
95% intervals use 5,000 participant bootstrap resamples (seed 20261017).
