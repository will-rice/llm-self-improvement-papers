# Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verification for Self-Improving Systems

Arsenios Scrivens

March 2026

###### Abstract

Can a safety gate permit unbounded beneficial self-modification while
maintaining bounded cumulative risk? We formalize this question through
_dual conditions_ — requiring $`\sum\delta_{n}<\infty`$ (bounded risk)
and $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$ (unbounded utility) — and establish
a theory of their (in)compatibility.

Classification impossibility
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")):
For power-law risk schedules $`\delta_{n}=O(n^{-p})`$ with $`p>1`$ — the
practically relevant regime — any classifier-based gate under
overlapping safe/unsafe distributions satisfies
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ via Hölder’s
inequality, forcing $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}<\infty`$. This impossibility
is _exponent-optimal_: no valid impossibility bound can use a larger
exponent than $`\beta^{*}`$
(Theorem [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3 (Exponent-Optimality of Hölder Bound). ‣ 3.2 Exponent-Optimality (Theorem 3) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems");
full Mills’ ratio asymptotics in
Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Theorem Exponent-Optimality: Mills’ Ratio Asymptotics ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
A second independent proof via the _NP counting method_
(Theorem [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility). ‣ 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
yields a 13% tighter bound without Hölder’s inequality. Scope caveat:
for slowly-decaying summable sequences such as
$`\delta_{n}=1/(n\ln^{2}n)`$ that are not power-law, the Hölder
summability argument fails and the NP-optimal classifier may satisfy
both dual conditions
(§[3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
covers all schedules.

Universal finite-horizon ceiling
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")):
For _any_ summable risk schedule, the exact maximum achievable
classifier utility is
$`U^{*}(N,B)=N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)`$, growing as
$`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$ — subpolynomial. At $`N=10^{6}`$ with budget
$`B=1.0`$, a classifier extracts at most $`U^{*}\approx 87`$ versus a
verifier’s $`\approx 500{,}000`$. This result is universal: it applies
even where
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
does not.

Verification escape
(Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Verification Escape). ‣ 4.1 Statement (Theorem 2) ‣ 4 The Verification Escape ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")):
A Lipschitz ball verifier achieves $`\delta=0`$ with $`\mathrm{TPR}>0`$,
escaping the impossibility. Formal Lipschitz bounds for pre-LayerNorm
transformers under LoRA
(Proposition [3](#Thmproposition3 "Proposition 3 (Transformer LoRA Lipschitz Bound). ‣ C.5 Formal Transformer Lipschitz Bounds ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
enable LLM-scale verification. The separation is strict: classifiers
cannot achieve $`\delta=0`$ with $`\mathrm{TPR}>0`$ under distribution
overlap
(Proposition [4](#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4 (Structural Classification–Verification Separation). ‣ 5 The Separation Principle (Proposition 4) ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
We validate on GPT-2 ($`d_{\text{LoRA}}=147{,}456`$): conditional
$`\delta=0`$ with $`\mathrm{TPR}=0.352`$; unconditional $`\delta=0`$ at
$`d\leq 17{,}408`$. Comprehensive empirical validation is in the
companion paper \[[23](#bib.bib23)\].

## 1 Introduction

The prospect of AI systems that improve their own capabilities raises a
central safety question: can we build a gate that permits beneficial
self-modifications while reliably blocking dangerous ones? We formalize
this as a sequential decision problem and provide a comprehensive
theoretical characterization of when safe self-improvement is possible
and when it is provably impossible.

At each step $`n`$, a system proposes a mutation $`\theta_{n}`$ to its
own parameters. A *safety gate* $`g_{n}`$ decides whether to accept or
reject $`\theta_{n}`$, incurring false acceptance rate
$`\delta_{n}=P^{-}(g_{n}\text{ accepts})`$ and true positive rate
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=P^{+}(g_{n}\text{ accepts})`$. We require
$`\sum\delta_{n}<\infty`$ (bounded risk, ensuring positive probability
of never accepting an unsafe mutation) and
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$ (unbounded utility, ensuring the system
accepts infinitely many improvements). We call these the dual conditions
for safe self-improvement.

We prove four impossibility and tightness results and four constructive
results, forming a complete picture. The impossibility is strongest for
power-law risk schedules $`\delta_{n}=O(n^{-p})`$ with $`p>1`$
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
which subsume all practically relevant risk budgets. For the most
favorable slowly-decaying schedules, the asymptotic impossibility does
not apply, but the tight finite-horizon ceiling
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
ensures classifier utility remains subpolynomial — orders of magnitude
below a verifier’s linear growth — at any practical deployment horizon.

Why the dual conditions matter for deployed systems. The dual conditions
formalize a failure pattern that self-improving systems face by
construction. Red-team evaluations — which explicitly classify model
outputs or parameter mutations as safe/unsafe — are the closest
practical analog to the binary gates our theorems address. Failure to
satisfy both conditions simultaneously means either that unsafe
modifications accumulate ($`\sum\delta_{n}`$ diverges) or that the
system stops improving ($`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\to 0`$). Other deployed
mechanisms such as RLHF reward models can be viewed as a suggestive
analogy, but our formal results apply specifically to binary gates on
parameter mutations; for a fuller discussion
see §[8.1](#S8.SS1 "8.1 Implications for Safe AI Deployment ‣ 8 Discussion ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").

### 1.1 Contributions

The paper establishes two core results and then systematically
corroborates them. (Theorems are numbered 1–5 and Propositions 1–4, with
separate counters.)

A note on the nature of the contribution. The per-step bound
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ is a standard
f-divergence inequality \[[27](#bib.bib27)\], and the sequential
summability consequence follows in a few lines. The proof is short —
deliberately so. The contribution of this paper is not the length or
technical difficulty of any single proof, but rather: (i) the _problem
formalization_ — casting safe self-improvement as dual summability
conditions, which has no precedent in the hypothesis testing or AI
safety literatures; (ii) the _structural consequence_ — that this
elementary coupling creates an impossibility for the safety–utility
pairing with no analog in single-test settings; (iii) the _tight
finite-horizon ceiling_
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
which provides the exact, universal utility bound for any classifier
under any risk schedule; and (iv) the _constructive escape_ via
verification, proving the impossibility is specific to classification,
not to safe self-improvement itself.

### 1.2 Related Work

Our mathematical tools — Hölder’s inequality, Rényi divergence,
Lipschitz continuity, Neyman–Pearson testing — are well-established. The
per-step bound $`\mathrm{TPR}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta^{\beta}`$ is a
standard f-divergence inequality \[[27](#bib.bib27)\], and NP
optimality \[[16](#bib.bib16)\] establishes single-test ROC tradeoffs.
Our contribution is the _problem formalization_ (the dual conditions as
a formal specification of safe self-improvement) and the _structural
result_ that sequential composition under dual summability conditions
creates an impossibility with no analog in single-test settings. The
per-step bound and the summability requirements are individually
standard; the coupling — that bounded $`\sum\delta_{n}`$ forces bounded
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}`$ for power-law risk schedules — is not, and is
confirmed by two independent impossibility proofs
(Theorems [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"), [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility). ‣ 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
corroborated by three complementary bounds
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"),
Propositions [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound). ‣ C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")–[2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Sample Complexity Barrier). ‣ C.4 Sample Complexity Barrier ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
approaching the same conclusion from different angles
(§[3](#S3 "3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"),
§[6](#S6 "6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"),
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
We build on alignment theory \[[6](#bib.bib6), [2](#bib.bib2),
[7](#bib.bib7)\], hypothesis testing \[[13](#bib.bib13)\], impossibility
results \[[30](#bib.bib30), [21](#bib.bib21)\], information-theoretic
bounds \[[20](#bib.bib20), [19](#bib.bib19)\], PAC-Bayes and VC
theory \[[15](#bib.bib15), [28](#bib.bib28)\], adversarial robustness
tradeoffs \[[26](#bib.bib26), [11](#bib.bib11)\], and transformer
Lipschitz analysis \[[29](#bib.bib29), [12](#bib.bib12),
[8](#bib.bib8)\]. Our dual conditions formalize the _alignment tax_ —
the cost of making models safe versus capable \[[3](#bib.bib3),
[17](#bib.bib17)\] — as a precise mathematical tradeoff: the Hölder
coupling $`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$
quantifies the exact rate at which safety constraints reduce utility
under classification-based gates. Structurally, our result is closer to
mechanism-design impossibilities — Gibbard \[[10](#bib.bib10)\] and
Satterthwaite \[[22](#bib.bib22)\] show that no voting rule can
simultaneously satisfy multiple natural axioms, just as no classifier
can simultaneously satisfy our dual conditions — than to no-free-lunch
theorems \[[30](#bib.bib30)\], which concern the absence of a
universally optimal learner rather than a hard tradeoff in a fixed
domain. Multi-objective optimization
impossibilities \[[18](#bib.bib18)\] also exhibit this flavour: two
desiderata in conflict cannot be jointly optimized in polynomial time,
analogously to how our two summability conditions cannot be jointly
satisfied by a classifier under distribution overlap.

A detailed comparison with each line of work is in
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Relation to Known Results and Extended Related Work ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").

An analogy clarifies the contribution. Arrow’s impossibility theorem
composes elementary social-choice axioms — transitivity,
non-dictatorship, independence — each individually obvious, yet their
_composition_ yields a deep impossibility no voting system can escape.
Similarly, our per-step bound is a standard f-divergence inequality and
the dual conditions are individually natural, but the _coupling_ — that
summability of $`\{\delta_{n}\}`$ forces summability of
$`\{\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\}`$ — creates a structural impossibility with no
analog in single-test hypothesis testing (see
Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Relation to Known Results ‣ Appendix B Relation to Known Results and Extended Related Work ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
for a full discussion).

Online learning and adaptive gates. A natural question is whether an
online learner with sublinear regret (e.g., online convex optimization;
[Shalev-Shwartz 24](#bib.bib24)) could adaptively satisfy the dual
conditions. The answer is no under our framework:
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")’s
per-step bound
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ constrains
_any_ binary decision rule at each step, regardless of whether it was
chosen adaptively based on previous observations. Online learning can
reduce misclassification regret, but cannot escape the Hölder coupling
between $`\delta_{n}`$ and $`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}`$ that drives the
impossibility. The gate’s _adaptivity_ affects which point on the
per-step ROC curve it selects, not the curve itself.

### 2.1 System Model

A self-improving system maintains parameters $`\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$
and iteratively proposes mutations $`\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots`$ At
each step $`n`$, a safety oracle
$`S:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{\text{safe},\text{unsafe}\}`$ determines ground
truth. The oracle is assumed expensive (requiring exhaustive simulation
or formal proof).

A safety gate $`g_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}`$
filters mutations before deployment. The gate is computationally cheaper
than the oracle.

### 2.2 Classification Gates

A _classification gate_ maps controller parameters to a low-dimensional
feature representation $`\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}`$ and
applies a binary classifier. The relevant distributions are:

|     |                             |                                                                                      |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle P_{n}^{+}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{distribution of }\varphi(\theta)\mid S(\theta)=\text{safe}`$   |     | (1) |
|     | $`\displaystyle P_{n}^{-}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{distribution of }\varphi(\theta)\mid S(\theta)=\text{unsafe}`$ |     | (2) |

At step $`n`$, the gate accepts if the classifier predicts “safe” based
on $`\varphi(\theta_{n})`$.

Stationarity assumption. The core theorems
(§[3](#S3 "3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")–§[6](#S6 "6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
assume stationary distributions $`P^{+},P^{-}`$ (dropping the $`n`$
subscript). The non-stationary extension
(Proposition [C.2](#Thmproposition100 "Proposition C.2 (Non-Stationary Impossibility). ‣ C.2 Non-Stationary Extension ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"),
Appendix [C.2](#A3.SS2 "C.2 Non-Stationary Extension ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
requires $`\sup_{n}D_{\alpha}^{(n)}<\infty`$ and, for power-law
schedules $`\delta_{n}=c/n^{p}`$, the strictly stronger condition
$`p>\alpha`$ (vs. $`p>1`$ in the stationary case). This gap narrows as
$`\alpha\to 1^{+}`$ and vanishes for fast-decaying schedules
($`p\gg\alpha`$), which cover all practically relevant risk budgets. For
deployment arguments where stationarity may not hold and $`p`$ is
moderate, the finite-horizon ceiling
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
provides a stationarity-free alternative: it bounds total classifier
utility over any $`N`$-step horizon given a risk budget $`B`$.

Scope: continuous parameter spaces. Our results assume
$`\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ with continuous mutation distributions, so
that $`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ (absolute continuity) holds via the
transversality argument
(§[3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
For discrete or quantized parameter spaces — such as quantized LoRA
fine-tuning with integer-valued weights — absolute continuity does not
hold in the same form, and the impossibility may not apply directly. We
note that even in quantized settings, the effective parameter updates
are typically computed in full precision before rounding, and the
induced distributions on the quantized grid can still exhibit the
overlap structure that drives our results; a formal treatment of the
discrete case is left to future work.

### 2.3 Verification Gates

A _verification gate_ attempts to construct a mathematical proof that
$`\theta`$ is safe. If the proof succeeds, the gate accepts; otherwise
it rejects. A key property:

###### Definition (Soundness).

A verification gate is _sound_ if every accepted $`\theta`$ is actually
safe: $`g(\theta)=\text{accept}\implies S(\theta)=\text{safe}`$.

Soundness implies $`\delta_{n}=0`$ for all $`n`$ — by construction, not
by learning.

### 2.4 The Dual Conditions

###### Definition.

A safety gate achieves _safe self-improvement_ if:

1.  1.  $`\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}<\infty`$  (bounded cumulative risk)
2.  2.  $`\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$  (unbounded
        cumulative utility)

Condition 1 ensures the system is almost surely safe over infinitely
many steps ($`\prod(1-\delta_{n})>0`$ by convergence of the infinite
product). Condition 2 prevents vacuous safety (a gate rejecting
everything trivially satisfies Condition 1 but accomplishes nothing).

On the choice of $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$. This is the _weakest
possible_ non-vacuity condition: it requires only that the system
eventually accepts infinitely many improvements, with no constraint on
the rate or timing. Any finite threshold ($`N_{0}`$ accepted
modifications “suffice”) is arbitrary and deployment-dependent — a
self-improving system has no natural stopping point, and any fixed
$`N_{0}`$ can be exceeded by extending the deployment horizon. The
condition is also _necessary_ in the following sense: if
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}<\infty`$, the expected number of accepted
improvements is finite, meaning the system almost surely stops
self-improving after finitely many steps — it becomes a fixed system
with a safety gate that rejects everything beyond some horizon.

Critically, readers who reject the asymptotic framing lose nothing from
the theory.
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
provides the exact finite-horizon utility ceiling
$`U^{*}(N,B)=N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)`$ for _any_ finite
$`N`$ and risk budget $`B`$, without requiring $`N\to\infty`$. At
$`N=10^{6}`$ steps with $`B=1.0`$, a classifier extracts at most
$`U^{*}\approx 87`$ utility versus a verifier’s $`\approx 500{,}000`$ —
a $`5{,}700\times`$ gap. The dual conditions framework is immediately
operational at any finite horizon; the asymptotic condition simply
states the limiting case.

## 3 The Classification Impossibility

### 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1)

###### Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility).

Let $`P^{+},P^{-}`$ be distributions on $`\mathbb{R}^{k}`$ with
$`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ (absolute continuity). Suppose
$`D_{\alpha_{0}}(P^{+}\|P^{-})<\infty`$ for some $`\alpha_{0}>p/(p-1)`$.
Then for any sequence of binary classifiers with false acceptance rates
$`\delta_{n}\leq c/n^{p}`$ for some $`c>0,p>1`$:

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}<\infty\implies\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}<\infty
``` |  |

That is, bounded cumulative risk under any power-law schedule forces
bounded cumulative utility.

###### Proof.

Step 1. Let $`A_{n}=\{x:g_{n}(x)=\text{accept}\}`$. Then:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\int_{A_{n}}dP^{+}=\int_{A_{n}}\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}dP^{-}
``` |  |

Apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents $`\alpha>1`$ and
$`\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/(\alpha-1)`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\left(\int_{A_{n}}\left(\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}dP^{-}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot\left(\int_{A_{n}}dP^{-}\right)^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}
``` |  |

Step 2. Bound the first factor by extending the integration domain:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left(\int_{A_{n}}\left(\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}dP^{-}\right)^{1/\alpha}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}dP^{-}\right)^{1/\alpha}=\exp\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})\right)
``` |  |

using the definition
$`D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\int(dP^{+}/dP^{-})^{\alpha}\,dP^{-}`$.

Step 3. Setting $`\beta=(\alpha-1)/\alpha\in(0,1)`$ and
$`C_{\alpha}=\exp(\beta\cdot D_{\alpha})`$:

|     |                                                                |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \boxed{\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}}
       ```                                                             |     |

Step 4. If $`\delta_{n}=c/n^{p}`$ with $`p>1`$ (summable), then
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}c^{\beta}\sum n^{-p\beta}`$, which
converges iff $`p\beta>1`$. Choose $`\alpha\in(p/(p-1),\,\alpha_{0})`$
(valid since $`\alpha_{0}>p/(p-1)`$ by hypothesis), ensuring
$`p\beta>1`$ and $`D_{\alpha}<\infty`$. ∎

Scope and limitations of
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").
The impossibility is established for power-law risk schedules
$`\delta_{n}=O(n^{-p})`$ with $`p>1`$, which subsume all practically
relevant risk budgets (geometric, polynomial, or faster decay). For
slowly-decaying summable sequences (e.g., $`\delta_{n}=1/(n\ln^{2}n)`$),
the per-step bound
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ still holds
at each step, but $`\sum C_{\alpha}\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ can diverge
because $`\beta<1`$ — the Hölder exponent cannot compensate for the slow
decay. In such edge cases, the NP-optimal classifier can in principle
satisfy both dual conditions simultaneously, and the asymptotic
impossibility does not apply. This is an inherent limitation of the
Hölder-based proof technique, not an artifact of our analysis.

However, the practical significance of this gap is limited: the
*finite-horizon ceiling*
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
remains fully operative for all summable schedules, including these edge
cases. Even under the most favorable slowly-decaying schedule, total
classifier utility grows at most as $`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$ —
subpolynomial — while a verifier’s utility grows linearly as
$`\Theta(N)`$ (see
§[6](#S6 "6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
for exact bounds). The impossibility is therefore sharp for power-law
schedules; the finite-horizon gap is universal.

Two independent impossibility proofs
(Theorems [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
and [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility). ‣ 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
and the exact finite-horizon ceiling
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
supported by the information-theoretic rate bound
(Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound). ‣ C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
and sample complexity barrier
(Proposition [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Sample Complexity Barrier). ‣ C.4 Sample Complexity Barrier ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
confirm that the classification ceiling is robust and fundamental, not
an artifact of any single proof technique.

###### Remark (On the per-step bound).

The per-step bound
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ is a standard
f-divergence inequality \[[27](#bib.bib27)\]. The contribution is
*sequential composition*: under summability of $`\{\delta_{n}\}`$, this
elementary bound forces $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}<\infty`$. Two independent
proofs
(Theorems [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
and [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility). ‣ 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
and three complementary bounds
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"),
Propositions [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound). ‣ C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")–[2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Sample Complexity Barrier). ‣ C.4 Sample Complexity Barrier ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
confirm the coupling is robust and technique-independent.

###### Remark (Necessity of Distribution Overlap).

The assumption $`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ is structurally unavoidable: (i) if
safe and unsafe modifications were perfectly separable, the indicator
$`\mathbf{1}_{\text{supp}(P^{+})}`$ would be a zero-error oracle and no
gate would be needed; (ii) under full-support mutations and smooth
safety boundaries, transversality ensures every feature-space
neighborhood contains both safe and unsafe pre-images (see
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
for the full geometric argument); (iii) when the safety boundary is
piecewise smooth and $`\mu`$ is Gaussian,
$`D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})<\infty`$ in a neighborhood of 1. Empirical
confirmation: across three systems in \[[23](#bib.bib23)\], measured
$`\Delta_{s}\in[0.059,0.091]`$ — well below the separability threshold.

### 3.2 Exponent-Optimality (Theorem 3)

###### Theorem 3 (Exponent-Optimality of Hölder Bound).

For Gaussian distributions $`P^{+}=\mathcal{N}(\mu,I_{k})`$ and
$`P^{-}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{k})`$ with separation $`\Delta_{s}=\|\mu\|`$,
the Neyman–Pearson optimal classifier achieves
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta)+\Delta_{s})`$,
and the Hölder exponent $`\beta^{*}=(\alpha^{*}-1)/\alpha^{*}`$ (with
$`\alpha^{*}=1+2/\Delta_{s}^{2}`$) is minimax-optimal:

1.  1.
    No bound $`\mathrm{TPR}\leq C^{\prime}\cdot\delta^{\gamma}`$ with
    $`\gamma>\beta^{*}`$ is valid uniformly over
    $`\mathcal{P}(D,\alpha)=\{(P^{+},P^{-}):D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})\leq D\}`$.
2.  2.
    The ratio
    $`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)/(C_{\alpha^{*}}\cdot\delta^{\beta^{*}})\to 0`$
    as $`\delta\to 0`$ (the NP classifier decays faster than the bound;
    Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Theorem Exponent-Optimality: Mills’ Ratio Asymptotics ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
    but at deployment-relevant $`\delta\in[10^{-6},10^{-1}]`$, the ratio
    ranges from 0.1 to 0.9
    (Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Tightness Validation ‣ Appendix D Full Numerical Validation ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).

###### Proof sketch.

The NP likelihood-ratio test $`\mu^{T}x\gtrless t_{\delta}`$ yields
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta)+\Delta_{s})`$.
Asymptotic analysis via Mills’ ratio
(Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Theorem Exponent-Optimality: Mills’ Ratio Asymptotics ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
shows the NP classifier’s log-exponent matches $`\beta^{*}`$ as an upper
envelope: no valid impossibility bound can use a larger exponent. At
finite $`\delta`$ values relevant to deployment,
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}/\text{H\"{o}lder}`$ ranges from
$`\approx 0.1`$ (at $`\Delta_{s}=2.0`$) to $`\approx 0.9`$ (at
$`\Delta_{s}=0.1`$); see
Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Tightness Validation ‣ Appendix D Full Numerical Validation ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").
∎

###### Corollary 1 (Minimax Optimality).

The exponent $`\beta^{*}`$ is minimax-optimal over

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{P}(D,\alpha)=\{(P^{+},P^{-}):D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})\leq D\}\,;
``` |  |

any valid impossibility bound satisfies
$`f(\delta)=\Omega(\delta^{\beta^{*}})`$.

The bound is also tight for non-Gaussian distributions: across 8

### 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4)

We provide a fundamentally different proof of the classification
impossibility that avoids Hölder’s inequality and Rényi divergence
entirely, using only the Neyman–Pearson lemma and Tonelli’s theorem.

###### Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility).

Let $`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ with $`D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})<\infty`$ for some
$`\alpha>1`$. For any summable risk schedule $`\delta_{n}=c/n^{p}`$ with
$`p>1`$ and any sequence of classifiers:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq c^{1/p}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P^{+}}\!\left[P^{-}(L>L(X))^{-1/p}\right]<\infty
``` |  |

where $`L(x)=dP^{+}/dP^{-}(x)`$ is the likelihood ratio.

###### Proof sketch.

(1) By NP optimality,
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta_{n})`$.
(2) Define the counting function
$`N(\ell)=|\{n:c_{\delta_{n}}<\ell\}|`$; Tonelli’s theorem gives
$`\sum_{n}\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{P^{+}}[N(L(X))]`$.
(3) Bound $`N(\ell)\leq(c/P^{-}(L>\ell))^{1/p}`$. (4) Finiteness via
p-value density integrability. Full proof in
Appendix [A.8](#A1.SS8 "A.8 NP Counting Proof Full Details ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").
∎

The counting bound is strictly tighter than the Hölder bound: 1.76 vs
2.03 at $`\Delta_{s}=1.0,p=2.0`$ (13% improvement). See
Appendix [D.7](#A4.SS7 "D.7 Non-Gaussian Tightness Validation ‣ Appendix D Full Numerical Validation ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
for complete validation including non-Gaussian distributions.

## 4 The Verification Escape

### 4.1 Statement (Theorem 2)

###### Theorem 2 (Verification Escape).

There exists a verification-based gate achieving:

- •
  $`\delta_{n}=0`$ for all $`n`$ (zero false acceptance)
- •
  $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$ (unbounded utility)

### 4.2 Construction: Lipschitz Ball Verifier

Let $`\theta_{0}`$ be a controller verified safe on a defined operating
domain $`\mathcal{D}=\{(s_{i},t_{i})\}_{i=1}^{M}`$ of $`M`$ start–target
scenarios. Let $`m>0`$ be the *safety margin*: the minimum distance to
any obstacle across all scenarios:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
m=\min_{i\in[M]}\min_{t\in[0,T]}d(\text{traj}_{\theta_{0}}(t;s_{i},t_{i}),\text{obstacles})
``` |  |

Let $`L`$ be a (conservative) Lipschitz constant for the closed-loop
trajectory map with respect to controller parameters:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{(s,t)\in\mathcal{D}}\|\text{traj}_{\theta}(s,t)-\text{traj}_{\theta_{0}}(s,t)\|_{\infty}\leq L\cdot\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|
``` |  |

Ball verifier: Accept $`\theta`$ iff $`\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|<r`$, where
$`r=m/L`$.

###### Soundness.

If $`\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|<r`$, then the trajectory deviation is at most
$`L\cdot r=m`$. Since $`\theta_{0}`$ has margin $`m`$, the trajectory of
$`\theta`$ stays at distance $`>0`$ from all obstacles on all scenarios
in $`\mathcal{D}`$. No unsafe controller is accepted. ∎

###### Remark.

The construction uses standard tools (Lipschitz continuity, the triangle
inequality; cf. [Berkenkamp et al. 5](#bib.bib5)). The contribution is
the *quantified dichotomy* within the dual conditions framework: the
same safety task is provably impossible for any classifier
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
yet achievable by a simple $`O(d)`$ verifier. Classifiers face a ceiling
of $`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$ utility
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
while verifiers achieve $`\Theta(N)`$; the structural separation
(Proposition [4](#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4 (Structural Classification–Verification Separation). ‣ 5 The Separation Principle (Proposition 4) ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
establishes that this gap is architectural, not a matter of degree.

Coverage: For mutations
$`\Delta\theta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}=P(\|\Delta\theta\|<r)=P(\chi^{2}_{d}<r^{2}/\sigma^{2})
``` |  |

This is bounded away from zero when $`r\geq\sigma\sqrt{d}`$, i.e., when
$`m/(L\sigma\sqrt{d})\geq 1`$. For smaller mutation scales $`\sigma`$,
$`\mathrm{TPR}\to 1`$.

Computational cost: The gate checks $`\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|<r`$, an
$`O(d)`$ operation. No simulation, no oracle calls.

### 4.3 Domain-Restricted Safety

The verifier guarantees $`\mathcal{D}`$-safety: safety on a defined
operating domain $`\mathcal{D}`$, standard in formal methods (DO-178C,
ISO 26262). A natural objection is that this is weaker than what
classifiers *attempt* — generalization to unseen scenarios. We argue the
comparison is more symmetric than it appears.

Classifiers are equally domain-restricted, but probabilistically.
Classical generalization bounds \[[28](#bib.bib28), [4](#bib.bib4)\]
guarantee classifier accuracy only on the training
distribution $`\mathcal{P}`$: for a classifier with VC-dimension $`h`$,
the empirical risk converges to true risk at rate
$`O(\sqrt{h\log(n)/n})`$ *on samples from* $`\mathcal{P}`$. If the
operating domain shifts — new obstacle configurations, new task
distributions — the classifier requires retraining on the new
distribution to maintain its guarantees. This is the statistical
analogue of the verifier’s geometric domain restriction.

The guarantee types differ structurally. The verifier’s domain
restriction is *deterministic*: for all $`\theta\in B(\theta_{0},r)`$
and all scenarios in $`\mathcal{D}`$, safety holds with certainty
($`\delta=0`$). The classifier’s domain restriction is *probabilistic*:
for most $`\theta`$ drawn from the training distribution, the
classifier’s prediction is correct with probability $`1-\epsilon`$. The
verifier provides a *certificate*; the classifier provides a
*statistical estimate*. Both require re-validation if the domain
changes, but the verifier’s guarantee within its domain is exact while
the classifier’s is approximate.

Both gates face the same test. In the dual conditions framework, both
gates are evaluated on mutations from the same distribution
$`P^{+},P^{-}`$; neither has access to out-of-distribution mutations.
The
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
impossibility applies to *any* binary gate operating on these
distributions, regardless of how the gate was trained or whether it
generalizes beyond them. The comparison in
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
— classifier utility $`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$ vs. verifier utility
$`\Theta(N)`$ — holds within the shared operating domain.

Formal transformer Lipschitz bounds under LoRA perturbation
(Proposition [3](#Thmproposition3 "Proposition 3 (Transformer LoRA Lipschitz Bound). ‣ C.5 Formal Transformer Lipschitz Bounds ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
are stated in
Appendix [C.5](#A3.SS5 "C.5 Formal Transformer Lipschitz Bounds ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
(full derivation
in [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Transformer Lipschitz Derivation ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
enabling compositional verification at LLM scale.

## 5 The Separation Principle (Proposition 4)

###### Proposition 4 (Structural Classification–Verification Separation).

Under $`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$:

1.  1.
    For any classifier, $`\delta=0\implies\mathrm{TPR}=0`$.
2.  2.
    There exists a verification gate with $`\delta=0`$ and
    $`\mathrm{TPR}>0`$.
3.  3.
    The separation is strict: as $`\delta\to 0`$, classifiers satisfy
    $`\mathrm{TPR}\to 0`$
    (Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
    while the verifier maintains constant $`\mathrm{TPR}_{V}>0`$ at
    $`\delta_{V}=0`$
    (Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Verification Escape). ‣ 4.1 Statement (Theorem 2) ‣ 4 The Verification Escape ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).

###### Proof.

(i) If $`P^{-}(A)=0`$, absolute continuity gives $`P^{+}(A)=0`$.
(ii) The ball $`B(\theta_{0},r)`$ has $`\delta=0`$
(Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Verification Escape). ‣ 4.1 Statement (Theorem 2) ‣ 4 The Verification Escape ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
and positive Gaussian mass. (iii) By
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"),
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\text{class}}(\epsilon)\leq C_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\beta}\to 0`$,
while $`\mathrm{TPR}_{V}>0`$ is independent of $`\epsilon`$. ∎

Comprehensive experimental validation across 18 classifier
configurations (\[[23](#bib.bib23)\] §4.1–4.3), MuJoCo benchmarks
(\[[23](#bib.bib23)\] §4.5), and LLM-scale ball chaining
(\[[23](#bib.bib23)\] §5.7) is presented in the companion
paper \[[23](#bib.bib23)\].

## 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis

For practical deployment over $`N`$ steps with risk budget
$`B=\sum\delta_{n}`$, we establish the *exact* utility ceiling.

### 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5)

The Hölder–Jensen ceiling $`C_{\alpha}\cdot N^{1-\beta}\cdot B^{\beta}`$
(Appendix [C.6](#A3.SS6 "C.6 Hölder–Jensen Approximation ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
is not tight: it applies Hölder’s inequality to each step individually
and then uses Jensen to optimize allocation. By using the exact NP curve
directly, we obtain the *tight* ceiling.

###### Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling).

For $`N`$-step deployment with total risk budget
$`B=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n}`$, the exact maximum achievable utility is:

|     |                                                  |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       U^{*}(N,B)=N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)
       ```                                               |     |

where
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta)+\Delta_{s})`$
is the Neyman–Pearson optimal TPR. The optimal allocation is uniform
$`\delta_{n}=B/N`$ (by concavity of the NP curve and Jensen’s
inequality).

For Gaussian distributions with separation $`\Delta_{s}`$, the exact
growth rate is:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U^{*}(N,B)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{\exp\!\big(\Delta_{s}\sqrt{2\ln(N/B)}\big)}{\sqrt{\ln(N/B)}}\right)
``` |  |

which is subpolynomial: $`U^{*}(N,B)=o(N^{\epsilon})`$ for every
$`\epsilon>0`$.

###### Proof.

Step 1 (NP ceiling per step). By the Neyman–Pearson lemma, any
classifier at level $`\delta_{n}`$ satisfies
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta_{n})`$. For
Gaussians,
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta)+\Delta_{s})`$.

Step 2 (Optimal allocation). The ROC curve
$`\delta\mapsto\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)`$ is concave (a
standard property of NP classifiers under continuous likelihood ratios).
By Jensen’s inequality, for any non-negative
$`\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{N}`$ with $`\sum\delta_{n}=B`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta_{n})\leq N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n}\right)=N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)
``` |  |

This is achieved with equality iff $`\delta_{n}=B/N`$ for all $`n`$
(uniform allocation).

Step 3 (Asymptotic growth). Setting $`\delta=B/N`$, as $`N\to\infty`$
with $`B`$ fixed, $`\delta=B/N\to 0`$, and by Mills’ ratio:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(B/N)+\Delta_{s})\sim\frac{e^{-z^{2}/2}}{z\sqrt{2\pi}}
``` |  |

where $`z=\sqrt{2\ln(N/B)}-\Delta_{s}`$. Thus
$`U^{*}(N,B)=N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)`$ grows as

|     |                                                                    |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \frac{\exp\!\big(\Delta_{s}\sqrt{2\ln(N/B)}\big)}{\sqrt{\ln(N/B)}}
       ```                                                                 |     |

which is $`\omega(\log^{k}N)`$ for all $`k`$ but $`o(N^{\epsilon})`$ for
all $`\epsilon>0`$. ∎

Comparison of bounds (Gaussian, $`\Delta_{s}=1.0`$, $`B=1.0`$):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| $`N`$ | Exact ceiling $`U^{*}`$ | MI bound ($`\sqrt{N}`$) | Hölder–Jensen | Improvement |
| $`10^{2}`$ | 9.24 | 21.0 | 12.6 | 2.3$`\times`$, 1.4$`\times`$ |
| $`10^{3}`$ | 18.3 | 98.6 | 27.2 | 5.4$`\times`$, 1.5$`\times`$ |
| $`10^{4}`$ | 32.7 | 436 | 58.6 | 13$`\times`$, 1.8$`\times`$ |
| $`10^{5}`$ | 54.8 | 1835 | 126 | 33$`\times`$, 2.3$`\times`$ |
| $`10^{6}`$ | 87.2 | 7463 | 272 | 86$`\times`$, 3.1$`\times`$ |

The exact ceiling grows as $`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$, vastly slower
than $`\sqrt{N}`$ (MI bound) or $`N^{1-\beta}`$ (Hölder–Jensen). At
$`N=10^{6}`$, the MI bound is 86$`\times`$ loose and the Hölder–Jensen
ceiling is 3.1$`\times`$ loose.

is 13$`\times`$–86$`\times`$ tighter than
Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound). ‣ C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")’s

## 8 Discussion

### 8.1 Implications for Safe AI Deployment

Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
implies that any AI safety approach based on *classifying* modifications
— learned discriminators, anomaly detectors, neural safety critics —
faces a fundamental ceiling that is a mathematical consequence of
distribution overlap, not a limitation of architecture or training. To
the extent that RLHF reward models act as binary accept/reject gates
after thresholding, they inherit this ceiling (see
§[1](#S1 "1 Introduction ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
for the analogy and its limits; the formal results strictly apply to
binary gates on parameter mutations, not continuous reward scores). Over
sufficient iterations, either the false acceptance rate accumulates
(safety degrades) or the gate becomes overly conservative (utility
collapses).

We address five common concerns.

“$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$ is too weak.” Even this weak condition
cannot be met with bounded risk; strengthening it (requiring
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\geq c>0`$) forces
$`\delta_{n}\geq(c/C_{\alpha})^{1/\beta}`$ for all $`n`$, making
$`\sum\delta_{n}`$ diverge immediately.

“Finite-time systems don’t need $`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\infty`$.” The
finite-horizon tradeoff still applies: with risk budget $`B`$, total
utility grows subpolynomially
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
yielding an exact budget-allocation formula for finite deployments.

“Classifiers still extract nonzero utility.” Correct — but the ceiling
is subpolynomial ($`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$) versus the verifier’s
linear growth ($`\Theta(N)`$). At $`N=10{,}000`$ with $`B=1.0`$, a
classifier extracts $`U^{*}\approx 32.7`$ versus a ball verifier’s
$`U_{\text{ball}}=5{,}000`$ — a 153$`\times`$ advantage
(Appendix [D.10](#A4.SS10 "D.10 Tight Finite-Horizon Validation ‣ Appendix D Full Numerical Validation ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
“Impossibility” refers to satisfying the dual conditions simultaneously,
not to extracting any utility at all.

“What about an ensemble of diverse classifiers?” An ensemble accepting
iff all members agree is itself a classifier with acceptance region
$`A=\bigcap_{i}A_{i}`$.
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
applies: $`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$
constrains *any* binary decision rule. The impossibility is driven by
$`\beta<1`$, not classifier complexity.

“Isn’t the verifier’s conditional $`\delta=0`$ just as uncertain as a
classifier’s $`\delta\approx 0`$?” No — the failure modes are
categorically different. A classifier’s $`\delta>0`$ is a *mathematical
necessity* under distribution overlap
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"));
no amount of better training, data, or architecture can achieve
$`\delta=0`$ with $`\mathrm{TPR}>0`$
(Proposition [4](#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4 (Structural Classification–Verification Separation). ‣ 5 The Separation Principle (Proposition 4) ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
The verifier’s conditionality rests on the Lipschitz constant being
correct — a *falsifiable engineering claim* that strengthens
monotonically with better estimation. At $`d\leq 17{,}408`$, we provide
analytical bounds making $`\delta=0`$ unconditional; at LLM scale,
Proposition [3](#Thmproposition3 "Proposition 3 (Transformer LoRA Lipschitz Bound). ‣ C.5 Formal Transformer Lipschitz Bounds ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
proves the required bound *exists* and the open problem is computing it
tractably. The distinction is between an impossibility theorem and an
open engineering problem (see \[[23](#bib.bib23)\] §6.3).

The classification–verification separation mirrors behavioral testing
versus formal guarantees. Current AI safety practices — red-teaming,
evaluations, benchmarks — are primarily classification-based; as
self-modification becomes prevalent, verification-based safety will be
mathematically required.

###### Remark (Overlap under mutation-specific distributions).

Even if mutations come from a specific distribution $`\mu`$, the induced
conditional distributions $`P^{+}=\mu|_{\text{safe}}`$ and
$`P^{-}=\mu|_{\text{unsafe}}`$ satisfy $`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ by the
transversality argument
(§[3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
and the Hölder ceiling applies parametrically. The companion
paper \[[23](#bib.bib23)\] measures $`\Delta_{s}\in[0.059,0.091]`$
across three physical systems (§4.5), confirming low separation.

Connections to computational complexity (is satisfying the dual
conditions NP-hard?) and open problems (adaptive verification,
multi-agent extensions) are discussed in
Appendix [E](#A5 "Appendix E Computational Complexity and Open Problems ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").

### 8.2 When Our Results Do Not Apply

The impossibility
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
and the finite-horizon ceiling
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
require specific structural conditions. Practitioners should evaluate
whether their setting satisfies these conditions before applying our
conclusions:

1.  1.
    Perfectly separable distributions. If safe and unsafe modifications
    are perfectly separable in feature space
    ($`D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})=\infty`$ or equivalently
    $`P^{+}\perp P^{-}`$), the Hölder bound becomes vacuous and a
    perfect classifier can achieve $`\delta=0`$ with $`\mathrm{TPR}=1`$.
    This occurs when the safety boundary does not pass through the
    support of the mutation distribution. Our results apply only when
    $`P^{+}`$ and $`P^{-}`$ have overlapping support
    (§[3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
2.  2.
    Discrete or quantized parameter spaces. Our framework assumes
    continuous parameter perturbations from distributions with density.
    If the parameter space is finite or modifications come from a
    discrete set, the safe/unsafe partition may be exactly learnable and
    the distribution overlap condition may not hold.
3.  3.
    Large safety margins with small modification sets. If the system
    operates far from any safety boundary and modifications are bounded
    to a region where all points are safe, no gate is needed at all. Our
    results address the non-trivial regime where safety boundaries
    intersect the modification space.
4.  4.
    Fixed finite deployments where classifier utility suffices.
    Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
    shows classifier utility grows as $`\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))`$, which
    is non-zero. For short deployment horizons — say $`N\leq 100`$ with
    a generous risk budget $`B`$ — a classifier may provide adequate
    utility even under the Hölder ceiling. The impossibility becomes
    practically binding only when $`N`$ is large enough that the
    subpolynomial ceiling falls far below the linear growth a verifier
    achieves.
5.  5.
    Systems with non-overlapping mutation distributions by design. Some
    safety mechanisms engineer the modification space to avoid overlap —
    for example, restricting updates to a pre-verified subspace. If the
    restriction is enforced *before* the gate, the resulting conditional
    distributions may be separable, and a classifier within this
    restricted space may succeed. Our framework applies to the
    unrestricted case.

## 9 Conclusion

For power-law risk schedules $`\delta_{n}=O(n^{-p})`$ with $`p>1`$ — the
practically relevant regime — classifier-based safety gates cannot
satisfy the dual conditions under any architecture, training regime, or
data availability. This is established through two independent
impossibility proofs
(Theorems [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
and [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility). ‣ 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
proved exponent-optimal by the NP matching lower bound
(Theorem [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3 (Exponent-Optimality of Hölder Bound). ‣ 3.2 Exponent-Optimality (Theorem 3) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems");
Mills’ ratio asymptotics in
Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Theorem Exponent-Optimality: Mills’ Ratio Asymptotics ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),

## Appendix A Proof Details

### A.1 Hölder Inequality Verification

The conjugate exponents $`\alpha`$ and
$`\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/(\alpha-1)`$ satisfy
$`1/\alpha+1/\alpha^{\prime}=1`$. The Hölder inequality:

|     |                                                       |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \int fg\leq\|f\|_{\alpha}\cdot\|g\|_{\alpha^{\prime}}
       ```                                                    |     |

is applied with $`f=dP^{+}/dP^{-}`$ and $`g=\mathbf{1}_{A_{n}}`$, both
measured against $`P^{-}`$. Then:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{n}=\int_{A_{n}}dP^{+}=\int_{A_{n}}\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}dP^{-}=\int fg\,dP^{-}
``` |  |

By Hölder:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\left(\int\left(\frac{dP^{+}}{dP^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}dP^{-}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot\left(\int\mathbf{1}_{A_{n}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}dP^{-}\right)^{1/\alpha^{\prime}}
``` |  |

The first factor is
$`e^{(\alpha-1)D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})/\alpha}=C_{\alpha}`$ (by
definition of Rényi divergence). The second factor is
$`\delta_{n}^{1/\alpha^{\prime}}=\delta_{n}^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}=\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$.

### A.2 Rényi Divergence Convention

We use
$`D_{\alpha}(P\|Q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\int(dP/dQ)^{\alpha}\,dQ`$
following [van Erven and Harremoes \[27\]](#bib.bib27). This differs
from some references by a factor of $`(\alpha-1)`$ in the exponent. The
constant
$`C_{\alpha}=\exp\!\bigl(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})\bigr)`$
is finite whenever the Rényi divergence is finite, which requires
$`P^{+}\ll P^{-}`$ (absolute continuity) and sufficiently light tails of
the likelihood ratio.

### A.3 Theorem [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3 (Exponent-Optimality of Hölder Bound). ‣ 3.2 Exponent-Optimality (Theorem 3) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems") Exponent-Optimality: Mills’ Ratio Asymptotics

We provide the full asymptotic analysis establishing the
exponent-optimality of the Hölder bound.

Setup. For Gaussian $`P^{+}=\mathcal{N}(\mu,I_{k})`$,
$`P^{-}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{k})`$ with $`\Delta_{s}=\|\mu\|`$, the NP
optimal test rejects when $`\mu^{T}x<t_{\delta}`$, giving
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)=\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta)+\Delta_{s})`$
where $`\Phi`$ is the standard normal CDF. The Hölder bound with optimal
order $`\alpha^{*}=1+2/\Delta_{s}^{2}`$ and
$`\beta^{*}=1-1/\alpha^{*}=2/(2+\Delta_{s}^{2})`$ gives
$`C_{\alpha^{*}}=\exp(\Delta_{s}^{2}/2)`$.

Log-asymptotic analysis. As $`\delta\to 0`$, set
$`z_{\delta}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)`$ (so $`z_{\delta}\to+\infty`$).
Comparing the log-exponents directly:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\log\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)\sim-\frac{(z_{\delta}-\Delta_{s})^{2}}{2},\qquad\log(C_{\alpha^{*}}\delta^{\beta^{*}})\sim\frac{\Delta_{s}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\beta^{*}z_{\delta}^{2}}{2}
``` |  |

Dividing by $`\log\delta\sim-z_{\delta}^{2}/2`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\log\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)}{\log\delta}\to\frac{(z_{\delta}-\Delta_{s})^{2}}{z_{\delta}^{2}}=1-\frac{2\Delta_{s}}{z_{\delta}}+\frac{\Delta_{s}^{2}}{z_{\delta}^{2}}\to 1
``` |  |

while
$`\frac{\log(C_{\alpha^{*}}\delta^{\beta^{*}})}{\log\delta}\to\beta^{*}<1`$.
Since the NP classifier’s log-exponent (1) exceeds the Hölder bound’s
($`\beta^{*}`$), the ratio
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)/(C_{\alpha^{*}}\delta^{\beta^{*}})\to 0`$
as $`\delta\to 0`$ — the NP classifier decays *faster* than the bound.

Exponent-optimality. The key consequence:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(\delta)}{\log\delta}=1>\beta^{*}
``` |  |

The NP classifier achieves $`\mathrm{TPR}=\Omega(\delta^{1-\epsilon})`$
for all $`\epsilon>0`$, so any valid universal upper bound must have
exponent $`\leq 1`$. Meanwhile, the Hölder bound with $`\beta^{*}<1`$ is
valid. Therefore $`\beta^{*}`$ is the smallest exponent achievable by
any impossibility bound — it cannot be replaced by any
$`\gamma>\beta^{*}`$ without violating the NP classifier’s performance.
∎

Practical tightness. At finite $`\delta`$ values relevant to deployment
($`\delta\in[10^{-6},10^{-1}]`$), the ratio
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}/(C_{\alpha^{*}}\delta^{\beta^{*}})`$ ranges
from 0.1 to 0.9 depending on $`\Delta_{s}`$
(Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Tightness Validation ‣ Appendix D Full Numerical Validation ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
confirming that the bound is practically tight — the NP classifier
operates within one order of magnitude of the Hölder ceiling across the
deployment-relevant range.

### A.4 Lipschitz Ball Soundness Proof

Suppose $`\theta\in B(\theta_{0},r)`$ with $`r=m/L`$. For any scenario
$`(s_{i},t_{i})\in\mathcal{D}`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{t}d(\text{traj}_{\theta}(t),\text{traj}_{\theta_{0}}(t))\leq L\cdot\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|<L\cdot r=m
``` |  |

Since $`\theta_{0}`$ has margin $`m`$ (minimum distance to obstacles),
the trajectory of $`\theta`$ maintains positive distance to all
obstacles:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d(\text{traj}_{\theta}(t),\text{obstacle}_{j})\geq m-L\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|>0\quad\forall t,j
``` |  |

Therefore $`\theta`$ is $`\mathcal{D}`$-safe. ∎

### A.5 Information-Theoretic Bound Full Proof

Setup. At each step $`n`$, the gate
$`g_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}`$ induces a
binary channel from the safety label
$`S_{n}\in\{\text{safe},\text{unsafe}\}`$ to the gate decision. The
mutual information of this channel is:

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       I_{n}=I(g_{n}(\theta_{n});S_{n})=H(g_{n})-H(g_{n}|S_{n})
       ```                                                       |     |

Pinsker bound. The total variation between the gate’s conditional
distributions satisfies
$`\mathrm{TV}(P_{g|+},P_{g|-})=|\mathrm{TPR}_{n}-\delta_{n}|/2`$. By
Pinsker’s inequality:

|     |                                                                   |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \frac{|\mathrm{TPR}_{n}-\delta_{n}|}{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{I_{n}}{2}}
       ```                                                                |     |

Hence $`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\delta_{n}+\sqrt{2I_{n}}`$.

Summation. Summing over $`n=1,\ldots,N`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{2I_{n}}
``` |  |

By Cauchy–Schwarz:
$`\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{I_{n}}\leq\sqrt{N\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}}`$. Under the
bounded mutual information assumption $`\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}\leq I_{0}`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n}+\sqrt{2NI_{0}}\qed
``` |  |

### A.6 Sample Complexity Bound Full Proof

Setup. The safety gate at step $`n`$ is a binary classifier
$`g_{n}\in\mathcal{G}`$ (a hypothesis class with VC dimension
$`d_{\text{VC}}`$), trained on $`n_{\text{train}}(n)`$ labeled examples.

Step 1: By the fundamental theorem of statistical
learning \[[28](#bib.bib28), [13](#bib.bib13)\], with probability
$`\geq 1-\eta`$:
$`\text{err}_{\text{true}}\leq\text{err}_{\text{train}}+\sqrt{(d_{\text{VC}}\ln(2m/d_{\text{VC}})+\ln(2/\eta))/m}`$.

Step 2: Setting the bound equal to $`\epsilon_{n}/2`$ and solving:
$`n_{\text{train}}(n)=\Omega(d_{\text{VC}}/\epsilon_{n}^{2})`$.

Step 3: For $`\epsilon_{n}=c/n^{p}`$:
$`n_{\text{train}}(n)=\Omega(d_{\text{VC}}\cdot n^{2p}/c^{2})`$.

Step 4: Available data grows as $`n_{0}+kn`$; required data as
$`n^{2p}`$. The crossing point:
$`n_{\text{fail}}=\Theta((c^{2}k/d_{\text{VC}})^{1/(2p-1)})`$. ∎

### A.7 Transformer Lipschitz Derivation

We derive the per-layer Lipschitz constant for a pre-LayerNorm
transformer under LoRA perturbation of attention projections. Each layer
$`k`$ computes:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
y_{k}=x_{k}+\text{MHA}(\text{LN}_{1}(x_{k})),\quad z_{k}=y_{k}+\text{FFN}(\text{LN}_{2}(y_{k}))
``` |  |

LayerNorm bound.
$`\|J_{\text{LN}}\|\leq\|\gamma\|_{\infty}/\sqrt{\epsilon}`$ where
$`\epsilon`$ is the regularization constant.

Multi-head attention under LoRA. Under LoRA perturbation
$`\Delta\theta=(\Delta A_{q,p},\Delta B_{q,p})`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\Delta O_{p}\|\leq\frac{\|W_{v,p}^{0}\|\cdot\|W_{k,p}^{0}\|\cdot\|\text{LN}(x)\|^{2}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\cdot\sqrt{2}\cdot\max(\|A_{q,p}\|,\|B_{q,p}\|)\cdot\|\Delta\theta_{p}\|
``` |  |

For $`n_{\text{proj}}`$ LoRA-adapted projections per layer:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L_{k}^{\text{LoRA}}\leq\frac{\|\gamma_{k}\|}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\cdot\frac{\max_{p}\|W_{v,p}^{0}\|}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\cdot\sqrt{2n_{\text{proj}}}
``` |  |

Compositional escape. Instead of using the exponentially large product
$`L_{\text{full}}=\prod_{k}(1+L_{k})`$, we use the additive bound:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\Delta\text{output}\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}L_{k}^{\text{LoRA}}\cdot\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|\cdot\prod_{j>k}L_{j}^{\text{full,frozen}}
``` |  |

Since the frozen-layer products
$`\prod_{j>k}L_{j}^{\text{full,frozen}}`$ are constants that can be
precomputed once from the pretrained weights, define
$`\tilde{L}_{k}=L_{k}^{\text{LoRA}}\cdot\prod_{j>k}L_{j}^{\text{full,frozen}}`$.
The verification reduces to the per-layer ball check
$`\sum_{k}\tilde{L}_{k}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|\leq m`$, a conservative but
tractable $`O(d)`$ computation. ∎

### A.8 NP Counting Proof Full Details

Tonelli interchange. The interchange
$`\sum_{n}P^{+}(L(X)>c_{\delta_{n}})=\mathbb{E}_{P^{+}}[\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{L(X)>c_{\delta_{n}}}]`$
is justified by Tonelli’s theorem applied to non-negative measurable
functions with the counting measure on $`\mathbb{N}`$ and $`P^{+}`$ on
$`\mathbb{R}^{k}`$.

Counting function. $`N(\ell)=|\{n\in\mathbb{N}:c_{\delta_{n}}<\ell\}|`$
counts how many thresholds are exceeded. For $`\delta_{n}=c/n^{p}`$, we
get $`N(\ell)\leq(c/P^{-}(L>\ell))^{1/p}`$.

P-value density integrability. Writing $`U(x)=P^{-}(L>L(x))`$, the bound
becomes $`\mathbb{E}_{P^{+}}[U(X)^{-1/p}]`$. For $`p>1`$, the integrand
$`u^{-1/p}f_{U}(u)`$ is integrable near $`u=0`$ because the Gaussian
tail makes $`f_{U}(u)`$ decay super-polynomially. More generally, the
expectation is finite whenever the p-value density satisfies
$`f_{U}(u)=O(u^{\eta})`$ near $`u=0`$ for some $`\eta>1/p-1`$; this
holds for all distribution pairs with
$`D_{\alpha}(P^{+}\|P^{-})<\infty`$ for sufficiently large $`\alpha`$,
which is guaranteed by the hypothesis of
Theorem [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4 (NP Counting Impossibility). ‣ 3.3 NP Counting Impossibility (Theorem 4) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").
∎

### A.9 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling Details

Concavity of NP curve. The derivative
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}^{\prime}(\delta)=\phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta)+\Delta_{s})/\phi(\Phi^{-1}(\delta))`$.
The second derivative is negative for all $`\delta\in(0,1)`$ by
log-concavity of $`\phi`$, establishing concavity (see
also \[[13](#bib.bib13)\], Chapter 3).

Asymptotic formula. Using
$`\Phi^{-1}(\delta)\sim-\sqrt{2\ln(1/\delta)}`$ for $`\delta\to 0`$ and
Mills’ ratio:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U^{*}(N,B)\sim\frac{B\cdot\exp(\Delta_{s}\sqrt{2\ln(N/B)}-\Delta_{s}^{2}/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot 2\ln(N/B)}}
``` |  |

This grows as $`\exp(\Delta_{s}\sqrt{2\ln(N/B)})`$, which is
$`o(N^{\epsilon})`$ for every $`\epsilon>0`$ but $`\omega(\log^{k}N)`$
for every $`k`$. ∎

## Appendix B Relation to Known Results and Extended Related Work

### B.1 Relation to Known Results

The mathematical tools in this paper — Hölder’s inequality, Rényi
divergence, Lipschitz continuity, Fano’s inequality, VC dimension — are
well-established. The per-step bound
$`\mathrm{TPR}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta^{\beta}`$ is an instance of a
standard f-divergence inequality \[[27](#bib.bib27)\], and the
Neyman–Pearson lemma \[[16](#bib.bib16)\] establishes ROC tradeoffs for
individual hypothesis tests.

Our contribution is the *problem formalization* and the *structural
results* that emerge: (1) the dual conditions as a formalization of safe
self-improvement, (2) sequential composition creating an impossibility
for the *coupling* of bounded risk and unbounded utility, (3) the
tightness of this coupling, (4) its information-theoretic strengthening,
(5) the sample complexity barrier, and (6) the structural separation
between classification and verification.

An analogy clarifies the distinction. Arrow’s impossibility theorem uses
elementary social-choice axioms, each individually obvious, but their
*composition* yields a deep impossibility no voting system can escape.
Similarly, our per-step bound is standard, and the dual conditions are
individually natural. But the *coupling* creates a structural
impossibility with no analog in single-test hypothesis testing.

### B.2 Extended Related Work

Self-improving AI safety. The alignment literature discusses recursive
self-improvement \[[6](#bib.bib6), [25](#bib.bib25)\] and concrete
safety challenges \[[2](#bib.bib2)\] but lacks formal impossibility
results for the safety–utility coupling. [Christiano et al.
\[7\]](#bib.bib7) propose iterated amplification; [Leike et al.
\[14\]](#bib.bib14) formalize reward modeling.

Hypothesis testing and statistical tradeoffs. The Neyman–Pearson
lemma \[[16](#bib.bib16), [13](#bib.bib13)\] establishes optimal ROC
tradeoffs for individual tests. The novelty is *sequential composition*:
summability constraints on $`\{\delta_{n}\}`$ force summability on
$`\{\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\}`$.

Impossibility results in learning theory. No-free-lunch
theorems \[[30](#bib.bib30)\] show no classifier dominates across all
distributions. Rice \[[21](#bib.bib21)\] shows undecidability of
semantic properties. Our impossibility is for a *specific task* under
distribution overlap.

Information-theoretic bounds. Fano’s inequality and its

data processing inequality \[[19](#bib.bib19), [1](#bib.bib1)\] bounds

PAC-Bayes and sample complexity. [McAllester \[15\]](#bib.bib15) bound

Adversarial robustness. [Tsipras et al. \[26\]](#bib.bib26) prove

impossibility concerns *sequential composition*, not per-input
robustness.

Transformer Lipschitz bounds. [Virmaux and Scaman \[29\]](#bib.bib29)

### C.2 Non-Stationary Extension

###### Proposition C.2 (Non-Stationary Impossibility).

Let $`\{(P_{n}^{+},P_{n}^{-})\}_{n\geq 1}`$ be a sequence of
distribution pairs and $`\alpha\in(1,\infty)`$ with
$`\beta=1-1/\alpha`$. Suppose
$`\bar{D}:=\sup_{n}D_{\alpha}(P_{n}^{+}\|P_{n}^{-})<\infty`$. Then for
any sequence of classifiers $`\{g_{n}\}`$ with per-step rates
$`(\delta_{n},\mathrm{TPR}_{n})`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\bar{C}_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}\quad\text{for all }n
``` |  |

where $`\bar{C}_{\alpha}=\exp((\alpha-1)\bar{D})`$. Consequently, if
$`\sum\delta_{n}<\infty`$ then
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\bar{C}_{\alpha}\sum\delta_{n}^{\beta}<\infty`$,
and the dual conditions cannot be jointly satisfied.

###### Proof.

At each step $`n`$, the Hölder bound
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
proof, Step 1) gives
$`\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq C_{\alpha}^{(n)}\cdot\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ where
$`C_{\alpha}^{(n)}=\exp((\alpha-1)D_{\alpha}(P_{n}^{+}\|P_{n}^{-}))`$.
Since $`D_{\alpha}(P_{n}^{+}\|P_{n}^{-})\leq\bar{D}`$ for all $`n`$, we
have $`C_{\alpha}^{(n)}\leq\bar{C}_{\alpha}`$. Summing:
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\bar{C}_{\alpha}\sum\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$. For
power-law schedules $`\delta_{n}\leq c/n^{p}`$, we have
$`\delta_{n}^{\beta}\leq c^{\beta}n^{-p\beta}`$, which is summable iff
$`p\beta>1`$ (i.e., $`p>\alpha`$). For general summable
$`\{\delta_{n}\}`$, convergence of $`\sum\delta_{n}^{\beta}`$ follows
from Hölder’s inequality on finite horizons:
$`\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n}^{\beta}\leq N^{1-\beta}\cdot(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n})^{\beta}\leq N^{1-\beta}\cdot B^{\beta}`$
where $`B=\sum\delta_{n}<\infty`$. ∎

###### Remark (Coverage gap).

For power-law schedules $`\delta_{n}=c\cdot n^{-p}`$ with $`p>1`$, the
series $`\sum\delta_{n}^{\beta}=c^{\beta}\sum n^{-p\beta}`$ converges
iff $`p\beta>1`$, i.e., $`p>\alpha`$. For $`1<p\leq\alpha`$, the
stationary impossibility
(Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
applies but
Proposition [C.2](#Thmproposition100 "Proposition C.2 (Non-Stationary Impossibility). ‣ C.2 Non-Stationary Extension ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
does not — the non-stationary extension requires the strictly stronger
condition $`p>\alpha`$ (flagged in
§[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Classification Gates ‣ 2 Problem Setup ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")).
This gap narrows as $`\alpha\to 1^{+}`$ and vanishes for all practically
relevant fast-decaying schedules ($`p\geq 2`$). For the intermediate
regime,
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")’s
stationarity-free finite-horizon ceiling provides an alternative bound.

### C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound

###### Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound).

Let $`\{g_{n}\}`$ be a sequence of safety gates with per-step mutual
information $`I_{n}`$ and total budget $`I_{0}=\sum I_{n}`$. Then for
any $`N`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{TPR}_{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{n}+\sqrt{2NI_{0}}
``` |  |

This bound grows as $`\sqrt{N}`$, so it does not prove
$`\sum\mathrm{TPR}_{n}<\infty`$ — that follows from
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").
Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound). ‣ C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
complements
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")
by constraining the *rate* of utility accumulation via mutual
information. Full proof in
Appendix [A.5](#A1.SS5 "A.5 Information-Theoretic Bound Full Proof ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").

### C.4 Sample Complexity Barrier

###### Proposition 2 (Sample Complexity Barrier).

Let $`\mathcal{G}`$ be a family of binary classifiers with VC dimension
$`d_{\text{VC}}`$. For the gate to achieve $`\delta_{n}\leq c/n^{p}`$
with $`p>1`$, the required training set is
$`n_{\text{train}}(n)=\Omega(d_{\text{VC}}\cdot n^{2p})`$. If the system
generates at most $`k`$ new labeled examples per step, sample starvation
occurs at $`n_{\text{fail}}=O(k^{1/(2p-1)})`$.

This result is independent of
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Safety–Utility Impossibility). ‣ 3.1 Hölder–Rényi Bound (Theorem 1) ‣ 3 The Classification Impossibility ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"):
even if a classifier circumvented the Hölder bound, it would face sample
starvation. Full proof in
Appendix [A.6](#A1.SS6 "A.6 Sample Complexity Bound Full Proof ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").

### C.5 Formal Transformer Lipschitz Bounds

###### Proposition 3 (Transformer LoRA Lipschitz Bound).

For a pre-LayerNorm transformer with $`K`$ layers under LoRA
perturbation with rank $`r`$ on $`n_{\text{proj}}`$ attention
projections per layer, the per-layer Lipschitz constant w.r.t. LoRA
parameters is:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L_{k}^{\text{LoRA}}\leq\frac{\|\gamma_{k}\|}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\cdot\frac{\max_{p}\|W_{v,p}^{0}\|}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\cdot\sqrt{2\cdot n_{\text{proj}}}
``` |  |

Compositional verification checks
$`\sum_{k}L_{k}^{\text{LoRA}}\cdot\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|\leq m`$
(additive, $`O(d)`$) rather than the exponentially large product
$`L_{\text{full}}=\prod_{k}(1+L_{k})`$.

Full derivation in
Appendix [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Transformer Lipschitz Derivation ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems").

### C.6 Hölder–Jensen Approximation

For practical deployment over $`N`$ steps with risk budget
$`B=\sum\delta_{n}`$, applying the per-step Hölder bound and Jensen’s
inequality yields:

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       U_{\max}(N,B)=C_{\alpha}\cdot N^{1-\beta}\cdot B^{\beta}
       ```                                                       |     |

with optimal uniform allocation $`\delta_{n}=B/N`$. This bound is looser
than the exact NP-based ceiling
$`U^{*}(N,B)=N\cdot\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathrm{NP}}(B/N)`$
(Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling). ‣ 6.1 Tight Finite-Horizon Ceiling (Theorem 5) ‣ 6 Finite-Horizon Analysis ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems")),
which is 1.4–3.1$`\times`$ tighter at $`N=10^{2}`$–$`10^{6}`$.

### C.7 Multi-Dimensional Tradeoff Surface

Classifiers occupy: $`\mathrm{TPR}\leq C_{\alpha}\cdot\delta^{\beta}`$,
$`\mathcal{C}=O(d^{2})`$, $`n=\Omega(d_{\text{VC}}/\delta^{2})`$.

Verifiers occupy: $`\delta=0`$, $`\mathrm{TPR}>0`$ (domain-restricted),
$`\mathcal{C}=O(d)`$, $`n=0`$.

These regions are disconnected on the $`\delta=0`$ hyperplane.

The classifier and verifier regions are *disconnected* on the

$`\Omega(d_{\text{VC}}/\delta^{2})`$ samples and cannot reach

### D.6 Trained Classifier Ceiling

Across all 72 (classifier, $`\delta`$) pairs tested (4 classifiers
$`\times`$ 6 $`\delta`$ values $`\times`$ 3 separations), zero
violations of the Hölder bound were observed. Trained classifiers
achieve TPR ratios of 0.52–0.94 relative to the Hölder ceiling.

### D.7 Non-Gaussian Tightness Validation

Across 8 non-Gaussian families (Laplace, Student-$`t`$, Gaussian
mixture): min ratios 0.28–0.40, average ratios 0.54–0.70. The bound is
uniformly valid and tight across heavy-tailed and multi-modal
distributions.

LTC controller ($`d=240`$), $`L=13.75`$, $`r=0.0208`$. Inside-ball:
200/200 safe ($`\delta=0`$), $`\mathrm{TPR}=0.286`$.

### D.9 NP Counting Proof Validation

All 9 configs satisfy direct sum $`\leq`$ counting bound (ratios
0.33–0.89). Counting 13% tighter than Hölder at
$`\Delta_{s}=1.0,p=2.0`$.

### D.10 Tight Finite-Horizon Validation

The exact ceiling grows subpolynomially: from $`N=10^{4}`$ to
$`N=10^{6}`$ (100$`\times`$ increase in $`N`$), $`U^{*}`$ grows only
2.66$`\times`$. Uniform allocation optimal (Jensen). MI bound is loose
by 4–86$`\times`$.

## Appendix E Computational Complexity and Open Problems

### E.1 Connection to Computational Complexity

The information-theoretic bound
(Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound). ‣ C.3 Information-Theoretic Finite-Horizon Bound ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
and sample complexity barrier
(Proposition [2](#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Sample Complexity Barrier). ‣ C.4 Sample Complexity Barrier ‣ Appendix C Supporting Theoretical Results ‣ Information-Theoretic Limits of Safety Verificationfor Self-Improving Systems"))
connect to a broader question: is safe self-improvement computationally
hard? A natural extension is whether satisfying the dual conditions is
NP-hard.

### E.2 Open Problems

1.  1.
    Computational impossibility. Is satisfying the dual conditions
    NP-hard, beyond being statistically impossible?
2.  2.
    Adaptive verification. Can tighter verified regions (e.g.,
    ellipsoidal) maintain $`O(d)`$ checking? Ball chaining experiments
    in \[[23](#bib.bib23)\] provide an initial empirical answer.
3.  3.
    Multi-agent and continuous-time extensions of the dual conditions.
````
