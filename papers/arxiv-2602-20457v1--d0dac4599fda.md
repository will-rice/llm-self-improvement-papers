# Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models

Zimeng Li    Mudit Gaur    Vaneet Aggarwal Affiliation: Purdue
University

###### Abstract

We study online alignment of large language models under misspecified
preference feedback, where the observed preference oracle deviates from
an ideal but unknown ground-truth oracle. The online LLM alignment
problem is a bi-level reinforcement problem due to the coupling between
data collection and policy updates. Recently, the problem has been
reduced to tractable single-level objective in the SAIL (Self-Improving
Efficient Online Alignment) framework. In this paper, we introduce a
pointwise oracle uncertainty set in this problem and formulate an
oracle-robust online alignment objective as a worst-case optimization
problem. For log-linear policies, we show that this robust objective
admits an exact closed-form decomposition into the original loss
function plus an explicit sensitivity penalty. We develop projected
stochastic composite updates for the resulting weakly convex objective
and prove $`\widetilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})`$ oracle complexity for
reaching approximate stationarity.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as interactive
systems, where failures in instruction following or safety can have
immediate impact. A common alignment pipeline is reinforcement learning
from human feedback (RLHF), which updates a policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$
using pairwise preference feedback on sampled responses [Christiano et
al. (2017)](#bib.bib1); [Ouyang et al. (2022)](#bib.bib2); [Ziegler et
al. (2019)](#bib.bib37); [Stiennon et al. (2020)](#bib.bib38); [Bai et
al. (2022)](#bib.bib39). In practice, the feedback is produced by a
preference oracle $`P`$, which may be a pool of human annotators or a
learned reward/preference model [Ouyang et al. (2022)](#bib.bib2). Such
oracles can deviate from an idealized true oracle $`P^{\star}`$ in
structured ways, for example due to population heterogeneity across
users or temporal drift in labeling standards, often mediated by
style/value confounders (e.g., preferring verbosity over correctness),
thereby inducing preference shift [Poddar et al. (2024)](#bib.bib6);
[Ramesh et al. (2024)](#bib.bib5). In offline alignment with a fixed
preference dataset, this manifests as a train/test preference mismatch
between the dataset and the deployed user population [Xu et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib4). In online or on-policy alignment, the policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ controls the distribution of queried comparisons, so
systematic oracle deviations can be amplified by the feedback loop and
lead to over-optimization of oracle quirks rather than the intended
preferences [Skalse et al. (2022)](#bib.bib7); [Karwowski et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib8); [Eisenstein et al. (2023)](#bib.bib9); [Gao et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib40). Therefore, robustness should be modeled explicitly
through an uncertainty set and a worst-case objective, rather than via
simplistic i.i.d. label-noise assumptions.

Existing work on LLM alignment robustness largely falls into two
categories. On the one hand, online RLHF methods explicitly account for
the coupling between data collection and policy updates, using bilevel
formulations that reduce to tractable single-level objectives [Ding et
al. (2024)](#bib.bib10); [Bai et al. (2025)](#bib.bib12); [Zhang et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib11). However, these approaches typically assume the
oracle matches the modeling assumptions and do not provide
distributionally robust guarantees under oracle misspecification. On the
other hand, recent offline methods robustify direct preference
optimization (DPO) on a fixed dataset by solving a minimax problem over
a distributional uncertainty set, improving performance under preference
shift [Rafailov et al. (2023)](#bib.bib3); [Xu et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib4); [Wu et al. (2024)](#bib.bib13). These robust DPO
formulations are tailored to static datasets and do not address the
on-policy setting where the comparison distribution changes with
$`\pi_{\theta}`$, and where oracle perturbations can interact with the
evolving sampling distribution. This gap motivates a robustness model
that is compatible with online preference collection and still yields a
tractable objective and optimization theory.

The authors of [Ding et al. (2024)](#bib.bib10) proposed an approach,
Self-Improving Efficient Online Alignment (SAIL), that reduces the
bi-level reinforcement learning problem of LLM alignment [Chakraborty et
al. (2023)](#bib.bib14); [Gaur et al. (2025)](#bib.bib15) to an
efficient single-level first-order method using the reward-policy
equivalence approach. In this paper, we aim to consider this problem in
the presence of oracle misspecification. We introduce a pointwise
uncertainty set $`\mathcal{U}^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ that bounds the
deviation of the preference probability $`P(1\mid z)`$ from
$`P^{\star}(1\mid z)`$ for every comparison $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ that
may be generated under the policy-induced sampling distribution
$`d_{\theta}`$. We then define an oracle-robust objective
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ as the worst-case negative log-likelihood over
$`P\in\mathcal{U}^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$, which guards against worst-case
exploitation of structured oracle deviations in the on-policy feedback
loop. Although robust bilevel formulations are generally difficult,
under the log-linear preference model we show that
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ admits an exact closed-form decomposition into
the nominal loss $`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$ plus an explicit
robustness penalty $`\lambda R(\theta)`$, where
$`R(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\!\big[\lvert R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})\rvert\big]`$
is the expected absolute pairwise score and $`\lambda=\rho\beta`$. Since
the constrained objective can be non-smooth, we measure first-order
stationarity via the Moreau-envelope $`F_{\lambda_{env}}`$. By standard
envelope/proximal properties, an $`\epsilon`$-stationary point of
$`F_{\lambda_{env}}`$ implies that the associated proximal point is
$`\epsilon`$-nearly stationary for the original constrained problem (and
the iterate lies within $`\mathcal{O}(\epsilon\lambda_{env})`$ of the
proximal point). Using this stationarity surrogate, we obtain an
$`\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-2})`$ oracle complexity bound for
reaching an $`\varepsilon`$-stationary point of the envelope. Finally,
because $`\rho=0`$ recovers the nominal SAIL objective, our analysis
also yields a convergence-to-stationarity guarantee for optimizing
$`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$ as a special case. We summarize our main
contributions as follows.

- •
  We formulate oracle-robust online alignment by combining SAIL with a
  pointwise oracle uncertainty set $`\mathcal{U}^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$,
  leading to the robust objective $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ that
  optimizes against worst-case preference perturbations under
  policy-induced sampling.
- •
  We give an exact closed-form characterization of
  $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ as
  $`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta)`$ and interpret
  $`R(\theta)`$ as an explicit sensitivity penalty given by an expected
  absolute pairwise score term.
- •
  We show the resulting constrained robust objective is weakly convex
  and analyze projected stochastic composite updates using the
  Moreau-envelope as a smooth stationarity surrogate. We prove an
  $`\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-2})`$ oracle complexity bound for
  reaching an $`\epsilon`$-stationary point of the envelope. As a
  corollary, setting $`\rho=0`$ yields a convergence-to-stationarity
  guarantee for the original SAIL objective.

## 2 Related Work

#### Bilevel reinforcement learning and RLHF.

Bilevel optimization provides a principled abstraction for hierarchical
learning problems such as hyperparameter optimization and meta-learning,
where an upper-level objective depends on the solution of a lower-level
training problem ([Domke, 2012](#bib.bib41); [Maclaurin et al.,
2015](#bib.bib42); [Franceschi et al., 2018](#bib.bib22)). This
perspective is increasingly relevant to alignment: RLHF couples (i)
learning preferences/rewards and (ii) policy optimization (often
implemented with PPO/TRPO-style updates), and the alignment objective is
evaluated on data whose distribution is induced by the policy produced
by the lower-level optimization ([Christiano et al., 2017](#bib.bib1);
[Ouyang et al., 2022](#bib.bib2); [Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib23);
[Schulman et al., 2015](#bib.bib24)). PARL (Policy Alignment in
Reinforcement Learning) formalizes policy alignment in RL as a
stochastic bilevel program that explicitly accounts for
decision-dependent data collection at the upper level, and develops an
algorithm with finite-sample guarantees ([Chakraborty et al.,
2023](#bib.bib14)). Focusing on online LLM alignment, SAIL similarly
argues that the alignment process is underpinned by bilevel
optimization, and derives an efficient single-level first-order
surrogate via reward–policy equivalence, enabling iterative on-policy
data generation and self-improving alignment ([Ding et al.,
2024](#bib.bib10)). Complementing these algorithmic frameworks, recent
theory studies the statistical and computational limits of bilevel RL in
nonconvex settings; for example, [Gaur et al. (2025)](#bib.bib15)
establish sample complexity bounds for bilevel reinforcement learning
with parameterized settings. Related developments on general nonconvex
bilevel optimization further analyze and mitigate the cost of
hypergradient computation through penalty-based approaches ([Shen and
Chen, 2023](#bib.bib25); [Shen et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)).

#### Robust alignment under distribution shift.

A central challenge in alignment is robustness: preference data are
typically collected from a narrow, static source distribution, while
deployment-time preferences can vary across populations and drift over
time, causing brittleness for offline objectives such as DPO ([Rafailov
et al., 2023](#bib.bib3); [Son et al., 2024](#bib.bib27)). A recent line
of work imports distributionally robust optimization (DRO)[Rahimian and
Mehrotra (2019)](#bib.bib45) to explicitly hedge against such preference
shifts. In particular, [Xu et al. (2025)](#bib.bib4) propose
distributionally robust DPO with Wasserstein and KL uncertainty sets
(WDPO/KLDPO), provide sample-complexity characterizations, and develop
scalable gradient-based algorithms suitable for large-scale LLM
fine-tuning. Concurrently, robust variants of direct preference learning
consider alternative uncertainty sets and regularizers; e.g., [Wu et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib13) distributionally robustify DPO and empirically study
robustness to preference/data perturbations, and [Ramesh et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib5) optimize for worst-case group mixtures to handle
heterogeneous preferences. These methods connect robust alignment to
foundational DRO results and tractable reformulations ([Mohajerin
Esfahani and Kuhn, 2018](#bib.bib30); [Kuhn et al., 2019](#bib.bib29);
[Duchi and Namkoong, 2021](#bib.bib28)), as well as classical robust
RL/MDP formulations that optimize against worst-case transition models
([Iyengar, 2005](#bib.bib31)).

## 3 Problem Setup

Let $`X`$ be the prompt (context) space and $`Y`$ the response space.
For each parameter vector $`\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}`$,
the language model induces a conditional distribution (policy)
$`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)\in\Delta(Y)`$ for each $`x\in X`$, where
$`\Delta(Y)`$ denotes the probability simplex over $`Y`$. We view each
$`y\in Y`$ as a finite token sequence and assume $`\pi_{\theta}`$ is
generated autoregressively, i.e.,
$`\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)=\prod_{t=1}^{|y|}\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x,y_{<t}),`$
with the usual convention that generation terminates at an
end-of-sequence event.

We model preferences over response pairs by a (possibly unknown)
preference oracle
$`\mathcal{O}:=\Bigl\{P\Bigm|\ P(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)=1-P(y_{2}\succ y_{1}\mid x)\Bigr\}.`$
For $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in Z:=X\times Y\times Y`$, we write
$`P(1\mid z):=P(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)`$ and interpret the (stochastic)
preference label $`\mathbb{I}\in\{0,1\}`$ as
$`y\mid z\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\bigl(P(1\mid z)\bigr),\mathbb{I}=1\Leftrightarrow y_{1}\succ y_{2}.`$
The sampling procedure is policy-dependent: draw a prompt $`x\sim\mu`$
from a fixed distribution $`\mu`$ over $`X`$, then sample two responses
independently from $`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$,
$`x\sim\mu,\;y_{1},y_{2}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x).`$
This induces the data-generating distribution on $`Z`$,

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
d_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2}):=\mu(x)\,\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}\mid x)\,\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}\mid x).
``` |  | (1) |

Let $`P^{\star}\in\mathcal{O}`$ denote the (unknown) true oracle. Fix
$`\rho>0`$. For each $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in Z`$, define the pointwise
uncertainty set

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U^{W}_{z}(P^{\star},\rho):=\Bigl\{P\in\mathcal{O}\ \Bigm|\ \bigl|P(1\mid z)-P^{\star}(1\mid z)\bigr|\leq\rho\Bigr\}.
``` |  | (2) |

Although $`U^{W}_{z}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ is specified as a pointwise
(uniform) neighborhood in the scalar preference probability, it admits a
Wasserstein interpretation at fixed $`z`$[Peyré et al.
(2019)](#bib.bib44): viewing $`P(\cdot\mid z)`$ and
$`P^{\star}(\cdot\mid z)`$ as Bernoulli distributions on $`\{0,1\}`$
with ground cost $`c(y,y^{\prime})=|y-y^{\prime}|`$, the
$`1`$-Wasserstein distance satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
W_{1}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Ber}(P(1\mid z)),\mathrm{Ber}(P^{\star}(1\mid z))\bigr)=|P(1\mid z)-P^{\star}(1\mid z)|.
``` |  |

Hence, under binary support, the Wasserstein ball constraint
$`W_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)\leq\rho`$ is equivalent to the interval constraint
in ([2](#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

The corresponding global uncertainty set requires the pointwise
constraint to hold uniformly over all comparison triples:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ | $`\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{z\in Z}U^{W}_{z}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ |  | (3) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\Bigl\{P\in\mathcal{O}\ \Bigm|\ \sup_{z\in Z}\bigl|P(\cdot\mid z)-P^{\star}(\cdot\mid z)\bigr|\leq\rho\Bigr\}.`$ |  |  |

$`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ models an adversarial but uniformly bounded
misspecification of the (conditional) preference probability across all
prompts and response pairs that may be encountered under the
policy-induced sampling
in ([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

Fix a prompt (context) $`x\in X`$. The policy
$`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$ independently generates a pair of
responses $`y_{1},y_{2}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$, which are then
compared by a (possibly noisy) preference oracle. Although the latent
reward function $`r^{\star}(x,y)`$ underlying preferences is not
directly observed, we assume the oracle satisfies the Bradley–Terry (BT)
model[Bradley and Terry (1952)](#bib.bib43): for all $`x\in X`$ and
$`y_{1},y_{2}\in Y`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
P^{\star}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)=\sigma\!\bigl(r^{\star}(x,y_{1})-r^{\star}(x,y_{2})\bigr),
``` |  | (4) |

where $`\sigma(u):=(1+e^{-u})^{-1}`$ denotes the logistic sigmoid.

We impose a mild margin condition to ensure that all admissible oracles
remain valid. We state this non-degeneracy requirement in the following
assumption.

###### Assumption 1 (Nondegenerate true oracle and admissible radius).

There exists a constant $`\delta\in(0,1/2]`$ such that for all
$`(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in X\times Y\times Y`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta\ \leq\ P^{\star}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)\ \leq\ 1-\delta.
``` |  | (5) |

We restrict the oracle uncertainty radius to $`\rho\in(0,\delta)`$, so
that every preference oracle $`P`$ is nondegenerate, i.e.
$`\forall(x,y_{1},y_{2}),\ \forall P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\leq\delta-\rho\ \leq\ P(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)\ \leq\ 1-(\delta-\rho)\leq 1.
``` |  |

###### Remark 3.1.

Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Nondegenerate true oracle and admissible radius). ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
rules out *nearly deterministic* preferences: for every comparison
$`(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$, the oracle assigns nontrivial probability to either
outcome. When the oracle obeys the Bradley–Terry
form ([4](#S3.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
this condition is equivalent to a uniform bound on the reward gap.
Indeed, writing
$`\Delta r^{\star}:=r^{\star}(x,y_{1})-r^{\star}(x,y_{2})`$, the
monotonicity of $`\sigma`$ gives
$`\log\!\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\ \leq\ \Delta r^{\star}\ \leq\ \log\!\frac{1-\delta}{\delta},`$
and therefore

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bigl|r^{\star}(x,y_{1})-r^{\star}(x,y_{2})\bigr|\leq\log\!\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}.
``` |  | (6) |

Since $`y_{1},y_{2}`$ are drawn i.i.d. from
$`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$,
([6](#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Remark 3.1. ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
can be read as a structural condition on the comparisons induced by the
policy: the sampling procedure does not generate response pairs whose
latent rewards differ so drastically that the preference label becomes
essentially deterministic.

#### Background (SAIL).

SAIL is a preference-based RLHF framework for online alignment that
explicitly models the coupling between (i) learning from pairwise
preference feedback and (ii) updating the policy that generates the
responses being compared. In the online regime, the preference data
distribution is policy-dependent, and SAIL represents this dependence
via a bilevel program: a reward model $`r`$ is fit from Bradley–Terry
comparisons on responses sampled from the KL-regularized optimal policy
induced by $`r`$,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{(upper)}`$ | $`\displaystyle\min_{r}-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu,\,y_{i}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x),(y_{w}\succ y_{\ell})\sim P^{\star}}\Bigl[\log\sigma\bigl(r(x,y_{w})-r(x,y_{\ell})\bigr)\Bigr]`$ |  | (7) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\text{(lower)}`$ | $`\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\ \pi_{r}^{\star}\in\arg\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu,\,y\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x)}\Bigl[r(x,y)-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(\pi(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\cdot\mid x)\bigr)\Bigr],`$ |  |  |

Direct differentiation through the inner solution mapping
$`r\mapsto\pi_{r}^{\star}`$ requires hypergradient computations. SAIL
circumvents this by exploiting the reward–policy equivalence for
KL-regularized RL: any optimizer $`\pi_{r}^{\star}`$ satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_{r}^{\star}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z_{r}(x),
``` |  | (8) |

for a normalization $`Z_{r}(x)`$ independent of $`y`$.
Substituting ([8](#S3.E8 "Equation 8 ‣ Background (SAIL). ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
into the upper-level BT likelihood reduces the bilevel program to a
tractable single-level policy objective; parametrizing $`\pi_{\theta}`$
yields

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta):=-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu,\;y_{1},y_{2}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x),\;(y_{w},y_{\ell})\sim P^{\star}}\!\left[\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{w}\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\ell}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{\ell}\mid x)}\right)\right].`$ |  | (9) |

The single-level SAIL objective $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$ in Eq.
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ Background (SAIL). ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
evaluates a policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ under preference feedback generated
by the true oracle $`P^{\star}`$. In practice, however, we do not have
access to $`P^{\star}`$, and the observed preference labels may be
produced by a perturbed oracle whose conditional preference
probabilities deviate from those of $`P^{\star}`$. We model this
misspecification by allowing the data-generating oracle $`P`$ to range
over the global uncertainty set $`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ in Eq.
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
which enforces a uniform pointwise deviation bound across all prompts
and response pairs that may be encountered under policy-induced
sampling. Concretely, given $`\theta`$, we draw $`x\sim\mu`$ and
$`y_{1},y_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$,
and the oracle $`P`$ induces an ordered pair $`(y_{w},y_{\ell})`$
corresponding to the preferred and less preferred response. We then
define the oracle-robust alignment objective as the worst-case SAIL
value over $`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta):=\sup_{P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)}-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu,\;y_{1},y_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x),\;(y_{w},y_{\ell})\sim P}\Biggl[\log\sigma\!\Biggl(\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{w}\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\ell}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{\ell}\mid x)}\Biggr)\Biggr].`$ |  | (10) |

## 4 Proposed Approach

In this section, we first relate our oracle-robust objective
$`L^{W}_{\rho}`$ to the nominal SAIL objective $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$ by
showing that $`L^{W}_{\rho}`$ admits an *exact* decomposition into the
SAIL loss and an explicit regularization term. We then study the
regularity of the two components separately: we impose a smoothness
condition on $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$ and analyze the policy-dependent
penalty $`R(\theta)`$. Under mild assumptions, these results together
imply that $`L^{W}_{\rho}`$ is weakly convex.

### 4.1 Decomposition of $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$

Our robust objective $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ is defined via a
worst-case expectation over the global oracle uncertainty set
$`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$. The following assumption specifies the
log-linear SAIL comparison model and enables an exact reduction of the
inner worst-case problem to an explicit regularizer.

###### Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class).

Let $`\psi:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ be a known $`d`$-dimensional
feature map We consider the class of log-linear (softmax) policies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pi:=\left\{\pi_{\theta}:\ \pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)=\frac{\exp\bigl(\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y)\bigr)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in Y}\exp\bigl(\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime})\bigr)}\right\}.
``` |  |

For notational convenience, let $`\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\Theta`$
denote the fixed parameter of the reference (SFT) policy, i.e

|     |                                                     |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\;=\;\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}.
       ```                                                  |     | (11) |

###### Theorem 4.1 (Decomposition of $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$).

Recall $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$ denotes the non-robust SAIL
objective and $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ denotes the robust objective
defined by the worst-case oracle in $`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$.Define the
pairwise score

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2}):=(\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\top}\!\bigl(\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})\bigr),
``` |  |

and the robust penalty

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}\mathbb{E}_{y_{1},y_{2}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}\left[\bigl|R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})\bigr|\right].
``` |  | (12) |

Then under Assumptions
[1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Nondegenerate true oracle and admissible radius). ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
the robust objective admits the exact decomposition

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta),\qquad\lambda:=\rho\beta.
``` |  | (13) |

###### Proof sketch of Theorem [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1 (Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃)). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

Fix $`\theta`$ and write $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in Z`$. Define the pairwise
log-ratio score

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2}):=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{1}\mid x)}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{2}\mid x)}
``` |  |

and the two per-sample losses

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z):=-\log\sigma\!\bigl(\beta\,h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2})\bigr),
``` |  |

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z):=-\log\sigma\!\bigl(\beta\,h_{\theta}(x,y_{2},y_{1})\bigr).
``` |  |

Let $`p^{\star}(z):=P^{\star}(1\mid z)`$ and $`p(z):=P(1\mid z)`$. Then
the SAIL objective admits the representation

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Big[p^{\star}(z)\,\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)+\bigl(1-p^{\star}(z)\bigr)\,\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)\Big],
``` |  |

while the robust objective is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=\sup_{P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Big[p(z)\,\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)+\bigl(1-p(z)\bigr)\,\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)\Big].
``` |  |

Since $`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ enforces $`|p(z)-p^{\star}(z)|\leq\rho`$
pointwise, the inner supremum is separable across $`z`$ and (by
linearity in $`p(z)`$) is attained at an endpoint, yielding

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sup_{P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P(\cdot\mid z)}\big[y\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)+(1-y)\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)\big]=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P^{\star}(\cdot\mid z)}\big[y\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)+(1-y)\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)\big]+\rho\,\bigl|\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)-\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)\bigr|.`$ |  | (14) |

Taking $`\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}`$ gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\rho\,\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl|\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)-\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)\bigr|.
``` |  |

Finally,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell^{1}_{\theta}(z)-\ell^{0}_{\theta}(z)=\log\frac{\sigma(-\beta h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2}))}{\sigma(\beta h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2}))}=-\beta\,h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2}),
``` |  |

so the extra term equals
$`\rho\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\left[|h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2})|\right]`$.
Under the log-linear policy assumption (Assumption
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
$`h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2})=R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ , hence
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta)`$
with $`\lambda=\rho\beta`$. ∎

###### Remark 4.2 (Interpretation of the decomposition).

Theorem [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1 (Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃)). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
separates the robust objective into a nominal fitting term
$`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$ and an explicit robustness penalty
$`\lambda R(\theta)`$ that depends on the policy-induced sampling
distribution. The penalty $`R(\theta)`$ measures the expected magnitude
of the pairwise score $`R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ over i.i.d. response
pairs $`(y_{1},y_{2})\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$, and thus
quantifies sensitivity of the likelihood to adversarial perturbations of
the pointwise preference probability. The uncertainty radius enters only
through the linear prefactor $`\lambda=\rho\beta`$: increasing $`\rho`$
monotonically strengthens the penalty, while $`\rho\downarrow 0`$
recovers the nominal objective $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$.

### 4.2 Regularity of of $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$ .

Prior analyses of preference-based objectives often invoke stronger
global conditions (e.g., PL-type geometries [Gaur et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib15)). Here we treat $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$ under
the following smoothness assumption.

###### Assumption 3 (Smooth SAIL objective).

The SAIL objective $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$ has
$`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$-Lipschitz gradient:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\nabla L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)-\nabla L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta^{\prime})\|\leq L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\;\forall\,\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
``` |  |

###### Remark 4.3.

Assumption
[3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Smooth SAIL objective). ‣ 4.2 Regularity of of 𝐿^SAIL . ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
is a standard smoothness condition in first-order optimization, and it
is routinely imposed in analyses of gradient-based methods for both
convex and nonconvex objectives, including stochastic settings
([Nesterov, 2013](#bib.bib16); [Ghadimi and Lan, 2013](#bib.bib17);
[Bottou et al., 2018](#bib.bib18)). We adopt it here as a mild
regularity requirement on $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$.

### 4.3 Weak convexity of $`R(\theta)`$

It remains to understand the regularity of the robustness penalty
$`R(\theta)`$. Although $`|R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})|`$ is a convex
function of $`\theta`$ for fixed $`(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$, the expectation in
$`R(\theta)`$ is taken under the policy-dependent distribution
$`(x,y_{1},y_{2})\sim d_{\theta}`$ (Eq.
([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))).
Since $`d_{\theta}`$ itself depends on $`\theta`$, convexity of the
pointwise quantity $`|R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})|`$ does *not* carry over
to $`R(\theta)`$. This coupling can destroy convexity, as evidenced by
the following example.

###### Example 4.4.

Consider the simplest setting with a single prompt $`X=\{x\}`$, two
responses $`Y=\{a,b\}`$, and $`d=1`$. Fix $`\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}=0`$
and define features by $`\psi(x,a)=1`$ and $`\psi(x,b)=0`$, so that for
$`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})=\theta\bigl(\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})\bigr)\in\{0,\pm\theta\}.
``` |  |

Let the policy be $`\pi_{\theta}(a\mid x)=\sigma(\theta)`$ and
$`\pi_{\theta}(b\mid x)=\sigma(-\theta)`$, and sample
$`y_{1},y_{2}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)`$.
Then
$`|R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})|=|\theta|\mathbf{1}\{y_{1}\neq y_{2}\}`$, and
hence

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=|\theta|\Pr(y_{1}\neq y_{2})=2|\theta|\,\pi_{\theta}(a\mid x)\pi_{\theta}(b\mid x)`$ |  | (15) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=2|\theta|\,\sigma(\theta)\sigma(-\theta)=\frac{2|\theta|e^{\theta}}{(1+e^{\theta})^{2}}.`$ |  |  |

Convexity would imply
$`R(1)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(R(0)+R(2))=\tfrac{1}{2}R(2)`$, but using
([15](#S4.E15 "Equation 15 ‣ Example 4.4. ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
we obtain

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(1)-\tfrac{1}{2}R(2)=\frac{2e(e-1)(e^{3}-1)}{(1+e)^{2}(1+e^{2})^{2}}>0,
``` |  |

so $`R`$ is not convex in general.

Given the nonconvexity exhibited above, we control the curvature of
$`R(\theta)`$ through weak convexity: $`f`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex
if $`f(\cdot)+\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}`$ is convex. First we
introduce several assumptions required for the analysis.

###### Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features).

The response space $`Y`$ is finite, and the feature map satisfies
$`\|\psi(x,y)\|\leq B_{\psi}<\infty`$ for all $`(x,y)\in X\times Y`$.

###### Remark 4.5.

The boundedness in Assumption
[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
is standard in analyses of log-linear and softmax models [Agarwal et al.
(2021)](#bib.bib19); [Zhang et al. (2022)](#bib.bib20). Without loss of
generality, $`B_{\psi}`$ can be normalized to $`1`$ by rescaling the
feature map. However, we keep $`B_{\psi}`$ explicit to highlight its
impact on the weak convexity constant.

###### Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set).

The feasible set $`\Theta`$ is nonempty, closed, convex, and bounded. In
particular, with $`\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ as in
Eq.([11](#S4.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
D\ :=\ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\|<\infty.
``` |  |

###### Remark 4.6.

Assumption
[5](#Thmassumption5 "Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
also ensures that the Euclidean projection operator onto $`\Theta`$,
$`\Pi_{\Theta}(u):=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\theta-u\|_{2},`$ is
well-defined and nonexpansive[Bauschke and Combettes
(2020)](#bib.bib21).

###### Theorem 4.7 (Weak convexity of the robust penalty).

Under Assumptions
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[5](#Thmassumption5 "Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
the robust penalty $`R:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}`$ defined in Eq.
([12](#S4.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ Theorem 4.1 (Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃)). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
is $`\kappa_{R}`$-weakly convex on $`\Theta`$, with

|     |                                               |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \kappa_{R}\leq 16B_{\psi}^{2}+4DB_{\psi}^{3}.
       ```                                            |     |

###### Proof sketch of Theorem [4.7](#S4.Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 4.7 (Weak convexity of the robust penalty). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

Write $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ and
$`\Pi_{\theta}(z):=\mu(x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}|x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}|x)`$,
so $`R(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\Pi_{\theta}}|s_{\theta}(z)|`$ with
$`s_{\theta}(z):=(\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\top}(\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2}))`$.
First, we smooth the absolute value via
$`\varphi_{\varepsilon}(u):=\sqrt{u^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}`$ and considers
$`R_{\varepsilon}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\Pi_{\theta}}[\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s_{\theta}(z))]`$.
Since $`Y`$ is finite , $`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is a finite sum and
derivatives can be exchanged with expectation. A log-derivative
(score-function) calculation expresses
$`\nabla^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)`$ as an expectation of terms
involving: (i) derivatives of $`\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ s_{\theta}`$,
(ii) the score $`S_{\theta}(z):=\nabla_{\theta}\log\Pi_{\theta}(z)`$ and
its Jacobian. Bounded features bounds $`\|S_{\theta}(z)\|`$ and
$`\|\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}`$, while bounded
parameter set bound $`\|\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})\|`$ and
$`|s_{\theta}(z)|`$ uniformly on $`\Theta`$. Combining these bounds
yields a uniform lower bound
$`\nabla^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\succeq-\kappa_{\varepsilon}I`$ with
$`\kappa_{\varepsilon}:=8GB_{\psi}+4MDB_{\psi}+2M\varepsilon`$. Hence
$`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is $`\kappa_{\varepsilon}`$-weakly convex, and
letting $`\varepsilon\downarrow 0`$ preserves weak convexity with
$`\kappa_{R}\leq 16B_{\psi}^{2}+4DB_{\psi}^{3}`$. ∎

###### Theorem 4.8 (Weak convexity of the composite objective ).

Under Assumptions
[1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Nondegenerate true oracle and admissible radius). ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Smooth SAIL objective). ‣ 4.2 Regularity of of 𝐿^SAIL . ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[5](#Thmassumption5 "Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
the robust objective
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta)`$ is
$`\kappa`$-weakly convex on $`\Theta`$, with

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\kappa:=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}+\lambda\kappa_{R},\qquad\lambda=\rho\beta.
``` |  |

###### Proof sketch.

By Assumption
[3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Smooth SAIL objective). ‣ 4.2 Regularity of of 𝐿^SAIL . ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$ has $`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$-Lipschitz gradient and
is therefore $`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$-weakly convex. By Theorem
[4.7](#S4.Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 4.7 (Weak convexity of the robust penalty). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`R`$ is $`\kappa_{R}`$-weakly convex on $`\Theta`$, so $`\lambda R`$ is
$`(\lambda\kappa_{R})`$-weakly convex. The sum of weakly convex
functions is weakly convex with parameter given by the sum of the
parameters, yielding $`\kappa=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}+\lambda\kappa_{R}`$ for
$`L^{W}_{\rho}=L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}+\lambda R`$ . ∎

### 4.4 Algorithm development

We optimize the oracle-robust alignment objective
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ over the bounded convex parameter set
$`\Theta`$. To enforce feasibility explicitly, we consider the
constrained objective

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(\theta)\ :=\ L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)+I_{\Theta}(\theta),\qquad I_{\Theta}(\theta)\ :=\ \begin{cases}0,&\theta\in\Theta,\\
+\infty,&\theta\notin\Theta.\end{cases}
``` |  | (16) |

###### Remark 4.9.

By Theorem 4.4, $`L^{W}_{\rho}`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex on
$`\Theta`$. Since $`I_{\Theta}`$ is convex, the constrained objective
$`F`$
in ([16](#S4.E16 "Equation 16 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
is also $`\kappa`$-weakly convex with the same constant $`\kappa`$.

Because $`F`$ may be nonsmooth and only weakly convex, we measure
first-order stationarity using the Moreau envelope[Moreau
(1965)](#bib.bib46).

###### Definition 4.10 (Moreau envelope and proximal point).

Fix $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$. The Moreau envelope of
$`F`$ with parameter $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\ :=\ \min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{F(u)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-\theta\|^{2}\right\},
``` |  | (17) |

and the associated proximal point mapping is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta)\ :=\ \arg\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{F(u)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-\theta\|^{2}\right\}.
``` |  | (18) |

When convenient, we write
$`\hat{\theta}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta)`$.

###### Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ([Davis and Drusvyatskiy, 2018](#bib.bib36))).

Assume $`F`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex and bounded below, and let
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$. Then:

1.  1.
    $`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$ is finite and continuously
    differentiable on $`\mathbb{R}^{d}`$.
2.  2.
    Its gradient is
    $`\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\bigl(\theta-\hat{\theta}\bigr),`$
    and in particular,

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|\ =\ \lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|.
    ``` |  | (19) |
3.  3.
    The gradient $`\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$ is Lipschitz with
    constant

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    L_{\mathrm{env}}\ :=\ \frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})}.
    ``` |  | (20) |
4.  4.
    (Approximate stationarity) For
    $`\hat{\theta}=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta)`$,

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta})\bigr)\ \leq\ \|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|.
    ``` |  | (21) |

Lemma [4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
motivates $`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|`$ as a smooth
stationarity measure: controlling
$`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|`$ ensures (i) $`\theta`$
is close to its proximal point $`\hat{\theta}`$
via ([19](#S4.E19 "Equation 19 ‣ Item 2 ‣ Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
and (ii) $`\hat{\theta}`$ is nearly stationary for the original
constrained problem
via ([21](#S4.E21 "Equation 21 ‣ Item 4 ‣ Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
The envelope parameter $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}`$ governs the
smoothing–bias trade-off: smaller $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}`$ reduces
smoothing bias but increases $`L_{\mathrm{env}}`$
in ([20](#S4.E20 "Equation 20 ‣ Item 3 ‣ Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

###### Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles).

Let $`d_{\theta}`$ denote the policy-induced sampling distribution on
$`Z=X\times Y\times Y`$ from Eq.
([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
At iteration $`t`$,
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
samples a mini-batch $`Z_{t}=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{B}`$ with i.i.d. draws
$`z_{i}\sim d_{\theta_{t}}`$. There exist measurable mappings

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G^{\mathrm{SAIL}}:\Theta\times Z^{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad G^{R}:\Theta\times Z^{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},
``` |  |

such that for all $`\theta\in\Theta`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\!\left[G^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta;Z_{t})\mid\theta\right]=\nabla L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta),\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[G^{R}(\theta;Z_{t})\mid\theta\right]\in\partial R(\theta).
``` |  |

Moreover, there exist constants
$`\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{SAIL}},\sigma^{2}_{R}<\infty`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl\|G^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta;Z_{t})-\nabla L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\bigr\|^{2}\mid\theta\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{SAIL}}}{B},
``` |  |

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|

``` math
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\operatorname{dist}^{2}\!\Bigl(G^{R}(\theta;Z_{t}),\partial R(\theta)\Bigr)\mid\theta\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}_{R}}{B}.
``` |  |

Finally define the composite direction $`G(\theta;Z_{t})`$ as
in ([22](#S4.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

###### Remark 4.12.

Assumption [6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
instantiates the standard *stochastic first-order oracle* model. Such
assumptions are ubiquitous in the analysis of stochastic approximation
and stochastic (sub)gradient methods; see, e.g.,[Ghadimi and Lan
(2013)](#bib.bib17); [Agarwal et al. (2021)](#bib.bib19); [Chen et al.

We use a projected stochastic composite gradient method to minimize
$`F(\theta)`$
in ([16](#S4.E16 "Equation 16 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
At iterate $`\theta_{t}`$, we sample prompts and response pairs
according to the policy-induced sampling in
Eq.([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")):
draw $`x\sim\mu`$ and then
$`y_{1},y_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot\mid x)`$,
forming a triple $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$. For the SAIL term
$`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$, we additionally query a preference label
from the oracle, which determines the ordered pair $`(y_{w},y_{\ell})`$
(equivalently, a Bernoulli label $`y\in\{0,1\}`$ indicating whether
$`y_{1}\succ y_{2}`$). Using a mini-batch $`Z_{t}`$ of $`B`$ i.i.d.
triples, we form a stochastic gradient estimator for
$`\nabla L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta_{t})`$ and a stochastic (sub)gradient
estimator for the robust penalty $`R(\theta_{t})`$ , and combine them
with weight $`\lambda=\rho\beta`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\ :=\ G^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta_{t};Z_{t})+\lambda\,G^{R}(\theta_{t};Z_{t}).
``` |  | (22) |

The constrained formulation then yields the projected update

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta_{t+1}\ :=\ \mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}\!\left(\theta_{t}-\eta\,G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\right),
``` |  | (23) |

where $`\mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}(v):=\arg\min_{u\in\Theta}\|u-v\|`$ and
$`\eta>0`$ is a stepsize. The projection ensures $`\theta_{t}\in\Theta`$
for all $`t`$, so the composite objective $`F=L^{W}_{\rho}+I_{\Theta}`$
is well-defined along the iterates.

1:  Input: $`\theta_{0}\in\Theta`$, stepsize $`\eta>0`$, horizon $`T`$,
batch size $`B`$, weight $`\lambda=\rho\beta`$.

2:  for $`t=0,1,\dots,T-1`$ do

3:   Sample a mini-batch $`Z_{t}=\{(x_{i},y_{1,i},y_{2,i})\}_{i=1}^{B}`$
with $`x_{i}\sim\mu`$ and
$`y_{1,i},y_{2,i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot\mid x_{i})`$.

4:   Query preference labels for the sampled pairs to construct
$`(y_{w,i},y_{\ell,i})`$ (equivalently $`y_{i}\in\{0,1\}`$).

5:   Compute stochastic oracles $`G^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta_{t};Z_{t})`$
and $`G^{R}(\theta_{t};Z_{t})`$, and form
$`G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})=G^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta_{t};Z_{t})+\lambda G^{R}(\theta_{t};Z_{t})`$.

6:   Update
$`\theta_{t+1}\leftarrow\mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}\!\bigl(\theta_{t}-\eta\,G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\bigr)`$.

7:  end for

8:  Sample $`R\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,1,\dots,T-1\}`$.

9:  Output: $`\theta_{R}`$ .

## 6 On the practical role of the proximal point

Our analysis measures stationarity of the *constrained* robust objective

|     |                                                         |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       F(\theta)\ :=\ L_{\rho}^{W}(\theta)+I_{\Theta}(\theta),
       ```                                                      |     |

via the Moreau envelope $`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$ with parameter
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$ (Definition
[4.10](#S4.Thmtheorem10 "Definition 4.10 (Moreau envelope and proximal point). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
where $`\kappa`$ is the weak convexity constant from Theorem
[4.8](#S4.Thmtheorem8 "Theorem 4.8 (Weak convexity of the composite objective ). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").
Algorithm 1 outputs a uniformly random iterate $`\theta_{R}`$. The
theory (Theorem
[5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2 (Convergence rate for the Moreau envelope). ‣ 5.1 Convergence analysis ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
certifies *envelope stationarity* at $`\theta_{R}`$ by controlling
$`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$, rather than
directly bounding $`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta_{R}))`$. The
interpretation is instead through the associated proximal point

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}_{R}\ :=\ \operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta_{R})\ \in\ \arg\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big\{F(u)+\tfrac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-\theta_{R}\|_{2}^{2}\Big\},
``` |  |

which is unique since $`F`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}<1/\kappa`$.

Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
provides the key link between envelope stationarity and proximity:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\theta_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}\ =\ \lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2},
``` |  |

and moreover
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta}_{R}))\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$.
Thus, small $`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$
simultaneously implies (i) $`\theta_{R}`$ lies in a small neighborhood
of $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$, and (ii) $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ is nearly
first-order stationary for the original constrained objective.

A practical issue is that computing $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ exactly still
requires solving the (generally nontrivial) strongly convex proximal
subproblem above. Even when
$`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$ is small
(hence $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ is nearby), proximal operators frequently do
not admit closed forms and are evaluated via iterative inner solvers,
which can be expensive in realistic models [Salzo and Villa
(2012)](#bib.bib33).

Lemma
[D.1](#A4.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
shows that while
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta))\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|`$
always holds, under $`\kappa`$-weak convexity alone there is *no*
universal constant $`C`$ such that
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta))\leq C\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|`$
for all $`\theta`$ (even when $`\kappa=0`$). This motivates phrasing
guarantees in terms of the proximal point $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ rather
than the raw iterate $`\theta_{R}`$.

In practice, one can compute an *inexact* proximal refinement
$`\bar{\theta}_{R}\approx\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ via a warm-started inner
loop on
$`\Psi_{R}(\theta):=F(\theta)+\tfrac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|\theta-\theta_{R}\|_{2}^{2}`$,where
proximal subproblems are solved only up to a prescribed accuracy (e.g.,
[Schmidt et al.
2011](#bib.bib32)).Appendix [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
formalizes this viewpoint for our setting and provides a residual-based
stopping rule. Lemma
[D.3](#A4.Thmtheorem3 "Lemma D.3 (Stationarity of an inexact proximal point). ‣ A simple inner-loop solver. ‣ D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
yields the practical takeaway: if $`\bar{\theta}_{R}`$ satisfies the
proximal residual condition
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial\Psi_{R}(\bar{\theta}_{R}))\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{prox}}`$,
then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\bar{\theta}_{R}))\ \leq\ \|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}\ +\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{prox}}\ +\ \tfrac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}.
``` |  |

Consequently, controlling
$`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$ together with
the inexactness terms $`\varepsilon_{\mathrm{prox}}`$ and
$`\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}`$ yields an explicit
near-stationarity certificate for the original objective $`F`$.

## 7 Conclusion

We studied *oracle-robust* online alignment of language models, where
preference feedback is collected on-policy but the true preference
oracle can deviate from the assumed model in a structured, worst-case
manner. We introduced a pointwise oracle uncertainty set
$`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ and defined the robust objective
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ as the worst-case negative log-likelihood over
$`P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$. Our main novelty is an *exact
closed-form decomposition* of this otherwise difficult minimax
objective:
$`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta)`$
with $`\lambda=\rho\beta`$; We cast the constrained problem as
minimizing $`F(\theta)=L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)+I_{\Theta}(\theta)`$,
analyze it as a weakly convex composite objective, and measure
stationarity using the Moreau envelope $`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$.
We establish a $`\widetilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})`$ stochastic-oracle
(sample) complexity for reaching an $`\varepsilon`$-stationary point of
$`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$, which in turn implies proximity to a
nearly stationary point of the original constrained robust objective; as
a special case, setting $`\rho=0`$ recovers a
convergence-to-stationarity guarantee for optimizing
$`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$.

## Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$

### A.1 A pointwise maximization identity

###### Lemma A.1 (Pointwise worst-case Bernoulli perturbation).

Fix any $`p^{\star}\in[0,1]`$, any $`\rho\geq 0`$ such that
$`[p^{\star}-\rho,p^{\star}+\rho]\subseteq[0,1]`$, and any real numbers
$`a,b\in\mathbb{R}`$. Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{p\in[p^{\star}-\rho,p^{\star}+\rho]}\Bigl(p\,a+(1-p)\,b\Bigr)=p^{\star}a+(1-p^{\star})b+\rho\,|a-b|.
``` |  | (26) |

Moreover, an optimizer is $`p^{\star}+\rho`$ if $`a\geq b`$ and
$`p^{\star}-\rho`$ if $`a<b`$.

###### Proof.

The map $`p\mapsto p\,a+(1-p)\,b=b+p(a-b)`$ is affine in $`p`$, hence it
attains its maximum over the interval
$`[p^{\star}-\rho,p^{\star}+\rho]`$ at an endpoint. If $`a-b\geq 0`$ the
maximizer is $`p^{\star}+\rho`$, giving value
$`b+(p^{\star}+\rho)(a-b)=p^{\star}a+(1-p^{\star})b+\rho(a-b)`$. If
$`a-b<0`$ the maximizer is $`p^{\star}-\rho`$, giving
$`b+(p^{\star}-\rho)(a-b)=p^{\star}a+(1-p^{\star})b+\rho(b-a)`$.
Combining the two cases
yields ([26](#A1.E26 "Equation 26 ‣ Lemma A.1 (Pointwise worst-case Bernoulli perturbation). ‣ A.1 A pointwise maximization identity ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
∎

### A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1

###### Proof of Theorem 4.1.

Recall: (i) the policy-dependent sampling law $`d_{\theta}`$ on
$`\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}`$ (Eq.
([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
(ii) the pointwise oracle uncertainty set $`U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ (Eq.
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))),
(iii) the robust objective $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ (Eq.
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ Background (SAIL). ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))),
and (iv) the SAIL objective $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$.

#### Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in $`p(z)`$.

For $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$, let
$`p(z):=P(1\mid z)=P(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)`$ and
$`p^{\star}(z):=P^{\star}(1\mid z)`$. By Eq.
([3](#S3.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
$`P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)`$ iff for all $`z`$,

|     |                              |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       |p(z)-p^{\star}(z)|\leq\rho.
       ```                           |     | (27) |

Assumption 1 ensures $`p^{\star}(z)\in[\delta,1-\delta]`$ and
$`\rho\in(0,\delta)`$, so
$`[p^{\star}(z)-\rho,p^{\star}(z)+\rho]\subseteq[0,1]`$ for all $`z`$.

Define the pairwise logit as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{\theta}(z)\equiv h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2}):=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{1}\mid x)}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y_{2}\mid x)}.
``` |  |

For notational convenience, set the two label-conditional losses

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z):=-\log\sigma\bigl(\beta\,h_{\theta}(z)\bigr),\qquad\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z):=-\log\sigma\bigl(\beta\,h_{\theta}(x,y_{2},y_{1})\bigr)=-\log\sigma\bigl(-\beta\,h_{\theta}(z)\bigr),
``` |  | (28) |

where we used $`h_{\theta}(x,y_{2},y_{1})=-h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$.

Then, for any oracle $`P`$ with Bernoulli parameter $`p(z)`$, the
conditional risk equals

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P(\cdot\mid z)}\bigl[\ell_{\theta}(z,y)\bigr]=p(z)\,\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)+(1-p(z))\,\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)=:\ell\bigl(p(z);h_{\theta}(z)\bigr).
``` |  |

Therefore $`L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ can be written as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=\sup_{P\in U^{W}(P^{\star},\rho)}\ \mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Bigl[p(z)\,\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)+(1-p(z))\,\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)\Bigr].
``` |  | (29) |

Because the
constraint ([27](#A1.E27 "Equation 27 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
is pointwise in $`z`$ and the objective is an integral (expectation) of
a pointwise affine function of $`p(z)`$, the worst-case oracle can be
chosen pointwise in $`z`$. Equivalently, the supremum
in ([29](#A1.E29 "Equation 29 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
equals the expectation of the pointwise supremum:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\left[\ \sup_{p\in[p^{\star}(z)-\rho,\,p^{\star}(z)+\rho]}\Bigl(p\,\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)+(1-p)\,\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)\Bigr)\right].
``` |  | (30) |

By
Lemma [A.1](#A1.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma A.1 (Pointwise worst-case Bernoulli perturbation). ‣ A.1 A pointwise maximization identity ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
an optimizer is always an endpoint, hence a measurable selector can be
obtained by taking $`p(z)=p^{\star}(z)+\rho`$ when
$`\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)\geq\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)`$ and
$`p(z)=p^{\star}(z)-\rho`$ otherwise.

Apply
Lemma [A.1](#A1.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma A.1 (Pointwise worst-case Bernoulli perturbation). ‣ A.1 A pointwise maximization identity ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
to ([30](#A1.E30 "Equation 30 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
with $`a=\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)`$, $`b=\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)`$ and
$`p^{\star}=p^{\star}(z)`$. This gives

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Bigl[p^{\star}(z)\,\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)+(1-p^{\star}(z))\,\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)\Bigr]+\rho\,\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Bigl[|\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)-\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)|\Bigr].`$ |  | (31) |

The first expectation
in ([31](#A1.E31 "Equation 31 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
is exactly $`L^{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)`$.

For the second term, use the identity

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\log\sigma(t)-\log\sigma(-t)=t\quad\text{(since $\sigma(t)/\sigma(-t)=e^{t}$).}
``` |  |

With $`t=\beta h_{\theta}(z)`$ and
definitions ([28](#A1.E28 "Equation 28 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
we obtain

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)-\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)=-\log\sigma(\beta h_{\theta}(z))+\log\sigma(-\beta h_{\theta}(z))=-\beta h_{\theta}(z).
``` |  |

Hence
$`|\ell_{\theta}^{1}(z)-\ell_{\theta}^{0}(z)|=\beta|h_{\theta}(z)|`$,
and ([31](#A1.E31 "Equation 31 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
becomes

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\rho\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl[|h_{\theta}(z)|\bigr].
``` |  | (32) |

Define $`\lambda:=\rho\beta`$.

Under Assumption
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
for fixed $`x`$ we have the log-linear / softmax form

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)=\frac{\exp(\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y))}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}\exp(\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime}))}.
``` |  |

By Eq.
([11](#S4.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
$`\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}=\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}}`$ for some
$`\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y\mid x)}=(\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\top}\psi(x,y)-\log\sum_{y^{\prime}}e^{\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime})}+\log\sum_{y^{\prime}}e^{\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime})}.
``` |  |

Taking the difference between $`y_{1}`$ and $`y_{2}`$ cancels the
log-partition terms, yielding

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2})=(\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\top}\bigl(\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})\bigr).
``` |  | (33) |

Then ([33](#A1.E33 "Equation 33 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
shows $`h_{\theta}(z)=R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ where
$`R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ is defined in Theorem
[4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1 (Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃)). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
and thus the robust correction term
in ([32](#A1.E32 "Equation 32 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
is precisely $`\lambda R(\theta)`$ with

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl[|R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})|\bigr].
``` |  |

Substituting
into ([32](#A1.E32 "Equation 32 ‣ Step 1: Rewrite the robust objective pointwise in 𝑝(𝑧). ‣ A.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1 ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem 4.1: Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
gives the claimed decomposition

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta),\qquad\lambda=\rho\beta.
``` |  |

This completes the proof. ∎

## Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8

### B.1 Auxiliary lemmas

###### Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class).

Assume Assumption 2 (log-linear / softmax policy class) and Assumption 4
(finite response set and bounded features). Define the policy score
$`g_{\theta}(x,y):=\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)`$. Then for
all $`\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ and all
$`(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|g_{\theta}(x,y)\|_{2}\leq G,\qquad\|g_{\theta}(x,y)-g_{\theta^{\prime}}(x,y)\|_{2}\leq M\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2},
``` |  |

with explicit constants $`G:=2B_{\psi}`$ and $`M:=B_{\psi}^{2}`$.

Moreover, for $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})`$ and
$`d_{\theta}(z)=\mu(x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}\mid x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}\mid x)`$,
define score \[[46](#bib.bib47)\]

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S_{\theta}(z):=\nabla_{\theta}\log d_{\theta}(z)=g_{\theta}(x,y_{1})+g_{\theta}(x,y_{2}).
``` |  |

Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq 2G=4B_{\psi},\qquad\|\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2M=2B_{\psi}^{2}.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Under Assumption 2, for fixed $`x`$ the policy is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)=\frac{\exp(\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y))}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}\exp(\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime}))}.
``` |  |

Hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\log\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)=\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y)-\log\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}e^{\theta^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime})},
``` |  |

so differentiating gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g_{\theta}(x,y)=\psi(x,y)-\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi_{\theta}(y^{\prime}\mid x)\psi(x,y^{\prime})=\psi(x,y)-\mu_{\theta}(x),\quad\mu_{\theta}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}[\psi(x,y^{\prime})].
``` |  |

By Assumption 4, $`\|\psi(x,y)\|_{2}\leq B_{\psi}`$ and Jensen implies
$`\|\mu_{\theta}(x)\|_{2}\leq B_{\psi}`$, hence
$`\|g_{\theta}(x,y)\|_{2}\leq 2B_{\psi}`$. This gives $`G:=2B_{\psi}`$.

Next,
$`\nabla_{\theta}g_{\theta}(x,y)=-\nabla_{\theta}\mu_{\theta}(x)`$. A
standard calculation yields that the Jacobian of $`\mu_{\theta}(x)`$ is
the covariance matrix

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla_{\theta}\mu_{\theta}(x)=\mathrm{Cov}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}\!\bigl(\psi(x,y^{\prime})\bigr).
``` |  |

For any unit vector $`v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
v^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}\mu_{\theta}(x)v=\mathrm{Var}\bigl(v^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime})\bigr)\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[(v^{\top}\psi(x,y^{\prime}))^{2}\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\|\psi(x,y^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}\leq B_{\psi}^{2}.
``` |  |

Taking the supremum over $`\|v\|_{2}=1`$ yields
$`\|\nabla_{\theta}\mu_{\theta}(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B_{\psi}^{2}`$,
so $`\|\nabla_{\theta}g_{\theta}(x,y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B_{\psi}^{2}`$
as well. By the mean value theorem,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|g_{\theta}(x,y)-g_{\theta^{\prime}}(x,y)\|_{2}\leq B_{\psi}^{2}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2},
``` |  |

so we may take $`M:=B_{\psi}^{2}`$.

Finally, since
$`S_{\theta}(z)=g_{\theta}(x,y_{1})+g_{\theta}(x,y_{2})`$, the bounds
$`\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq 2G`$ and
$`\|\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2M`$ follow by
triangle inequality. ∎

###### Lemma B.2 (Bounded Fisher information).

Define the Fisher information matrix

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{F}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu,\ y\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[g_{\theta}(x,y)g_{\theta}(x,y)^{\top}\bigr].
``` |  |

Then $`\|\mathcal{F}(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq G^{2}`$ for all
$`\theta\in\Theta`$.

###### Proof.

For any unit vector $`u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u^{\top}\mathcal{F}(\theta)u=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle u,g_{\theta}(x,y)\rangle^{2}\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{\theta}(x,y)\|_{2}^{2}\bigr]\leq G^{2}.
``` |  |

Taking the supremum over $`\|u\|_{2}=1`$ yields
$`\|\mathcal{F}(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq G^{2}`$. ∎

###### Remark B.3.

In the weak convexity analysis of $`R(\theta)`$, we differentiate an
expectation taken under the *$`\theta`$-dependent* law $`d_{\theta}`$.
The resulting Hessian formula contains curvature terms involving the
score and its Jacobian, notably
$`r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\,\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)`$ where
$`S_{\theta}(z)=\nabla_{\theta}\log d_{\theta}(z)`$ and
$`r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)`$ is a smoothed absolute value. To obtain a
*uniform* lower bound
$`\nabla^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\succeq-\kappa_{\varepsilon}I`$
(hence weak convexity), we must control the worst-case quadratic form
$`v^{\top}(\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z))v`$ uniformly over
$`\|v\|_{2}=1`$. This is exactly an operator-norm control.
Lemma [B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Lemma B.2 (Bounded Fisher information). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
exemplifies this type of bound for second-moment (information) matrices,
and the proof of
Lemma [B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
uses the same operator-norm reasoning to bound
$`\|\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}`$, which is the
quantity that directly enters the proof of Theorem
[4.7](#S4.Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 4.7 (Weak convexity of the robust penalty). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

### B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty)

###### Proof of Theorem 4.7.

Recall the robust penalty

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl[|R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2})|\bigr],\;R(\theta;x,y_{1},y_{2}):=(\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\top}\bigl(\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})\bigr),\quad z=(x,y_{1},y_{2}).
``` |  |

#### Step 1: Smooth approximation.

Fix $`\varepsilon>0`$ and define
$`\phi_{\varepsilon}(u):=\sqrt{u^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}`$. Set

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{\varepsilon}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl[\phi_{\varepsilon}(s_{\theta}(z))\bigr].
``` |  |

Since $`\mathcal{Y}`$ is finite, the expectation over $`d_{\theta}`$ is
a finite sum, so $`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is twice continuously
differentiable and we may differentiate under the sum.

We will show that
$`\nabla^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\succeq-\kappa_{\varepsilon}I`$ for
all $`\theta\in\Theta`$ with

|     |                                                             |     |      |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \kappa_{\varepsilon}:=8GB_{\psi}+4MDB_{\psi}+2M\varepsilon,
       ```                                                          |     | (34) |

where $`G,M`$ are as in
Lemma [B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").
Since $`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is $`C^{2}`$ and has a global Hessian lower
bound, the function
$`R_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)+\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}`$
has positive semidefinite Hessian and is convex; equivalently,
$`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is $`\kappa_{\varepsilon}`$-weakly convex.

#### Step 2: Uniform bounds.

Define $`\Delta\psi(z):=\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})`$. By Assumption
[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\leq\|\psi(x,y_{1})\|_{2}+\|\psi(x,y_{2})\|_{2}\leq 2B_{\psi}.
``` |  |

By Assumption
[5](#Thmassumption5 "Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`\|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{2}\leq D`$ for all
$`\theta\in\Theta`$, hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|s_{\theta}(z)|\leq\|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{2}\,\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\leq 2DB_{\psi}.
``` |  |

Moreover, $`\phi_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq|u|+\varepsilon`$, so

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\leq\phi_{\varepsilon}(s_{\theta}(z))\leq 2DB_{\psi}+\varepsilon.
``` |  | (35) |

#### Step 3: Score-function calculus for $`d_{\theta}`$.

Write
$`d_{\theta}(z)=\mu(x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}\mid x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}\mid x)`$
and define the score\[[46](#bib.bib47)\]

|     |                                                   |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{\theta}(z):=\nabla_{\theta}\log d_{\theta}(z).
       ```                                                |     |

Then $`S_{\theta}(z)=g_{\theta}(x,y_{1})+g_{\theta}(x,y_{2})`$, hence
Lemma [B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq 2G,\qquad\|\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2M.
``` |  | (36) |

For the integrand
$`r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z):=\phi_{\varepsilon}(s_{\theta}(z))`$, note
that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla_{\theta}s_{\theta}(z)=\Delta\psi(z),\qquad\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s_{\theta}(z))\,\Delta\psi(z),
``` |  |

and since
$`|\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u)|=|u|/\sqrt{u^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 1`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\leq 2B_{\psi}.
``` |  | (37) |

Also,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla_{\theta}^{2}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(s_{\theta}(z))\,\Delta\psi(z)\Delta\psi(z)^{\top},\qquad\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(u)=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(u^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{3/2}}\geq 0,
``` |  |

so $`\nabla_{\theta}^{2}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\succeq 0`$ for all
$`(\theta,z)`$.

Because $`\mathcal{Y}`$ is finite, we can differentiate
$`R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)=\sum_{z}d_{\theta}(z)\,r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)`$
term-by-term and use
$`\nabla_{\theta}d_{\theta}(z)=d_{\theta}(z)S_{\theta}(z)`$ to obtain:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl[\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)+r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)S_{\theta}(z)\bigr],`$ |  | (38) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Bigl[\nabla_{\theta}^{2}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)+S_{\theta}(z)\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)^{\top}+\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)S_{\theta}(z)^{\top}+r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\bigl(S_{\theta}(z)S_{\theta}(z)^{\top}+\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\bigr)\Bigr].`$ |  | (39) |

#### Step 4: Hessian lower bound.

Fix any unit vector $`u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$.
Using ([39](#A2.E39 "Equation 39 ‣ Step 3: Score-function calculus for 𝑑_𝜃. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
and the facts that
$`\nabla_{\theta}^{2}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\succeq 0`$ and
$`S_{\theta}(z)S_{\theta}(z)^{\top}\succeq 0`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle u^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)u`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\Bigl[u^{\top}\bigl(S_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}^{\top}+\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}S_{\theta}^{\top}\bigr)u+r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\,u^{\top}(\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z))u\Bigr].`$ |  |

For the first term,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u^{\top}\bigl(S_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}^{\top}+\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}S_{\theta}^{\top}\bigr)u=2\langle u,S_{\theta}(z)\rangle\,\langle u,\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\rangle\geq-2\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\,\|\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\|_{2},
``` |  |

and
using ([36](#A2.E36 "Equation 36 ‣ Step 3: Score-function calculus for 𝑑_𝜃. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
and ([37](#A2.E37 "Equation 37 ‣ Step 3: Score-function calculus for 𝑑_𝜃. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
gives the pointwise bound

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u^{\top}\bigl(S_{\theta}\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}^{\top}+\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}S_{\theta}^{\top}\bigr)u\geq-2(2G)(2B_{\psi})=-8GB_{\psi}.
``` |  |

For the second term,
by ([36](#A2.E36 "Equation 36 ‣ Step 3: Score-function calculus for 𝑑_𝜃. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
$`u^{\top}(\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z))u\geq-\|\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq-2M`$,
so
with ([35](#A2.E35 "Equation 35 ‣ Step 2: Uniform bounds. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\,u^{\top}(\nabla_{\theta}S_{\theta}(z))u\geq-(2DB_{\psi}+\varepsilon)\,2M=-4MDB_{\psi}-2M\varepsilon.
``` |  |

Combining the two bounds and taking expectation yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)u\geq-(8GB_{\psi}+4MDB_{\psi}+2M\varepsilon)=-\kappa_{\varepsilon},
``` |  |

for all unit $`u`$, hence
$`\nabla_{\theta}^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\succeq-\kappa_{\varepsilon}I`$.

#### Step 5: Pass to the nonsmooth limit $`\varepsilon\downarrow 0`$.

For any $`u\in\mathbb{R}`$,
$`0\leq\phi_{\varepsilon}(u)-|u|\leq\varepsilon`$, hence
$`0\leq R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)-R(\theta)\leq\varepsilon`$ for all
$`\theta`$. Thus $`R_{\varepsilon}\to R`$ uniformly as
$`\varepsilon\downarrow 0`$. Since each $`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is
$`\kappa_{\varepsilon}`$-weakly convex and
$`\kappa_{\varepsilon}\downarrow\kappa_{R}:=8GB_{\psi}+4MDB_{\psi}`$,
taking $`\varepsilon\downarrow 0`$ in the defining weak convexity
inequality yields that $`R`$ is $`\kappa_{R}`$-weakly convex.

Finally, by
Lemma [B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
we may take $`G=2B_{\psi}`$ and $`M=B_{\psi}^{2}`$, giving

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\kappa_{R}\leq 8(2B_{\psi})B_{\psi}+4(B_{\psi}^{2})DB_{\psi}=16B_{\psi}^{2}+4DB_{\psi}^{3},
``` |  |

which is the claimed bound in Theorem 4.7. ∎

### B.3 Proof of Theorem 4.8

###### Proof of Theorem 4.8.

By Theorem
[4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1 (Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃)). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
we have the exact decomposition

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+\lambda R(\theta),\qquad\lambda=\rho\beta.
``` |  |

Under Assumption
[3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Smooth SAIL objective). ‣ 4.2 Regularity of of 𝐿^SAIL . ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$ has $`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}`$-Lipschitz gradient on
$`\mathbb{R}^{d}`$. A basic fact is that any $`L`$-smooth function is
$`L`$-weakly convex. This can be verified directly from the second-order
characterization: if $`f`$ is $`L`$-smooth, then
$`\nabla^{2}f(\theta)\succeq-LI`$. Equivalently, the function

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \theta\;\mapsto\;f(\theta)+\frac{L}{2}\|\theta\|_{2}^{2}
       ```                                                       |     |

is convex.

By Theorem
[4.7](#S4.Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 4.7 (Weak convexity of the robust penalty). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`R`$ is $`\kappa_{R}`$-weakly convex on $`\Theta`$. Scaling preserves
weak convexity, so $`\lambda R`$ is $`(\lambda\kappa_{R})`$-weakly
convex. Sums of weakly convex functions add their curvature parameters,
hence $`L^{W}_{\rho}=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}+\lambda R`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly
convex on $`\Theta`$ with

|     |                                              |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \kappa:=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}+\lambda\kappa_{R}.
       ```                                           |     |

This concludes the proof of Theorem
[4.8](#S4.Thmtheorem8 "Theorem 4.8 (Weak convexity of the composite objective ). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").
∎

### B.4 Auxiliary lemma for stochastic gradient oracle

###### Lemma B.4 (Second-moment bound for gradient oracle).

Fix any mini-batch size $`B\in\mathbb{N}`$, and recall the composite
direction in
Eq.[22](#S4.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G(\theta;Z)\ :=\ G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta;Z)\ +\ \lambda\,G_{R}(\theta;Z),
``` |  |

where $`Z`$ denotes a generic mini-batch of size $`B`$ drawn i.i.d. from
$`d_{\theta}`$ (Assumption
[6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
Under Assumptions
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
[5](#Thmassumption5 "Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
and
[6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
the composite direction has bounded conditional second moment: for all
$`\theta\in\Theta`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G(\theta;Z)\|_{2}^{2}\,\big|\,\theta\bigr]\ \leq\ G_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2},
``` |  |

with the explicit choice

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}\ :=\ 4\left(G_{\nabla\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}\;+\;\lambda^{2}\,G_{\partial R}^{2}\;+\;\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}}{B}\right),
``` |  |

where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{\nabla\mathrm{SAIL}}\ :=\ 2\beta B_{\psi}\;+\;4B_{\psi}\bigl(\log 2+2\beta DB_{\psi}\bigr),\qquad G_{\partial R}\ :=\ 2B_{\psi}\;+\;8DB_{\psi}^{2},
``` |  |

and $`\sigma_{\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2},\sigma_{R}^{2}`$ are as in Assumption
[6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

###### Proof.

We bound the deterministic (mean) components
$`\|\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\|_{2}`$ and
$`\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial R(\theta))`$, then combine with the variance
bounds in Assumption
[6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
and the definition
([22](#S4.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

#### Step 1: Uniform bounds on the pairwise logit and score.

For $`z=(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathcal{Z}`$, define
$`\Delta\psi(z):=\psi(x,y_{1})-\psi(x,y_{2})`$. By Assumption
[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Finite response set and bounded features). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\leq 2B_{\psi}`$. Under Assumption
[2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Log-linear policy class). ‣ 4.1 Decomposition of 𝐿^𝑊_𝜌⁢(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")(log-linear
policy class), the pairwise logit admits the cancellation

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h_{\theta}(z)\ =\ h_{\theta}(x,y_{1},y_{2})\ =\ (\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\top}\Delta\psi(z)
``` |  |

Hence, by Assumption
[5](#Thmassumption5 "Assumption 5 (Bounded feasible set). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|h_{\theta}(z)|\ \leq\ \|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{2}\;\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\ \leq\ D\cdot 2B_{\psi}\ =\ 2DB_{\psi}.
``` |  |

Moreover, recalling
$`d_{\theta}(z)=\mu(x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{1}|x)\pi_{\theta}(y_{2}|x)`$
(Eq.([1](#S3.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 3 Problem Setup ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")))
and $`S_{\theta}(z):=\nabla_{\theta}\log d_{\theta}(z)`$, Lemma
[B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
gives $`\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq 4B_{\psi}`$.

#### Step 2: Uniform bound on $`\|\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\|_{2}`$.

Recall that
$`L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\bigl[p^{\star}\ell^{1}(h)+(1-p^{\star})\ell^{0}(h)\bigr]`$,
where $`\ell^{1}(h)=-\log\sigma(\beta h)`$ and
$`\ell^{0}(h)=-\log\sigma(-\beta h)`$ , and $`p^{\star}(z)\in[0,1]`$.
Define the pointwise SAIL loss

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\ell_{\theta}(z)\ :=\ p^{\star}(z)\,\ell^{1}\!\bigl(h_{\theta}(z)\bigr)\ +\ (1-p^{\star}(z))\,\ell^{0}\!\bigl(h_{\theta}(z)\bigr).
``` |  |

Since $`\ell^{1}(h)=\log(1+e^{-\beta h})`$ and
$`\ell^{0}(h)=\log(1+e^{\beta h})`$, we have for all $`h\in\mathbb{R}`$,
$`\max\{\ell^{1}(h),\ell^{0}(h)\}=\log(1+e^{|\beta h|})\leq\log 2+|\beta h|`$.
Using $`|h_{\theta}(z)|\leq 2DB_{\psi}`$,

|     |                                                           |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       0\ \leq\ \ell_{\theta}(z)\ \leq\ \log 2+2\beta DB_{\psi}.
       ```                                                        |     |

Next, by direct differentiation, the derivatives of
$`\ell^{1},\ell^{0}`$ with respect to $`h`$ satisfy
$`\bigl|\tfrac{d}{dh}\ell^{1}(h)\bigr|\leq\beta`$ and
$`\bigl|\tfrac{d}{dh}\ell^{0}(h)\bigr|\leq\beta`$ for all $`h`$.
Together with $`\nabla_{\theta}h_{\theta}(z)=\Delta\psi(z)`$ and
$`\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\leq 2B_{\psi}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\ \leq\ \beta\,\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\ \leq\ 2\beta B_{\psi}.
``` |  |

Because $`\mathcal{Y}`$ is finite, we may apply the same score-function
calculus as in Eq.
([38](#A2.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ Step 3: Score-function calculus for 𝑑_𝜃. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
to write

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\ =\ \mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\!\left[\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(z)+\ell_{\theta}(z)\,S_{\theta}(z)\right].
``` |  |

Therefore,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\|_{2}\ \leq\ \mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\!\left[\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}+\ell_{\theta}(z)\,\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\right]\ \leq\ 2\beta B_{\psi}+\bigl(\log 2+2\beta DB_{\psi}\bigr)\,4B_{\psi}\ =:\ G_{\nabla\mathrm{SAIL}}.
``` |  |

#### Step 3: Uniform bound on $`\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial R(\theta))`$.

Recall $`R(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}[|h_{\theta}(z)|]`$ .
For $`\varepsilon>0`$, define the smooth approximation
$`\varphi_{\varepsilon}(u):=\sqrt{u^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}`$ and
$`R_{\varepsilon}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}[\varphi_{\varepsilon}(h_{\theta}(z))]`$.
As in
Eq.([38](#A2.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ Step 3: Score-function calculus for 𝑑_𝜃. ‣ B.2 Proof of Theorem 4.7 (weak convexity of the robust penalty) ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\ =\ \mathbb{E}_{z\sim d_{\theta}}\!\left[\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)+r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\,S_{\theta}(z)\right],\qquad r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z):=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(h_{\theta}(z)).
``` |  |

Since $`|\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u)|\leq 1`$ and
$`\nabla_{\theta}h_{\theta}(z)=\Delta\psi(z)`$, we obtain
$`\|\nabla_{\theta}r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta\psi(z)\|_{2}\leq 2B_{\psi}`$.
Also, $`0\leq\varphi_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq|u|+\varepsilon`$, so
$`r_{\varepsilon,\theta}(z)\leq 2DB_{\psi}+\varepsilon`$. Using
$`\|S_{\theta}(z)\|_{2}\leq 4B_{\psi}`$ (Lemma
[B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1 (Policy-regularity constants for the log-linear policy class). ‣ B.1 Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Appendix B Weak convexity: Proofs of Theorems 4.7 and 4.8 ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
we conclude

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\nabla R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\|_{2}\ \leq\ 2B_{\psi}+(2DB_{\psi}+\varepsilon)\,4B_{\psi}\ =\ 2B_{\psi}+8DB_{\psi}^{2}+4\varepsilon B_{\psi}.
``` |  |

Hence $`R_{\varepsilon}`$ is
$`(2B_{\psi}+8DB_{\psi}^{2}+4\varepsilon B_{\psi})`$-Lipschitz on
$`\Theta`$. Moreover, $`|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(u)-|u||\leq\varepsilon`$
implies
$`\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)-R(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon`$.
Thus for any $`\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|R(\theta)-R(\theta^{\prime})|\ \leq\ |R(\theta)-R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)|+|R_{\varepsilon}(\theta)-R_{\varepsilon}(\theta^{\prime})|+|R_{\varepsilon}(\theta^{\prime})-R(\theta^{\prime})|\ \leq\ 2\varepsilon+\bigl(2B_{\psi}+8DB_{\psi}^{2}+4\varepsilon B_{\psi}\bigr)\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}.
``` |  |

Letting $`\varepsilon\downarrow 0`$ yields that $`R`$ is
$`G_{\partial R}`$-Lipschitz on $`\Theta`$, i.e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|R(\theta)-R(\theta^{\prime})|\;\leq\;G_{\partial R}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2},\qquad\forall\,\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta,
``` |  |

with $`G_{\partial R}:=2B_{\psi}+8DB_{\psi}^{2}`$. Hence the local
Lipschitz modulus satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\limsup_{\theta^{\prime}\to\theta,\;\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\frac{|R(\theta^{\prime})-R(\theta)|}{\|\theta^{\prime}-\theta\|_{2}}\;\leq\;G_{\partial R},\qquad\forall\,\theta\in\Theta.
``` |  |

A standard variational-analytic fact for locally Lipschitz functions
implies that any (limiting) subgradient is norm-bounded by the local
Lipschitz modulus\[[47](#bib.bib35)\]; namely,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
v\in\partial R(\theta)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|v\|_{2}\leq\limsup_{\theta^{\prime}\to\theta,\;\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta}\frac{|R(\theta^{\prime})-R(\theta)|}{\|\theta^{\prime}-\theta\|_{2}}\leq G_{\partial R}.
``` |  |

Since $`\partial R(\theta)\neq\emptyset`$ for all $`\theta\in\Theta`$
(Assumption
[6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
we conclude

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{dist}\bigl(0,\partial R(\theta)\bigr)\;\leq\;G_{\partial R},\qquad\forall\,\theta\in\Theta.
``` |  |

Fix $`\theta\in\Theta`$ and abbreviate
$`G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}:=G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta;Z)`$ and
$`G_{R}:=G_{R}(\theta;Z)`$. By
Eq.[22](#S4.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
and $`\|a+b\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|a\|_{2}^{2}+2\|b\|_{2}^{2}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G(\theta;Z)\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr]\ \leq\ 2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr]\;+\;2\lambda^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G_{R}\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr].
``` |  |

For the SAIL term, write
$`G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}=\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)+(G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}-\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta))`$
and apply $`\|a+b\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|a\|_{2}^{2}+2\|b\|_{2}^{2}`$ together
with Assumption 6:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr]\ \leq\ 2\|\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\|_{2}^{2}\;+\;2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G_{\mathrm{SAIL}}-\nabla L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}(\theta)\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr]\ \leq\ 2G_{\nabla\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}\;+\;2\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}}{B}.
``` |  |

For the robust-penalty term, note that for any closed nonempty set
$`A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ and any $`u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$,
$`\|u\|_{2}\leq\mathrm{dist}(u,A)+\mathrm{dist}(0,A)`$, hence
$`\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\,\mathrm{dist}^{2}(u,A)+2\,\mathrm{dist}^{2}(0,A)`$.
Applying this with $`A=\partial R(\theta)`$ and using Assumption
[6](#Thmassumption6 "Assumption 6 (Stochastic gradient/subgradient oracles). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
and Step 3 gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G_{R}\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr]\ \leq\ 2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{dist}^{2}\bigl(G_{R},\partial R(\theta)\bigr)\mid\theta\right]\;+\;2\,\mathrm{dist}^{2}\bigl(0,\partial R(\theta)\bigr)\ \leq\ 2\frac{\sigma_{R}^{2}}{B}\;+\;2G_{\partial R}^{2}.
``` |  |

Combining the last three displays yields, for all $`\theta\in\Theta`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G(\theta;Z)\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta\bigr]\ \leq\ 2\left(2G_{\nabla\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}+2\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}}{B}\right)\;+\;2\lambda^{2}\left(2G_{\partial R}^{2}+2\frac{\sigma_{R}^{2}}{B}\right)\ =\ 4\left(G_{\nabla\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}G_{\partial R}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{SAIL}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}}{B}\right).
``` |  |

This is exactly the claimed bound with $`G_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}`$ as
stated. ∎

## Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis

### C.1 Proof of Lemma [4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models") (Properties of the Moreau envelope)

###### Proof of Lemma [4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

Fix $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$ and recall the definition
of the Moreau envelope and proximal point (Definition
[4.10](#S4.Thmtheorem10 "Definition 4.10 (Moreau envelope and proximal point). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta):=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{F(u)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\right\},\qquad\hat{\theta}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta):=\arg\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{F(u)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.
``` |  |

#### 1–2. Finiteness, uniqueness, and the gradient formula.

Since $`F`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex, the function
$`u\mapsto F(u)+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}`$ is convex. Therefore,
for any fixed $`\theta`$, the function

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
u\mapsto F(u)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-\theta\|_{2}^{2}
``` |  |

is $`(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}-\kappa)`$-strongly convex in
$`u`$ (because $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}<1/\kappa`$), hence it has a
unique minimizer $`\hat{\theta}`$ and the minimum value
$`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)`$ is finite.

Moreover, by first-order optimality of $`\hat{\theta}`$ for the strongly
convex problem,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\in\partial F(\hat{\theta})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\hat{\theta}-\theta),\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})\in\partial F(\hat{\theta}).
``` |  | (40) |

Standard properties of the Moreau envelope for weakly convex functions
imply that $`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$ is continuously differentiable
and that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta}).
``` |  |

Eq.
([19](#S4.E19 "Equation 19 ‣ Item 2 ‣ Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
follows immediately:
$`\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|_{2}=\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|_{2}`$.

#### 3. Lipschitz continuity of $`\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$.

A standard result for $`\kappa`$-weakly convex $`F`$ is that
$`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$ has Lipschitz gradient with constant

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
L_{\mathrm{env}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})},
``` |  |

which is Eq.
([20](#S4.E20 "Equation 20 ‣ Item 3 ‣ Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

#### 4. Approximate stationarity of the proximal point.

By ([40](#A3.E40 "Equation 40 ‣ 1–2. Finiteness, uniqueness, and the gradient formula. ‣ C.1 Proof of Lemma (Properties of the Moreau envelope) ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),
the vector $`\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})`$
belongs to $`\partial F(\hat{\theta})`$. Therefore

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta})\bigr)\leq\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})\right\|_{2}=\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|_{2}
``` |  |

This completes the proof. ∎

### C.2 Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem [5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2 (Convergence rate for the Moreau envelope). ‣ 5.1 Convergence analysis ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")

###### Lemma C.1 (Monotonicity inequality).

Assume $`F`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex and let
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$. Fix any
$`\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ and let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta),\qquad\xi:=\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta}).
``` |  |

Then for every $`v\in\partial F(\theta)`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\langle\xi,v\rangle\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

By $`\kappa`$-weak convexity, for all $`x,y`$ and all
$`g\in\partial F(x)`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(y)\geq F(x)+\langle g,y-x\rangle-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|y-x\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  | (41) |

Apply ([41](#A3.E41 "Equation 41 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
twice:

(i) with $`x=\theta`$, $`y=\hat{\theta}`$, and
$`g=v\in\partial F(\theta)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(\hat{\theta})\geq F(\theta)+\langle v,\hat{\theta}-\theta\rangle-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  | (42) |

(ii) with $`x=\hat{\theta}`$, $`y=\theta`$, and any
$`\hat{v}\in\partial F(\hat{\theta})`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(\theta)\geq F(\hat{\theta})+\langle\hat{v},\theta-\hat{\theta}\rangle-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  | (43) |

By optimality of the proximal point (as
in ([40](#A3.E40 "Equation 40 ‣ 1–2. Finiteness, uniqueness, and the gradient formula. ‣ C.1 Proof of Lemma (Properties of the Moreau envelope) ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))),
we may choose
$`\hat{v}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})=\xi\in\partial F(\hat{\theta})`$.
Substituting this choice
into ([43](#A3.E43 "Equation 43 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F(\theta)\geq F(\hat{\theta})+\langle\xi,\theta-\hat{\theta}\rangle-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

Add this inequality
to ([42](#A3.E42 "Equation 42 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
to eliminate $`F(\theta)`$ and $`F(\hat{\theta})`$, yielding

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\geq\langle v,\hat{\theta}-\theta\rangle+\langle\xi,\theta-\hat{\theta}\rangle-\kappa\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

Rearrange and use $`\theta-\hat{\theta}=\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\xi`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\langle\xi,\theta-\hat{\theta}\rangle\leq\langle v,\theta-\hat{\theta}\rangle+\kappa\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\langle v,\xi\rangle+\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}^{2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

Divide by $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}>0`$ and rearrange:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\langle v,\xi\rangle\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\xi\|_{2}^{2},
``` |  |

as claimed. ∎

###### Lemma C.2 (One-step inequality).

Assume $`F`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex and bounded below by
$`F_{\inf}>-\infty`$. Fix $`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$ and
define
$`L_{\mathrm{env}}:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})}`$
as in Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

Consider the projected update (Algorithm
[1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta_{t+1}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}\bigl(\theta_{t}-\eta\,G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\bigr),
``` |  |

where $`G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})`$ satisfies
$`\mathbb{E}\bigl[G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\mid\theta_{t}\bigr]\in\partial F(\theta_{t})`$.
Then for any stepsize $`\eta>0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t+1})\mid\theta_{t}\bigr]\leq F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t})-\eta(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t})\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{L_{\mathrm{env}}\eta^{2}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta_{t}\bigr].
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Write $`F=L^{W}_{\rho}+I_{\Theta}`$, where $`I_{\Theta}`$ is the
indicator of $`\Theta`$
(Eq.([16](#S4.E16 "Equation 16 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))).
Then the envelope can be written as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(z)=\min_{u\in\Theta}\left\{f(u)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}\|u-z\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.
``` |  |

Let $`\tilde{z}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}(z)`$. For any $`u\in\Theta`$,
Euclidean projection optimality implies
$`\langle z-\tilde{z},u-\tilde{z}\rangle\leq 0`$, hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|u-\tilde{z}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u-z+(z-\tilde{z})\|_{2}^{2}=\|u-z\|_{2}^{2}+\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{2}^{2}+2\langle u-z,z-\tilde{z}\rangle\leq\|u-z\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

Taking the minimum over $`u\in\Theta`$ gives the monotonicity under
projection:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}(z))\leq F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(z)\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
``` |  | (44) |

Now define the unprojected point
$`z_{t}:=\theta_{t}-\eta G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})`$, so
$`\theta_{t+1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\Theta}(z_{t})`$.
By ([44](#A3.E44 "Equation 44 ‣ Proof. ‣ C.2 Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")),

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t+1})\leq F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(z_{t}).
``` |  |

By Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
$`F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}`$ is $`L_{\mathrm{env}}`$-smooth, hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t}-\eta G(\theta_{t};Z_{t}))\leq F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t})-\eta\langle\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t}),G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\rangle+\frac{L_{\mathrm{env}}\eta^{2}}{2}\|G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

Combining with the previous display and taking conditional expectation
yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t+1})\mid\theta_{t}\bigr]\leq F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t}),\ \mathbb{E}[G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\mid\theta_{t}]\right\rangle+\frac{L_{\mathrm{env}}\eta^{2}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\mid\theta_{t}\bigr].
``` |  |

Let
$`v_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[G(\theta_{t};Z_{t})\mid\theta_{t}]\in\partial F(\theta_{t})`$
by assumption. Applying
Lemma [C.1](#A3.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma C.1 (Monotonicity inequality). ‣ C.2 Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem ‣ Appendix C Moreau envelope and convergence analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
with $`\theta=\theta_{t}`$,
$`\xi=\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t})`$, and $`v=v_{t}`$
gives
$`\langle\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t}),v_{t}\rangle\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{t})\|_{2}^{2}`$.
Substituting completes the proof. ∎

## Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point

This section provides a practical interpretation of the proximal-point
and Moreau-envelope constructions used in our analysis. Throughout,
recall the constrained objective

|     |                                                     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       F(\theta):=L^{W}_{\rho}(\theta)+I_{\Theta}(\theta),
       ```                                                  |     |

and the envelope parameter $`\lambda_{\rm env}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$ from
Definition
[4.10](#S4.Thmtheorem10 "Definition 4.10 (Moreau envelope and proximal point). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
where $`\kappa`$ is the weak convexity constant from Theorem
[4.8](#S4.Thmtheorem8 "Theorem 4.8 (Weak convexity of the composite objective ). ‣ 4.3 Weak convexity of 𝑅(𝜃) ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models").

The Moreau envelope

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta)\;=\;\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big\{F(u)+\tfrac{1}{2\lambda_{\rm env}}\|u-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\Big\}
``` |  |

can be viewed as a *smoothed* surrogate of the potentially nonsmooth,
nonconvex objective $`F`$. The quadratic term discourages large moves
away from the current iterate $`\theta`$, and the minimizer of the inner
problem—the *proximal point*—acts as a locally stabilized refinement of
$`\theta`$. Concretely, for any iterate $`\theta`$ we write

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\rm env}F}(\theta)\in\arg\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big\{F(u)+\tfrac{1}{2\lambda_{\rm env}}\|u-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\Big\}.
``` |  |

Because $`F`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly convex and
$`\lambda_{\rm env}<1/\kappa`$, the proximal subproblem is strongly
convex in $`u`$ and hence has a unique minimizer. This is the
fundamental reason the envelope is differentiable and why
$`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta)\|_{2}`$ is a meaningful
stationarity proxy (Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

Algorithm
[1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
does *not* require solving the proximal subproblem at each iteration.
The algorithm updates $`\theta_{t}`$ using stochastic subgradient
information for $`L^{W}_{\rho}=L_{\mathrm{SAIL}}+\lambda R`$ followed by
projection onto $`\Theta`$, and then outputs a random iterate
$`\theta_{R}`$. The proximal point

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}_{R}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\rm env}F}(\theta_{R})
``` |  |

is introduced only as a *theoretical device* to translate envelope
stationarity at $`\theta_{R}`$ into near-stationarity of the original
constrained objective at $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ (see Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
and Theorem
[5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2 (Convergence rate for the Moreau envelope). ‣ 5.1 Convergence analysis ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
In particular, Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
implies that $`\theta_{R}`$ and $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ are close whenever
the envelope gradient is small:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\theta_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}\;=\;\lambda_{\rm env}\,\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2},
``` |  |

and also that $`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ is nearly stationary for $`F`$ in the
sense that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta}_{R})\bigr)\;\leq\;\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}.
``` |  |

Thus, driving $`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$ small
(as guaranteed in expectation by Theorem
[5.2](#S5.Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 5.2 (Convergence rate for the Moreau envelope). ‣ 5.1 Convergence analysis ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"),
with sample complexity summarized in Corollary
[5.3](#S5.Thmtheorem3 "Corollary 5.3 (Sample complexity for envelope stationarity). ‣ 5.1 Convergence analysis ‣ 5 Analysis ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
simultaneously guarantees proximity to, and near-stationarity of, a
point for the original constrained robust objective.

###### Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?).

Assume $`F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,+\infty]`$ is $`\kappa`$-weakly
convex and bounded below, and fix
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$ as in Definition 4.10. For any
$`\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$, let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}F}(\theta),\qquad\xi:=\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})
``` |  |

(Definition
[4.10](#S4.Thmtheorem10 "Definition 4.10 (Moreau envelope and proximal point). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")and
Lemma
[4.11](#S4.Thmtheorem11 "Lemma 4.11 (Properties of the Moreau envelope ( , )). ‣ 4.4 Algorithm development ‣ 4 Proposed Approach ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
Then:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|`$ | $`\displaystyle=\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\|\xi\|,`$ |  | (46) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta})\bigr)`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\|,`$ |  | (47) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\theta)\bigr)`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\xi\|.`$ |  | (48) |

On the other hand, under $`\kappa`$-weak convexity alone, there is *no*
universal constant $`C`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\theta)\bigr)\;\leq\;C\,\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|\quad\text{for all $\theta$ and all $\kappa$-weakly convex $F$}
``` |  |

(even in the convex case $`\kappa=0`$).

###### Proof.

We first prove
([46](#A4.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))–([47](#A4.E47 "Equation 47 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
By Lemma 4.11 (see also the first-order optimality condition for the
proximal subproblem),

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0\in\partial F(\hat{\theta})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\hat{\theta}-\theta),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})\in\partial F(\hat{\theta}).
``` |  |

With
$`\xi:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta-\hat{\theta})=\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)`$,
taking norms yields
$`\|\theta-\hat{\theta}\|=\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\|\xi\|`$, which is
([46](#A4.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
Moreover, since $`\xi\in\partial F(\hat{\theta})`$, we have
$`\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta})\bigr)\leq\|\xi\|`$,
which is
([47](#A4.E47 "Equation 47 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

Next we prove
([48](#A4.E48 "Equation 48 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
By Lemma C.1 (Monotonicity inequality), for every
$`v\in\partial F(\theta)`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\langle\xi,v\rangle\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\xi\|^{2}.
``` |  |

By Cauchy–Schwarz, $`\langle\xi,v\rangle\leq\|\xi\|\,\|v\|`$, hence for
every $`v\in\partial F(\theta)`$,

|     |                                                   |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \|v\|\geq(1-\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{env}})\|\xi\|.
       ```                                                |     |

Taking the infimum over $`v\in\partial F(\theta)`$ gives
([48](#A4.E48 "Equation 48 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).

For the stated consequences with the algorithmic output, apply
([46](#A4.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))–([47](#A4.E47 "Equation 47 ‣ Lemma D.1 (What if we do not compute the proximal point?). ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
with $`\theta=\theta_{R}`$ and $`\hat{\theta}=\hat{\theta}_{R}`$. Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\theta_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|^{2}=\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}^{2}\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|^{2},\qquad\operatorname{dist}^{2}\bigl(0,\partial F(\hat{\theta}_{R})\bigr)\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|^{2}.
``` |  |

Taking expectations, using Jensen’s inequality for
$`\mathbb{E}\|\theta_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|`$, and plugging
$`\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta_{R})\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon`$
yields the two bounds.

Finally, we show that no universal constant $`C`$ can upper bound
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta))`$ by
$`\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|`$ under weak convexity
alone. Consider $`d=1`$ and $`\Theta=\mathbb{R}`$, and define
$`F(\theta)=|\theta|`$, which is convex (thus $`\kappa=0`$). Fix
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}=1`$ and any $`\theta\in(0,1)`$. The proximal
point is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}=\arg\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\Big\{|u|+\tfrac{1}{2}(u-\theta)^{2}\Big\}=0,
``` |  |

so
$`\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)=\theta-\hat{\theta}=\theta`$.
However, for $`\theta>0`$, $`\partial F(\theta)=\{1\}`$ and therefore
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta))=1`$. Hence

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta))}{\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}}(\theta)\|}=\frac{1}{\theta}\to\infty\quad\text{as }\theta\downarrow 0,
``` |  |

which rules out any finite universal $`C`$. ∎

###### Remark D.2 (Interpretation for practice).

Even if Algorithm 1 outputs only $`\theta_{R}`$ (without solving the
proximal subproblem), Theorem 5.2 still certifies that $`\theta_{R}`$
lies within $`O(\lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\sqrt{\varepsilon})`$ of a point
$`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$ that is $`O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})`$-stationary for
the original constrained objective $`F`$. In contrast, without
additional regularity beyond $`\kappa`$-weak convexity, one cannot
generally convert envelope stationarity into a bound on
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\theta_{R}))`$ itself; this is why
the analysis (and the stationarity notion) is phrased in terms of the
proximal point.

### D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point

In the analysis we introduce the proximal point

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}_{R}\;:=\;\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\rm env}}F(\theta_{R})\;=\;\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\Big\{L_{\rho}^{W}(\theta)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\rm env}}\|\theta-\theta_{R}\|_{2}^{2}\Big\},
``` |  | (49) |

where $`F(\theta):=L_{\rho}^{W}(\theta)+I_{\Theta}(\theta)`$ and
$`\lambda_{\rm env}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$. By weak convexity of $`F`$ and
$`\lambda_{\rm env}<1/\kappa`$, the objective
in ([49](#A4.E49 "Equation 49 ‣ D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
is strongly convex and hence admits a unique minimizer
$`\hat{\theta}_{R}`$. Although Algorithm 1 outputs $`\theta_{R}`$, one
can compute an approximation $`\bar{\theta}_{R}\approx\hat{\theta}_{R}`$
as an optional post-processing step, warm-started at $`\theta_{R}`$. A
simple inner-loop solver is provided below.

#### A simple inner-loop solver.

Let $`\theta^{(0)}:=\theta_{R}`$. For $`k=0,1,\ldots,K-1`$, sample
$`Z^{(k)}\sim d_{\theta^{(k)}}`$ and compute the stochastic direction

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G^{\rm prox}\big(\theta^{(k)};Z^{(k)}\big)\;:=\;G_{\rm SAIL}\big(\theta^{(k)};Z^{(k)}\big)\;+\;\lambda\,G_{R}\big(\theta^{(k)};Z^{(k)}\big)\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm env}}\big(\theta^{(k)}-\theta_{R}\big),
``` |  |

which is the usual oracle from Assumption 6 plus the deterministic
gradient of the proximal quadratic. Then take a projected step

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta^{(k+1)}\;:=\;\Pi_{\Theta}\!\left(\theta^{(k)}-\alpha_{k}\,G^{\rm prox}\big(\theta^{(k)};Z^{(k)}\big)\right),\qquad\bar{\theta}_{R}:=\theta^{(K)}.
``` |  | (50) |

Since the proximal quadratic improves conditioning, this inner loop is
typically stable in practice. Moreover, the theory only requires
$`\bar{\theta}_{R}`$ to be a sufficiently accurate approximation; exact
solves
of ([49](#A4.E49 "Equation 49 ‣ D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
are not necessary. This is consistent with standard practice in inexact
proximal methods, where the proximal subproblem is solved only
approximately while maintaining meaningful convergence guarantees (e.g.,
[Schmidt et al. 45](#bib.bib32)).

###### Lemma D.3 (Stationarity of an inexact proximal point).

Fix $`\lambda_{\rm env}\in(0,1/\kappa)`$ and an index $`R`$. Let
$`\hat{\theta}_{R}=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\rm env}}F(\theta_{R})`$
and let $`\bar{\theta}_{R}\in\Theta`$ be any point. Define the proximal
objective

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Psi_{R}(\theta)\;:=\;F(\theta)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\rm env}}\|\theta-\theta_{R}\|_{2}^{2}.
``` |  |

If $`\bar{\theta}_{R}`$ satisfies the (first-order) proximal residual
bound

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(0,\partial\Psi_{R}(\bar{\theta}_{R})\right)\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{\rm prox},
``` |  | (51) |

then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(0,\partial F(\bar{\theta}_{R})\right)\;\leq\;\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}\;+\;\varepsilon_{\rm prox}\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm env}}\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}.
``` |  | (52) |

In particular, if one ensures
$`\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}\leq\lambda_{\rm env}\varepsilon_{\rm geom}`$,
then
$`\operatorname{dist}\!\left(0,\partial F(\bar{\theta}_{R})\right)\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}+\varepsilon_{\rm prox}+\varepsilon_{\rm geom}.`$

###### Proof.

By subdifferential calculus,
$`\partial\Psi_{R}(\theta)=\partial F(\theta)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta-\theta_{R}).`$
The residual
condition ([51](#A4.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Lemma D.3 (Stationarity of an inexact proximal point). ‣ A simple inner-loop solver. ‣ D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models"))
implies there exists $`g\in\partial F(\bar{\theta}_{R})`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|g+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\bar{\theta}_{R}-\theta_{R})\right\|_{2}\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{\rm prox}.
``` |  |

Therefore,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(0,\partial F(\bar{\theta}_{R})\right)\;\leq\;\|g\|_{2}\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{\rm prox}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm env}}\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\theta_{R}\|_{2}.
``` |  |

Next, by the triangle inequality,
$`\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\theta_{R}\|_{2}\leq\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}+\|\hat{\theta}_{R}-\theta_{R}\|_{2}.`$
By Lemma 4.11 (property
$`\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda_{\rm env}}F(\theta))`$),
we have
$`\|\hat{\theta}_{R}-\theta_{R}\|_{2}=\lambda_{\rm env}\|\nabla F_{\lambda_{\rm env}}(\theta_{R})\|_{2}`$.
Substituting
yields ([52](#A4.E52 "Equation 52 ‣ Lemma D.3 (Stationarity of an inexact proximal point). ‣ A simple inner-loop solver. ‣ D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")).
∎

###### Remark D.4 (Practical stopping criteria.).

Lemma [D.3](#A4.Thmtheorem3 "Lemma D.3 (Stationarity of an inexact proximal point). ‣ A simple inner-loop solver. ‣ D.1 Practical computation of the proximal point ‣ Appendix D On the practical role of the proximal point ‣ Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models")
suggests two inexpensive targets for the inner loop: (i) keep the
proximal residual
$`\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial\Psi_{R}(\theta^{(k)}))`$ small (as
estimated by a minibatch), and (ii) exploit warm-starting so that
$`\|\bar{\theta}_{R}-\hat{\theta}_{R}\|_{2}`$ remains modest.
````
