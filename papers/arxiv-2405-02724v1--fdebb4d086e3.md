# Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

Yingjie Fei ^(†)^(†)thanks: yf275@cornell.edu    Ruitu Xu
^(†)^(†)thanks: Department of Statistics and Data Science, Yale
University; chris.xu@yale.edu

###### Abstract

We study risk-sensitive multi-agent reinforcement learning under
general-sum Markov games, where agents optimize the entropic risk
measure of rewards with possibly diverse risk preferences. We show that
using the regret naively adapted from existing literature as a
performance metric could induce policies with equilibrium bias that
favor the most risk-sensitive agents and overlook the other agents. To
address such deficiency of the naive regret, we propose a novel notion
of regret, which we call risk-balanced regret, and show through a lower
bound that it overcomes the issue of equilibrium bias. Furthermore, we
develop a self-play algorithm for learning Nash, correlated, and coarse
correlated equilibria in risk-sensitive Markov games. We prove that the
proposed algorithm attains near-optimal regret guarantees with respect
to the risk-balanced regret.

## 1 Introduction

Recent advancement in reinforcement learning research has witnessed much
development on multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). However, most
of the works focus on risk-neutral agents, which may not be suitable for
modeling the real world. For example, in investment activities,
different investors have different risk preferences depending on their
roles in the market. Some act as speculators and are risk-seeking, while
others are bound by regulatory constraints and are thus risk-averse.
Another example is multi-player online role-playing games, where each of
the players can be considered an agent. Whereas some (risk-seeking)
players enjoy exploring uncharted regions in the game, others
(risk-averse players) prefer to playing in areas that are well explored
and come with less uncertainty. It is not hard to see that in the above
examples, modeling each agent as uniformly risk-neutral is
inappropriate. This naturally calls for a more sophisticated modeling
framework that takes into account of heterogeneous risk preferences of
agents.

In this paper, we study the problem of risk-sensitive MARL under the
setting of general-sum Markov games (MGs), a more realistic multi-agent
model in which the agents may take different risk preferences. To that
end, we consider $`M\geq 1`$ agents who maximize the entropic risk
measure of rewards, informally defined as

|     |                                                                                                             |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle V_{m}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\beta_{m}}\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\beta_{m}R_{m}}],\quad\forall m\in[M],`$ |     |

where $`\beta_{m}`$ represents the risk parameter of agent $`m`$ and
$`R_{m}`$ is the corresponding reward. For each agent $`m`$,
$`\beta_{m}>0`$ means that the agent prefers more risk (or risk seeking)
and $`\beta_{m}<0`$ indicates that the agent favors less risk (or risk
averse); when $`\beta_{m}\to 0`$, the agent tends to be risk-neutral and
maximizes the expected reward $`\mathbb{E}[R_{m}]`$. We aim to learn
equilibria of the underlying MG in an online fashion, without access to
knowledge or simulator of the unknown transition kernels.

In the online setting, a standard metric for quantifying the performance
of an algorithm is regret, which computes the cumulative difference
between the best possible utility and the utility attained by the
algorithm. Unfortunately, the formulation of regret naively adapted from
the risk-neutral setting fails to represent a suitable performance
metric under the risk-sensitive setting: we show that the naive regret
is dominated by the sub-optimality of the most risk-sensitive agents,
demonstrated through a lower bound. Such deficiency may lead to
algorithms that appear nearly optimal but generate policies that suffer
equilibrium bias, by which the most risk-sensitive agents are falsely
favored against the remaining agents, and exponential sub-optimality is
incurred for all agents except for the most risk-sensitive ones.

To address the issue of equilibrium bias, we propose a novel definition
of regret tailored to the risk-sensitive setting, which we name as
risk-balanced regret. The risk-balanced regret takes into account of
possibly diverse risk sensitivity of all agents and treats all agent in
a symmetric way despite their different risk preferences. We prove a
lower bound based on this new notion of regret, which suggests that it
addresses the problem of equilibrium bias suffered by the naive regret.
In addition, we propose a novel self-play algorithm that learns Nash,
correlated, and coarse correlated equilibria of the general-sum MG
involving multiple risk-sensitive agents, based on value iteration and
optimistic exploration. We prove that the proposed algorithm achieves a
nearly optimal upper bound for the risk-balanced regret. To the best of
our knowledge, this work provides the first finite-sample guarantees in
risk-sensitive MARL based on the entropic risk measure.

In summary, our work presents the following theoretical contributions.

- •
  We consider risk-sensitive MARL under general-sum MGs where each agent
  may have heterogeneous risk preferences. We identify the pitfall of a
  regret metric naively adapted from the risk-neutral setting: it
  induces policies that incur equilibrium bias by improperly biasing
  towards agents with the largest risk sensitivity among the agent
  population.
- •
  We propose a novel notion of regret (risk-balanced regret) for
  quantifying the performance of online learning algorithms for
  risk-sensitive agents, which accounts for the risk sensitivity of each
  agent and overcomes the issue of equilibrium bias.
- •
  We develop a novel self-play algorithm for learning Nash, correlated,
  and coarse correlated equilibria, and we prove that the proposed
  algorithm achieves a nearly optimal rate w.r.t. the risk-balanced
  regret compared to the lower bound.

## 2 Related Work

MARL ([Littman, 1994](#bib.bib20)) has been a central topic in the
literature of reinforcement learning, with an extensive line of work
devoted to studying risk-neutral settings and finite-sample guarantees.
For example, theoretical studies have investigated two-player zero-sum
MGs and a number of near-optimal self-play algorithms ([Xie et al.,
2020](#bib.bib27); [Bai et al., 2020](#bib.bib2); [Bai and Jin,
2020](#bib.bib1)); a nearly minimax optimal regret has also become
available for two-player zero-sum MGs under a linear kernel setup ([Chen
et al., 2021](#bib.bib8)). Further, zero- and general-sum MGs are
studied by [Liu et al. (2020)](#bib.bib21) and [Zhang et al.
(2020)](#bib.bib31) with nearly optimal sample complexity bounds, while
[Dubey and Pentland (2021)](#bib.bib10) considers multi-agent
cooperative games with communication budget. The work of [Tian et al.
(2020)](#bib.bib25) develops an algorithm for multi-player general-sum
MGs that is agnostic of other agents’ actions.

Risk-sensitive MARL (especially based on the entropic risk measure) has
also been investigated in a thread of literature. The paper [Klompstra
(2000)](#bib.bib17) characterizes Nash equilibria of risk-sensitive
two-player general-sum control games, a special case of general-sum MGs.
The works of [Basu and Ghosh (2012)](#bib.bib3); [Basu and Ghosh
(2014)](#bib.bib4); [Bäuerle and Rieder (2017)](#bib.bib5) prove the
existence of equilibria for discounted risk-sensitive two-player
zero-sum games, and [Cavazos-Cadena and Hernández-Hernández
(2019)](#bib.bib7) establishes the convergence of risk-sensitive value
functions for zero-sum finite games. Moreover, the work [Huang et al.
(2019)](#bib.bib15) considers non-cooperative muti-agent games with
risk-averse agents and proposes an algorithm with almost-sure
convergence results. The author of [Wei (2019)](#bib.bib26) proves the
existence of Nash equilibria for risk-sensitive multi-agent general-sum
games. Our work differs from these works, which mostly focus on
asymptotic results, as we provide finite-sample guarantees for
risk-sensitive MARL.

## 3 Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the setup of general-sum MG with multiple
agents under entropic risk measure, starting with notations.

### 3.1 Notation

We denote $`[n]\coloneqq 1,2,\ldots,n`$ for any positive integer $`n`$.
For any functions $`f`$ and $`g`$ defined on $`{\mathbb{X}}`$, we let
$`f\leq g`$ denote $`f(x)\leq g(x)`$ for all $`x\in{\mathbb{X}}`$ and
$`f\geq g`$ to be defined similarly. For any functions $`f`$ and $`g`$
defined on $`{\mathbb{X}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}`$,
$`f(n)\lesssim g(n)`$ denotes $`f(n)\leq C\cdot g(n)`$ for every
$`n\in{\mathbb{X}}`$ with some universal constant $`C>0`$ and
$`f(n)\gtrsim g(n)`$ is defined similarly. The notation
$`f(n)\asymp g(n)`$ means that we have $`f(n)\lesssim g(n)`$ as well as
$`f(n)\gtrsim g(n)`$. We let $`\log(\cdot)`$ denote natural logarithm
with base $`e`$ unless specified otherwise. The expression
$`\mathrm{polylog}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})`$ is defined as
$`c_{0}\prod_{i\in[n]}\log(v_{i})^{c_{i}}`$ for some universal constants
$`\{c_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}`$ and variables $`\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}`$. We also
define
$`\mathrm{poly}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\coloneqq c_{0}\prod_{i\in[n]}v_{i}^{c_{i}}`$.
Furthermore, we use $`\widetilde{O}(\cdot)`$ to denote
$`O(\cdot\ \mathrm{polylog}(\cdot))`$; we define
$`\widetilde{\Omega}(\cdot)`$ and $`\widetilde{\omega}(\cdot)`$
similarly.

### 3.2 Problem Setup

We consider a risk-sensitive general-sum MG with $`M`$ agents in the
tabular setting, which can be represented as
$`\mathrm{MG}(H,K,{\cal S},\{{\cal A}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M},{\mathcal{P}},\{r_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M},\{\beta_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M})`$.
Here, $`H`$ denotes the horizon (or the number of steps in each
episode), $`{\mathcal{S}}`$ denotes the state space of size
$`S\coloneqq|{\mathcal{S}}|`$. For each agent $`m\in[M]`$,
$`{\cal A}_{m}`$ denotes the action space available to the agent with
size $`A_{m}\coloneqq|{\mathcal{A}}_{m}|`$, and for the convenience of
notation, we further define
$`{\cal A}\coloneqq\prod_{m\in[M]}{\cal A}_{m}`$ to be the action space
of all $`M`$ agents, with cardinality
$`A\coloneqq\prod_{m\in[M]}A_{m}`$. In particluar, we use $`a_{h,m}`$ to
denote the action that agent $`m`$ takes at step $`h`$ and
$`a_{h}\coloneqq(a_{h,1},\ldots,a_{h,M})`$ to denote the actions of all
$`M`$ agents at step $`h`$. Each agent has reward functions
$`r_{m}\coloneqq\{r_{h,m}\}_{h=1}^{H}`$, where
$`r_{h,m}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\to[0,1]`$ is the reward
function at each step $`h`$. In the risk-sensitive setting, each agent
$`m`$ takes a risk parameter $`\beta_{m}\neq 0`$ representing its risk
preference; the agent is risk-seeking if $`\beta_{m}>0`$ and risk-averse
if $`\beta_{m}<0`$. We specify the way $`\{\beta_{m}\}`$ are involved in
the agents’ objectives in the next section. The transition kernels
$`{\mathcal{P}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{h}\}_{h=1}^{H}`$ provide the transition
probability of the underlying MG, _i.e._, the game transitions to
$`s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}`$ with probability
$`{\mathcal{P}}_{h}(s^{\prime}\given s,a)`$ at step $`h`$ given the
current state-action pair $`(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}`$.
The total number of episodes is represented by $`K`$. In each episode
$`k`$, we assume that the game begins at a fixed initial state
$`s^{k}_{1}=s_{1}`$.

In this paper, we study the self-play setting, and we outline the
interaction protocol between the learning algorithm and agents in
[Algorithm 1](#alg1 "In 3.2 Problem Setup ‣ 3 Preliminaries ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
On the agent side, at each step $`h\in[H]`$ of an episode $`k\in[K]`$,
each agent $`m`$ observes the current state $`s_{h}`$ and chooses an
action $`a_{h,m}`$ simultaneously with other agents. Then it receives
its reward $`r_{h,m}(s_{h},a_{h})`$ and the game state transitions to
the next state
$`s_{h+1}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{h}(\cdot\given s_{h},a_{h})`$. An episode
ends after the game reaches step $`H+1`$. We remark that the learning
algorithm does not have access to either the transition kernel or reward
functions.

### 3.3 Policy and Value Functions

For each agent $`m\in[M]`$, we define
$`\pi_{h,m}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\Delta_{{\mathcal{A}}_{m}}`$ as its policy
at step $`h`$ that maps from each state to a distribution on the action
space $`{\mathcal{A}}_{m}`$. We denote $`\pi_{h}`$ as the joint policy
for all $`M`$ agents, where
$`\pi_{h}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}`$ maps from each state to
a distribution on the joint action space $`{\mathcal{A}}`$ at each step
$`h`$. When the decomposition
$`\pi_{h}\coloneqq\prod_{m=1}^{M}\pi_{h,m}`$ exists, we say that
$`\pi_{h}`$ is a product policy.

Given any policy $`\pi=\{\pi_{h}\}`$ and risk parameters
$`\{\beta_{m}\}`$ of the $`M`$ agents, we define the value function
$`V_{h,m}^{\pi}`$ for agent $`m`$ at step $`h`$ as the expected
cumulative reward under the entropic risk measure for each state
$`s\in{\mathcal{S}}`$, _i.e._,

|     |                                                                                                                                                     |                              |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle V*{h,m}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\beta*{m}}\log\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[e^{\beta_{m}\sum*{i=h}^{H}r*{i,m}(s*{i},a*{i})}\ \bigg | \ s\_{h}=s\right]\right\},`$ |     |

and the corresponding action-value function can be similarly defined for
all state-action pairs $`(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}`$,
_i.e._,

|     |                                      |                                                                                                                                       |                                 |
| --- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle Q_{h,m}^{\pi}(s,a)`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\beta*{m}}\log\Big\{\expect*{\pi}\Big[e^{\beta*{m}\sum*{i=h}^{H}r*{i,m}(s*{i},\pi*{i}(s*{i}))}\ \Big | \ s*{h}=s,a*{h}=a\Big]\Big\}.`$ |     |

We remark that $`\beta_{m}>0`$ represents a risk-seeking agent and
$`\beta_{m}<0`$ represents a risk-averse agent; the agent tends to be
risk-neutral as $`\beta_{m}\to 0`$.

### 3.4 Equilibrium

We aim to learn a policy for all agents through $`K`$ episodes of
interactions with the environment, so as to reach certain equilibria for
the given general-sum MGs. In this paper, we consider three types of
equilibria: Nash equilibrium (NE), correlated equilibrium (CE), and
coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE). Before we provide their
definitions, we first set some additional notations. For any policy
$`\pi`$, we denote $`\pi_{-m}`$ to be the joint policy of all agents
except agent $`m`$. We say that $`\pi_{m}^{*}(\pi_{-m})`$ is a best
response policy for agent $`m`$ given $`\pi_{-m}`$, if it holds that
$`V_{h,m}^{\pi_{m}^{*}(\pi_{-m}),\pi_{-m}}(s)=\sup_{\nu}V_{h,m}^{\nu,\pi_{-m}}(s)`$
for all $`(h,s)\in[H]\times{\cal S}`$. We denote the value function
under the best response of agent $`m`$ as
$`V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}}\coloneqq V_{h,m}^{\pi_{m}^{*}(\pi_{-m}),\pi_{-m}}`$
for short. The action-value function for the best response
$`Q_{h,m}^{\pi_{m}^{*}(\pi_{-m}),\pi_{-m}}`$ as well as its shorthand
$`Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}}`$ is similarly defined for all agents
$`m\in[M]`$.

An equilibrium implies that altering the policy of any single agent
alone cannot improve its utility. We say that a product policy $`\pi`$
is a NE if the maximum payoff difference over all agents is zero,
_i.e._, $`\max_{m\in[M]}(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi})(s_{1})=0.`$
Moreover, we say that a joint policy $`\pi`$ (not necessarily a product
policy) is a CCE if
$`\max_{m\in[M]}(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi})(s_{1})=0`$. With
regard to CE, we define strategy modification
$`\psi_{m}\coloneqq\{\psi_{h,s,m}\}_{(h,s)\in[H]\times{\mathcal{S}}}`$,
where $`\psi_{h,s,m}`$ maps from $`{\mathcal{A}}_{m}`$ to itself for
each agent $`m\in[M]`$, state $`s\in{\mathcal{S}}`$, and step
$`h\in[H]`$. Let $`\Psi_{m}`$ denote the set of all possible strategy
modifications $`\psi_{m}`$ available to agent $`m`$. When we apply a
strategy modification $`\psi_{m}\in\Psi_{m}`$ to a policy $`\pi`$, if
$`(a_{h,1},\ldots,a_{h,M})`$ is the $`\pi`$-induced actions for all
agents $`m\in[M]`$ given state $`s`$ and step $`h`$, then the modified
policy $`\psi_{m}\diamond\pi`$ plays the modified joint action
$`(a_{h,1},\ldots,a_{h,m-1},\psi_{h,s,m}(a_{h,m}),a_{h,m+1},\ldots,a_{h,M})`$.
Given the definition of strategy modification, we say that a joint
policy $`\pi`$ is a CE if it satisfies that
$`\max_{m\in[M]}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}(V_{1,m}^{\psi\diamond\pi}-V_{1,m}^{\pi})(s_{1})=0`$,
which means that the best strategy modification for any single agent
cannot improve its utility. We remark that the concepts of NE, CE and
CCE are closely related. Indeed, it can be shown that CCE is a sub-class
of CE, which is in turn a sub-class of NE ([Nisan et al.,
2007](#bib.bib23)). Since NE always exists, CE and CCE always exist as
well.

## 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias

Before diving into our algorithm and regret analysis, we first discuss
some of the pitfalls of standard regrets as a natural generalization of
their risk-neutral counterparts, _i.e._,
$`\overline{\mathrm{Regret}}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$,
$`\overline{\mathrm{Regret}}_{\mathsf{CCE}}(K)`$, and
$`\overline{\mathrm{Regret}}_{\mathsf{CE}}(K)`$.

### 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls

In existing literature, most research has focused on risk-neutral MARL,
where algorithm performance is measured by regret:

|     |                                                                                                                                                                     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\text{RN-Regret}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}(\widetilde{V}_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-\widetilde{V}_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1}),`$ |     |

In the above,
$`\widetilde{V}^{\pi}_{h,m}(s)\coloneqq\expect_{\pi}[\sum_{i=h}^{H}r_{i,m}(s_{i},\pi_{i}(s_{i}))\given s_{h}=s]`$
is the risk-neutral value function. A natural extension of this regret
to the risk-sensitive setting would be to replace the risk-neutral value
functions by their risk-sensitive counterparts, as in

|     |                                                                                                                                            |     |       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ----- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1}).`$ |     | (4.1) |

Similar definitions can be made for CE and CCE. It can be seen that the
definition in
([4.1](#S4.E1 "Equation 4.1 ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
generalizes the regret for risk-sensitive RL studied in the single-agent
setting ([Fei et al., 2020](#bib.bib14); [Fei et al., 2021](#bib.bib13);
[Fei and Xu, 2022a](#bib.bib11)) to the multi-agent setting.

Unfortunately, the naive regret definition in
([4.1](#S4.E1 "Equation 4.1 ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
has its shortcomings. Before we discuss them in details, we introduce
some notations. Let $`m_{*}\coloneqq\argmax_{m\in[M]}|\beta_{m}|`$
denote the index of the agent that is the most risk-sensitive among all
$`[M]`$ agents, and if necessary, we may break the tie in an arbitrary
way. We also set $`\beta_{*}\coloneqq\beta_{m_{*}}`$ to be the risk
parameter of agent $`m_{*}`$. For notational convenience, we define a
risk-dependent factor $`\Phi_{u}(\beta)`$ for any $`\beta\neq 0`$ and
$`u>0`$ as

|     |                                                        |             |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------- | ----------- | ----------- | -------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\Phi\_{u}(\beta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\left | \beta\right | u}(e^{\left | \beta\right | u}-1).`$ |     |

Note that $`\Phi_{u}(\beta)`$ is an even function in $`\beta`$ and
increases exponentially in $`|\beta|`$ and $`u`$. To showcase the
deficiency of the naive definition of regret in
([4.1](#S4.E1 "Equation 4.1 ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")),
we start by presenting a lower bound for the regret in the following
theorem.

###### Theorem 4.1.

For $`H\geq 8`$, $`K\geq\max\{16e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)},16H\}`$, and
$`\log\log K\gtrsim|\beta_{*}|(H-1)`$, there exists an MG such that any
algorithm obeys

|     |                                                                                                                                  |     |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\big]=\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})\cdot\widetilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{KH^{2}}).`$ |     |

The same bound holds for
$`\mathbb{E}\big[\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{CE}}(K)\big]`$ and
$`\mathbb{E}\big[\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{CCE}}(K)\big]`$.

The proof is provided in
[Appendix A](#A1 "Appendix A Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
generalizes the lower bound of the single-agent setting ([Fei et al.,
2020](#bib.bib14), Theorem 3) to the multi-agent setting . We note that
the lower bound in
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
depends on the largest risk parameter $`\beta_{*}`$ (in absolute value)
among all agents through the exponential factor $`\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})`$.
This yields undesirable implications both in theory and practice.

#### Theoretical pitfalls.

The naive regret defined in
([4.1](#S4.E1 "Equation 4.1 ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
may induce equilibrium bias, by which some learned policies only account
for the most risk-sensitive agents while overlooking the remaining
agents. To see this, let us consider the following instance of MG.
Assume $`{\mathcal{S}}=\{s\}`$, $`{\mathcal{A}}_{m}=\{g,b\}`$ for each
$`m\in[M]`$ and $`\{\beta_{m}\}`$ are such that their absolute values
are increasing in $`m`$ (so that $`\beta_{*}=\beta_{M}`$). We denote by
$`a_{-m}`$ a joint action of all agents except agent $`m`$. For each
$`m`$, we assume
$`r_{h,m}(s,(g,a_{-m}))=\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}`$ and
$`r_{h,m}(s,(b,a_{-m}))=0`$ for all possible $`a_{-m}`$, and we consider
$`K\geq\Phi^{2}_{H}(\beta_{*})`$. In particular, for each agent, its
reward functions only depend on its own action (and independent of
actions of all the other agents). Under this setting, the MARL problem
can be decomposed into $`M`$ single-agent ones, and an NE corresponds to
each agent executing its own optimal policy in the respective
single-agent problem. Now suppose an algorithm generates
$`\{\overline{\pi}^{k}\}`$ that incurs
$`\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)=\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})\sqrt{KH^{2}}`$
(_e.g._ by taking $`\overline{\pi}^{k}_{h,m}(s)=b`$ for $`m\in[M-1]`$
and $`\overline{\pi}^{k}_{h,M}(s)=g`$). It can be seen that the attained
naive regret matches the lower bound in
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
and the algorithm appears nearly optimal. However, existing results in
[Fei et al. (2021)](#bib.bib13) show that an exponentially smaller
regret, _i.e._ on the order of or smaller than
$`\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})\sqrt{KH^{2}}`$ (since $`\Phi_{H}(\beta)`$
increases exponentially in $`|\beta|`$), can be achieved by applying a
single-agent algorithm to each agent individually. Therefore, under the
naive regret, the policies $`\{\overline{\pi}^{k}\}`$ only perform
nearly optimally for the agent with the largest risk sensitivity while
being exponentially sub-optimal for all the other agents.

#### Practical pitfalls.

While the concept of equilibrium bias has been primarily explored in
theoretical contexts, its real-world implications are far-reaching and
often detrimental. In the realm of investment, this bias could
disproportionately favor the most risk-seeking or risk-averse investors,
potentially leading to adverse impacts and instability in economic
activities. Similarly, in the world of multiplayer online games, such a
bias tends to favor a select few of the most aggressive or passive
players, thereby creating an imbalanced gaming environment and
diminishing the experience for other participants.

Given the significant shortcomings of equilibrium bias, as highlighted
by the naive regret formulation
([4.1](#S4.E1 "Equation 4.1 ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")),
there emerges a compelling need for an alternative performance metric in
risk-sensitive MARL that can more effectively address these issues.

### 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret

The above discussion motivates us to propose a new notion of regret,
which we call _risk-balanced regret_. We provide its definition below.

###### Definition 4.2.

For product policies $`\{\pi^{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}`$, we define the
risk-balanced regret with respect to NE as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)=\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}.
``` |  | (4.2) |

Moreover, for joint policies $`\{\pi^{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}`$, we define the
risk-balanced regret with respect to CE as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{Regret}_{\mathsf{CE}}(K)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{\psi\diamond\pi^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})},`$ |  |

and with respect to CCE as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{Regret}_{\mathsf{CCE}}(K)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}.`$ |  |

It is important to note that while the regret definitions for NE and CCE
bear similarities, the definition for CCE is more inclusive, applying to
all joint policies without the necessity of them being product policies,
as is mandated in the NE definition. The term $`\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$
can be interpreted as a normalization of the regret concept within the
single-agent learning paradigm. Here, the risk-balanced factors
$`{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}`$ play a pivotal role, symmetrically moderating
the sub-optimality experienced by each agent in a dynamic manner,
reflective of their respective risk sensitivities. In conjunction with
these concepts, we also introduce a spectrum of notions pertaining to
approximate equilibria, each intricately connected to the framework of
risk-balanced regret.

###### Definition 4.3.

We say a product policy $`\pi`$ is $`(\beta,\varepsilon)`$-approximate
NE if

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}\leq\varepsilon.`$ |  |

In addition, we say a joint policy $`\pi`$ is
$`(\beta,\varepsilon)`$-approximate CE if

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\max_{m\in[M]}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{\psi\diamond\pi}-V_{1,m}^{\pi})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}\leq\varepsilon,`$ |  |

and $`(\beta,\varepsilon)`$-approximate CCE if

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}\leq\varepsilon.`$ |  |

Furthermore, a simple relationship between $`\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ and
$`\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ may be observed, as in

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\max_{\ell\in[M]}\Phi_{H}(\beta_{\ell})}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})}\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})}\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K),`$ |  | (4.3) |

where the inequality holds since $`\Phi_{H}(\beta)`$ is increasing in
$`|\beta|`$. It is not hard to see that similar equations to
([4.3](#S4.E3 "Equation 4.3 ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
can be derived for $`\reg_{\mathsf{CE}}(K)`$ and
$`\reg_{\mathsf{CCE}}(K)`$. In view of
([4.3](#S4.E3 "Equation 4.3 ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")),
we have that the risk-balanced regret
([4.2](#S4.E2 "Equation 4.2 ‣ Definition 4.2. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
is more general than the naive regret
([4.1](#S4.E1 "Equation 4.1 ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
in the sense that, for any algorithm, attaining an upper bound on
$`\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ implies that it also attains an upper bound on
$`\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$. Specifically, if
$`\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\leq U`$ for some $`U\geq 0`$, then we have
$`\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\leq\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\leq\Phi_{H}(\beta_{*})U`$,
thanks to
([4.3](#S4.E3 "Equation 4.3 ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")).

Combined with
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
the inequality
([4.3](#S4.E3 "Equation 4.3 ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
directly leads to the following lower bound for risk-balanced regret.

###### Theorem 4.4.

Under the same setting as
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
any algorithm obeys the lower bound

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\big]=\widetilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{KH^{2}}).`$ |  |

The same result holds for $`\mathbb{E}\big[\reg_{\mathsf{CE}}(K)\big]`$
and $`\mathbb{E}\big[\reg_{\mathsf{CCE}}(K)\big]`$.

We remark that the results of
[Theorem 4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4.4. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
coincide with those of
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
for $`M=1`$ or when all agents take the same risk parameter.

Given
[Theorem 4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4.4. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
let us discuss how the risk-balanced regret in
[Definition 4.2](#S4.Thmtheorem2 "Definition 4.2. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
overcomes the issue of equilibrium bias suffered by the naive regret. We
observe that a nearly optimal algorithm under the risk-balanced regret
has to learn a policy for *each* agent such that the (un-normalized)
regret of agent $`m`$ is upper bounded by a quantity proportional to
risk-balanced factor $`\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})`$. To see this, we let
$`\reg_{m,\mathsf{NE}}(K)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in[K]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}`$
be the (normalized) regret incurred by agent $`m`$ alone. In order to
match with a lower bound (*e.g.*, the single-agent version of
[Theorem 4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4.4. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")),
an algorithm must achieve the near-optimal regret bound
$`\reg_{m,\mathsf{NE}}(K)=\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{K\cdot\mathrm{poly}(H)})`$
for each individual agent $`m\in[M]`$; otherwise, if for some agent
$`m^{\prime}`$ the algorithm incurs
$`\reg_{m^{\prime},\mathsf{NE}}(K)=\widetilde{\omega}({\sqrt{K\cdot\mathrm{poly}(H)}})`$,
then we would have
$`\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)\geq\reg_{m^{\prime},\mathsf{NE}}(K)=\widetilde{\omega}({\sqrt{K\cdot\mathrm{poly}(H)}})`$,
which is sub-optimal compared to the lower bound in
[Theorem 4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4.4. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
and results in a contradiction to our assumption that the algorithm is
nearly optimal.

In the remaining paper, we present an algorithm and proves that it
nearly attains the lower bound of
[Theorem 4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4.4. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").

Within each episode $`k`$, we update the upper and lower confidence
bounds $`\overline{Q}_{h,m}`$ and $`\underline{Q}_{h,m}`$ of the
action-value function using the estimated transition kernel based on
samples collected from the past $`k-1`$ episodes. We present the
detailed updates of the confidence bounds in
[Algorithm 3](#alg3 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
For each agent $`m`$, step $`h`$, and state-action pair $`(s,a)`$, we
compute $`\overline{q}_{h,m}(s,a)`$ and $`\underline{q}_{h,m}(s,a)`$ as
estimates of the exponential action-value functions
$`\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}}[e^{\beta_{m}[r_{h,m}(s,a)+\overline{V}_{h+1,m}(s^{\prime})]}]`$
and
$`\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}}[e^{\beta_{m}[r_{h,m}(s,a)+\underline{V}_{h+1,m}(s^{\prime})]}]`$,
respectively. We then transform $`\overline{q}_{h,m}`$ and
$`\underline{q}_{h,m}`$ in
[3](#alg2.l3 "In Algorithm 3 ‣ 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")

[Algorithm 3](#alg3 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
into estimates $`\overline{Q}_{h,m}`$ and $`\underline{Q}_{h,m}`$,
respectively, by incorporating a bonus term $`\gamma_{h,m}`$ and
applying proper truncation:

|  |  |  |  |

|  |

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{h,m}(s,a)\leftarrow\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\beta_{m}}\log\{\min\{\overline{q}_{h,m}(s,a)+\gamma_{h,m}(s,a),e^{\beta_{m}(H-h+1)}\}\}&\text{if }\beta_{m}>0;\\
\frac{1}{\beta_{m}}\log\{\max\{\overline{q}_{h,m}(s,a)-\gamma_{h,m}(s,a),e^{\beta_{m}(H-h+1)}\}\}&\text{if }\beta_{m}<0,\end{cases}
``` |  | (5.1) |
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle\underline{Q}_{h,m}(s,a)\leftarrow\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\beta_{m}}\log\{\max\{\underline{q}_{h,m}(s,a)-\gamma_{h,m}(s,a),1\}\}&\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{if }\beta_{m}>0;\\

``` |  | (5.2) |

The bonus term $`\gamma_{h,m}`$ facilitates Risk-Sensitive Optimism in
the Face of Uncertainty ([Fei et al., 2020](#bib.bib14)) by augmenting
$`\overline{q}_{h,m}`$ and $`\underline{q}_{h,m}`$. Notice that the way
$`\gamma_{h,m}`$ joins the formulae depends on the sign of $`\beta_{m}`$
for each agent $`m`$. For the upper bound $`\overline{Q}_{h,m}`$, we add
bonus for $`\beta_{m}>0`$ and subtract it for $`\beta_{m}<0`$, whereas
in the lower bound $`\underline{Q}_{h,m}`$, we subtract bonus for
$`\beta_{m}>0`$ while adding it for $`\beta_{m}<0`$. This is because the
exponential function $`z\mapsto e^{\beta z}`$ is an increasing function
when $`\beta>0`$ and decreasing function when $`\beta<0`$; so are
$`\overline{q}_{h,m}`$ and $`\underline{q}_{h,m}`$ given their
construction. Subtracting bonus, *i.e.*,
$`\overline{q}_{h,m}(s,a)-\gamma_{h,m}(s,a)`$, under $`\beta<0`$ yields
a smaller estimation of the exponential value function, and this implies
a *larger* upper confidence bound $`\overline{Q}_{h,m}`$ of the
action-value function. A similar argument holds for
$`\underline{Q}_{h,m}`$. Another feature of
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
is the difference in thresholding applied to $`\overline{Q}_{h,m}`$ and
$`\underline{Q}_{h,m}`$. This is due to the fact that
$`\overline{q}_{h,m}`$ and $`\underline{q}_{h,m}`$ are designed to
estimate the upper and lower exponential value functions, whose upper
and lower bounds are $`e^{\beta(H-h+1)}`$ and 1 respectively.

In
[11](#alg1.l11 "In Algorithm 2 ‣ 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
of
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
we update the policy by solving a one-step multi-agent game via the
oracle subroutine $`\mathsf{EquilSolver}`$, which can be instantiated by
existing solvers for NE, CE and CCE, respectively ([Berg and Sandholm,
2017](#bib.bib6)). Here, we assume that $`\mathsf{EquilSolver}`$
maximizes the utility of all agents. We therefore use the signed
exponential value estimates
$`\{(-1)^{{\mathbb{I}}(\beta_{m}<0)}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}(s,\cdot)}\}`$
as input to the solver; it can be seen that for $`\beta_{m}<0`$,
maximizing with respect to $`-e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}(s,\cdot)}`$
amounts to maximizing with respect to $`\overline{Q}_{h,m}(s,\cdot)`$.
We also note that existing solvers for CCE and CE based on linear
programming have polynomial time complexity, while those for NE are
PPAD-hard ([Daskalakis, 2013](#bib.bib9)).

## 6 Main Results

In this section we present our main theoretical results, which comprise
of upper bounds on the risk-balanced regret attained by
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
and its induced sample complexity.

###### Theorem 6.1.

For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$, with probability $`1-\delta`$, Algorithm
[2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
attains the following regret upper bound when $`\mathsf{EquilSolver}`$
is instantiated as a NE solver:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)=\widetilde{O}\big(\sqrt{KH^{4}S^{2}A}\big).`$ |  |

The same result holds for $`\reg_{\mathsf{CE}}(K)`$ and
$`\reg_{\mathsf{CCE}}(K)`$ when $`\mathsf{EquilSolver}`$ is instantiated
as a CE and CCE solver, respectively. Moreover, with probability at
least $`\frac{2}{3}`$, $`\widehat{\pi}`$ output by
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
is a $`(\beta,\varepsilon)`$-approximate NE, CE or CCE if
$`K=\widetilde{\Omega}(H^{4}S^{2}A/\varepsilon^{2})`$.

The proof is provided in
[Appendix B](#A2 "Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
We make the following remarks for
[Theorem 6.1](#S6.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 6.1. ‣ 6 Main Results ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").

#### Comparison with the lower bound.

In view of Theorem
[4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4.4. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
we see that the above regret upper bound is nearly optimal up to a
logarithmic factor in $`K`$ and polynomial factors of $`H`$, $`S`$ and
$`A`$. Note that although agents may take different risk parameters
$`\{\beta_{m}\}`$, the upper bound in Theorem
[6.1](#S6.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 6.1. ‣ 6 Main Results ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
is not influenced by the difference among $`\{\beta_{m}\}`$ as they have
been “normalized out” through the risk-balanced factor
$`\{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})\}`$ in the definition of regret.

#### Comparison with existing results.

Our results can also be connected back to some of the existing works,
showing that our bounds generalize theirs:

- •
  When $`\beta_{m}\to 0`$ for all $`m\in[M]`$, our
  [Theorem 6.1](#S6.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 6.1. ‣ 6 Main Results ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
  recovers the upper bound for MGs with risk-neutral agents ([Liu
  et al., 2020](#bib.bib21), Theorem 16), since
  $`\lim_{b\to 0}\Phi_{H}(b)=1`$ and $`\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ tends to
  $`\text{RN-Regret}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$
- •
  When $`M=1`$, the multi-agent setting reduces to the single-agent
  setting, and
  [Theorem 6.1](#S6.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 6.1. ‣ 6 Main Results ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
  implies that an agent with risk parameter $`\beta`$ incurs the
  normalized regret (by $`\Phi_{H}(\beta)`$) of order
  $`\widetilde{O}\big(\sqrt{KH^{4}S^{2}A}\big)`$, recovering the bound
  presented in [Fei et al. (2021, Theorem 1)](#bib.bib13) up to
  logarithmic factors.

#### Technical highlights.

The proof crucially relies on controlling the differences between upper
and lower confidence bounds $`\{\overline{V}^{k}_{h,m}\}`$ and
$`\{\underline{V}^{k}_{h,m}\}`$. In particular, we show that the
quantities
$`\{\frac{e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}}}{\rho_{h,m}}\}`$
(where $`\rho_{h,m}`$ is a quantity that depends on $`h,m`$) is upper
bounded by a carefully constructed sequence $`\{U_{h}^{k}\}`$, such that
$`U_{1}^{k}(s^{k}_{1})\geq\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi^{k}_{-m}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s^{k}_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}`$
for all $`m\in[M]`$. We then analyze the evolution of the sequence
$`\{U_{h}^{k}\}`$, which yields the upper bound of regret. Perhaps
interestingly, the normalization sequence $`\{\rho_{h,m}\}`$ evolves in
different ways for risk-seeking and risk-averse agents, thus revealing
the inherent asymmetry between the two types of agents. Such finding
helps highlight, in a quantitative way, the role of the risk-balanced
factors $`\{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})\}`$ in symmetrizing the sub-optimality
of each agent in computation of regret. We believe that this finding
could be of independent interest for further research in risk-sensitive
multi-agent games.

## 7 Conclusion

In this paper, we study the problem of risk-sensitive MARL under the
setting of general-sum MG, where agents optimize the entropic risk
measure of rewards and possibly take different risk preferences. We
demonstrate that a naive definition of regret adapted from risk-neutral
MARL suffers equilibrium bias by inducing policies that favor the most
risk-sensitive agents without taking into account of the other agents in
the same game. Motivated by such deficiency of the naive regret, we
propose a novel notion of regret, named as risk-balanced regret. We
derive a lower bound w.r.t risk-balanced regret, from which we show that
the proposed regret overcomes the issue of equilibrium bias. In
addition, we propose a self-play MARL algorithm based on value iteration
for learning NE, CE and CCE of the general-sum MG, and we provide a
nearly optimal upper bound for the proposed algorithm w.r.t. the
risk-balanced regret. The bound is shown to generalize existing results
derived under risk-neutral or single-agent settings.

## Appendix A Proof of Theorem [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")

We provide a regret lower bound that any algorithm has to incur, and
this is achieved through considering a hard instance of the
$`K`$-episode and $`H`$-step MGs. Let $`m_{*}`$ be the index of the most
risk-sensitive agent, *i.e.*,
$`m_{*}\coloneqq\argmax_{m\in[M]}|\beta_{m}|`$ with ties broken
arbitrarily. We assume that the transition kernel of the MG depends only
on the action of $`m_{*}`$, and it then reduces to an Markov decision
process (MDP) with respect to $`m_{*}`$. To further simplify the MG and
remove the effect of all agents other than $`m_{*}`$ on
$`\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$, we define reward functions such
that all agents except $`m_{*}`$ receive a constant reward no matter
which action they take. More specifically, the MG has three states: an
initial state $`s_{0}`$ that serves as a dummy state, an absorbing state
$`s_{1}`$ in which the agent $`m_{*}`$ keeps getting positive rewards,
and an absorbing state $`s_{2}`$ where the agent $`m_{*}`$ gets no
reward at all. There are also two actions $`a_{1},a_{2}`$ available to
all agents at every state, *i.e.*, $`r_{h,m_{*}}(s_{0},a)=0`$,
$`r_{h,m_{*}}(s_{1},a)=1`$, and $`r_{h,m_{*}}(s_{2},a)=0`$ for the most
risk-sensitive agent $`m_{*}`$, and $`r_{h,m}(s_{0},a)=0`$,
$`r_{h,m}(s_{1},a)=1`$, and $`r_{h,m}(s_{2},a)=1`$ for all other agents
$`m\neq m_{*}`$ and all $`a\in\{a_{1},a_{2}\}`$. In other words, the
rewards for all agents except agent $`m_{*}`$ are always the same and
contains no stochasticity. The transition kernel is simple as it only
depends on the action of agent $`m_{*}`$ at the initial state $`s_{0}`$.
Especially, we let the state transitions from $`s_{0}`$ to $`s_{1}`$ if
agent $`m_{*}`$ takes $`a_{1}`$ and to $`s_{2}`$ if it takes $`a_{2}`$.

Recall that the regret is defined as the sum of the regrets on the worst
performing agent at each episode, and all agents except $`m_{*}`$ incur
no regret under the MG we defined. Thus, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}(V_{1,m_{*}}^{*,\pi_{-m_{*}}^{k}}-V_{1,m_{*}}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}(V_{1,m_{*}}^{*}-V_{1,m_{*}}^{\pi^{k}_{m_{*}}})(s_{1}).`$ |  |

Then $`\overline{\reg}_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ equals to the regret of the
most risk-sensitive agent $`m_{*}`$, and the MG is reduced to an MDP
with respect to $`m_{*}`$, where $`V_{1,m_{*}}^{*}`$ denotes the optimal
value function of $`m_{*}`$ and $`V_{1,m_{*}}^{\pi^{k}_{m_{*}}}`$
denotes the value function of $`m_{*}`$ under policy $`\pi^{k}`$. To
simplify the notation, we will denote them as $`V_{1}^{*}`$ and
$`V_{1}^{\pi^{k}}`$ without explicitly referring the agent $`m_{*}`$.
The NE in this case corresponds to agent $`m_{*}`$ playing the optimal
action with respect to its own MDP and all the other agents playing
arbitrary policy. Therefore, we drop the subscript of NE and use
$`\overline{\reg}(K)`$ to simplify notation. Notice that such MG is
equivalent to a $`K`$-episode and $`H`$-step MDP of $`m_{*}`$, which is
further equivalent to a $`K`$-round bandit of $`m_{*}`$ due to the
nature of absorbing states $`s_{1}`$ and $`s_{2}`$.

In particular, we construct two bandit problems with two arms for the
agent $`m_{*}`$. The idea is to construct two bandits, each with a pair
of hard-to-distinguish arms. The first bandit machine has the following
two arms: the first arm has reward $`H-1`$ with probability $`p_{1}`$
and reward $`0`$ with probability $`1-p_{1}`$ if $`\beta_{*}>0`$ (reward
$`H-1`$ with probability $`1-p_{1}`$ and reward $`0`$ with probability
$`p_{1}`$ if $`\beta_{*}<0`$); the second arm has reward $`H-1`$ with
probability $`p_{2}`$ and reward $`0`$ with probability $`1-p_{2}`$ if
$`\beta_{*}>0`$ (and reward $`H-1`$ with probability $`1-p_{2}`$ and
reward $`0`$ with probability $`p_{2}`$ if $`\beta_{*}<0`$). The second
bandit machine also has two arms: the first arm has reward $`H-1`$ with
probability $`q_{1}`$ and reward $`0`$ with probability $`1-q_{1}`$ if
$`\beta_{*}>0`$ (reward $`H-1`$ with probability $`1-q_{1}`$ and reward
$`0`$ with probability $`q_{1}`$ if $`\beta_{*}<0`$); the second arm has
reward $`H-1`$ with probability $`q_{2}`$ and reward $`0`$ with
probability $`1-q_{2}`$ if $`\beta_{*}>0`$ (and reward $`H-1`$ with
probability $`1-q_{2}`$ and reward $`0`$ with probability $`q_{2}`$ if
$`\beta_{*}<0`$). To see the correspondence between the MDP of $`m_{*}`$
and the bandit problem, agent $`m_{*}`$ taking action $`a_{1}`$ is
equivalent to pressing the first arm of the bandit machine; similarly,
taking action $`a_{2}`$ is equivalent to pressing the second arm.

With proper setup of $`p_{1},p_{2}`$ and $`q_{1},q_{2}`$, we are able to
show that no policy $`\pi`$ can do well on both of the bandit problems.
We denote the regret of $`\pi`$ on the first bandit to be
$`\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)`$ and that on the second bandit to be
$`\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)`$. The worst regret between the two can be
lower bounded through

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\max\{\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)+\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)\}`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\reg}_{2}(K).`$ |  |

Following
[Lemma A.1](#A1.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma A.1. ‣ Appendix A Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
with proper choice on $`p_{1},p_{2}`$ and $`q_{1},q_{2}`$, we conclude
that the lower bound of $`\overline{\reg}(K)`$ on the bandit with the
worst regret

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{2}\left[\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)+\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)\right]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1}{|\beta_{*}|}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}Ke^{-|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\gtrsim}\frac{e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1}{|\beta_{*}|}\sqrt{\frac{K}{\log K}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\gtrsim}\frac{e^{|\beta_{*}|H}-1}{|\beta_{*}|}\sqrt{\frac{K}{(\log K)^{3}}}.`$ |  |

Here $`(i)`$ is due to $`\log\log K\gtrsim|\beta_{*}|(H-1)`$ and
$`(ii)`$ is due to
$`e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1\geq(1-\frac{1}{H})e^{-|\beta_{*}|}(e^{|\beta_{*}|H}-1)`$
and $`\log\log K\gtrsim|\beta_{*}|`$. More specifically, notice that we
have a decomposition

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{|\beta_{*}|}(e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1)=(e^{|\beta_{*}|H}-1)-(e^{|\beta_{*}|}-1)`$ |  |

and it holds that
$`(e^{|\beta_{*}|H}-1)/(e^{|\beta_{*}|}-1)\geq\lim_{x\to 1_{+}}\frac{x^{H}-1}{x-1}=H`$
for any $`\beta_{*}\neq 0`$ due to the convexity of the function
$`x^{H}`$. It then follows that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{|\beta_{*}|}(e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1)\geq\Big(1-\frac{1}{H}\Big)(e^{|\beta_{*}|H}-1).`$ |  |

###### Lemma A.1.

We assume the bandit machines in
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
For any corresponding MG with $`H\geq 8`$ and
$`K\geq\max\{16e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)},16H\}`$, there exists a set of
$`p_{1},p_{2}`$ and $`q_{1},q_{2}`$, such that the regret of any policy
obeys

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)+\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1}{|\beta_{*}|}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}Ke^{-|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}}.`$ |  |

###### Proof.

The proof follows a similar argument in [Fei et al. (2020)](#bib.bib14)
and [Fei and Xu (2022b)](#bib.bib12), and we supply a complete proof
here that adapts to the alternative conditions of the lemma. For the
bandit machines defined in
[Theorem 4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 4.1. ‣ 4.1 A Naive Definition of Regret and Its Pitfalls ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
if we let $`p_{2}=e^{-|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}`$ and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{1}=q_{1}=\begin{cases}p_{2}+\overline{p},&\beta_{*}>0;\\
p_{2}-\overline{p},&\beta_{*}<0;\end{cases}\qquad q_{2}=\begin{cases}p_{2}+2\overline{p},&\beta_{*}>0;\\
p_{2}-2\overline{p},&\beta_{*}<0,\end{cases}`$ |  |

then it holds that $`p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}`$ for any
$`\overline{p}\leq\frac{1}{4}e^{-|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}`$ if
$`|\beta_{*}|(H-1)\geq\log 4`$ and $`H\geq 2`$. If we let
$`p_{2}=\frac{1}{H}`$ and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{1}=q_{1}=\begin{cases}p_{2}+\overline{p},&\beta_{*}>0;\\
p_{2}-\overline{p},&\beta_{*}<0;\end{cases}\qquad q_{2}=\begin{cases}p_{2}+2\overline{p},&\beta_{*}>0;\\
p_{2}-2\overline{p},&\beta_{*}<0,\end{cases}`$ |  |

then it holds that $`p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}`$ for any
$`\overline{p}\leq\frac{1}{4H}`$ if $`|\beta_{*}|(H-1)\leq\log H`$ and
$`H>8`$. The following argument holds for both scenarios.

Recall that in the $`K`$-round bandit problem, any policy $`\pi^{k}`$
can only pick one of the two arms that are corresponding to action
$`a_{1}`$ and $`a_{2}`$. Let us define $`r_{a}`$ to be the reward from
taking action $`a\in\{a_{1},a_{2}\}`$. Without loss of generality, we
look at the first bandit machine and assume that the action $`a_{1}`$
corresponds to the optimal arm and $`a_{2}`$ corresponds to the
sub-optimal arm. The regret can be written as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}\frac{1}{|\beta_{*}|}\Big|\log\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}-\log\Big(\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{1}]\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}+\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}\Big)\Big|`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}\frac{1}{|\beta_{*}|}\bigg|\log\frac{\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{1}]\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}+\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}}{\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}}\bigg|,`$ |  |

where the probability $`\prob_{p}`$ is with respect to both the policy
$`\pi`$ and the success probability of the arms. Notice that
$`\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}\geq\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}`$
for $`\beta_{*}>0`$, and it follows that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bigg|\log\frac{\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{1}]\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}+\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}}{\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}}\bigg|`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\log\Big(\frac{|\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}-\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}|}{\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}}\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]+1\Big).`$ |  |

Therefore, under either setup of $`p_{1}`$ and $`p_{2}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2|\beta_{*}|}\frac{|\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}-\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}|}{\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}}\sum_{k\in[K]}\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}],`$ |  |

where the inequality follows from $`\log(1+x)\geq x/2`$ for
$`x\in[0,1]`$ and the assumption that
$`\overline{p}\leq\frac{1}{4}p_{2}`$. Swap the order of $`a_{1}`$ and
$`a_{2}`$, we similarly get a lower bound for either pair of $`q_{1}`$
and $`q_{2}`$ that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)\geq\frac{1}{2|\beta_{*}|}\frac{|\expect_{q}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}-\expect_{q}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}|}{\expect_{q}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}}\sum_{k\in[K]}\prob_{q}[a^{k}=a_{1}].`$ |  |

In particular, for the first bandit machine we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{|\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}-\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}|}{\expect_{p}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{|(\prob_{p}[a_{1}]-\prob_{p}[a_{2}])e^{\beta_{*}(H-1)}-(\prob_{p}[a_{1}]-\prob_{p}[a_{2}])|}{\prob_{p}[a_{1}]e^{\beta_{*}(H-1)}+(1-\prob_{p}[a_{1}])}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{|\overline{p}(e^{\beta_{*}(H-1)}-1)|}{\prob_{p}[a_{1}]e^{\beta_{*}(H-1)}+(1-\prob_{p}[a_{1}])}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{\overline{p}}{4}(e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1),`$ |  |

where the inequality holds for both pairs of $`p_{1}`$ and $`p_{2}`$.
Similarly, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{|\expect_{q}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{1}}}-\expect_{q}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}|}{\expect_{q}e^{\beta_{*}r_{a_{2}}}}`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{\overline{p}}{4}(e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1)`$ |  |

for both pairs of $`q_{1}`$ and $`q_{2}`$. Hence, we can combine both
lower bounds on $`\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)`$ and
$`\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)+\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{\overline{p}}{4|\beta_{*}|}(e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1)\cdot\sum_{k\in[K]}\big(\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]+\prob_{q}[a^{k}=a_{1}]\big).`$ |  |

It is note-worthy here that $`a_{2}`$ is sub-optimal for the first
bandit and $`a_{1}`$ is sub-optimal for the second bandit. Notice that
for any policy $`\pi`$ applied to both bandit machines, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[K]}\big(\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]+\prob_{q}[a^{k}=a_{1}]\big)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\expect_{p}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{2}\}\Big]+\expect_{q}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}\Big],`$ |  |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\expect_{p}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{2}\}\Big]`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{K}{2}\prob_{p}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{2}\}>\frac{K}{2}\Big],`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\expect_{q}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}\Big]`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{K}{2}\prob_{q}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}>\frac{K}{2}\Big]`$ |  |

due to a simple lower bound on the cases where the sub-optimal arm is
selected more than half of the time. We denote the shorthand
$`p_{\beta_{*}}\coloneqq p_{2}`$ if $`\beta_{*}>0`$ and
$`p_{\beta_{*}}\coloneqq 1-p_{2}`$ if $`\beta_{*}<0`$;
$`q_{\beta_{*}}\coloneqq p_{2}`$ if $`\beta_{*}>0`$ and
$`q_{\beta_{*}}\coloneqq 1-p_{2}`$ if $`\beta_{*}<0`$. A lower bound on
the sum of the paired probabilities is given by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\prob_{p}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}\leq\frac{K}{2}\Big]+\prob_{q}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}>\frac{K}{2}\Big]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-\mathsf{KL}\big(\mathrm{Ber}(p_{\beta_{*}})\|\mathrm{Ber}(q_{\beta_{*}})\big)K\right),`$ |  |

where it follows from [Lattimore and Szepesvári (2020, Theorem
14.2)](#bib.bib18) that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\prob_{p}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}\leq\frac{K}{2}\Big]+\prob_{q}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{1}\}>\frac{K}{2}\Big]\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp(-\mathsf{KL}(\prob_{p}\|\prob_{q}))`$ |  |

and [Lattimore and Szepesvári (2020, Lemma 15.1)](#bib.bib18) that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}(\prob_{p}\|\prob_{q})=\mathsf{KL}\big(\mathrm{Ber}(p_{\beta_{*}})\|\mathrm{Ber}(q_{\beta_{*}})\big)\cdot\expect_{p}\Big[\sum_{k\in[K]}{\mathbb{I}}\{a^{k}=a_{2}\}\Big].`$ |  |

Following the definition of Kullback-Leibler (kl) divergence and some
simple calculations, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}\big(\mathrm{Ber}(p_{\beta_{*}})\|\mathrm{Ber}(q_{\beta_{*}})\big)`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q_{\beta_{*}}-p_{\beta_{*}})^{2}}{q_{\beta_{*}}(1-q_{\beta_{*}})}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\overline{p}^{2}}{p_{2}(1-p_{2})},`$ |  |

where the first inequality follows from $`\log(1+x)\leq x`$ on
$`{\mathbb{R}}`$ and the second inequality follows from the definition
of the bandit such that $`|p_{2}-q_{2}|=2\overline{p}`$ and
$`p_{2}\leq q_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}`$ for $`\beta_{*}>0`$ and
$`\frac{1}{2}p_{2}\leq q_{2}\leq p_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}`$ for
$`\beta_{*}<0`$. Consequently, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[K]}\big(\prob_{p}[a^{k}=a_{2}]+\prob_{q}[a^{k}=a_{1}]\big)\geq\frac{K}{4}\exp\Big(-\frac{8K\overline{p}^{2}}{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}\Big).`$ |  |

Finally, we combine the lower bounds on $`\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)`$ and
$`\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)`$ together to get

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\reg}_{1}(K)+\overline{\reg}_{2}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{K\overline{p}}{32|\beta_{*}|}(e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1)\exp\Big(-\frac{8K\overline{p}^{2}}{p_{2}(1-p_{2})}\Big)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{e^{|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}-1}{|\beta_{*}|}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}Ke^{-|\beta_{*}|(H-1)}},`$ |  |

where the second inequality follows from taking
$`\overline{p}=\sqrt{(p_{2}(1-p_{2}))/K}`$ for $`K\geq 16/p_{2}`$ such
that $`\overline{p}\leq\frac{1}{4}p_{2}`$. ∎

## Appendix B Proof of Theorem [6.1](#S6.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 6.1. ‣ 6 Main Results ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")

We focus on the proof for the case of NE; the proofs for CE and CCE
follow the same reasoning, with the differences presented in
[Section B.4](#A2.SS4 "B.4 Proofs for CCE and CE ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
For any state $`s\in{\mathcal{S}}`$, policy $`\pi`$ and function
$`Q^{\prime}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{R}`$, we denote
$`[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi}Q^{\prime}](s)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q^{\prime}(s,a)]`$.

### B.1 Some Useful Lemmas

Let us first present a uniform concentration result.

###### Lemma B.1.

For $`G>0`$, consider the function class

|     |                                                                   |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     | $`\displaystyle{\cal W}=\{e^{\beta g}\ |\ g:{\cal S}\to[0,G]\}.`$ |     |

For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$, and for all
$`(k,h,s,a)\in[K]\times[H]\times{\cal S}\times{\cal A}`$ with
$`N_{h}^{k}(s,a)\geq 1`$, there exists a universal constant $`c>0`$ such
that, with probability at least $`1-\delta`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|[(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathcal{P}_{h})e^{\beta g}](s,a)\right|\leq c\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|\sqrt{\frac{S\log(HSAK/\delta)}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}}.`$ |  |

###### Proof.

Define $`{\cal C}_{\varepsilon}({\cal W})`$ to be an
$`\varepsilon`$-covering of $`{\cal G}`$ with respect to the
$`\ell_{\infty}`$ norm for any $`\varepsilon>0`$. Mathematically, this
means that for any $`W\in{\cal W}`$, there exists
$`W^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{\varepsilon}({\cal W})`$ such that
$`\sup_{s\in{\cal S}}\left|W(s)-W^{\prime}(s)\right|\leq\varepsilon`$.
It is not hard to verify that
$`\left|{\cal C}_{\varepsilon}({\cal W})\right|\leq\left(3\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|/\varepsilon\right)^{S}`$.
Now fix a $`(k,h,s,a)\in[K]\times[H]\times{\cal S}\times{\cal A}`$, and
define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{k,W}(s,a)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}\sum_{\tau\in[k-1]}\mathbb{I}\{(s_{h}^{\tau},a_{h}^{\tau})=(s,a)\}\cdot W(s_{h+1}^{\tau})-(\mathcal{P}_{h}W)(s,a)].`$ |  |

By Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound over both $`W\in{\cal W}`$
and $`N_{h}^{k}\in[K]`$, with probability at least $`1-\delta/(HSA)`$,
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\sup_{W^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{\varepsilon}({\cal W})}\varphi_{h}^{k,W^{\prime}}(s,a)\right|\leq\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|\sqrt{\frac{S\log(3\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|/\varepsilon)+\log(HSAK/\delta)}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}}.`$ |  |

Set $`\varepsilon=3\delta\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|/\sqrt{HSAK}`$ and
the above equation yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\sup_{W^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{\varepsilon}({\cal W})}\varphi_{h}^{k,W^{\prime}}(s,a)\right|\leq c_{0}\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|\sqrt{\frac{S\log(HSAK/\delta)}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}}.`$ |  |

On the other hand, for any $`W\in{\cal W}`$, there exists
$`W^{\prime}\in{\cal C}_{\varepsilon}({\cal W})`$ and a universal
constant $`c^{\prime}>0`$ such that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\varphi_{h}^{k,W}(s,a)-\varphi_{h}^{k,W^{\prime}}(s,a)\right|`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq 6\delta\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|\sqrt{\frac{1}{HSAK}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\left|e^{\beta G}-1\right|\sqrt{\frac{S\log(HSAK/\delta)}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}}.`$ |  |

We combine the previous displays and take a union bound over
$`(h,s,a)\in[H]\times{\cal S}\times{\cal A}`$ to conclude the proof. ∎

We have the following lemma that bounds the sample exponential value
functions.

###### Lemma B.2.

For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$ and $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, the following statements
hold with probability at least $`1-\delta`$. If $`\beta_{m}>0`$, then we
have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)},\quad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\leq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)},`$ |  |

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)},\quad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\leq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)},`$ |  |

and if $`\beta_{m}<0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\leq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)},\quad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)},`$ |  |

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\leq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)},\quad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)},`$ |  |

###### Proof.

Let us fix a tuple $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$ and $`\delta\in(0,1]`$. We focus on
the inequalities
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)}`$
and
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}`$
with $`\beta_{m}>0`$; the other inequalities can be established in
similar ways.

From the update procedure of the algorithm and the Bellman equation, we
have the recursion

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}})(s,a)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}](s,a)+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)-e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\mathcal{P}_{h}e^{\beta_{m}V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}](s,a)`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\begin{split}&=e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}})](s,a)+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)\\
&\qquad+[(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathcal{P}_{h})e^{\beta_{m}[r_{h,m}(s,a)+V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}]}](s,a)\end{split}`$ |  |  | (B.1) |

Note that by
[Lemma B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle-\gamma^{k}_{h,m}(s,a)\leq[(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathcal{P}_{h})e^{\beta_{m}[r_{h,m}(s,a)+V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}]}](s,a)\leq\gamma^{k}_{h,m}(s,a).`$ |  | (B.2) |

The remaining proof proceeds by induction. We first check the base case:
by definition, $`\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}=V_{H+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}=0`$
and so
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}}=e^{\beta_{m}V_{H+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}`$.
Now suppose
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}`$
for some $`h\in[H-1]`$. Then by
([B.1](#A2.E1 "Equation B.1 ‣ Proof. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
and
([B.2](#A2.E2 "Equation B.2 ‣ Proof. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)}.
``` |  | (B.3) |

By the construction of
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
and the definition of best response, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}=[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)=\max_{\nu}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\nu\times\pi_{h,-m}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)`$ |  |

and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}=\max_{\nu}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\nu\times\pi_{h,-m}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}](s).`$ |  |

It follows from
([B.3](#A2.E3 "Equation B.3 ‣ Proof. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}.`$ |  |

This completes the induction. ∎

###### Lemma B.3.

For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$ and $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, the following statements
hold with probability at least $`1-\delta`$. If $`\beta_{m}>0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|

and if $`\beta_{m}<0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle(e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}})(s,a)\leq e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}(e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}})](s,a)+2\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a).`$ |  |

###### Proof.

For $`\beta_{m}>0`$, the update procedure of Algorithm
[2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
implies that for all $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}](s,a)+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a),`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}](s,a)-\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a).`$ |  |

Combining the above displayed equations yields the result. The proof for
the case of $`\beta_{m}<0`$ holds similarly. ∎

The next lemma controls $`\{U^{k}_{h}\}`$ through upper and lower

###### Lemma B.4.

For all $`(k,h,s)\in[K]\times[H]\times{\cal S}`$, we have
$`U_{h}^{k}(s)\leq 1`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U_{h}^{k}(s)\geq\max_{m\in[M]:\beta_{m}>0}\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}})(s)}{e^{\beta_{m}(H-h+1)}-e^{-\beta_{m}(h-1)}}\geq 0,`$ |  |

and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U_{h}^{k}(s)\geq\max_{m\in[M]:\beta_{m}<0}\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}})(s)}{1-e^{\beta_{m}H}}\geq 0.`$ |  |

###### Proof.

Let us write $`[M]={\cal M}^{+}\cup{\cal M}^{-}`$ where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle{\cal M}^{+}`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\{m\in[M]:\beta_{m}>0\}`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle{\cal M}^{-}`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\{m\in[M]:\beta_{m}<0\}.`$ |  |

Case I. We first prove the lemma w.r.t. $`{\cal M}^{+}`$. We first
verify the base case. Since
$`U_{H+1}^{k}=\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}=\underline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}=0`$, we
have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle 1\geq U_{H+1}^{k}(s)=0=\max_{m\in{\cal M}^{+}}\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}})(s)}{1-e^{-\beta_{m}H}}.`$ |  |

Now assume that our claim holds for $`U_{h+1}^{k}`$ for some
$`h\in[H-1]`$. We can deduce

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}U_{h+1}^{k}](s,a)`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\left[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}\max_{m\in{\cal M}^{+}}\frac{e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}}{e^{\beta_{m}(H-h)}-e^{-\beta_{m}h}}\right](s,a)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}\max_{m\in{\cal M}^{+}}\frac{e^{\beta_{m}}(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}})}{e^{\beta_{m}}(e^{\beta_{m}(H-h)}-e^{-\beta_{m}h})}\right](s,a)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}\max_{m\in{\cal M}^{+}}\frac{e^{\beta_{m}}(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}})}{e^{\beta_{m}(H-h+1)}-e^{-\beta_{m}(h-1)}}\right](s,a)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq\max_{m\in{\cal M}^{+}}\left[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}\frac{e^{\beta_{m}}(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}})}{e^{\beta_{m}(H-h+1)}-e^{-\beta_{m}(h-1)}}\right](s,a)`$ |  | (B.7) |

We claim that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
W_{h}^{k}\geq\max_{m\in{\cal M}^{+}}\left[\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}})(s,a)}{e^{\beta_{m}(H-h+1)}-e^{-\beta_{m}(h-1)}}\right].
``` |  | (B.8) |

If
$`[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}U_{h+1}^{k}](s,a)+2z_{h}^{k}(s,a)\geq 1`$,
then
([B.4](#A2.E4 "Equation B.4 ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
implies $`W_{h}^{k}=1`$ and

is verified; otherwise, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|

### B.2 Controlling $`U_{1}^{k}`$

Let us define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Delta_{h}^{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq U_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k}),`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\zeta_{h}^{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq({\mathcal{G}}_{\pi^{k}}W_{h}^{k})(s_{h}^{k})-W_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k}),`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{p}_{h}^{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mathcal{P}_{h}U_{h+1}^{k})(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})-U_{h+1}^{k}(s_{h+1}^{k}).`$ |  |

We have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k})`$ | $`\displaystyle=({\mathcal{G}}_{\pi^{k}}W_{h}^{k})(s_{h}^{k})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\zeta_{h}^{k}+W_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\leq}\zeta_{h}^{k}+2z_{h}^{k}+(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}U_{h+1}^{k})(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\zeta_{h}^{k}+2z_{h}^{k}+[(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathcal{P}_{h})U_{h+1}^{k}](s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})+[\mathcal{P}_{h}U_{h+1}^{k}](s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\zeta_{h}^{k}+3z_{h}^{k}+[\mathcal{P}_{h}U_{h+1}^{k}](s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\zeta_{h}^{k}+3z_{h}^{k}+\overline{p}_{h}^{k}+U_{h+1}^{k}(s_{h+1}^{k}),`$ |  |

where step $`(i)`$ holds by
([B.4](#A2.E4 "Equation B.4 ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
and step $`(ii)`$ follows from Lemma
[B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
Recursing and summing over $`k\in[K]`$ gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{k\in[K]}U_{1}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})\leq\sum_{k\in[K]}\sum_{h\in[H]}(\zeta_{h}^{k}+3z_{h}^{k}+\overline{p}_{h}^{k}).
``` |  | (B.10) |

Note that $`\{\zeta_{h}^{k}\}`$ and $`\{\overline{p}_{h}^{k}\}`$ are
martingale difference sequences. Also, by
([B.4](#A2.E4 "Equation B.4 ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
and Lemma
[B.4](#A2.Thmtheorem4 "Lemma B.4. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
we have that
$`\left|\zeta_{h}^{k}\right|,\left|\overline{p}_{h}^{k}\right|\leq 1`$
for $`(k,h)\in[K]\times[H]`$. Therefore, the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality
implies that with probability at least $`1-\delta`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k\in[K]}\sum_{h\in[H]}\zeta_{h}^{k}\right|`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2HK\log(1/\delta)},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k\in[K]}\sum_{h\in[H]}\overline{p}_{h}^{k}\right|`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2HK\log(1/\delta)}.`$ |  |

On the other hand, recalling $`z_{h}^{k}`$ defined in
([B.5](#A2.E5 "Equation B.5 ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
and the construction of $`\gamma^{k}_{h,m}`$ in
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle z_{h}^{k}\leq\sqrt{\frac{S\iota}{N_{h}^{k-1}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})}}.`$ |  |

This implies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[K]}\sum_{h\in[H]}z_{h}^{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h\in[H]}\sum_{k\in[K]}\sqrt{\frac{S\iota}{\min\{1,N_{h}^{k-1}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\}}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\leq}\sum_{h\in[H]}\sqrt{K\sum_{k\in[K]}\frac{S\iota}{\min\{1,N_{h}^{k-1}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\}}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\sum_{h\in[H]}\sqrt{SK\iota\sum_{(s,a)\in{\cal S}\times{\cal A}}\sum_{k\in[K]}\frac{1}{\min\{1,N_{h}^{k-1}(s,a)\}}}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(iii)}{\leq}\sum_{h\in[H]}\sqrt{SK\iota\cdot SA(1+\log K)}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=2\sqrt{H^{2}S^{2}AK\iota^{2}},`$ |  |

where step $`(i)`$ follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, step
$`(ii)`$ holds by the pigeonhole principle, and step $`(iii)`$ holds by
the fact that $`\sum_{k\in[K]}\frac{1}{k}\leq\log K`$. Combining the
above results with
([B.10](#A2.E10 "Equation B.10 ‣ B.2 Controlling 𝑈_1^𝑘 ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{k\in[K]}U_{1}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})\lesssim\sqrt{HK\iota}+\sqrt{H^{2}S^{2}AK\iota^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{H^{2}S^{2}AK\iota^{2}}.
``` |  | (B.11) |

### B.3 Putting All Together

By Lemma
[B.4](#A2.Thmtheorem4 "Lemma B.4. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
note that for $`\beta_{m}>0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U_{1}^{k}(s)\geq\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}})(s)}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}`$ |  |

and for $`\beta_{m}<0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U_{1}^{k}(s)\geq\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}})(s)}{1-e^{\beta_{m}H}},`$ |  |

which together imply

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U_{1}^{k}(s)\geq\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}})(s)}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}.
``` |  | (B.12) |

Note that on the event of Lemma
[B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Lemma B.2. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}})(s_{1}^{k})}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}\leq\frac{(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}})(s_{1}^{k})}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1},
``` |  | (B.13) |

where the above two quantities are non-negative since for
$`\beta_{m}>0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}}\geq 0,\qquad e^{\beta_{m}H}-1>0,`$ |  |

and for $`\beta_{m}<0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}}\leq 0,\qquad e^{\beta_{m}H}-1<0.`$ |  |

We now connect the above results with the regret of
([4.2](#S4.E2 "Equation 4.2 ‣ Definition 4.2. ‣ 4.2 Risk-Balanced Regret ‣ 4 Regret and Equilibrium Bias ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")).
For each $`m\in[M]`$, it is clear that
$`V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}\geq V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}`$ by definition. If
$`\beta_{m}>0`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1}^{k})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{H}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}\left[\log\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)-\log\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\leq}\frac{H}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\frac{H}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}\left(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq H\cdot U_{1}^{k}(s_{1}^{k}),`$ |  | (B.14) |

where step $`(i)`$ holds since $`f(x)=\log x`$ is 1-Lipschitz for
$`x\geq 1`$, step $`(ii)`$ holds on the event of Lemma
[B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Lemma B.2. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
and the last step holds by
([B.12](#A2.E12 "Equation B.12 ‣ B.3 Putting All Together ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")).
If $`\beta_{m}<0`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1}^{k})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{H}{e^{-\beta_{m}H}-1}\left[\log\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)-\log\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\leq}\frac{He^{-\beta_{m}H}}{e^{-\beta_{m}H}-1}\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{H}{1-e^{\beta_{m}H}}\left(e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\frac{H}{1-e^{\beta_{m}H}}\left(e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})}-e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\frac{H}{e^{\beta_{m}H}-1}\left(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{1,m}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{1,m}^{k}(s_{1}^{k})}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq H\cdot U_{1}^{k}(s_{1}^{k}),`$ |  | (B.15) |

where step $`(i)`$ holds since $`f(x)=\log x`$ is
$`(e^{-\beta_{m}H})`$-Lipschitz for $`x\in[e^{\beta_{m}H},1]`$, step
$`(ii)`$ holds on the event of Lemma
[B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Lemma B.2. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
and the last step holds by
([B.12](#A2.E12 "Equation B.12 ‣ B.3 Putting All Together ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")).
The proof for the regret upper bound is completed by applying
([B.11](#A2.E11 "Equation B.11 ‣ B.2 Controlling 𝑈_1^𝑘 ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
to
([B.14](#A2.E14 "Equation B.14 ‣ B.3 Putting All Together ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
and
([B.15](#A2.E15 "Equation B.15 ‣ B.3 Putting All Together ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"))
combined.

Finally, a sample complexity guarantee can be derived based on the
regret bound, following an argument similar to that presented in [Jin
et al. (2018)](#bib.bib16). In particular, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\reg_{\mathsf{NE}}(K)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[K]}\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{k}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}\lesssim\sqrt{H^{4}S^{2}AK\iota^{2}},`$ |  |

and a random policy $`\pi^{\ddagger}`$ defined as a uniform sample from
$`\{\pi^{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}`$ enjoys

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\max_{m\in[M]}\frac{(V_{1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{\ddagger}}-V_{1,m}^{\pi^{\ddagger}})(s_{1})}{\Phi_{H}(\beta_{m})}\lesssim\sqrt{3H^{4}S^{2}A\iota^{2}/K}`$ |  |

with probability at least $`\frac{2}{3}`$. It follows that
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
finds $`\varepsilon`$-optimal policy with
$`K=\widetilde{O}(H^{4}S^{2}A/\varepsilon^{2})`$ episodes.

### B.4 Proofs for CCE and CE

We supply the proofs of upper and lower bounds of exponential value
functions involved in
[Algorithm 2](#alg2 "In 5 Algorithm ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
for CCE and CE in this section, which complement the analogous results
on NE in
[Lemma B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Lemma B.2. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
The regret upper bounds for CCE and CE then follow from substituting
[Lemma B.2](#A2.Thmtheorem2 "Lemma B.2. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
with
[Lemmas B.5](#A2.Thmtheorem5 "Lemma B.5 (Upper and Lower Bounds for CCE). ‣ B.4 Proofs for CCE and CE ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning")
and [B.6](#A2.Thmtheorem6 "Lemma B.6 (Upper and Lower Bounds for CE). ‣ B.4 Proofs for CCE and CE ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"),
respectively, and replacing the exponential value function
$`e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}`$ under the optimal response
with $`e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}`$
for CE.

###### Lemma B.5 (Upper and Lower Bounds for CCE).

For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$ and $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, the following statements
hold with probability at least $`1-\delta`$ for any policy $`\pi^{k}`$.
If $`\beta_{m}>0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)},
``` |  |
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)};
``` |  |

and if $`\beta_{m}<0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)},
``` |  |
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)}.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

We prove this result through induction. We only show the argument for
the case of $`\beta_{m}>0`$, and we can multiply $`-1`$ to the argument
below for $`\beta_{m}<0`$. For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$, fixed tuple
$`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, and $`\beta_{m}>0`$, if we assume that the value
functions at the next step satisfies
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}\geq 0`$,
then we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle=\ `$ | $`\displaystyle(e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}](s,a)-e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[{\mathcal{P}}_{h}e^{\beta_{m}V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}}](s,a))+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle=\ `$ | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}})](s,a)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+[(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}-{\mathcal{P}}_{h})e^{\beta_{m}(r_{h,m}(s,a)+V_{h+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}})}](s,a)+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\geq\ `$ | $`\displaystyle 0,`$ |  |

where the first component is non-negative due to induction assumption,
the sum of the second and the third component is also non-negative due
to
[Lemma B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
Notice that
$`\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}(s)=V_{H+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)=0`$ for all
state $`s\in{\mathcal{S}}`$, and
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{H+1,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}\geq 0`$
always holds for step $`H+1`$. Consequently, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)}`$ |  |

for all agents, state-action pairs, and $`h\in[H]`$.

Further, given the assumption
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}-e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s,a)}\geq 0`$
for any fixed tuple $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, it follows that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)-e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}\geq 0,`$ |  |

where by the definition of CCE equilibrium that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)=\max_{\nu}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\nu\times\pi_{h,-m}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}`$ |  |

for any $`\beta_{m}>0`$ and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)=\min_{\nu}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\nu\times\pi_{h,-m}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)\leq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{*,\pi_{-m}^{k}}(s)}`$ |  |

for any $`\beta_{m}<0`$.

Recursion proofs for
$`(-1)^{{\mathbb{I}}\{\beta_{m}<0\}}\cdot(e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)})\geq 0`$
and
$`(-1)^{{\mathbb{I}}\{\beta_{m}<0\}}\cdot(e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)}-e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)})\geq 0`$
follow the same reasoning. ∎

###### Lemma B.6 (Upper and Lower Bounds for CE).

For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$ and $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, the following statements
hold with probability at least $`1-\delta`$ for any CE policy
$`\pi^{k}`$. If $`\beta_{m}>0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)},
``` |  |
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s,a)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)};
``` |  |

if $`\beta_{m}<0`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}V_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s)},
``` |  |
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)},\qquad e^{\beta_{m}\underline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}Q_{h,m}^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)}.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

We prove this result with induction. We only show the argument for the
case of $`\beta_{m}>0`$, and for $`\beta_{m}<0`$ we can multiply $`-1`$
to the argument below. For any $`\delta\in(0,1]`$, fixed tuple
$`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, and $`\beta_{m}>0`$, if
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}\geq 0`$,
then we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s,a)}`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle=\ `$ | $`\displaystyle(e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}](s,a)-e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[{\mathcal{P}}_{h}e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}}](s,a))+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle=\ `$ | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}r_{h,m}(s,a)}[\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}(e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h+1,m}^{k}}-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}})](s,a)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+[(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{h}^{k}-{\mathcal{P}}_{h})e^{\beta_{m}(r_{h,m}(s,a)+\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})})}](s,a)+\gamma_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\geq\ `$ | $`\displaystyle 0,`$ |  |

where the first component is non-negative due to induction assumption,
the sum of the second and the third component is also non-negative due
to
[Lemma B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Lemma B.1. ‣ B.1 Some Useful Lemmas ‣ Appendix B Proof of Theorem ‣ Taming Equilibrium Bias in Risk-Sensitive Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning").
Notice that
$`\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}(s)=V_{H+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)=0`$ for all
state $`s\in{\mathcal{S}}`$, and
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{H+1,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{H+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}\geq 0`$
holds for step $`H+1`$. Consequently, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}\geq e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s,a)}`$ |  |

for all agents, state-action pairs, and $`h\in[H]`$.

Further, given the assumption
$`e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}(s,a)}-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s,a)}\geq 0`$
for any fixed tuple $`(k,h,m,s,a)`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle e^{\beta_{m}\overline{V}_{h,m}^{k}(s)}-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)-e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\geq 0,`$ |  |

where by the definition of CE that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)=\max_{\psi}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\psi(\pi_{h}^{k})}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)\geq e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}`$ |  |

for any $`\beta_{m}>0`$ and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle[{\mathcal{G}}_{\pi_{h}^{k}}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)=\min_{\psi}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\psi(\pi_{h}^{k})}e^{\beta_{m}\overline{Q}_{h,m}^{k}}](s)\leq e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}`$ |  |

for any $`\beta_{m}<0`$. Recall that
$`e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}=\max_{\psi}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\psi(\pi_{h}^{k})}e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi^{\prime}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi^{\prime}(\pi_{h}^{k})}}](s)`$
for $`\beta_{m}>0`$ and
$`e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi\in\Psi_{m}}V_{h+1,m}^{\psi(\pi^{k})}(s)}=\min_{\psi}[{\mathcal{G}}_{\psi(\pi_{h}^{k})}e^{\beta_{m}\max_{\psi^{\prime}}Q_{h,m}^{\psi^{\prime}(\pi_{h}^{k})}}](s)`$
for $`\beta_{m}<0`$.

The recursion arguments for the other two inequalities follow the same
reasoning. ∎

#### Future directions and broad impact.

With the recent developments in variance-aware learning, an exciting
avenue for future exploration is examining how noise-adaptive algorithms
([Xu et al., 2023a](#bib.bib29)) can enhance risk-sensitive
reinforcement learning (RL). There is also significant potential in
developing efficient algorithms for risk-sensitive RL in varied
contexts, such as matching and finding competitive equilibrium in
macroeconomics ([Min et al., 2022](#bib.bib22); [Xu et al.,
2023b](#bib.bib30)). Furthermore, given the close connection between
risk-sensitive RL and human learning behaviors, it would be fascinating
to investigate its integration with fields like meta-learning and
bio-inspired learning, as discussed in studies by [Xu et al.
(2021)](#bib.bib28) and [Song et al. (2021)](#bib.bib24). Additionally,
a critical area for future research lies in exploring the role of risk
sensitivity in augmenting unsupervised learning algorithms, a concept
touched upon by [Ling et al. (2019)](#bib.bib19).
````
