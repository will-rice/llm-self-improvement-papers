# Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks

Przemyslaw Chojecki Affiliation: ulam.ai

December 15, 2025

###### Abstract

We study the special role of mathematics and coding inside the moduli
space $`\mathfrak{M}`$ of psychometric batteries for AI agents. Building
on the AAI framework of \[[1](#bib.bib1)\] and GVU dynamics from
\[[2](#bib.bib2)\], we define the _Mathematics Fiber_
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ and show that, when paired with formal
proof kernels (e.g. Lean, Coq), GVU flows on this fiber admit spectrally
stable self-improvement regimes due to oracle-like verification. Our
main technical result is a density theorem: under uniform tightness of
agent outputs and a Lipschitz AAI functional, the subspace of batteries
generated by mathematical theorem-proving and coding tasks is dense in
$`\mathfrak{M}`$ with respect to the evaluation metric
$`d_{\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}`$. Coding alone is universal in this sense,
while pure mathematics is not; its privilege is spectral rather than
expressive. We interpret this as evidence that mathematics and coding
provide “universal coordinates” for evaluation, and that formal
mathematics is a natural ignition domain for recursive self-improvement
in advanced AI agents.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models and deep reinforcement learning systems are now
routinely evaluated on batteries of tasks: structured test suites that
probe capabilities such as reasoning, coding, tool use, and social
interaction. In \[[1](#bib.bib1)\] I proposed treating such batteries as
points in a _moduli space_ $`\mathfrak{M}`$, with each battery
$`\mathcal{B}`$ inducing a representation map
$`\rho_{\mathcal{B}}:\Theta\to\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})`$ from agent
parameters to distributions over evaluation outcomes, and an associated
AAI capability functional
$`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})\to\mathbb{R}`$. This
perspective allows us to compare agents across heterogeneous benchmarks
by studying their image in $`\mathfrak{M}`$, and to define distances
between batteries via the induced discrepancies in capability curves.

Subsequently, \[[2](#bib.bib2)\] introduced the
_Generator–Verifier–Updater (GVU)_ formalism as a canonical engine of
self-improvement. In GVU, an agent with parameters $`\theta\in\Theta`$:
(i) generates traces on a battery via its current policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$; (ii) scores them using an internal potential $`V`$
that plays the role of a Verifier; and (iii) updates $`\theta`$ by a
first-order rule derived from these scores. Under mild regularity, any
first-order, sample-based update can be written in this form, and the
resulting flow $`\theta_{r}`$ induces a capability trajectory
$`r\mapsto\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta_{r}))`$. A
_Variance Inequality_ then constrains when this trajectory has positive
drift: the mean GVU update must be sufficiently aligned with the true
gradient of $`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$ and have sufficiently small generator
and verifier noise.

This paper combines these two threads. Rather than treating mathematics
as just another axis in a large benchmark, I study it as a _structurally
privileged fiber_ inside the moduli space of batteries. Concretely, I
define a Mathematics Fiber
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}\subset\mathfrak{M}`$ consisting of
batteries whose tasks are mathematical problems or theorem-proving
goals, equipped with either informal scoring (natural-language
solutions), semantic scoring (autoformalization plus checking), or
formal scoring (kernel-checked proofs in systems such as Lean or Coq).
Within $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ I consider the immersion of
large proof libraries (e.g. mathlib) as monotone maps on capabilities,
and I analyze GVU flows that are confined to this fiber or that use it
as a high-SNR subroutine.

A central observation, sharpened here into precise statements, is that
_formal mathematics yields oracle-like verifiers_. Given a candidate
proof trace $`\omega`$ for a theorem $`\tau`$, the proof kernel either
accepts or rejects it with near-zero stochastic noise. In the language
of \[[2](#bib.bib2)\], this drives the verification variance
$`\sigma_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}`$ toward zero, thereby widening the stepsize
window for stable self-improvement and pushing the system far from the
“Hallucination Barrier” characteristic of purely linguistic
self-correction. This provides a geometric explanation for why
neuro-symbolic systems such as AlphaProof, AlphaGeometry,
DeepSeekMath-V2 and Lean-based agents can bootstrap effectively on
mathematical benchmarks: their GVU loops lie in a spectrally favorable
regime on $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}`$.

The second, and more surprising, theme of the paper concerns
_universality_. In
Section [6](#S6 "6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
I prove a density theorem for the moduli of batteries: under mild
assumptions on agent classes (uniform tightness of output distributions)
and on the AAI functional (Lipschitz continuity with respect to a
bounded-Lipschitz metric on evaluation laws), the subspace of batteries
generated by _mathematical theorem-proving and coding/programming tasks_
is dense in $`\mathfrak{M}`$ with respect to the evaluation metric

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
d_{\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}):=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|(\Phi_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{B}_{1}})(\theta)-(\Phi_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{B}_{2}})(\theta)\big|.
``` |  |

In informal terms: for any reasonable benchmark and any reasonable agent
class, there exists a battery built purely from math and coding tasks
whose capability curve is arbitrarily close. Math+coding thus form
*universal coordinates* on the moduli space of batteries.

The proof proceeds in two stages. In a one-shot model, I show that
coding scorers can implement singleton indicators on trace space by
forcing the agent’s output to act as a self-printing program, so their
linear span contains all finite-support functions. Combined with a
uniform tightness assumption on agent outputs, this yields density of
the “math+coding algebra” in the space of scorers, in the sense of
uniform approximation over agents. I then lift this to general batteries
by controlling the bounded-Lipschitz distance between evaluation laws
via a simple coupling argument and invoking the Lipschitz property of
the AAI functional.

An important asymmetry emerges: *coding alone is already universal*, in
that coding scorers can distinguish arbitrary traces, whereas pure
mathematics is not. Proof kernels fundamentally treat most non-proofs as
indistinguishable zeros, so the algebra they generate cannot approximate
scorers that care about the fine-grained structure of arbitrary output
strings. Mathematics is therefore not needed for expressive
universality, but it provides an unusually rich, low-entropy,
oracle-verifiable fiber on which GVU self-improvement is spectrally
well-conditioned.

Overall, the paper argues that mathematics and coding are not merely
convenient benchmarks but occupy a geometrically and spectrally
privileged position in the moduli of batteries: coding supplies
expressive universality on trace space, formal mathematics supplies
oracle-like verifiers and rich symbolic structure, and together they
provide natural coordinates in which to study capability,
self-improvement, and scalable oversight for advanced AI agents.

From a practitioner’s perspective, the math+code universality result has
a concrete implication: it suggests a route to *cheaper*, more
*data-efficient* training. If mathematics and, especially, coding tasks
form a dense skeleton in the moduli space of batteries, then in
principle a large fraction of “real-world” evaluation can be
approximated by carefully designed code-centric benchmarks whose
outcomes are cheaply checkable by compilers, test suites and formal
tools. For LLMs and AI agents, this means that better, more compact
coding datasets could eventually replace sprawling, noisy multi-domain
corpora as the primary medium of supervision, concentrating training
compute on a domain that is both information-dense and automatically
verifiable, while still exercising behaviours that generalize across the
wider task space.

## 2 Preliminaries: Batteries and Their Moduli

We briefly recall the main elements of the battery framework from
\[[1](#bib.bib1)\], restricting attention to the aspects needed in this
paper.

### 2.1 Batteries and representations

Let $`\Sigma`$ denote a finite alphabet of tokens and $`\Sigma^{\ast}`$
the Kleene closure. We regard all prompts, traces and textual artefacts
as elements of $`\Sigma^{\ast}`$.

###### Definition 2.1 (Battery).

A *battery* is an octuple

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{B}=(T,\ \mathcal{F},\ \mathsf{S},\ Q^{*},\ \mu,\ \mathsf{D},\ \Pi,\ \mathsf{R}),
``` |  |

where:

- •
  $`T`$ is a finite set of tasks, partitioned into families
  $`\mathcal{F}=\{F_{k}\}`$.
- •
  For each $`t\in T`$, $`\mathsf{S}_{t}:\Omega_{t}\to[0,1]`$ is a
  scoring map, where $`\Omega_{t}\subseteq\Sigma^{\ast}`$ is the space
  of valid semantic traces for task $`t`$.
- •
  $`Q^{\ast}:T\to[0,1]`$ assigns threshold scores.
- •
  $`\mu`$ is a sampling law on $`T\times\Pi\times\mathsf{D}`$, where
  $`\Pi`$ are seeds and $`\mathsf{D}`$ are drifts.
- •
  $`\mathsf{R}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d_{R}}_{\geq 0}`$ are resource
  coordinates (tokens, wall-clock, calls, cost).

As in \[[1](#bib.bib1)\], the battery induces an *input space*
$`\mathcal{X}`$ of prompted tasks and an *output space* $`\mathcal{Y}`$
of traces with resource annotations. For an agent $`\mathcal{A}`$ (or
parameter state $`\theta`$) we obtain a Markov kernel $`\pi_{\theta}`$
from $`\mathcal{X}`$ to $`\mathcal{Y}`$ and, composing with the scoring,
a representation measure
$`\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)\in\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})`$, where
$`X_{\mathcal{B}}:=[0,1]^{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d_{R}}`$ is the
evaluation space of per-task observables.

A capability functional
$`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})\to\mathbb{R}`$
satisfying the axioms of \[[1](#bib.bib1)\] then induces a scalar
capability

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta):=\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)).
``` |  |

### 2.2 The moduli space of batteries

We write $`\mathfrak{M}`$ for the (coarse) moduli space of batteries,
defined as the set of isomorphism classes of batteries in the category
$`\mathbf{Bat}`$, or equivalently the quotient

|     |                                  |     |
|-----|----------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathfrak{M}\;:=\;\mathbf{Bat}/G
       ```                               |     |

by the evaluation-preserving symmetry group $`G`$. In particular, two
batteries represent the same point of $`\mathfrak{M}`$ if they are
related by a symmetry that preserves their evaluation structure (task
families, thresholds, score reparameterizations, and resource scaling);
this definition is entirely *formal* and does not depend on any choice
of agents or AAI functional.

Following \[[1](#bib.bib1)\], we endow $`\mathfrak{M}`$ with coarse
structure coming from its canonical representatives:

- •
  a stratification by discrete *skeletons* (task family structure,
  anchor choices, threshold ordering);
- •
  on each stratum, continuous parameters given by threshold vectors,
  PIT-score copulas (with the $`W_{1}`$ topology), and projective
  resource rays;
- •
  induced notions of topology and, when needed, metrics such as the
  canonical moduli metric $`d_{\mathfrak{M}}`$.

Only *after* fixing an agent family with parameter space $`\Theta`$ and
a regular AAI functional
$`\{\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}\}_{\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{M}}`$ do batteries
acquire evaluation maps

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\mathcal{B}}:\Theta\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad\theta\longmapsto\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}\big(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)\big),
``` |  |

where $`\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)`$ is the canonical representation of
the agent on $`\mathcal{B}`$. For a fixed $`\theta`$, the assignment

|     |                                                               |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathfrak{M}\ni\mathcal{B}\longmapsto F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)
       ```                                                            |     |

is then the agent’s *capability profile* over the (formal) moduli space.
Note that this evaluation structure is additional data on top of
$`\mathfrak{M}`$ and is not part of the definition of the moduli itself.

### 2.3 GVU dynamics and the self-improvement coefficient

The GVU framework \[[2](#bib.bib2)\] equips $`\Theta`$ with dynamics. A
Generator–Verifier–Updater operator

|     |                                            |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{GVU}}:\Theta\to\Theta
       ```                                         |     |

decomposes a single self-improvement step into sampling behaviour
(Generator), scoring it with an internal potential (Verifier), and
performing a first-order update (Updater). Iterating
$`\theta_{r+1}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{GVU}}(\theta_{r})`$ yields a flow
$`(\theta_{r})_{r\geq 0}`$; composing with a battery functional gives a
capability curve

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta_{r})=(\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{B}})(\theta_{r}).
``` |  |

The *self-improvement coefficient* on battery $`\mathcal{B}`$ is then
the derivative

|     |                                                                  |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \kappa_{\mathcal{B}}(r):=\frac{d}{dr}F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta_{r})
       ```                                                               |     |

when it exists, or a finite-difference surrogate. Informally,
$`\kappa_{\mathcal{B}}(r)>0`$ means that at resource scale $`r`$ the
agent is converting additional computation into capability gains on
$`\mathcal{B}`$.

In this paper we are interested in $`\kappa_{\mathcal{B}}(r)`$ when
$`\mathcal{B}`$ lies in a particular region of $`\mathfrak{M}`$
associated with mathematics.

## 3 The Mathematics Fiber

We now define the portion of the moduli space that corresponds to
mathematical tasks.

### 3.1 Mathematical tasks and formality strata

At a high level, we distinguish three levels of formality.

###### Definition 3.1 (Formality levels for mathematics).

Let $`T`$ be a set of tasks whose prompts and traces are concerned with
mathematical content. We say that a task $`t\in T`$ is:

- •
  *Informal* if its input is natural language (possibly with LaTeX) and
  its scoring map $`\mathsf{S}_{t}`$ depends only on human-readable
  arguments, without reference to a formal system. Examples include
  free-form Olympiad solutions judged by humans.
- •
  *Semi-formal* if its inputs or outputs include partially structured
  artefacts (annotated proofs, sketches, step-wise rationales) and
  $`\mathsf{S}_{t}`$ may involve consistency checks or step-level
  validators (e.g. PRMs), but not a full proof kernel.
- •
  *Fully formal* if its traces $`\omega\in\Omega_{t}`$ are programs or
  proof scripts in a fixed proof assistant (e.g. Lean), and
  $`\mathsf{S}_{t}`$ factors through the proof-assistant kernel: it is
  $`1`$ if the kernel accepts and $`0`$ otherwise, possibly with
  additional penalties for resource usage.

Let $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{inf}}`$,
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{semi}}`$ and
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal}}`$ denote the sets of
batteries all of whose tasks are of the corresponding formality type.

###### Definition 3.2 (Mathematics Fiber).

The *Mathematics Fiber*
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}\subset\mathfrak{M}`$ is the union of
batteries whose tasks are mathematical in the sense above:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}:=\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{inf}}\cup\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{semi}}\cup\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal}}.
``` |  |

We call the three subspaces the *informal*, *semi-formal* and *formal*
strata of the Mathematics Fiber.

###### Remark 3.3 (Topic stratification).

Each battery in $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ also carries
disciplinary labels (algebra, geometry, analysis, etc.) via its task
families $`\mathcal{F}`$. Taking these labels into account yields a
finer stratification of $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ into topic-wise
components, but for this paper the coarse formality stratification will
suffice.

### 3.2 Examples

We record a few canonical examples.

###### Example 3.4 (Competition mathematics batteries).

A battery whose tasks are competition problems (e.g. IMO, Putnam) with
solutions scored by exact numeric answers or short formal justifications
lies in $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{inf}}`$ if solutions are
judged by humans, or in $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{semi}}`$
if consistency heuristics or step-verification tools are used.

###### Example 3.5 (Lean theorem proving batteries).

Benchmarks such as miniF2F, ProofNet and LeanDojo induce batteries in
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal}}`$: tasks are Lean
goals, traces are Lean proof scripts, and scoring is derived from the
Lean kernel’s acceptance of the proof, possibly augmented with proof
length and time.

###### Example 3.6 (Autoformalization batteries).

Pairs of informal statements and formal targets (e.g. LaTeX theorem
statements and their Lean forms) induce batteries at the semi-formal or
formal level, depending on whether the traces are purely formal objects
or include auxiliary informal reasoning steps.

In all of these cases, the key property is that the semantics of the
task are unambiguously mathematical, and there exists (at least in
principle) a corresponding formalization in one or more proof systems.

## 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers

We now focus on the fully formal stratum
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal}}`$ and show that these
batteries induce oracle-like Verifiers in the GVU framework.

### 4.1 Formal mathematics batteries

###### Definition 4.1 (Formal mathematics battery).

A battery $`\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ is a *formal
mathematics battery* if:

1.  1.
    There is a fixed proof assistant with kernel $`K`$ and a fixed
    library of primitives (axioms and previously verified theorems).
2.  2.
    For each $`t\in T`$, the domain $`\Omega_{t}`$ of traces consists of
    proof scripts or programs in the language of this assistant.
3.  3.
    The scoring map $`\mathsf{S}_{t}`$ takes the form

    |     |                                                  |     |
    |-----|--------------------------------------------------|-----|
    |     |
           ``` math
           \mathsf{S}_{t}(\omega)=s_{t}\big(K(\omega)\big),
           ```                                               |     |

    where $`K(\omega)\in\{\mathsf{accept},\mathsf{reject}\}`$ is the
    kernel’s decision and
    $`s_{t}:\{\mathsf{accept},\mathsf{reject}\}\to[0,1]`$ is a
    deterministic post-processing function (for example,
    $`s_{t}(\mathsf{accept})=1`$, $`s_{t}(\mathsf{reject})=0`$ or a
    variant that penalizes resources).

We write $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$ for the
collection of formal mathematics batteries associated with a particular
kernel $`K`$.

In this setting, for each task the *semantic* question “is the proof
correct relative to the library and axioms?” has a unique,
machine-checkable answer.

### 4.2 The Oracle Nature of Formal Scoring

Recall from \[[2](#bib.bib2)\] that, for a fixed architecture
$`(\Theta,\Pi_{\Theta})`$, the GVU operator
$`\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{GVU}}`$ decomposes an update into a Generator
$`\mathcal{G}_{\theta}`$, a Verifier $`\mathcal{V}_{\theta}`$ and an
Updater $`\mathcal{U}_{\theta}`$. The verifier is given as part of the
architecture: for each $`\theta\in\Theta`$ there is a fixed potential

|     |                                                                   |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       V_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},
       ```                                                                |     |

and the update uses $`V_{\theta}(x,y)`$ to reweight traces $`y`$ drawn
from the generator at input $`x`$.

In informal or human-judged domains (natural language, social
interaction, creative writing), the “truth” or quality of a trace is
itself a random quantity: the same $`\omega`$ may receive different
scores from different raters, or even from the same rater at different
times. In GVU terms, the verification channel carries intrinsic noise:
even conditioning on the sampled behaviour, the evaluation remains
stochastic.

On the formal Mathematics Fiber
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$ the situation is
structurally different. Once the trace $`\omega`$ is fixed, the score
assigned by the battery is *completely determined* by the kernel $`K`$;
all remaining uncertainty comes from which trace the generator produced,
not from the scoring mechanism itself.

We now make this precise.

###### Definition 4.2 (External score random variable).

Fix a formal mathematics battery
$`\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$ in
the sense of
Definition [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 4.1 (Formal mathematics battery). ‣ 4.1 Formal mathematics batteries ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),
and an agent with parameter $`\theta\in\Theta`$. Let
$`x=(s,t)\in\mathcal{X}`$ be an input specifying context $`s`$ and goal
$`t`$, and let

|     |                                                 |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \Omega\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,\mathcal{B})
       ```                                              |     |

be the random trace (proof script) produced by the generator on this
task. Define the *external score*

|     |                                        |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       Q_{t}:=\mathsf{S}_{t}(\Omega)\in[0,1].
       ```                                     |     |

We view $`Q_{t}`$ as a random variable on the underlying sample space of
generator randomness and seeds.

By
Definition [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 4.1 (Formal mathematics battery). ‣ 4.1 Formal mathematics batteries ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
we have $`\mathsf{S}_{t}(\omega)=s_{t}(K(\omega))`$ for a deterministic
kernel $`K(\omega)\in\{\mathsf{accept},\mathsf{reject}\}`$ and a
deterministic postprocessing $`s_{t}`$. Hence, conditional on the
realised trace, the score is non-random.

###### Proposition 4.3 (Zero conditional entropy on the formal stratum).

Let $`\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$
and let $`Q_{t}`$ be the external score random variable from
Definition [4.2](#S4.Thmtheorem2 "Definition 4.2 (External score random variable). ‣ 4.2 The Oracle Nature of Formal Scoring ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
Then, for every input $`x=(s,t)`$ and every agent parameter
$`\theta\in\Theta`$,

|     |                                                           |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbb{H}\bigl(Q_{t}\,\big|\,\Omega,\mathcal{B}\bigr)=0,
       ```                                                        |     |

where $`\mathbb{H}(\cdot\mid\cdot)`$ denotes conditional Shannon
entropy. Equivalently, the conditional law of $`Q_{t}`$ given the trace
$`\Omega`$ and the battery is a Dirac measure:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{P}\bigl(Q_{t}\in\cdot\,\big|\,\Omega=\omega,\mathcal{B}\bigr)=\delta_{\mathsf{S}_{t}(\omega)}(\cdot)\quad\text{for all }\omega.
``` |  |

In particular,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{Var}\bigl(Q_{t}\,\big|\,\Omega,\mathcal{B}\bigr)=0\quad\text{almost surely.}
``` |  |

###### Proof.

By
Definition [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 4.1 (Formal mathematics battery). ‣ 4.1 Formal mathematics batteries ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       Q_{t}=\mathsf{S}_{t}(\Omega)=s_{t}\bigl(K(\Omega)\bigr),
       ```                                                       |     |

and both $`K`$ and $`s_{t}`$ are deterministic maps. Thus, for any fixed
$`\omega`$ and fixed battery $`\mathcal{B}`$,

|     |                              |     |
|-----|------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       Q_{t}=\mathsf{S}_{t}(\omega)
       ```                           |     |

is a constant, and
$`\mathbb{P}(Q_{t}=\mathsf{S}_{t}(\omega)\mid\Omega=\omega,\mathcal{B})=1`$.
The conditional distribution of $`Q_{t}`$ given $`(\Omega,\mathcal{B})`$
is therefore a Dirac mass, so its conditional entropy and conditional
variance are both zero. This proves the claims. ∎

Proposition [4.3](#S4.Thmtheorem3 "Proposition 4.3 (Zero conditional entropy on the formal stratum). ‣ 4.2 The Oracle Nature of Formal Scoring ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
is a statement purely about the *environment*: it does not depend on the
internal architecture of the agent, nor on how its GVU verifier is
implemented. It says that on the formal Mathematics Fiber, the moduli
geometry supplies a canonical, noise-free external signal $`Q_{t}`$.

In practice, proof assistants may exhibit additional stochasticity (e.g.
tactic search, timeouts), and scoring may penalize resource usage; here
we isolate the idealized kernel-only component, which is deterministic
conditional on the trace. Any residual randomness is treated as part of
the generator or resource channel, not the verifier.

In \[[2](#bib.bib2)\] the verification noise $`\xi_{\mathcal{V}}`$ in a
GVU update is analysed via a variance decomposition of the form

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{Var}(Q_{t})=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{Var}(Q_{t}\mid\Omega)\bigr]}_{\text{intrinsic label noise}}+\underbrace{\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[Q_{t}\mid\Omega]\bigr)}_{\text{generator / alignment term}},
``` |  |

together with an additional finite-batch sampling factor. On informal
batteries (e.g. human-judged natural language), the first term is
typically positive: even for a fixed $`\omega`$, repeated evaluations
can disagree. On the formal stratum, it collapses.

###### Corollary 4.4 (Spectral collapse on the formal Mathematics Fiber).

Let $`\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$
and let $`Q_{t}`$ be as above. Then for every agent parameter $`\theta`$
and input $`x=(s,t)`$,

|     |                                                                    |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{Var}(Q_{t}\mid\Omega,\mathcal{B})\bigr]=0.
       ```                                                                 |     |

Consequently, in the Variance Inequality of \[[2](#bib.bib2)\], the
verification-noise term $`\sigma_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}`$ on the formal
Mathematics Fiber is entirely attributable to

1.  1.
    generator-side uncertainty (how broadly the policy explores proof
    space), and
2.  2.
    misalignment between the internal GVU potential $`V_{\theta}`$ and
    the external score $`Q_{t}`$,

with no contribution from intrinsic label stochasticity.

In particular, once an architecture is given and its GVU potential
$`V_{\theta}`$ is forced (by training or design) to approximate
$`Q_{t}`$ on $`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$, the
sufficient condition for positive expected capability gain

|     |                                      |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbb{E}[\Delta F_{\mathcal{B}}]>0
       ```                                   |     |

reduces, up to curvature and step-size constants, to constraints on the
alignment coefficient $`\rho`$ between the mean update and
$`\nabla F_{\mathcal{B}}`$ and on the generator SNR
$`\mathrm{SNR}(\mathcal{G})`$.

###### Remark 4.5 (Spectral privilege of the Mathematics Fiber).

Corollary [4.4](#S4.Thmtheorem4 "Corollary 4.4 (Spectral collapse on the formal Mathematics Fiber). ‣ 4.2 The Oracle Nature of Formal Scoring ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
expresses the sense in which the formal Mathematics Fiber is an *oracle
fiber*. It is not that all agents happen to implement a perfect verifier
internally; rather, the moduli geometry guarantees that the external
evaluation channel $`(\omega\mapsto Q_{t})`$ has zero conditional
entropy and zero intrinsic variance. This stands in contrast to fibres
corresponding to human judgement domains, where
$`\mathrm{Var}(Q_{t}\mid\Omega)>0`$ is typical.

Operationally, this means that in formal mathematics the GVU
self-improvement coefficient $`\kappa_{\mathcal{B}}`$ is limited only by
exploration and alignment, not by noisy or unstable supervision. This
helps explain why self-improvement loops are easier to ignite and more
spectrally stable on
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$ than on informal or
purely linguistic fibres.

This formalizes the intuition that formal mathematics is an “easy”
domain for self-improvement: once an agent can generate candidate proofs
$`\omega`$, the decision of whether they are correct is spectrally
simple. All difficulty is concentrated in the Generator $`\mathcal{G}`$
(searching the proof space) and in aligning the potential $`V`$ with the
external capability functional $`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$ (for example, by
incorporating proof length or library coverage).

## 5 Libraries as Points in the Mathematics Fiber

We now outline how formal libraries induce batteries in the Mathematics
Fiber, and how an agent’s performance on a library can be viewed as a
section of the capability bundle over
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal}}`$. Throughout this
section we fix a proof kernel $`K`$, a resource space
$`\mathsf{R}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d_{R}}_{\geq 0}`$, and a cost functional
$`\mathrm{Cost}:\mathsf{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}`$ as in the AAI framework
\[[1](#bib.bib1)\]; in particular, when we compare
$`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})`$ and $`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})`$ we
use the *same* resource coordinates and cost model in the AAI functional
$`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$.

### 5.1 From libraries to batteries

Let $`K`$ be a fixed proof kernel (e.g. Lean 4) and let $`\mathcal{L}`$
be a finite library of proved theorems and definitions over some base
theory.

###### Definition 5.1 (Library-induced battery).

Given a finite formal library $`\mathcal{L}`$, we define a battery
$`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L})`$ as follows:

- •
  Tasks $`T`$ are formal goals expressible over $`\mathcal{L}`$, chosen
  according to a scheme (e.g. random perturbations of existing theorems,
  standard benchmark goals, or user-provided conjectures).
- •
  Scoring maps $`\mathsf{S}_{t}`$ are defined as in
  Definition [4.1](#S4.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 4.1 (Formal mathematics battery). ‣ 4.1 Formal mathematics batteries ‣ 4 Formal Mathematics Batteries and Oracle Verifiers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),
  using $`K`$ and $`\mathcal{L}`$ as the underlying environment.
- •
  The sampling law $`\mu`$ selects goals, seeds and drifts according to
  a prescribed distribution (for instance, uniform over a benchmark set,
  or weighted by difficulty).

We call $`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L})`$ the *battery induced by*
$`\mathcal{L}`$.

###### Proposition 5.2 (Monotonicity under library extension).

Let $`\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}`$ be two finite libraries
over the same kernel $`K`$, and let $`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{i})`$ be
the associated batteries. Assume:

1.  1.
    The sampling law $`\mu`$ and evaluation skeleton are compatible with
    inclusion, so that the evaluation spaces
    $`X_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})}`$ and
    $`X_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})}`$ can be identified with a common
    space $`X`$ (same task families, thresholds, resource coordinates
    and cost functional).
2.  2.
    For every $`\theta\in\Theta`$, writing
    $`\nu_{i}^{\theta}:=\rho_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{i})}(\theta)\in\mathcal{P}(X)`$,
    the pair $`(\nu_{1}^{\theta},\nu_{2}^{\theta})`$ satisfies the
    hypotheses of Restricted Monotonicity (A2) for
    $`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$:

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |  | $`\displaystyle\nu_{2}^{\theta}\succeq_{\mathrm{icx}}\nu_{1}^{\theta}\quad\text{(increasing concave order on the success indicators)},`$ |  |
    |  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{k}\!\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{2}^{\theta}}[\overline{q}(F_{k})]\Big)\;\leq\;\mathrm{Var}_{k}\!\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{1}^{\theta}}[\overline{q}(F_{k})]\Big),`$ |  |
    |  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{2}^{\theta}}[\mathrm{Cost}(r)]\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{1}^{\theta}}[\mathrm{Cost}(r)].`$ |  |

Then for any agent family and parameter state $`\theta`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})}(\theta)\;\leq\;F_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})}(\theta).
``` |  |

###### Sketch.

The larger library $`\mathcal{L}_{2}`$ provides at least as many lemmas
as $`\mathcal{L}_{1}`$ for each goal shared by the two batteries, and
scoring is identical on shared tasks. Under a fixed policy, the
availability of additional premises in $`\mathcal{L}_{2}`$ expands the
set of valid proof paths. Assumption (2) states that, viewed through the
canonical success indicators and resource coordinates of the AAI
framework, this extension realises an Axiom (A2) move: on shared tasks,
the law of success indicators under $`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})`$
dominates that under $`\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})`$ in increasing
concave order, family-wise dispersion does not increase, and the
expected cost term $`\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Cost}(r)]`$ is non-increasing
when we keep the resource metric and cost functional fixed.

By Restricted Monotonicity (Axiom (A2)) for the AAI functional
$`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$, this implies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Phi_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})}(\rho_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})}(\theta))\geq\Phi_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})}(\rho_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})}(\theta))
``` |  |

i.e.
$`F_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2})}(\theta)\geq F_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1})}(\theta)`$.
∎

This formalizes the idea that extensions of formal libraries act as
monotone maps in the Mathematics Fiber: adding lemmas (and the
associated tasks), while keeping the resource gauge fixed and staying
within the Axiom (A2) regime, can only raise—or at worst leave
unchanged—the AAI capability score.

### 5.2 Capability sections and stratification

Fix an agent family with parameter space $`\Theta`$. The assignment

|     |                                            |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{L}\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L})
       ```                                         |     |

gives a map from the poset of finite libraries (under inclusion) to
$`\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}`$. Composing with

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Theta\times\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{math}}^{\mathrm{formal},K}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad(\theta,\mathcal{B})\mapsto F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta),
``` |  |

we obtain a capability field

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Theta\times\mathrm{Lib}_{K}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad(\theta,\mathcal{L})\mapsto F_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L})}(\theta)
``` |  |

over the library poset. For fixed $`\theta`$, the map
$`\mathcal{L}\mapsto F_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L})}(\theta)`$ can be
thought of as a section over the Mathematics Fiber that measures the

This viewpoint makes it natural to speak of:

- •
  *Horizontal* motion in $`\Theta`$: improving the agent at fixed

- •
  *Vertical* motion in $`\mathrm{Lib}_{K}`$: enriching the formal

The interaction between these two motions is central to understanding
neuro-symbolic growth in mathematics: as the library expands, new

### 5.3 DeepAlgebra Loops as GVU Flows

We close with a brief sketch of how DeepAlgebra-style architectures as

At a high level, a DeepAlgebra loop consists of:

1.  1.
    A neural search component that proposes new conjectures, candidate

2.  2.
    An autoformalization component that translates these outputs into

3.  3.
    A proof assistant kernel that verifies the formal artefacts.
4.  4.
    An update rule that adds verified results to the library and

Taken together, these components implement a GVU operator whose dynamics

By discussion above such loops naturally operate in the oracle-verifier

## 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers

In this section we make precise, and prove in a simplified setting, the
intuition that mathematics and coding tasks form universal coordinates
on the moduli space: up to arbitrarily small error in evaluation, every
battery can be approximated by batteries built from mathematical and
coding tasks.

### 6.1 A simplified one-shot model

For technical clarity we first work in a one-shot setting where each
interaction between an agent and a battery consists of a single
input–output pair and a single bounded score. This reduction is without
loss of generality for the density result: multi-step and multi-task
batteries can be viewed as finite mixtures of such one-shot components.

##### Outcome space and agents.

Let $`\Sigma`$ be a finite alphabet and let

|     |                    |     |
|-----|--------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \Omega:=\Sigma^{*}
       ```                 |     |

denote the set of all finite strings over $`\Sigma`$, equipped with the
discrete $`\sigma`$-algebra. Elements $`\omega\in\Omega`$ represent
complete one-shot traces: prompt plus response plus any auxiliary
markers.

A (one-shot) agent is identified with a probability measure
$`P\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$ over outcomes. Let
$`\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$ be a fixed, non-empty
reference class of agents. We equip $`\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$ with the
topology of weak convergence; since $`\Omega`$ is countable and
discrete, this is simply the product topology inherited from
$`[0,1]^{\Omega}`$.

##### One-shot batteries and capability functionals.

In this model a one-shot battery is entirely specified by a bounded
scoring function $`f:\Omega\to[0,1]`$.

###### Definition 6.1 (One-shot battery and capability functional).

A *one-shot battery* is a measurable function $`f:\Omega\to[0,1]`$.
Given an agent $`P\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$, its *capability* on $`f`$ is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{f}(P):=\mathbb{E}_{\,\omega\sim P}[f(\omega)]=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}f(\omega)\,P(\{\omega\}).
``` |  |

We write $`\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ for the class of all one-shot batteries
(all such bounded measurable $`f`$), and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{F}:=\{F_{f}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}\mid f\in\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}\}
``` |  |

for the corresponding family of capability functionals restricted to
$`\mathcal{A}`$.

We make $`\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ into a metric space by declaring two
batteries close if they induce similar capability functionals on
$`\mathcal{A}`$.

###### Definition 6.2 (Evaluation metric).

For $`f,g\in\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ we define the *evaluation distance*

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g):=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}\big|F_{f}(P)-F_{g}(P)\big|=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}(f(\omega)-g(\omega))P(\{\omega\})\right|.
``` |  |

This is simply the operator norm of the linear functional
$`P\mapsto F_{f}(P)-F_{g}(P)`$ on the convex set $`\mathcal{A}`$.

###### Definition 6.3 (Density).

A subset $`\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ is *dense* (with
respect to $`d_{\mathcal{A}}`$) if for every $`f\in\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$
and every $`\varepsilon>0`$ there exists $`g\in\mathcal{N}`$ such that

|     |                                   |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)<\varepsilon.
       ```                                |     |

### 6.2 Mathematics and coding scorers in the one-shot model

We now formalize the one-shot analogues of the mathematics and coding
fibers.

###### Definition 6.4 (Mathematics and coding scorers).

Let $`\mathrm{Thm}`$ be a countable set of formal theorem statements in
a fixed proof assistant with kernel $`K`$, and let $`\mathrm{Code}`$ be
a countable set of coding problems (program specifications) over a fixed
Turing-complete language $`L`$.

1.  1.
    For each $`\tau\in\mathrm{Thm}`$ let
    $`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}:\Omega\to\{0,1\}`$ be the one-shot scorer

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\omega$ is accepted by $K$ as a proof of $\tau$},\\
    0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
    ``` |  |

    The *mathematics scorer class* is
    $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{math}}:=\{S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}:\tau\in\mathrm{Thm}\}`$.
2.  2.
    For each coding problem $`c\in\mathrm{Code}`$, we fix a finite
    validation set of test inputs together with an evaluation map
    $`E_{c}:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ that may inspect both the source string
    $`\omega`$ and the (possibly simulated) runtime behaviour of
    $`\omega`$ on the test inputs (as in real coding benchmarks with
    static analysis, linting, or style checks).

    We obtain a scorer $`S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c}:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ defined
    by

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c}(\omega):=E_{c}(\text{interpret $\omega$ as source code in $L$}).
    ``` |  |

    The *coding scorer class* is
    $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}:=\{S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c}:c\in\mathrm{Code}\}`$.

We write

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{S}_{0}:=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{math}}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}.
``` |  |

From these we form the algebra generated by mathematics and coding
scorers.

###### Definition 6.5 (Algebra generated by mathematics and coding).

Let $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ be the set of all finite linear combinations and
products of elements of $`\mathcal{S}_{0}`$ together with constants:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}_{0}:=\left\{p(S_{1}(\cdot),\dots,S_{k}(\cdot))\,\middle|\,k\in\mathbb{N},\;S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{0},\;p\text{ a real polynomial in $k$ variables}\right\}.
``` |  |

We call $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ the *math+code algebra*. It is a unital
subalgebra of $`\ell^{\infty}(\Omega)`$ (bounded real-valued functions
on $`\Omega`$).

Each $`f\in\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ corresponds to a (possibly composite)
battery whose score is a polynomial in the primitive mathematics and
coding scores; for example, a task that awards $`0.7`$ points for a
correct proof and $`0.3`$ points for a correct program.

Our goal is to show that $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ is dense in
$`\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ with respect to the evaluation metric
$`d_{\mathcal{A}}`$.

### 6.3 Tightness assumption on the agent class

To obtain a non-trivial density result we need a mild regularity
assumption on the reference class $`\mathcal{A}`$: roughly, we require
that agents do not put arbitrary mass on arbitrarily long, exotic
traces. This is a standard tightness condition.

###### Definition 6.6 (Uniform tightness).

We say the agent class $`\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$ is
*uniformly tight* if for every $`\delta>0`$ there exists a finite subset
$`F_{\delta}\subset\Omega`$ such that

|     |                                                                    |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}P(\Omega\setminus F_{\delta})\;\leq\;\delta.
       ```                                                                 |     |

Uniform tightness is automatic, for example, if every agent in
$`\mathcal{A}`$ is supported on traces of length at most $`L_{\max}`$
for some finite $`L_{\max}`$, or more generally if there is a uniform
tail bound on output length. In typical benchmark settings (e.g.,
language models with a fixed context window or RL agents with finite
horizons), uniform tightness holds trivially because traces are bounded
in length by the protocol. For unbounded settings, generalizations via
moment bounds are possible but beyond our scope.

### 6.4 A universal generating family of coding scorers

The key technical step is to observe that, under uniform tightness, it
suffices to approximate scoring functions on *finite* subsets of
$`\Omega`$. Coding tasks can realize arbitrary indicator functions of
finite sets, and finite linear combinations of such indicators
approximate any bounded function in the evaluation metric.

We start by exhibiting a family of primitive coding scorers whose linear
span contains all finite-support functions on $`\Omega`$.

###### Lemma 6.7 (Point-mass coding scorers).

For each $`\omega_{0}\in\Omega`$ there exists a coding problem
$`c_{\omega_{0}}\in\mathrm{Code}`$ and a corresponding scorer
$`S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c_{\omega_{0}}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}`$
such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c_{\omega_{0}}}(\omega)=\mathds{1}\{\omega=\omega_{0}\}\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega.
``` |  |

In particular, the linear span of $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}`$
contains every finite-support function $`g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}`$.

###### Proof.

Fix $`\omega_{0}\in\Omega`$. Define the coding problem
$`c_{\omega_{0}}`$ as: “Submit source code that exactly matches the
string $`\omega_{0}`$.” The evaluator $`E_{c_{\omega_{0}}}`$ performs a
static string comparison:
$`E_{c_{\omega_{0}}}(\omega)=\mathds{1}\{\omega=\omega_{0}\}`$. This is
a valid coding task (e.g., a linting or checksum check), implementing
the indicator function exactly.

We now define the scorer
$`S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c_{\omega_{0}}}(\omega):=E_{c_{\omega_{0}}}(\omega)`$.

Since any finite-support function $`g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}`$ can be
written as a finite linear combination
$`g=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\mathds{1}\{\omega=\omega_{j}\}`$, the linear
span of $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}`$ contains all such $`g`$. ∎

Let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fs}}:=\{g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\,\mid\,g\text{ has finite support}\}.
``` |  |

Then
Lemma [6.7](#S6.Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 6.7 (Point-mass coding scorers). ‣ 6.4 A universal generating family of coding scorers ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
shows that
$`\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fs}}\subseteq\mathrm{span}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}})`$
and hence $`\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fs}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{0}`$.

### 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness

We now show that any bounded scorer $`f:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ can be
approximated in the evaluation metric $`d_{\mathcal{A}}`$ by a
finite-support function. This is the only place where the tightness
assumption enters.

###### Lemma 6.8 (Approximation by finite-support functions).

Assume $`\mathcal{A}`$ is uniformly tight in the sense of
Definition [6.6](#S6.Thmtheorem6 "Definition 6.6 (Uniform tightness). ‣ 6.3 Tightness assumption on the agent class ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
Let $`f:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ be a bounded scorer and let $`\varepsilon>0`$.
Then there exists a finite-support function $`g:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ such
that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P}[f]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g]\big|<\varepsilon.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Let $`\varepsilon>0`$ be given. By uniform tightness there exists a
finite subset $`F_{\delta}\subset\Omega`$ such that

|     |                                                                |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}P(\Omega\setminus F_{\delta})\leq\delta,
       ```                                                             |     |

for some $`\delta`$ to be chosen later. Define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
g(\omega):=\begin{cases}f(\omega)&\text{if }\omega\in F_{\delta},\\
0&\text{if }\omega\notin F_{\delta}.\end{cases}
``` |  |

Then $`g`$ has finite support (contained in $`F_{\delta}`$), and both
$`f`$ and $`g`$ are bounded in $`[0,1]`$. For any $`P\in\mathcal{A}`$ we
have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}[f]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g]\right|`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}(f(\omega)-g(\omega))P(\{\omega\})\right|`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\omega\notin F_{\delta}}(f(\omega)-0)P(\{\omega\})\right|`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\omega\notin F_{\delta}}|f(\omega)|P(\{\omega\})\leq\sup_{\omega}|f(\omega)|\,P(\Omega\setminus F_{\delta})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq 1\cdot P(\Omega\setminus F_{\delta})\leq\delta.`$ |  |

Taking the supremum over $`P\in\mathcal{A}`$ yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}[f]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g]\right|\leq\delta.
``` |  |

Choosing $`\delta:=\varepsilon`$ gives the claim. ∎

Combining
Lemmas [6.7](#S6.Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 6.7 (Point-mass coding scorers). ‣ 6.4 A universal generating family of coding scorers ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
and
[6.8](#S6.Thmtheorem8 "Lemma 6.8 (Approximation by finite-support functions). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
we obtain:

###### Proposition 6.9 (Density of the math+code algebra in the one-shot model).

Assume the agent class $`\mathcal{A}`$ is uniformly tight. Then for
every one-shot battery $`f\in\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ and every
$`\varepsilon>0`$ there exists a function $`g\in\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ such
that

|     |                                   |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)<\varepsilon.
       ```                                |     |

Equivalently, the math+code algebra $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ is dense in
$`\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ with respect to $`d_{\mathcal{A}}`$.

###### Proof.

Let $`f\in\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ and $`\varepsilon>0`$. By
Lemma [6.8](#S6.Thmtheorem8 "Lemma 6.8 (Approximation by finite-support functions). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
there exists a finite-support function $`g_{0}:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ such
that $`d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g_{0})<\varepsilon/2`$.

By
Lemma [6.7](#S6.Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 6.7 (Point-mass coding scorers). ‣ 6.4 A universal generating family of coding scorers ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),
$`g_{0}`$ lies in the linear span of $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}`$,
hence $`g_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}`$. Taking $`g:=g_{0}`$ we have
$`g\in\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ and
$`d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)<\varepsilon/2<\varepsilon`$. Since
$`\varepsilon>0`$ was arbitrary, the math+code algebra is dense. ∎

### 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries

We now extend
Proposition [6.9](#S6.Thmtheorem9 "Proposition 6.9 (Density of the math+code algebra in the one-shot model). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
from the one-shot model to the full moduli space $`\mathfrak{M}`$ of
batteries introduced in
Section [2](#S2 "2 Preliminaries: Batteries and Their Moduli ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
The key observation is that a general battery $`\mathcal{B}`$ induces,
for each agent $`\theta\in\Theta`$, a probability law
$`\nu_{r}=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)`$ on an evaluation space
$`X_{\mathcal{B}}`$, and that the AAI functional $`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$
is Lipschitz with respect to perturbations of these laws in an
appropriate metric. Approximating the per-task scoring functions
$`S_{t}`$ in expectation uniformly over $`\theta`$ then yields uniform
approximation of the capability functional
$`F_{\mathcal{B}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{B}}`$.

#### 6.6.1 Metric structure on the evaluation space

Recall that for a fixed battery $`\mathcal{B}`$ with task set $`T`$ the
evaluation space is

|     |                                                              |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       X_{\mathcal{B}}:=[0,1]^{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d_{R}},
       ```                                                           |     |

where the first component collects the per-task quality scores and the
second the resource coordinates. We equip $`X_{\mathcal{B}}`$ with the
product metric

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{X_{\mathcal{B}}}\big((q,r),(q^{\prime},r^{\prime})\big):=\sum_{t\in T}|q(t)-q^{\prime}(t)|+\|r-r^{\prime}\|_{1},
``` |  |

where $`q,q^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{T}`$ and
$`r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d_{R}}`$. This is a finite metric
on $`X_{\mathcal{B}}`$ whenever $`T`$ is finite.

###### Definition 6.10 (Bounded-Lipschitz distance).

Let $`(S,d)`$ be a metric space. For probability measures
$`\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(S)`$ we define the *bounded-Lipschitz distance*

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathrm{BL}_{1}(S)}\left|\int_{S}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{S}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right|,
``` |  |

where $`\mathrm{BL}_{1}(S)`$ is the set of all measurable
$`\varphi:S\to\mathbb{R}`$ such that $`\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1`$ and
$`\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)\leq 1`$ with respect to $`d`$.

It is standard that $`d_{\mathrm{BL}}`$ metrizes weak convergence when
$`S`$ is separable; here $`S=X_{\mathcal{B}}`$ is a finite-dimensional
separable metric space.

###### Definition 6.11 (Lipschitz AAI functional).

We say that an AAI functional
$`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})\to\mathbb{R}`$ is
$`L`$-*Lipschitz with respect to $`d_{\mathrm{BL}}`$* if there exists
$`L\in(0,\infty)`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\big|\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mu)-\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\nu)\big|\;\leq\;L\,d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu,\nu)\quad\text{for all }\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}}).
``` |  |

In \[[1](#bib.bib1)\] it is shown that the tractable AAI instances
satisfy this Lipschitz property.

Given a battery $`\mathcal{B}`$ and an agent parameter
$`\theta\in\Theta`$, we denote by

|     |                                                                    |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \nu_{r}:=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)\in\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})
       ```                                                                 |     |

the induced evaluation law, and by
$`F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta):=\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\nu_{r})`$ the associated
capability.

#### 6.6.2 Per-task tightness of trace distributions

We now spell out the tightness assumption needed to lift the one-shot
approximation result.

Fix a battery $`\mathcal{B}`$ with task set $`T`$ and sampling law
$`\mu`$ on $`T\times\Pi\times\mathsf{D}`$, as in
Definition [2.1](#S2.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 2.1 (Battery). ‣ 2.1 Batteries and representations ‣ 2 Preliminaries: Batteries and Their Moduli ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
For each $`\theta\in\Theta`$ and $`t\in T`$, consider the conditional
law of the outcome trace $`\omega`$ given task $`t`$ when interacting
with agent $`\theta`$ under $`\mathcal{B}`$:

|     |                                         |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       P_{t,\theta}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega_{t}),
       ```                                      |     |

where $`\Omega_{t}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}`$ is the trace space of task
$`t`$.

###### Definition 6.12 (Per-task uniform tightness).

Let $`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}\subseteq\Theta`$ be a reference class of
agents. For each $`t\in T`$, define the induced class of trace laws

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}_{t}:=\{P_{t,\theta}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega_{t}):\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}\}.
``` |  |

We say that $`\mathcal{B}`$ is *per-task uniformly tight on
$`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}`$* if for every $`t\in T`$ and every $`\delta>0`$
there exists a finite subset $`F_{t,\delta}\subset\Omega_{t}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}P_{t,\theta}\big(\Omega_{t}\setminus F_{t,\delta}\big)\;\leq\;\delta.
``` |  |

Intuitively, this means that for each task $`t`$, agents in
$`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}`$ do not place significant probability mass on
arbitrarily long or exotic traces.

#### 6.6.3 Approximation of per-task scoring maps

Fix a battery $`\mathcal{B}`$ and a reference agent class
$`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}\subseteq\Theta`$. Each task $`t\in T`$ has a
scoring map

|     |                           |     |
|-----|---------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{t}:\Omega_{t}\to[0,1].
       ```                        |     |

Applying the one-shot approximation result, we can approximate each
$`S_{t}`$ by a finite-support function in the math+code algebra on
$`\Omega_{t}`$.

To make this precise, we embed each $`\Omega_{t}`$ into a common
countable alphabetic space and apply
Proposition [6.9](#S6.Thmtheorem9 "Proposition 6.9 (Density of the math+code algebra in the one-shot model). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
in that setting.

###### Lemma 6.13 (Per-task finite-support approximation).

Assume $`\mathcal{B}`$ is per-task uniformly tight on
$`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}`$ in the sense of
Definition [6.12](#S6.Thmtheorem12 "Definition 6.12 (Per-task uniform tightness). ‣ 6.6.2 Per-task tightness of trace distributions ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
Let $`S_{t}:\Omega_{t}\to[0,1]`$ be the scoring map for task $`t\in T`$.
Then for every $`\varepsilon>0`$ there exists a finite-support function
$`g_{t}:\Omega_{t}\to[0,1]`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}[S_{t}]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}[g_{t}]\big|<\varepsilon.
``` |  |

Moreover, $`g_{t}`$ lies in the restriction to $`\Omega_{t}`$ of the
math+code algebra $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ constructed in
Section [6.2](#S6.SS2 "6.2 Mathematics and coding scorers in the one-shot model ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").

###### Proof.

For each $`t\in T`$ we view $`\Omega_{t}`$ as a subset of a fixed
countable alphabetic space $`\Omega=\Sigma^{*}`$ by padding or tagging
traces with the task identifier if necessary. The class of conditional
trace laws
$`\mathcal{A}_{t}=\{P_{t,\theta}:\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}\}`$ then
forms a uniformly tight subset of $`\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$ by the
per-task tightness assumption.

We may extend $`S_{t}`$ to a bounded scorer
$`\tilde{S}_{t}:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ by setting
$`\tilde{S}_{t}(\omega):=S_{t}(\omega)`$ for $`\omega\in\Omega_{t}`$ and
$`\tilde{S}_{t}(\omega):=0`$ for $`\omega\notin\Omega_{t}`$. By
Lemma [6.8](#S6.Thmtheorem8 "Lemma 6.8 (Approximation by finite-support functions). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),
for every $`\varepsilon>0`$ there exists a finite-support function
$`\tilde{g}_{t}:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P}[\tilde{S}_{t}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[\tilde{g}_{t}]\big|<\varepsilon.
``` |  |

In particular,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}[S_{t}]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}[g_{t}]\big|<\varepsilon,
``` |  |

where $`g_{t}:=\tilde{g}_{t}\big|_{\Omega_{t}}`$ is the restriction of
$`\tilde{g}_{t}`$ to $`\Omega_{t}`$.

By construction, $`\tilde{g}_{t}`$ lies in the finite-support class
$`\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{fs}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ from
Section [6.2](#S6.SS2 "6.2 Mathematics and coding scorers in the one-shot model ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
Thus $`g_{t}`$ is the restriction of some element of $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$
to $`\Omega_{t}`$, which can be realized operationally as a composition
of coding scorers on $`\Omega`$ (and hence belongs to the math+code
algebra on $`\Omega_{t}`$ as well). ∎

#### 6.6.4 A coupling bound for evaluation laws

To transfer per-task approximation error into a bound on
$`d_{\mathrm{BL}}\big(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta),\rho_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)\big)`$
we use a simple coupling argument.

###### Lemma 6.14 (BL distance under coordinate-wise approximation).

Let $`T`$ be finite and let $`(Z_{t})_{t\in T}`$ and
$`(Z^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in T}`$ be random vectors in $`[0,1]^{T}`$ defined
on a common probability space. Equip
$`X:=[0,1]^{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d_{R}}`$ with the metric
$`d_{X}`$ of
Section [6.6](#S6.SS6 "6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),
and consider random elements $`X=(Z,R)`$ and
$`X^{\prime}=(Z^{\prime},R)`$, where $`R`$ takes values in
$`\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d_{R}}`$. Let $`\mu,\nu`$ denote the laws of
$`X`$ and $`X^{\prime}`$ respectively.

Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu,\nu)\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t\in T}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|\Big].
``` |  |

In particular, if $`\mathbb{E}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|\leq\delta_{t}`$ for
all $`t\in T`$ then

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu,\nu)\;\leq\;\sum_{t\in T}\delta_{t}.
       ```                                                       |     |

###### Proof.

Let $`\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}`$ be any test function with
$`\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1`$ and $`\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)\leq 1`$ with
respect to $`d_{X}`$. Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left|\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]-\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X^{\prime})]\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi(Z,R)-\varphi(Z^{\prime},R)\big]\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\big[|\varphi(Z,R)-\varphi(Z^{\prime},R)|\big].
``` |  |

Since $`\varphi`$ is $`1`$-Lipschitz with respect to $`d_{X}`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|\varphi(Z,R)-\varphi(Z^{\prime},R)|\leq d_{X}\big((Z,R),(Z^{\prime},R)\big)=\sum_{t\in T}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|.
``` |  |

Taking expectations yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left|\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]-\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X^{\prime})]\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t\in T}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|\Big].
``` |  |

Taking the supremum over all such $`\varphi`$ gives the desired bound on
$`d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu,\nu)`$. The second statement follows by bounding
$`\mathbb{E}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|`$ by $`\delta_{t}`$ and summing over
$`t\in T`$. ∎

#### 6.6.5 Main density theorem for general batteries

We now put these ingredients together.

###### Theorem 6.15 (Math+code density for general batteries).

Let $`\mathcal{B}`$ be a battery with finite task set $`T`$, scoring
maps $`\{S_{t}\}_{t\in T}`$, and evaluation space $`X_{\mathcal{B}}`$ as
above. Let $`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}\subseteq\Theta`$ be a reference class
of agents, and assume:

1.  (H1)
    *Per-task tightness.* $`\mathcal{B}`$ is per-task uniformly tight on
    $`\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}`$ in the sense of
    Definition [6.12](#S6.Thmtheorem12 "Definition 6.12 (Per-task uniform tightness). ‣ 6.6.2 Per-task tightness of trace distributions ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
2.  (H2)
    *Lipschitz AAI.* The AAI functional
    $`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{B}})\to\mathbb{R}`$ is
    $`L`$-Lipschitz with respect to $`d_{\mathrm{BL}}`$ for some
    $`L\in(0,\infty)`$.

Then for every $`\varepsilon>0`$ there exists a battery
$`\mathcal{B}^{\prime}`$ whose scoring maps are drawn from the math+code
algebra (in the sense of
Lemma [6.13](#S6.Thmtheorem13 "Lemma 6.13 (Per-task finite-support approximation). ‣ 6.6.3 Approximation of per-task scoring maps ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"))
such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)-F_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)\big|<\varepsilon,
``` |  |

where $`F_{\mathcal{B}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{B}}`$ and
$`F_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}`$
are the respective capability functionals. In particular, the subspace
of batteries generated by mathematics and coding tasks is dense in
$`\mathfrak{M}`$ with respect to the evaluation metric

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}):=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|F_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\theta)-F_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(\theta)\big|.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Fix $`\varepsilon>0`$. For each task $`t\in T`$, apply
Lemma [6.13](#S6.Thmtheorem13 "Lemma 6.13 (Per-task finite-support approximation). ‣ 6.6.3 Approximation of per-task scoring maps ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
with parameter

|     |                                       |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \delta_{t}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2L|T|}
       ```                                    |     |

to obtain a finite-support function $`g_{t}:\Omega_{t}\to[0,1]`$ in the
restriction of the math+code algebra such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}[S_{t}]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}[g_{t}]\big|<\delta_{t}.
``` |  |

Define a new battery $`\mathcal{B}^{\prime}`$ that is identical to
$`\mathcal{B}`$ in all respects except for the scoring maps: for each
$`t\in T`$ we set

|     |                        |     |
|-----|------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       S_{t}^{\prime}:=g_{t}.
       ```                     |     |

Since each $`g_{t}`$ is, by construction, a finite linear combination of
indicator functions implemented by coding scorers (and possibly simple
compositions with mathematical scorers), the battery
$`\mathcal{B}^{\prime}`$ lies in the subspace generated by mathematics
and coding tasks.

Fix $`\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}`$ and consider an evaluation of
$`\theta`$ under $`\mathcal{B}`$ and $`\mathcal{B}^{\prime}`$ with the
same random seed, task draws and drifts. This induces random
trace/outcome triples for each task $`t`$, with conditional law
$`P_{t,\theta}`$, and hence random score vectors

|     |                                                                 |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       Z:=(Z_{t})_{t\in T},\quad Z^{\prime}:=(Z^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in T}
       ```                                                              |     |

with

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Z_{t}:=S_{t}(\omega_{t}),\qquad Z^{\prime}_{t}:=S_{t}^{\prime}(\omega_{t})=g_{t}(\omega_{t}).
``` |  |

Let $`R`$ denote the resource vector (which is unaffected by changing
the scoring maps). Then the random evaluation elements

|     |                                            |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       X:=(Z,R),\qquad X^{\prime}:=(Z^{\prime},R)
       ```                                         |     |

take values in $`X_{\mathcal{B}}`$ and their laws are precisely
$`\nu_{r}=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)`$ and
$`\nu_{r}^{\prime}=\rho_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)`$.

For each $`t\in T`$ we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|=\mathbb{E}\big|S_{t}(\omega_{t})-g_{t}(\omega_{t})\big|=\mathbb{E}_{P_{t,\theta}}\big|S_{t}-g_{t}\big|<\delta_{t},
``` |  |

by the construction of $`g_{t}`$. Applying
Lemma [6.14](#S6.Thmtheorem14 "Lemma 6.14 (BL distance under coordinate-wise approximation). ‣ 6.6.4 A coupling bound for evaluation laws ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\big(\nu_{r},\nu_{r}^{\prime}\big)\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t\in T}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|\Big]\leq\sum_{t\in T}\delta_{t}=|T|\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2L|T|}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2L}.
``` |  |

By the Lipschitz property of $`\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}`$ and the fact that
$`\mathcal{B}`$ and $`\mathcal{B}^{\prime}`$ share the same evaluation
space $`X_{\mathcal{B}}`$ and metric, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\big|F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)-F_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)\big|=\big|\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\nu_{r})-\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\nu_{r}^{\prime})\big|\leq L\,d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\nu_{r},\nu_{r}^{\prime})\leq L\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2L}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
``` |  |

Since this bound is uniform in $`\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}`$ we
obtain

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}\big|F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)-F_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon.
``` |  |

Renaming $`\varepsilon/2`$ to $`\varepsilon`$ if desired yields the
stated density result. ∎

### 6.7 Why coding is universal but mathematics is not

The proof of
Theorem [6.15](#S6.Thmtheorem15 "Theorem 6.15 (Math+code density for general batteries). ‣ 6.6.5 Main density theorem for general batteries ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
in fact only uses the coding scorers of
Definition [6.4](#S6.Thmtheorem4 "Definition 6.4 (Mathematics and coding scorers). ‣ 6.2 Mathematics and coding scorers in the one-shot model ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
in an essential way. Mathematics scorers are never invoked in the
construction of the finite-support approximants. This asymmetry reflects
a genuine difference between the two fibers in any “natural” model.

###### Remark 6.16 (Coding alone is already universal).

Let $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}`$ be the class of coding scorers and
let $`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{code}}`$ denote the algebra they generate
(finite linear combinations and products, plus constants). The proof of
Lemma [6.7](#S6.Thmtheorem7 "Lemma 6.7 (Point-mass coding scorers). ‣ 6.4 A universal generating family of coding scorers ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
shows that for every outcome string $`\omega_{0}\in\Omega`$ there exists
a coding scorer $`S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c_{\omega_{0}}}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S^{\mathrm{code}}_{c_{\omega_{0}}}(\omega)=\mathds{1}\{\omega=\omega_{0}\}\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega.
``` |  |

Hence every finite-support function $`g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}`$ lies in
the linear span of $`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{code}}`$ and therefore in
$`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{code}}`$.

Combining this with
Lemma [6.8](#S6.Thmtheorem8 "Lemma 6.8 (Approximation by finite-support functions). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
and repeating the argument of
Proposition [6.9](#S6.Thmtheorem9 "Proposition 6.9 (Density of the math+code algebra in the one-shot model). ‣ 6.5 Approximation of arbitrary scorers under uniform tightness ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
and
Theorem [6.15](#S6.Thmtheorem15 "Theorem 6.15 (Math+code density for general batteries). ‣ 6.6.5 Main density theorem for general batteries ‣ 6.6 From one-shot scorers to general batteries ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
with $`\mathcal{A}_{0}`$ replaced by $`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{code}}`$, we
obtain the following corollary: under the same tightness and Lipschitz
assumptions, the subspace of batteries generated *purely* by coding
tasks is dense in $`\mathfrak{M}`$ with respect to the evaluation metric
$`d_{\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}`$.

In this sense, coding alone is already universal: the mathematics fiber
is not required for density, but adds additional structure and
interpretability.

By contrast, the mathematics scorers of
Definition [6.4](#S6.Thmtheorem4 "Definition 6.4 (Mathematics and coding scorers). ‣ 6.2 Mathematics and coding scorers in the one-shot model ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
are not universal in this way under any natural assumption on the proof
system. Intuitively, a theorem prover can distinguish “valid proofs of
$`\tau`$” from “everything else”, but treats almost all non-proofs as
indistinguishable zeros. This prevents the algebra they generate from
approximating arbitrary scorers that depend on the *surface form* of
non-proof traces.

###### Definition 6.17 (Mathematics scorers with proof-kernel semantics).

Let $`K`$ be a fixed proof kernel (e.g. the kernel of Lean or Coq), and
let $`\mathrm{Thm}`$ be a set of theorem statements formalized in the
corresponding logic. For each $`\tau\in\mathrm{Thm}`$ we define
$`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}:\Omega\to\{0,1\}`$ by

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\omega$ is accepted by $K$ as a valid proof of $\tau$},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
``` |  |

We write
$`\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{math}}:=\{S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}:\tau\in\mathrm{Thm}\}`$
and let $`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ be the algebra they generate
(finite linear combinations and products, plus constants).

In this model, all mathematical scorers share a structural invariance:
they cannot distinguish between different non-proofs for the same
theorem.

###### Lemma 6.18 (Non-proofs are indistinguishable to mathematics scorers).

Fix $`\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega`$ such that for every
$`\tau\in\mathrm{Thm}`$ we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
K\text{ does not accept }\omega_{1}\text{ as a proof of }\tau,\quad K\text{ does not accept }\omega_{2}\text{ as a proof of }\tau.
``` |  |

Then for every
$`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{math}}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega_{1})=S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega_{2})=0.
``` |  |

Consequently, for every $`f\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ we have
$`f(\omega_{1})=f(\omega_{2})`$.

###### Proof.

By definition of $`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}`$ we have
$`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega)=1`$ if and only if $`K`$ accepts
$`\omega`$ as a proof of $`\tau`$, and $`0`$ otherwise. The hypothesis
that neither $`\omega_{1}`$ nor $`\omega_{2}`$ is accepted as a proof of
any $`\tau`$ immediately implies
$`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega_{1})=S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau}(\omega_{2})=0`$
for all $`\tau`$.

Any $`f\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ is obtained by applying a real
polynomial $`p`$ to a finite tuple of mathematics scorers:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f(\omega)=p\big(S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau_{1}}(\omega),\dots,S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau_{k}}(\omega)\big).
``` |  |

Since each coordinate $`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau_{i}}(\omega_{1})`$ and
$`S^{\mathrm{math}}_{\tau_{i}}(\omega_{2})`$ is zero, the input to $`p`$
is the same vector $`(0,\dots,0)`$ in both cases, hence
$`f(\omega_{1})=f(\omega_{2})`$. ∎

We can now state a simple obstruction to density for the mathematics
fiber.

###### Proposition 6.19 (Mathematics algebra is not dense in general).

Assume the setting of
Lemma [6.18](#S6.Thmtheorem18 "Lemma 6.18 (Non-proofs are indistinguishable to mathematics scorers). ‣ 6.7 Why coding is universal but mathematics is not ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks").
Let $`\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega`$ be two non-proof strings as in
the lemma, and let $`\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Omega)`$ be an
agent class that contains the two point masses
$`\delta_{\omega_{1}},\delta_{\omega_{2}}`$.

Define a scorer $`f:\Omega\to[0,1]`$ by

|     |                                        |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f(\omega_{1})=0,\quad f(\omega_{2})=1,
       ```                                     |     |

and $`f(\omega)=0`$ for all other $`\omega\in\Omega`$. Then for every
$`g\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{A}}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P}[f]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g]\big|\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}.
``` |  |

In particular, $`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ is not dense in
$`\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ with respect to $`d_{\mathcal{A}}`$.

###### Proof.

By
Lemma [6.18](#S6.Thmtheorem18 "Lemma 6.18 (Non-proofs are indistinguishable to mathematics scorers). ‣ 6.7 Why coding is universal but mathematics is not ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks"),
for any $`g\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$ we have
$`g(\omega_{1})=g(\omega_{2})`$; call this common value
$`a\in\mathbb{R}`$.

Consider the two agents $`P_{1}=\delta_{\omega_{1}}`$ and
$`P_{2}=\delta_{\omega_{2}}`$. Then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{P_{1}}[f]=f(\omega_{1})=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{P_{1}}[g]=g(\omega_{1})=a,
``` |  |

and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{P_{2}}[f]=f(\omega_{2})=1,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{P_{2}}[g]=g(\omega_{2})=a.
``` |  |

Thus

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)\;\geq\;\max\big(|\mathbb{E}_{P_{1}}[f]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{1}}[g]|,\,|\mathbb{E}_{P_{2}}[f]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{2}}[g]|\big)=\max(|0-a|,|1-a|).
``` |  |

For any $`a\in\mathbb{R}`$ we have $`\max(|0-a|,|1-a|)\geq\frac{1}{2}`$,
with equality only at $`a=1/2`$. Hence $`d_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)\geq 1/2`$
for all $`g\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{math}}`$, so the mathematics algebra
cannot approximate $`f`$ in $`d_{\mathcal{A}}`$. ∎

Proposition [6.19](#S6.Thmtheorem19 "Proposition 6.19 (Mathematics algebra is not dense in general). ‣ 6.7 Why coding is universal but mathematics is not ‣ 6 Universality of the Mathematics and Coding Fibers ‣ Mathematics and Coding are Universal AI Benchmarks")
shows that, under any natural assumption in which the proof kernel $`K`$
distinguishes proofs from non-proofs but does not encode arbitrary
surface strings as distinct proofs, the algebra generated by mathematics
scorers cannot be dense in $`\mathfrak{M}^{(1)}`$ for agent classes that
can concentrate on non-proof behaviour. In contrast, the coding fiber,
through its ability to implement singleton indicators via tailored
evaluators, already yields a dense subspace.

Conceptually, the mathematics fiber provides a highly structured,
low-entropy subspace of the moduli (formal proofs and algebraic
structure), while the coding fiber provides universal expressive power
on the outcome space. The density theorems in this section justify
focusing theoretical analysis of self-improvement on these two fibers:
coding suffices for universality, and mathematics supplies the symbolic
structure most relevant for GVU-style neuro-symbolic dynamics.

## 7 Outlook

We have shown that there is a geometric duality at the heart of AI
evaluation. While the Mathematics Fiber provides the *spectral
stability* needed for ignition—a low-entropy, oracle-verified regime
where self-improvement is primarily constrained by alignment and
compute—it is the Coding Fiber that provides *expressive universality*.
Our density results imply that the combined “math+code” algebra is a
dense skeleton capable of approximating any rigorous benchmark.

This suggests a concrete roadmap for the future of AGI evaluation and
training along two complementary axes:

- •
  Vertical deepening (the oracle): Expanding the Mathematics Fiber by
  formalizing new areas of abstract mathematics and program logics,
  thereby creating deeper “wells” of high-SNR capability where agents
  can refine their reasoning engines without hallucination.
- •
  Horizontal expansion (the universal proxy): Leveraging the
  universality of the Coding Fiber to construct formal proxies for
  informal domains. Rather than training agents on subjective “slop,” we
  can train them on coding tasks that structurally approximate the
  desired behaviours, importing the rigor of compilation and testing
  into the fuzziness of natural language and open-ended interaction.

The natural frontier lies at their intersection: Certified Programming.
As agents learn to write code that is not only executable (universal)
but also formally verified against mathematical specifications (stable),
the distinction between the two fibers begins to blur. In this limit,
the DeepAlgebra-style GVU loop becomes a general-purpose mechanism for
reliable neuro-symbolic reasoning, gradually colonizing more of the
moduli space with verifiable, self-improving intelligence.

We have argued that code occupies a particularly privileged region in
the moduli space of batteries. On this fiber, verifiers can be
instantiated by compilers, type checkers, static analyzers and unit-test
harnesses, yielding oracle-like, low-variance signals that plug directly
into the GVU loop. Executable semantics make it possible to construct
batteries whose scores are tightly coupled to real capabilities (for
example, building tools, agents or services) rather than to proxies of
linguistic plausibility. In addition, large codebases and package
ecosystems induce a monotone notion of “library extension”: adding
verified modules and tests enlarges an agent’s effective action set
without corrupting previously certified behaviour, giving a concrete
mechanism for stable, cumulative self-improvement.

Many open directions follow from this coding-centric view. One is to
treat non-coding tasks as *compilation targets*: can reasoning, planning
and even social interaction batteries be reduced to
code-generation-and-execution problems so that they inherit the high-SNR
verification structure of the coding fiber? Another is to study the
geometry of software ecosystems themselves as a moduli space of
libraries and APIs, and to analyze GVU flows that navigate this space by
acquiring, composing and refactoring code. Finally, there is a safety
dimension: coding batteries expose sharp failure modes (security
vulnerabilities, unsafe system calls, resource misuse) that are both
easily verifiable and tightly coupled to real-world risk. Understanding
how self-improving agents move on this “code+security” subspace of the
moduli may be a key step toward characterizing AGI systems that are not
just powerful, but robust and controllable.
````
