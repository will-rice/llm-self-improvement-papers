###### Abstract

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) converts feedback into dense
token-level supervision on student-generated trajectories, complementing
reinforcement learning with sparse outcome rewards. Its self-teacher,
derived from the student’s current or exponentially averaged parameters
and conditioned on additional context, evolves alongside the student and
its rollout context distribution. The benefit of modifying this moving
target depends on how target–student probability mismatches translate
into updates. We propose _Self-Referenced On-Policy Self-Distillation
(SR-OPSD)_, which constructs a normalized geometric target from the
self-teacher and a frozen initial policy, then minimizes the forward
Rényi divergence from this target to the student. The interpolation
coefficient controls the self-teacher’s contribution, while the Rényi
order controls the power weighting of target-to-student probability
ratios in the gradient. For fixed contexts and target components, we
establish a conditional variational characterization and derive the
exact token-logit gradient, revealing how anchoring and projection
jointly shape the effective update target. Experiments across scientific
reasoning, tool use, mathematical reasoning, and code generation
demonstrate strong performance across multiple model families and
scales. Ablations further show that reference anchoring can improve or
degrade performance depending on the projection objective, supporting
the joint design of target construction and projection geometry.

## 1 Introduction

Post-training of large language models (LLMs) encompasses supervised
instruction tuning, preference optimization, and reinforcement learning
(Wei et al., 2022; Sanh et al., 2022; Ouyang et al., 2022; Rafailov et
al., 2023; He et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026). Among
reinforcement-learning methods, RLHF methods commonly optimize a learned
reward model using proximal policy optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al.,
2017; Ouyang et al., 2022), whereas recent reasoning systems
increasingly employ reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards
(RLVR), using algorithms such as Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) and its variants (Yu et al., 2025). Methods
such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023)
optimize preference data without explicit reward modeling. In terms of
reasoning, RLVR is particularly attractive because the correctness of
the outcome can often be assessed automatically through external checks,
unit tests, or rule-based verifiers. However, these methods assign a
scalar-valued reward to an entire response. Although this reward induces
policy-gradient updates over the generated tokens, it does not directly
identify which intermediate tokens or reasoning steps caused success or
failure, resulting in coarse and sparse credit assignment over long
sequences.

On-policy distillation (OPD) offers an alternative approach by utilizing
the dense token-level supervision on trajectories sampled from the
policy to be optimized (also known as the student policy) (Gu et al.,
2023; Agarwal et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2026). A
teacher policy evaluates student-generated prefixes, reducing the
state-distribution mismatch associated with offline distillation.
Existing on-policy distillation methods can be viewed through three
design choices including: the divergence objective, the source of
supervision, and the mechanism used to stabilize policy optimization.
These choices are often designed largely in isolation, which is
relatively benign in conventional offline distillation where both the
teacher and the training policy are fixed. This benefit, however,
generally requires continued access to an extra teacher policy.
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) instead uses two views of the same
evolving model: the policy we aim to optimize is conditioned on the
original prompt, whereas the so-called self-teacher policy is the same
policy conditioned on feedback, privileged information, or richer
contextual signals (Zhao et al., 2026a; Hübotter et al., 2026). OPSD can
therefore convert feedback, reflections, verified traces, or successful
responses into dense token-level supervision.

#### Motivation

We consider on-policy self-distillation with an evolving
feedback-conditioned self-teacher. The policy to be optimized
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ generates its own training contexts, while the
supervisory target (also known as the self-teacher policy)
$`\pi_{\bar{\theta}}`$ is updated from it. Both the supervisory target
and the rollout context distribution therefore change throughout
training. At each visited prefix, target construction determines the
probability discrepancies presented to the student, and the divergence
determines how these discrepancies translate into updates. This raises a
central design question:

_How should the supervisory target and the student’s response to it be
controlled jointly?_

The distinction between forward KL (FKL) (Zhao et al., 2026a) and
reverse KL (RKL) (Hübotter et al., 2026) illustrates the role of the
projection objective. In the binary illustration in Figure 1(a), the
target assigns a fixed positive probability to a token. As the student’s
probability of that token approaches zero, the FKL logit correction
approaches a nonzero plateau, whereas the RKL and JSD corrections
vanish. Forward Rényi divergence provides an order-dependent response:
for $`0<\rho<1`$, its correction decays as a power of the student
probability, more slowly than under RKL or JSD in this regime. The order
$`\rho`$ thus controls the response to tokens that the student strongly
underweights relative to its target. Reference-policy anchoring provides
a complementary control through target construction (Yang et al., 2026).
Incorporating a reference policy changes the target probabilities and
can alter both the direction and magnitude of the resulting correction,
as illustrated in Figure 1(b). Because reference anchoring changes the
probability ratios presented to the student policy, its effect depends
on how the divergence weights those ratios. The runs in Figure 1(c)
exhibit distinct patterns: RKL produces more duplicate responses, FKL
generates longer responses, and SR-OPSD combines higher accuracy with
concise outputs and low duplication.

We therefore propose _Self-Referenced On-Policy Self-Distillation
(SR-OPSD)_, which anchors the supervisory target to a frozen copy of the
student’s own initial policy and matches this target through forward
Rényi projection. Specifically, using the frozen initial policy as a
reference policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$, SR-OPSD constructs the
normalized geometric target
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}\propto\pi_{\bar{\theta}}^{\alpha}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}^{1-\alpha}`$
and minimizes $`D_{\rho}(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}\|\pi_{\theta})`$ on
student-generated prefixes. The coefficient $`\alpha\in[0,1]`$ controls
_where_ the target lies along the geometric interpolation between the
self-teacher and reference policies, while $`\rho\in(0,1)`$ controls
_how_ target-to-student probability ratios are weighted in the logit
update. Together, they control how reference information and evolving
self-teacher supervision shape student learning.

#### Main Contributions

Our contributions are threefold. (i) We propose SR-OPSD, which jointly
controls target construction and policy updates by coupling a normalized
geometric target, formed from the self-teacher and the frozen initial
student policy, with forward Rényi projection. (ii) At fixed
student-generated contexts, we establish a conditional variational
characterization and derive the exact token-logit gradient. The gradient
identifies the effective update target as a normalized geometric mixture
of the self-teacher, reference, and current student, making the roles of
$`\alpha`$ and $`\rho`$ in the update explicit. (iii) We demonstrate
competitive performance across scientific reasoning, tool use,
mathematical reasoning, and code generation, covering different feedback
sources, model families, and scales.

## 2 Background

#### Notation

We denote the policy to be optimized by $`\pi_{\theta}`$, the
self-teacher policy by $`\pi_{\bar{\theta}}`$, and the reference policy
by $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. Given a prompt $`x\sim\mathcal{D}`$ and an
on-policy response $`y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{L})`$, the student prefix at
position $`t`$ is $`h_{t}=(x,y_{<t})`$. We write $`h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}}`$
for the corresponding self-feedback or privileged context supplied to
the self-teacher and $`h_{t}^{\mathrm{ref}}`$ for the context supplied
to the reference policy. When a statement is token-level and the
contexts are fixed, we suppress the position index and write $`h`$,
$`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. Thus, $`h`$,
$`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ always denote three
possibly different fixed contexts. In this work,
$`h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}}=h_{t}^{\mathrm{ref}}=(x^{+},y_{<t})`$, where
$`x^{+}`$ augments $`x`$ with successful examples or feedback. All
token-level distributions are defined over the same next-token
vocabulary $`\mathcal{V}`$. For the theoretical derivations, all
compared distributions are normalized on a common support. The operator
$`\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]`$ denotes stop-gradient.

#### On-Policy Self-Distillation

Given a self-teacher policy $`\pi_{\bar{\theta}}`$, on-policy
self-distillation trains the student on its own sampled prefixes, for
example through the reverse-KL loss

```math
\textstyle\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\sim\mathcal{D}\\
y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x)\end{subarray}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{L}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{t})\,\middle\|\,\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}\right]\right)\right].
```

Here $`\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}`$ denotes the rollout policy used to
collect the current on-policy batch; it coincides with the current
student before the optimization step. Such a self-teacher policy is
often an exponential-moving-average of $`\pi_{\theta}`$ with additional
context information. The additional context $`h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}}`$ can
contain textual feedback, verified solutions, reflections, or successful
sibling responses that are unavailable in the ordinary student context
$`h_{t}`$. This construction also supports forward KL and JSD, evaluated
between conditional token distributions at the same visited prefixes.

#### Self-Teacher Policy and Its Updates

The self-teacher policy $`\pi_{\bar{\theta}}`$ may be a stopped copy, a
lagged snapshot, or an exponential-moving-average version of the policy
to be optimized $`\pi_{\theta}`$. In this work, we follow (Hübotter et
al., 2026) and use $`\beta\in[0,1)`$ as the exponential moving average
coefficient and adopt the convention
$`\bar{\theta}_{k+1}=\beta\bar{\theta}_{k}+(1-\beta)\theta_{k+1}.`$
Thus, larger $`\beta`$ produces a slower-moving self-teacher policy.
During each update, $`\bar{\theta}_{k}`$ and the resulting self-teacher
policy are held fixed.

#### Rényi divergence

For positive probability distributions $`p`$ and $`q`$, the Rényi
divergence of order $`0<\rho<1`$ is
$`D_{\rho}(p\|q)=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}p(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}`$.
We focus on this order range and define
$`D_{1}(p\|q)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)`$ by continuity (van Erven and
Harremoës, 2014). When $`p`$ is the target and $`q`$ the student, we
refer to $`D_{\rho}(p\|q)`$ as the forward projection. The two argument
orders satisfy $`D_{\rho}(p\|q)=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}D_{1-\rho}(q\|p)`$.
This identity relates the projection direction to the divergence order.

## 3 Method

### 3.1 Revisiting On-Policy Self-Distillation: Reference-Regularized On-Policy Self-Distillation

We now consider the following token-level formulation of the
reverse-KL-based on-policy self-distillation objective at fixed
contexts:

```math
\textstyle\max_{\theta}\;\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\sim\mathcal{D}\\
y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x)\end{subarray}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{L}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{t})\,\middle\|\,\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})\right]\right)\right].
```

At each token position, let $`(h,h_{\mathrm{sf}})`$ denote the student,
self-teacher contexts. During the inner update, these contexts and the
self-teacher policy are held fixed. Under regular conditions, the
following objective

```math
\textstyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)}\left[\alpha\log\frac{\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})}\right]-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right) \tag{1}
```

satisfies
$`\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)+\log Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})`$
where
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\propto\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]^{\alpha}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1-\alpha}`$.
See Appendix B.1 for details of our derivation. Consequently, for fixed
contexts and frozen target components, maximizing
$`\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}`$ with respect to $`\theta`$ is equivalent to
minimizing the reverse KL from the student to $`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$.
When $`\alpha=1`$, $`Z_{1}=1`$ and
$`\pi_{1}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\operatorname{sg}[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})]`$,
recovering the token-level reverse-KL-based on-policy self-distillation
objective. For $`0<\alpha<1`$, this formulation incorporates reference
regularization into on-policy self-distillation through the supervisory
target (Hübotter et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026). The geometric target
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$ interpolates between the self-teacher and
reference policies.

### 3.2 Self-Referenced On-policy Self-distillation with Rényi Projection on Student Rollouts

Figures 1(a) and 1(b) illustrate how projection choice and reference
anchoring shape the token-logit correction. We use the Rényi family to
parameterize the projection, first expressing the objectives against the
self-teacher and then present how the forward objective being connected
to the geometric target $`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$.

During the inner update, the sampled contexts and the teacher and
reference policies are held fixed. For $`0<\rho<1`$ and strictly
positive token probabilities, the reverse Rényi projection maximizes the
following objective with respect to $`\theta`$:

```math
\textstyle-\sum_{t}D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{t})\,\middle\|\,\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})\right]\right)=\sum_{t}\frac{1}{1-\rho}\log\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{t})}[(\frac{\operatorname{sg}\![\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})]}{\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h_{t})})^{1-\rho}].
```

The forward Rényi projection maximizes the corresponding objective

```math
\textstyle-\sum_{t}D_{\rho}\!\left(\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})\right]\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{t})\right)=\sum_{t}\frac{1}{1-\rho}\log\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{t})}[(\frac{\operatorname{sg}\![\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})]}{\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h_{t})})^{\rho}]. \tag{2}
```

Both objectives admit a student-expectation representation, with
respective powers $`1-\rho`$ and $`\rho`$ applied to the
teacher-to-student probability ratio. We adopt the forward
parameterization, in which $`\rho`$ directly controls this power.

SR-OPSD applies forward Rényi projection to the normalized geometric
target $`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$, combining reference anchoring through
$`\alpha`$ with projection control through $`\rho`$. To connect this
projection to the reference-regularized formulation above, we construct
a Rényi-aggregated token functional from the same teacher-to-reference
log-ratio. The following proposition shows that combining this
functional with a Rényi penalty relative to $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$
yields an objective equivalent to minimizing
$`D_{\rho}(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}\|\pi_{\theta})`$ at fixed contexts.

###### Proposition 3.1 (Token-Level Variational Characterization of SR-OPSD).

Fix $`h`$, $`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. Let
$`\alpha\in[0,1]`$ and $`\rho>0`$, $`\rho\neq 1`$, and assume that all
token probabilities are strictly positive. Define the token-level
pseudo-reward
$`r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=\alpha\log\frac{\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})}`$.
Define the Rényi-aggregated token functional
$`\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=-\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log\frac{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)}{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{\rho}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1-\rho}}`$.
Let
$`\mathcal{J}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})-D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)`$.
Then

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=-D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\right)-\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}\log Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}). \tag{3}
```

Therefore, for fixed contexts and frozen target components, maximizing
$`\mathcal{J}_{\alpha,\rho}`$ with respect to $`\theta`$ is equivalent
to minimizing the forward Rényi projection from
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})`$ to
$`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)`$.

See Appendix B.2 for the proof. In this variational formulation,
$`\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}`$ depends on the current student policy.
When $`\alpha=1`$, we have $`Z_{1}=1`$, and the objective reduces to
on-policy self-distillation with forward Rényi divergence, with the
self-teacher $`\pi_{\bar{\theta}}`$ as the sole target.

The proposed method, SR-OPSD, utilizes this formulation at each
student-generated position $`t`$, using the geometric target
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}},h_{t}^{\mathrm{ref}})\propto\operatorname{sg}[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})]^{\alpha}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{ref}})^{1-\alpha}`$.
We use a frozen copy of the initial policy as the reference policy,
$`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}:=\operatorname{sg}[\pi_{\theta_{0}}]`$, where
$`\theta_{0}`$ denotes the initial parameters. Since the reference comes
from the student itself, we refer to this anchoring scheme as
_self-referenced_. The corresponding token-level objective is:

```math
\displaystyle\max_{\theta} \displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\sim\mathcal{D}\\
y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid x)\end{subarray}}\sum_{t=1}^{L}\frac{1}{1-\rho}[\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\exp((1-\rho)\log\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h_{t}) \tag{4}
```

```math
\displaystyle+\rho\alpha\log\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{sf}})\right]+\rho(1-\alpha)\log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{t}^{\mathrm{ref}}))].
```

See Section 3.2 for the pseudo-code, where $`\mathrm{Update}(\cdot)`$
denotes an optimizer such as stochastic gradient descent or Adam. The
algorithm states the normalized, unclipped objective. See Appendix D.2
for more details. The coefficient $`\alpha`$ determines the
self-referenced target: $`\alpha=1`$ recovers the self-teacher, whereas
$`\alpha=0`$ recovers the reference policy. The order $`\rho`$ controls
the projection toward this target.

### 3.3 How Anchoring and Projection Determine the Update

To characterize how anchoring and projection jointly shape the student
update, we derive the exact token-logit gradient of the SR-OPSD
objective. The following proposition expresses this gradient as the
difference between the student distribution and an effective target that
combines $`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$ with the current student.

###### Proposition 3.2 (Logit Gradient of SR-OPSD).

Fix $`h`$, $`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$, and treat
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})`$ as
frozen during the student update. Let $`z_{\theta}(a\mid h)`$ denote the
student logit such that
$`\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)=\frac{\exp(z_{\theta}(a\mid h))}{\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(z_{\theta}(b\mid h))}`$.
For $`\rho>0`$, $`\rho\neq 1`$, define
$`\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(a\mid h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\propto\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}~\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}`$.
Then
$`\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)}D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\right)=\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)-\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(a\mid h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})`$.

For $`0<\rho<1`$, the effective target
$`\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}`$ is a normalized geometric
mixture of the self-teacher, reference, and current student policies,
with exponents $`\alpha\rho`$, $`(1-\alpha)\rho`$, and $`1-\rho`$,
respectively. Thus, $`\alpha`$ sets the balance between self-teacher
supervision and reference anchoring, while $`\rho`$ controls their
combined weight relative to the current student. Together, these
parameters determine the effective target toward which the token-logit
update moves the student. This expression links the two controls in
Figures 1(a) and 1(b): reference anchoring changes the target-to-student
probability ratios, and the Rényi order determines how these ratios
enter the token-logit correction.

## 4 Experiments

We evaluate the proposed method across various representative scenarios
that capture different feedback regimes and levels of supervision,
including: (i) Science Q&A and tool use benchmarks without rich
environment feedback; (ii) mathematical reasoning on challenging
problems with access to ground-truth solutions; and (iii) scaling
behavior on coding tasks with rich execution-based feedback; and (iv)
ablation study on the effectiveness of each component of the proposed
method.

### 4.1 Performance with Bootstrapping on Generated Responses as Feedback

#### Does self-reference consistently improve performance?

The effect of reference anchoring depends on the projection objective.
Self-reference improves JSD from 73.4 to 77.2, a gain of 3.8 percentage
points, but reduces forward KL from 79.1 to 76.3, a drop of 2.8 points.
These opposing effects show that adding a reference target does not
consistently improve performance, motivating consideration of how the
student is projected toward that target.

#### Does Rényi projection alone improve over forward KL?

Without self-reference, forward Rényi achieves 75.9 at $`\rho=0.70`$ and
77.7 at $`\rho=0.95`$, both below forward KL at 79.1. Adding
self-reference raises these scores to 82.7 and 81.2, respectively. In
particular, at $`\rho=0.70`$, self-reference alone and Rényi projection
alone each underperform the unanchored forward-KL baseline, whereas
their combination exceeds it by 3.6 percentage points. This reversal
supports jointly designing the supervisory target and projection
geometry.

#### How do $`\alpha`$ and $`\rho`$ interact?

The effect of changing $`\alpha`$ depends on $`\rho`$. Increasing
$`\alpha`$ from 0.50 to 0.90 improves Avg@16 from 78.8 to 82.7 at
$`\rho=0.70`$, but reduces it from 78.2 to 77.2 at $`\rho=0.90`$.
Sensitivity to $`\rho`$ also varies with $`\alpha`$: scores span only
78.2–78.8 at $`\alpha=0.50`$, compared with 77.2–82.7 at
$`\alpha=0.90`$. The best tested configuration is
$`(\alpha,\rho)=(0.90,0.70)`$, achieving 82.7, followed by
$`(0.70,0.70)`$ at 82.0. In summary, neither self-reference nor Rényi
projection alone consistently improves performance. See Figure 7 in
Appendix D.3 for discussions on responses lengths.

## 5 Related Work

#### On-Policy Distillation

On-policy distillation uses student-generated trajectories for training,
allowing the teacher policy to provide dense supervision on prefixes
visited by the student (Agarwal et al., 2024; Wen et al., 2023; Gu et
al., 2023; Lu and Lab, 2025; Jin et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026; Yang et
al., 2026; Stein et al., 2026; Oh et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2026). Gu et
al. (2023) optimizes sequence-level reverse KL through policy-gradient
estimators, while Agarwal et al. (2024) evaluates forward KL, reverse
KL, and JSD on student rollouts. Later work studies entropy-dependent
divergence selection, reference-policy constraints, and rollout mixing
to improve stability (Jin et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026). Yang et al.
(2026) further formulates external-teacher on-policy distillation as
KL-regularized policy optimization, where a teacher-to-reference
log-probability ratio defines the dense reward and controls
interpolation or extrapolation between the two policies. See (Song and
Zheng, 2026) for a detailed review.

#### On-Policy Self-Distillation

These methods replace the external teacher with a self-teacher policy
derived from the policy being optimized and conditioned on privileged or
auxiliary context available only during training. Existing work uses
ground-truth solutions, environment feedback, successful sibling
responses, or document context to construct the self-teacher (Zhao et
al., 2026a; Hübotter et al., 2026; Penaloza et al., 2026; Ye et al.,
2026; Sang et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026b; Wang et al., 2026).
Subsequent work controls the amount of additional information exposed to
the teacher, restricts distillation to selected spans, or gates
token-level supervision according to teacher reliability and uncertainty
(Han et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Ke et al.,
2026). Other studies analyze the effects of additional context
information, Top-$`K`$ approximation, and update schemes of self-teacher
policies (Zhu et al., 2026; Guo et al., 2026). Existing objectives
mainly use forward KL, reverse KL, or JSD for direct distribution
matching (Zhao et al., 2026a; Hübotter et al., 2026), while
preference-based self-distillation replaces direct matching with
reward-regularized preference optimization (Yu et al., 2026).

## 6 Conclusion

Our study shows that reference anchoring can improve or degrade
self-distillation accuracy depending on the projection objective. This
dependence highlights the need to consider target construction and the
student’s response to the target jointly. SR-OPSD brings these choices
into a single objective by combining a geometric target anchored to the
frozen initial policy with forward Rényi projection. At fixed
student-generated contexts, our variational characterization and exact
token-logit gradient reveal an effective update target that
geometrically combines the self-teacher, reference, and current student
policies. Experiments across scientific reasoning, tool use,
mathematical reasoning, and code generation demonstrate strong
performance.

Understanding how this interaction evolves throughout training remains
an open question. Our analysis conditions on fixed rollout contexts and
frozen target components, leaving the joint evolution of the student,
self-teacher, and rollout distribution beyond its scope. Future work
could extend the analysis to these coupled dynamics and adapt $`\alpha`$
and $`\rho`$ to teacher reliability or policy drift.

## Appendix B Theoretical Results and Proofs

### B.1 Proof of Token-Level Variational Characterization of On-policy Self-distillation

###### Proposition B.1 (Token-Level Variational Characterization of OPSD).

Fix $`h`$, $`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$, and let
$`\alpha\in[0,1]`$. Assume that, for every token $`a\in\mathcal{V}`$,
$`\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)>0,\qquad\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]>0,\qquad\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})>0`$.
Define
$`Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]^{\alpha}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1-\alpha}`$
and
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=\frac{\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]^{\alpha}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1-\alpha}}{Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})}`$.
Then the conditional token-level objective

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}):=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)}\left[\alpha\log\frac{\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})}\right]-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

satisfies
$`\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)+\log Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})`$.
Consequently, for fixed contexts and frozen target components,
maximizing $`\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}`$ with respect to $`\theta`$ is
equivalent to minimizing the reverse KL from the student to
$`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$. When $`\alpha=1`$, $`Z_{1}=1`$ and
$`\pi_{1}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\operatorname{sg}[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})]`$,
recovering the token-level objective.

###### Proof.

Fix $`h`$, $`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$, and abbreviate

```math
q(a):=\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h),\qquad p(a):=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right],\qquad r(a):=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}}).
```

The conditional objective expands as

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha} \displaystyle=\sum_{a}q(a)\left[\alpha\log\frac{p(a)}{r(a)}-\log\frac{q(a)}{r(a)}\right]
```

```math
\displaystyle=\sum_{a}q(a)\left[-\log q(a)+\alpha\log p(a)+(1-\alpha)\log r(a)\right].
```

By definition,

```math
\log\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\alpha\log p(a)+(1-\alpha)\log r(a)-\log Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}).
```

Substituting this identity yields

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha} \displaystyle=\sum_{a}q(a)\left[-\log q(a)+\log\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})+\log Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right]
```

```math
\displaystyle=-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)+\log Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}),
```

where the last step uses $`\sum_{a}q(a)=1`$. Since the contexts and
target components are fixed, $`Z_{\alpha}`$ is constant with respect to
the inner optimization variable $`\theta`$.

When $`\alpha=1`$,

```math
Z_{1}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\sum_{a}p(a)=1
```

and

```math
\pi_{1}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=p(a)=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right].
```

The objective therefore reduces to the reverse-KL OPSD objective. The
constancy statement is conditional on the fixed contexts: after
averaging over a context distribution that itself changes with the
policy, the expected normalizer need not be constant. ∎

### B.2 Proof of Proposition 3.1

###### Proof.

Fix $`h`$, $`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$. To simplify
the notation, define

```math
\displaystyle q(a) \displaystyle:=\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h),
```

```math
\displaystyle p(a) \displaystyle:=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right],
```

```math
\displaystyle r(a) \displaystyle:=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}}).
```

Because all token probabilities are strictly positive, all logarithms,
probability ratios, and real-valued powers appearing below are
well-defined.

Under these abbreviations, the normalizing constant is

```math
\displaystyle Z_{\alpha}:=Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}p(a)^{\alpha}r(a)^{1-\alpha}.
```

The normalized self-referenced target satisfies

```math
\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\frac{p(a)^{\alpha}r(a)^{1-\alpha}}{Z_{\alpha}}.
```

Multiplying both sides of above equation by $`Z_{\alpha}`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle p(a)^{\alpha}r(a)^{1-\alpha}=Z_{\alpha}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}). \tag{5}
```

We first simplify the numerator of $`\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}`$. By the
definition of the pseudo-reward $`r_{\alpha}`$,

```math
\displaystyle r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\alpha\log\frac{p(a)}{r(a)}.
```

Multiplying both sides by $`\rho`$ yields

```math
\displaystyle\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\rho\alpha\log\frac{p(a)}{r(a)}. \tag{6}
```

Exponentiating both sides of Equation 6 gives

```math
\displaystyle\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right) \displaystyle=\exp\!\left(\rho\alpha\log\frac{p(a)}{r(a)}\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p(a)}{r(a)}\right)^{\rho\alpha}
```

```math
\displaystyle=p(a)^{\rho\alpha}r(a)^{-\rho\alpha}. \tag{7}
```

Consider one summand in the numerator of $`\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}`$.
Using Equation 7, we obtain

```math
\displaystyle r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}p(a)^{\rho\alpha}r(a)^{-\rho\alpha}. \tag{8}
```

Combining the two powers of $`r(a)`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle r(a)^{\rho}r(a)^{-\rho\alpha} \displaystyle=r(a)^{\rho-\rho\alpha}
```

```math
\displaystyle=r(a)^{\rho(1-\alpha)}. \tag{9}
```

Substituting Equation 9 into Equation 8 yields

```math
\displaystyle r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=q(a)^{1-\rho}p(a)^{\rho\alpha}r(a)^{\rho(1-\alpha)}. \tag{10}
```

Therefore, Equation 10 becomes

```math
\displaystyle r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=q(a)^{1-\rho}\left[p(a)^{\alpha}r(a)^{1-\alpha}\right]^{\rho}. \tag{11}
```

Applying Equation 5 to Equation 11 gives

```math
\displaystyle r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=q(a)^{1-\rho}\left[Z_{\alpha}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right]^{\rho}
```

```math
\displaystyle=Z_{\alpha}^{\rho}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{12}
```

Summing both sides of Equation 12 over $`a\in\mathcal{V}`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}Z_{\alpha}^{\rho}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{13}
```

Because $`Z_{\alpha}`$ does not depend on the summation variable $`a`$,
it can be taken outside the sum:

```math
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=Z_{\alpha}^{\rho}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{14}
```

By the definition of Rényi divergence,

```math
\displaystyle D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,q\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{15}
```

Multiplying both sides of Equation 15 by $`\rho-1`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle(\rho-1)D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,q\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{16}
```

Exponentiating both sides of Equation 16 gives

```math
\displaystyle\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,q\right)\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{17}
```

Substituting Equation 17 into Equation 14 yields

```math
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}r(a)^{\rho}q(a)^{1-\rho}\exp\!\left(\rho r_{\alpha}(a;h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=Z_{\alpha}^{\rho}\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,q\right)\right). \tag{18}
```

We next simplify the denominator of $`\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}`$. By
the definition of Rényi divergence,

```math
\displaystyle D_{\rho}(q\|r)=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}q(a)^{\rho}r(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{19}
```

Multiplying both sides of Equation 19 by $`\rho-1`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle(\rho-1)D_{\rho}(q\|r)=\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}q(a)^{\rho}r(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{20}
```

Exponentiating both sides of Equation 20 gives

```math
\displaystyle\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}(q\|r)\right)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}q(a)^{\rho}r(a)^{1-\rho}. \tag{21}
```

Using Equations 18 and 21 in the definition of
$`\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})
```

```math
\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log\frac{Z_{\alpha}^{\rho}\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,q\right)\right)}{\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}(q\|r)\right)}. \tag{22}
```

We then obtain

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})
```

```math
\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho-1}\Bigg[\log\!\left(Z_{\alpha}^{\rho}\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,q\right)\right)\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle\hskip 136.5733pt-\log\!\left(\exp\!\left((\rho-1)D_{\rho}(q\|r)\right)\right)\Bigg]. \tag{23}
```

Expanding the logarithms and suppressing the fixed context arguments
gives

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta) \displaystyle=-\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}\log Z_{\alpha}-D_{\rho}(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}\|q)+D_{\rho}(q\|r).
```

Subtracting the reference penalty therefore yields

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta) \displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta)-D_{\rho}(q\|r)
```

```math
\displaystyle=-D_{\rho}(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}\|q)-\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}\log Z_{\alpha}.
```

For fixed $`h`$, $`h_{\mathrm{sf}}`$, and $`h_{\mathrm{ref}}`$, the
distributions $`p`$ and $`r`$ are frozen during the inner student
update. Thus,

```math
Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{V}}p(a)^{\alpha}r(a)^{1-\alpha}
```

is independent of $`\theta`$, so the normalizer term is constant.
Restoring $`q=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)`$, we obtain

```math
\argmax_{\theta}\,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha,\rho}(\theta)=\argmin_{\theta}\,D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\right).
```

This proves the stated conditional variational equivalence.

When $`\alpha=1`$, $`Z_{1}=\sum_{a}p(a)=1`$ and $`\pi_{1}^{\star}=p`$.
Hence,

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1,\rho}(\theta;h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}) \displaystyle=-D_{\rho}\!\left(\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\right).
```

∎

### B.3 Proof of Proposition 3.2

###### Proof.

By the definition of Rényi divergence,

```math
\displaystyle D_{\rho}\!\left(\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(\cdot\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\middle\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h)\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(b\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)^{1-\rho}. \tag{24}
```

Define

```math
\displaystyle S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}:=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(b\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)^{1-\rho}. \tag{25}
```

Then

```math
\displaystyle D_{\rho}=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\log S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}.
```

We first compute the derivative of the student probability with respect
to the student logit. By the softmax definition,

```math
\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)=\frac{\exp(z_{\theta}(b\mid h))}{\sum_{c\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(z_{\theta}(c\mid h))}.
```

Therefore,

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{1}\{a=b\}\exp(z_{\theta}(b\mid h))}{\sum_{c}\exp(z_{\theta}(c\mid h))}
```

```math
\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\exp(z_{\theta}(b\mid h))\exp(z_{\theta}(a\mid h))}{\left(\sum_{c}\exp(z_{\theta}(c\mid h))\right)^{2}}
```

```math
\displaystyle=\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)\left[\mathbf{1}\{a=b\}-\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)\right]. \tag{26}
```

Since $`\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}`$ is frozen during the student update, only
$`\pi_{\theta}`$ depends on $`z_{\theta}`$. Hence,

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(b\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\frac{\partial\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)^{1-\rho}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)}
```

```math
\displaystyle=(1-\rho)\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(b\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)^{-\rho}\frac{\partial\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)}.
```

Substituting Equation 26 gives

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=(1-\rho)\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(b\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)^{1-\rho}
```

```math
\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\mathbf{1}\{a=b\}-\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)\right].
```

Separating the two terms,

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=(1-\rho)\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}
```

```math
\displaystyle\quad-(1-\rho)\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)\sum_{b\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(b\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(b\mid h)^{1-\rho}
```

```math
\displaystyle=(1-\rho)\left[\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}-\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}\right]. \tag{27}
```

Next, differentiating the Rényi divergence gives

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial D_{\rho}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\frac{1}{S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}\frac{\partial S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)}.
```

Substituting Equation 27,

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial D_{\rho}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho-1}\left[\frac{\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}}{S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}-\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)\right].
```

Since

```math
\frac{1-\rho}{\rho-1}=-1,
```

we obtain

```math
\displaystyle\frac{\partial D_{\rho}}{\partial z_{\theta}(a\mid h)} \displaystyle=\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)-\frac{\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}}{S_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}}
```

```math
\displaystyle=\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)-\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(a\mid h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}}).
```

Finally, by definition,

```math
\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\frac{\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]^{\alpha}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1-\alpha}}{Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})}.
```

Raising both sides to the power $`\rho`$ gives

```math
\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}^{\star}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}=\frac{\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]^{\alpha\rho}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{(1-\alpha)\rho}}{Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{\rho}}.
```

Then, the common factor
$`Z_{\alpha}(h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{-\rho}`$ appears in both
the numerator and denominator and therefore cancels. Hence,

```math
\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(a\mid h,h_{\mathrm{sf}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\propto \displaystyle\operatorname{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\bar{\theta}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{sf}})\right]^{\alpha\rho}
```

```math
\displaystyle\times\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid h_{\mathrm{ref}})^{(1-\alpha)\rho}\pi_{\theta}(a\mid h)^{1-\rho}.
```

∎
