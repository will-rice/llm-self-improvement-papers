# LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning

Tao Zhang Affiliation: Microsoft Research Asia
Email: [zhangt@microsoft.com](mailto:)    Yuhang He ^(†)^(†)thanks:
Corresponding author. Affiliation: Microsoft Research Asia
Email: [yuhanghe@microsoft.com](mailto:)

###### Abstract

Existing methods enhancing an LLM’s long-context reasoning capability
often rely on data synthesis and follow-up LLM post-training. However,
the data synthesis strategy and the post-training recipes are
empirically driven and computationally intensive. In this work, we
propose a new framework, dubbed _LongCapHarness_, to instead harness the
underlying capabilities for long-context reasoning. The harnessing
process recursively revises and updates capability primitive
implementation, capability state and control logic through recursive
self-improvement on the development set. With neither data synthesis nor
LLM post-training, _LongCapHarness_ is applied to Llama3-8B and
transferred to Qwen2.5-3B. _LongCapHarness_ substantially outperforms
recent data-synthesis-based baselines on HELMET and RULER benchmarks;
its estimated development cost is less than 50% of the estimated
training costs of NExtLong and PolicyLong. We hope these results
motivate further research into harnessing the latent capabilities of
LLMs to solve tasks beyond their explicit training.

## 1 Introduction

Existing methods for enhancing a pretrained LLMs’ long-context reasoning
capabilities follow the data synthesis pipeline: synthesizing a large
long-context corpus, followed by post-training the LLM on the
synthesized data (e.g., NExtLong [Gao et al. (2025b)](#bib.bib28),
PolicyLong [Jia et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib15), LongFilter [Deng et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib12), EntropyLong [Jia et al. (2025)](#bib.bib13),
LADM [Chen et al. (2025)](#bib.bib19), Quest [Gao et al.
(2025a)](#bib.bib14), LiteLong [Jia et al. (2026a)](#bib.bib16)).
Despite the observed gains on long-context benchmarks such as
HELMET [Yen et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20) and LongBench-v2 [Bai et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib27), these methods disentangle data synthesis from the
follow-up LLM post-training, and the data synthesis strategies and
post-training recipes are empirically motivated and computationally
intensive. Therefore, the direction on data synthesis and LLM
post-training is costly and difficult to scale-up.

To build up such a framework, we leverage the latest harness engineering
technologies [Chen et al. (2026)](#bib.bib3); [Lee et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib4); [Lin et al. (2026)](#bib.bib5); [Lou et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib23); [Nie et al. (2026)](#bib.bib6) to design a
long-context capability-guided auto-harnessing framework. We argue that
the central challenge in long-context reasoning is not merely fitting a
long input within the available context window. Solving such a task
often requires identifying its task structure, localizing relevant
evidence, selectively retaining intermediate results, and finally
integrating them into a globally consistent answer. In a monolithic
end-to-end call, these operations and dependencies remain implicit,
making it hard for the model to coordinate them while processing the
full input at once. To address this challenge, we propose a capability
harness to support long-context tasks. Capability decomposition further
makes this latent task structure explicit by breaking the long-context
task into operations with defined inputs and outputs. The harness can
then preserve shared state across operations and supply each model call
only with the context it needs, decoupling total input length from the
context budget of any individual call.

In _LongCapHarness_, we leverage a harnessing agent to construct
reusable capability harnesses from the development set. The harness
agent studies the task input to identify the required capability, then
decomposes it into capability primitives. The harnessing process
recursively revises and updates the primitive implementation, capability
state and control logic by comparing the answer generated by running the
capability harness against the reference answer. The final capability
harness is frozen and saved to a library. At test time, an execution LLM
selects and runs the appropriate capability harness on the held-out test
set.

We evaluate _LongCapHarness_ on HELMET ([Yen et al., 2025](#bib.bib20)),
RULER ([Hsieh et al., 2024](#bib.bib18)), and LongBench-v2 ([Bai et al.,
2025](#bib.bib27)), using GPT-5.6-Sol ([OpenAI, 2026](#bib.bib11)) for
harness development and Llama3-8B ([Meta, 2024](#bib.bib26)) for
execution before transferring the harness to Qwen2.5-3B ([Qwen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib1)). On HELMET and RULER, it outperforms the corresponding
model-training baselines ([Gao et al., 2025b](#bib.bib28); [Jia et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib15)) and RRSI ([Xia et al., 2026](#bib.bib7)) with both
execution models. On LongBench-v2, Qwen2.5-3B with the harness surpasses
PolicyLong ([Jia et al., 2026b](#bib.bib15)) on the Medium subset but
trails it on the Long subset.

Estimated development API costs are lower than the estimated training
costs of NExtLong and PolicyLong, but higher than RRSI development
costs. At inference, _LongCapHarness_ uses substantially fewer output
tokens than RRSI; estimated costs are comparable with Llama but higher
with Qwen, where increased input-token use offsets the output savings.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1.  1.  We propose _LongCapHarness_, a novel framework for long-context
        reasoning tasks with neither long-context data synthesis nor LLM
        post-training.
2.  2.  We demonstrate the superiority of _LongCapHarness_ in terms of
        performance on benchmarks and cost effectiveness via extensive
        experiments.
3.  3.  We hope to motivate more research into harnessing the latent
        capabilities of LLMs to solve complex tasks that go beyond their
        explicit training.

## 2 Related Work

##### Data-Synthesis-based Long-Context Reasoning.

Most recent methods on improving long-context reasoning first synthesize
long-context data and then use it to post-train LLMs [Jia et al.
(2026b)](#bib.bib15); [Jia et al. (2025)](#bib.bib13); [Gao et al.
(2025b)](#bib.bib28); [Chen et al. (2025)](#bib.bib19); [Gao et al.
(2025a)](#bib.bib14). These data strategies are often developed
empirically and separately from the post-training recipes. This
separation makes it difficult to determine which data properties are
essential for long-context reasoning and how data preparation should be
aligned with post-training. For example, ProLong [Gao et al.
(2025c)](#bib.bib17) studies training recipes and data mixtures, while
NExtLong ([Gao et al., 2025b](#bib.bib28)) constructs long sequences
with hard-negative distractors. EntropyLong [Jia et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib13) verifies useful context through predictive
uncertainty, and PolicyLong [Jia et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib15) refreshes
data using the evolving model. _LongCapHarness_ instead proposes an
alternating direction with neither data synthesis nor LLM post-training.
Benefiting from its carefully designed capability harnessing framewor,
_LongCapHarness_ exhibits both performance superiority and cost
effectiveness compared with these data-synthesis-based methods.

##### Model–program computation and semantic operators.

Model–program systems combine structured computation with learned
semantic operations. SUQL ([Liu et al., 2024](#bib.bib24)) extends SQL
with free-text question answering and summarization, while LOTUS ([Patel
et al., 2025](#bib.bib25)) provides optimized semantic operators for
filtering, joining, aggregation, and ranking. _LongCapHarness_ uses task
capabilities to guide how computational inputs are recovered from long
documents and composed into executable workflows.

##### Agent and harness design.

Automated agent design improves model-based systems by searching and
refining external execution logic. ADAS ([Hu et al., 2024](#bib.bib21))
searches over code-defined agents using past designs and evaluations.
AFlow ([Zhang et al., 2024](#bib.bib22)) optimizes workflows through
Monte Carlo Tree Search and execution feedback, and AutoHarness ([Lou et
al., 2026](#bib.bib23)) synthesizes harnesses and policies from
game-environment feedback. _LongCapHarness_ adds explicit capability
contracts to guide the construction and refinement of representations,
operators, and model calls for long-context reasoning.

## 3 _LongCapHarness_ Introduction

![Refer to caption](2611.10463v1/longcapharness_vis.png)

Figure 2: _LongCapHarness_ uses recursive self-improvement (RSI) on the
development set to construct and refine reusable capability harnesses.
The resulting library is used at test time to assist an LLM execution on
long-context tasks. _LongCapHarness_ requires neither data synthesis nor
LLM post-training.

_LongCapHarness_ starts with a development set of task inputs and
corresponding reference answers. The harnessing agent (in our case, we
adopt GPT-5.6-Sol ([OpenAI,](#bib.bib2) )) studies the task, identifies
the required capability, and further decomposes it into capability
primitives (Sec. [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Capability in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")).
The harnessing agent recursively revises and updates the primitive
implementation, capability state and control logic in recursive
self-improvement (RSI) (Sec. [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Harnessing Process in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")).
After the harnessing procedure, we freeze all capability harnesses and
save them to the capability harness library. Each capability harness in
the library is associated with a brief text description showing the
capability summary, input and output information. During test-time task
execution, the capability harness library is selectively loaded and
reused to complete the task in the test
set (Sec. [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Test-Time Task Execution ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")).
The whole capability auto-harnessing pipeline is shown in
Fig. [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning").

### 3.1 Capability in _LongCapHarness_

A capability $`\mathcal{C}`$ in _LongCapHarness_ is defined as an
essential abstract transformation from the an input $`\mathcal{I}`$ to
an output $`\mathcal{O}`$,

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}),\quad\mathcal{I}\neq\mathcal{O}
``` |  | (1) |

![Refer to caption](2611.10463v1/cap_harness_illu_v2.png)

Figure 3: Capability Illustration: we use the Reranking Capability as an
example. It is divided into Relevance Judgement and Ordering capability
primitives with sequential control logic. The auto-harnessing on
development set with recursive self-improvement (RSI) implements the two
capability primitives, and finally returns the Reranking Capability
Harness which is saved in the capability library.

The capability in
Eqn. ([1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Capability in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning"))
abstracts the transformation and is independent of a particular Q&A
pair. Two Q&A pairs can share the same capability if they require the
same transformation. For each capability, *LongCapHarness* constructs
its corresponding capability harness: the peripheral concrete,
executable workflow that specifies how to perform that transformation.
We define the harness as follows,

|     |                                                             |     |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{A}\leftarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{Q})
       ```                                                          |     | (2) |

where $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}`$ implements the harness for
capability $`\mathcal{C}`$. It usually includes the code, prompt
templates, and execution rules needed to produce the answer. To
construct a capability harness, the harnessing agent decomposes the
capability $`\mathcal{C}`$ into multiple capability primitives. Each
primitive $`\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}`$ abstracts a sub-transform
and specifies its input $`I_{i}`$ and output $`O_{i}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{C}:=\{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}},\cdots,\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}\};\quad\mathcal{O}_{i}\leftarrow\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}(I_{i})
``` |  | (3) |

where $`i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}`$ and $`i\leq m`$. The input to a capability
primitive may include the original question, selected task records, or
outputs from earlier primitives; its output may be an intermediate
result or part of the final answer. The capability harnessing in
Eqn. ([2](#S3.E2 "In 3.1 Capability in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning"))
then becomes capability primitive harnessing and the control logic
$`\mathbfcal{F}`$ harnessing,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}\leftarrow\mathbfcal{F}(\{\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}}(I_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m})
``` |  | (4) |

We use the Reranking Capability, which has been extracted by
*LongCapHarness*, to illustrate our capability design in
Fig. [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.1 Capability in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning").
Based on the development set, the auto-harnessing process identifies the
Reranking Capability and decomposes it into Relevance Judgement
primitive and Ordering with sequential control logic. The
auto-harnessing implements the two capability primitives and recursively
improves their harness through recursive self-improvement. The resulting
Reranking Capability Harness is saved into capability library for
potential use on the held-out test set.

### 3.2 Harnessing Process in *LongCapHarness*

The harnessing agent takes each Q&A pair in the development set as
input. By studying the task description from the input question, the
harness agent identifies the task’s input and output and decides to
propose a new capability (see
Eqn. ([1](#S3.E1 "In 3.1 Capability in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")))
or leverage an existing capability to address the question. Once the
required capability is decided, the harnessing agent tries to decompose
it into capability primitives (see
Eqn. ([3](#S3.E3 "In 3.1 Capability in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")))
and identify corresponding the control logic governing those primitives.
The harnessing agent then implements each capability primitive so that
each primitive becomes an executable operation. Executing those
capability primitives with the governing control logic provides us with
capability harness execution generated answer. We compare it with the
reference answer to continue to revise and improve the capability
harness.

In *LongCapHarness*, we adopt recursive self-improvement (RSI [Xia et
al. (2026)](#bib.bib7); [Chen et al. (2026)](#bib.bib3); [Lee et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib4); [Lin et al. (2026)](#bib.bib5); [Lou et al.
(2026)](#bib.bib23); [Nie et al. (2026)](#bib.bib6)) to continually
improve the capability harness. The RSI loop iteratively revises the
primitive implementations, their control logic, and the capability state
to improve the capability harness.

In Primitive Implementation, the harnessing agent implements a
capability primitive via either code or an LLM call, depending on the
exact primitive as well as its input and output requirement. Code
implementation is usually adopted where the primitive execution rules
are explicit and without ambiguity, such as assignment, occurrences
counting and sorting operations; LLM implementation is suitable when the
capability primitive’s input and output can be represented by text. The
harnessing agent then just requires to specify the text to supply, a
prompt template and the response format.

In Control Logic, the harnessing agent writes the corresponding control
logic to combine the capability primitive implementations into a
capability harness. The control logic orchestrates the capability
primitive operations execution, manage the input/output in-between and
further decides the execution stop condition. The capability primitive
operations execution governed by control logic generates the capability
harness guided answer for the given input question. Comparing it with
the reference answer provides important signal to improve the capability
harness.

In Capability State, the reusable protocol within a capability is
treated as the capability state. It defines how each new input question
is converted into a format that is directly usable by capability
primitives, what intermediate primitive output needs to be retained to
derive the final answer. It is worth noting that capability state is
agnostic to the actual input from the development set. The per-input
runtime state such as the actual question and intermediate results for
one input is created fresh during each run and thus does not count as
capability state.

![Refer to caption](2611.10463v1/harness_pipeline_vis.png)

Figure 4: Harnessing Illustration: given the input question, the
harnessing agent recursively revise and update the primitive
implementation, control logic and capability state until the answer by
executing the capability harness is close enough to the reference
answer.

In summary, primitive implementations compute results, while control
logic schedules and combines those results. The capability state
provides the shared workspace that allows these components to exchange
outputs without losing the source identity. As shown in
Fig. [4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.2 Harnessing Process in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning"),
during the capability harnessing with RSI, the harnessing agent
recursively revises and updates primitive implementation, control logic
and capability state until the difference between the answer generated
by running the capability and the reference answer is small enough (the
stop condition is decided by the harnessing agent). The resulting
capability harness, including primitive implementation, control logic
and capability state, is frozen and saved to the capability library.
Each capability harness is associated with a text description
summarizing the capability, input and output information, this text
description is used during the test-time task execution to select the
appropriate capability harness to execute the task.

### 3.3 Test-Time Task Execution

With the capability harness library obtained in
Sec. [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Harnessing Process in LongCapHarness ‣ 3 LongCapHarness Introduction ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning"),
we execute the task in the held-out test set. Given the question from
one Q&A pair in the test set, the task execution LLM (we adopt
Llama3-8B ([Meta, 2024](#bib.bib26)) and Qwen2.5-3B ([Qwen et al.,
2024](#bib.bib1))) first selects the capability harness entry by
comparing the capability harness text description with the question.
After selection, the runtime loads the selected harness’s code, prompt
templates, and execution rules, and passes the question together with
its source text and task information as input to the workflow. The
harness is executed as a program, where its control logic initializes
fresh per-input state and schedules the capability primitives. For each
LLM-based primitive, the harness constructs a bounded prompt from that
primitive’s template, the question or relevant question fields, and the
passages or intermediate results needed for the operation. Code-based
primitives operate directly on the stored records and intermediate
results. The harness retains these results outside the LLM context and
supplies them to subsequent primitives until the workflow produces the
final answer.

We aim to answer four main questions: *Q1:* How well does
*LongCapHarness* perform, and what explains its strengths and
limitations? *Q2:* Does *LongCapHarness* generalize across different LLM
execution models? *Q3:* How does capability primitive RSI affect task
performance? *Q4:* How cost-effective is *LongCapHarness*?

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

##### Benchmarks.

We use HELMET [Yen et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20), RULER [Hsieh et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib18) and LongBench-v2 ([Bai et al., 2025](#bib.bib27)) to
evaluate *LongCapHarness* under different context lengths.

We construct the development set based on the principle of exposing
harness development to each evaluated task type while testing reuse on
unseen inputs. The resulting split contains 2,242 development examples
and 59,826 held-out test examples, with no overlap between the two sets.
Appendix [C](#A3 "Appendix C Development and Evaluation Data ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")
details the development set and test set for each benchmarks.

##### Models and baselines.

We select Llama3-8B [Meta (2024)](#bib.bib26) and Qwen2.5-3B [Qwen et
al. (2024)](#bib.bib1) as execution models for *LongCapHarness* and
RRSI [Xia et al. (2026)](#bib.bib7). We compare *LongCapHarness* and
RRSI results against the base models and their respective 128K
context-extension variants from NExtLong [Gao et al.
(2025b)](#bib.bib28) and PolicyLong [Jia et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib15). We
also include EntropyLong, using the Llama results from EntropyLong
paper [Jia et al. (2025)](#bib.bib13) and the Qwen results from the
PolicyLong paper [Jia et al. (2026b)](#bib.bib15), averaged across
8K–128K. During development, both *LongCapHarness* and RRSI use
GPT-5.6-Sol as the developer model and Llama3-8B for candidate
execution. In RRSI, GPT-5.6-Sol serves as the proposer, selector,
critic, and pruner.

##### Harnessed primitives.

LongCapHarness for HELMET and RULER uses keyword matching, lookup,
relation following, state tracking, counting, ordering, relevance
judgement, rule inference, classification, evidence selection, and
evidence integration.
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Harnessed Capabilities and Primitives ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")
details the capability decompositions, task mappings, and each
primitive’s specification, inputs, and outputs.

##### Metrics and aggregation.

We report task scores on a 0–100 scale (the accuracy rate $`\uparrow`$).
Metrics include substring exact match for RAG, exact match for ICL and
InfiniteBench Choice, NDCG@10 for reranking, and the designated recall
or QA metric for RULER. InfiniteBench QA and NarrativeQA use ROUGE-L F1.
Category scores average each task across input lengths, then average the
tasks within the category.

### 4.2 Main Results

#### 4.2.1 Comparison of the Three Approaches

Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")
compares *LongCapHarness* with base models, model-training methods, and
RRSI. *LongCapHarness* achieves the highest aggregate score among the
evaluated methods for both execution models. With Llama3-8B,
*LongCapHarness* outperforms the base model, NExtLong, and RRSI by
64.18, 9.17, and 39.65 points. With Qwen2.5-3B, it outperforms the base
model, PolicyLong, and RRSI by 16.82, 14.63, and 40.27 points. These
results demonstrate the effectiveness of *LongCapHarness* on the
evaluated long-context benchmarks.

RRSI, however, falls below NExtLong by 30.48 points with Llama and below
PolicyLong by 25.64 points with Qwen. Inspection of RRSI’s intermediate
development code shows repeated attempts to address context overflow
through token limits and character-based chunking. Within the available
development rounds, these revisions approach task solving primarily
through the physical organization of the input and organize operators
around it. In contrast, LongCapHarness starts from the logical
organization of task-solving operations. Q1 Strength: The capability-
decomposition requirements make these operations and their dependencies
explicit, guiding RSI to optimize over the task’s computational
structure.

Figure 5: Helmet and Ruler category scores across input lengths with
Llama3-8B and Qwen2.5-3B.

We next examine the task categories to understand where this approach
yields the gains and where its benefits remain limited.

The largest gain appears in Recall, where LongCapHarness achieves 100.00
with both execution models, outperforming RRSI by 71.81 points with
Llama and 60.89 points with Qwen, and NExtLong by 17.44 points.
Figure [5](#S4.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 4.2.1 Comparison of the Three Approaches ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")
shows that this performance persists across the evaluated 8K–128K input
lengths. The advantage extends to RULER, where LongCapHarness achieves
94.62 with Llama and 94.28 with Qwen, outperforming RRSI by 61.30 and
62.19 points and the corresponding context-extension models by 13.12 and
17.61 points. Q1 Strength: These gains stem from the fact that the
underlying primitives have explicit rules that code can execute across
longer inputs, without requiring the model to maintain the complete task
state in its context. For example, Recall uses keyword matching and
lookup, while most RULER tasks use relation following, state tracking,
and counting.

The improvements are not limited to tasks that code can solve directly.
On reranking, LongCapHarness outperforms RRSI by 17.49 points with Llama
and 35.13 points with Qwen; Llama also exceeds NExtLong by 8.20 points.
Q1 Strength: The harness combines model-based relevance judgement with
code-controlled ordering, leaving semantic judgements as a limiting
factor.
Section [4.2.3](#S4.SS2.SSS3 "4.2.3 Capability Primitive Evolution ‣ 4.2 Main Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ LongCapHarness: Capability-Guided Auto-Harnessing for Long-Context Reasoning")
examines the effects of different primitive implementations.

The performance gap is smaller and less consistent on ICL, RAG, and
LongQA. On ICL, LongCapHarness exceeds RRSI by 0.40 points with Llama
and falls below it by 2.63 points with Qwen. On RAG, it trails RRSI by
4.93 and 0.55 points, respectively. On LongQA, Llama improves over RRSI
by 5.19 points but remains 0.46 points below NExtLong, while Qwen falls
below RRSI by 1.10 points. These mixed gains are consistent with
continued reliance on model capabilities: inferring mappings from
demonstrations in ICL, and identifying and integrating evidence in RAG
and LongQA. Q1 Limitation: Harnesses organize these operations but do
not replace their semantic judgements.

#### 4.2.2 Cross-Model Transfer

Replacing Llama3-8B with Qwen2.5-3B decreases LongCapHarness’s Avg by
only 0.59 points, from 79.32 to 78.73. The developed harnesses are
reused with adaptations to the model, tokenizer, evidence budget, and
serving configuration.

Code-based primitives follow the same execution rules across models, as
illustrated by Recall remaining at 100.00. Semantic primitives rely on
the new model’s judgements, leading to task-level differences: reranking
improves by 16.61 points with Qwen, while LongQA decreases by 10.39
points. Q2 Answer: Cross-model generalization is strong for the
code-based components of capability harnesses, while semantic components
depend on the execution model’s capabilities.

#### 4.2.3 Capability Primitive Evolution

We examine the RSI trajectory of capability primitives by HELMET
Reranking. The target capability remains fixed, while its
relevance-judgement and ordering primitives evolve through three
implementations: BM25 scoring with numeric sorting (V0), independent LLM
scoring with numeric sorting (V1), and relative LLM comparison with
group ordering and merging (V2).

The four LLM-based runs are evaluated on the same 500 records, with 100
at each input length from 8K to 128K. Each implementation is fixed
before evaluation and processes the complete candidate set within the

The first evolution, from V0 to V1, changes the relevance-judgement
primitive from lexical matching to semantic scoring. The LLM assigns
each query–passage pair a relevance grade, while the ordering primitive
continues to sort the resulting scores in code. This change improves
performance at 8K–16K for both models, while V0 retains higher scores at
32K–128K. Across all lengths, V1 achieves 38.95 with Llama and 40.50
with Qwen, compared with V0’s 40.35. This step introduces semantic
interpretation into the relevance primitive, with benefits concentrated
at shorter input lengths.

The second evolution, from V1 to V2, changes both the relevance output
and its downstream composition. The LLM expresses relative preferences
among passages, and the ordering primitive uses these preferences to
order small groups and merge them into a complete ranking. With Qwen,
this evolution improves the mean by 15.78 points to 56.28 and achieves
the highest score at every input length. At 128K, the gain over V1
reaches 26.75 points. With Llama, the mean improves by 0.72 points to
39.67, with gains at 32K–128K; V1 retains higher scores at 8K–16K.

Q3 Answer: These results show that capability primitive RSI improves
task performance by jointly refining local judgements and their
composition, with gains depending on the execution model and input
length.

## 5 Conclusion

*LongCapHarness* decomposes long-context tasks into capability
primitives and uses RSI to build reusable harnesses without data
synthesis or model post-training. Transferred from Llama3-8B to
Qwen2.5-3B, it improves average HELMET and RULER scores over
model-training baselines; its estimated development cost is below the
estimated training costs for NExtLong and PolicyLong. These results
support harnessing latent LLM capabilities beyond explicit training.

## 6 Limitation

*LongCapHarness* still relies on a powerful harnessing agent
(GPT-5.6-Sol) to construct its capability harnesses. We observe a sharp
performance drop when weaker models such as Llama3-8B and Qwen2.5-3B
serve as their own harnessing agents. Enabling weaker models to develop
effective harnesses independently remains an open challenge.

## 7 Ethical Considerations

Our method constructs and refines capability harnesses for long-context
reasoning without additional LLM training. All benchmark datasets used
in this study are publicly available and contain no personal
information. No human subjects are involved, and all LLMs used are
publicly available models or accessible through public APIs. We do not
identify additional ethical concerns specific to the proposed method
within this experimental setting.

## Appendix A Harnessed Capabilities and Primitives

The following list summarizes the capability decompositions described
for our HELMET and RULER harnesses. Capabilities are grouped by the
transformation required to solve a task. Primitive names describe
transformations, not particular code or prompt implementations. Multiple
task families can share a decomposition.

- •
  Matching and retrieval. *Keyword matching* and *lookup* of matching
  records or values support HELMET Recall and RULER needle retrieval.
- •
  Relation and state tracking. *Relation following* across records and
  *state tracking* through updates support RULER variable tracking.
- •
  Frequency-based selection. *Counting* occurrences and *ordering* by
  frequency support RULER common/frequent word extraction.
- •
  Passage reranking. *Relevance judgement* and *ordering* candidates
  support HELMET Reranking.
- •
  Example-based classification. *Rule inference* from examples and
  *classification* into allowed labels support HELMET ICL.
- •
  Evidence-based answering. *Evidence selection* of relevant passages
  and *evidence integration* into an answer support HELMET RAG and
  LongQA, and RULER QA.

### A.1 Primitive Specifications and Interfaces

For each primitive, *Spec* states the intended transformation, while
*Input* and *Output* define its interface. These are abstract
requirements, not implementation-specific data formats or guarantees of
model accuracy.

- •
  Keyword matching. *Spec:* Locate occurrences satisfying the supplied
  lexical matching rules. *Input:* Keywords, text records, and matching
  rules. *Output:* Matching spans and their record identities.
- •
  Lookup. *Spec:* Retrieve records or values satisfying a search
  condition. *Input:* Search condition and records. *Output:* Matching
  records or values.
- •
  Relation following. *Spec:* Follow the specified relations from a
  starting entity across records to recover the requested entity or
  value. *Input:* Starting entity, relations to follow, and records.
  *Output:* Related entity or value.
- •
  State tracking. *Spec:* Apply events according to the supplied update
  rules and event order to determine the state at a requested time.
  *Input:* Initial state, events, update rules, and requested time.
  *Output:* State at the requested time.
- •
  Counting. *Spec:* Aggregate occurrences according to the supplied
  counting rules. *Input:* Records and counting rules. *Output:* A count
  or per-item counts.
- •
  Ordering. *Spec:* Arrange candidates according to the supplied scores,
  preferences, or ordering rules while preserving candidate identities.
  *Input:* Candidates and ordering rules, including any required scores
  or comparison outputs. *Output:* Ordered candidate IDs.
- •
  Relevance judgement. *Spec:* Assess how well a passage addresses the
  query, or compare the relevance of candidate passages. *Input:* Query
  and passage(s). *Output:* Relevance score or passage preference.
- •
  Rule inference. *Spec:* Infer a mapping compatible with the supplied
  input–output examples and any candidate-rule constraints. *Input:*
  Input–output examples and candidate rules, when supplied. *Output:*
  Inferred mapping or compatible rule(s).
- •
  Classification. *Spec:* Assign an input to an allowed label using the
  supplied examples or inferred mapping. *Input:* Input to classify,
  allowed labels, and examples or mapping. *Output:* Predicted label.
- •
  Evidence selection. *Spec:* Identify passages or spans relevant to
  answering the question while retaining their source identities.
  *Input:* Question, candidate passages, and source IDs. *Output:*
  Selected evidence with source IDs.
- •
  Evidence integration. *Spec:* Combine information from the supplied
  evidence to answer the question, preserving the relationships needed
  for the answer. *Input:* Question, selected passages or evidence, and
  source IDs. *Output:* Answer.

## Appendix B Cost Estimation Details

##### API-price estimates.

For development, we apply GPT-5.6 Sol Standard short-context rates of
\$4/\$0.40/\$20 per million ordinary-input/cache-read/output tokens for
requests with at most 272K input tokens ([OpenAI, 2026](#bib.bib11)).
For inference, we price Llama3-8B at \$0.02/\$0.04 per million
input/output tokens using DeepInfra’s Llama 3.1 8B Instruct quotation on
OpenRouter ([OpenRouter, 2026a](#bib.bib8)), and Qwen2.5-3B at
\$0.10/\$0.10 using the Qwen2.5 Coder 3B quotation on OpenRouter
([OpenRouter, 2026b](#bib.bib9)).

##### Training rental estimates.

At an H100 rental rate of \$3.85 per GPU-hour ([Nebius,
2026](#bib.bib10)), NExtLong’s reported 64 GPUs for 15 hours ([Gao et
al., 2025b](#bib.bib28)) cost an estimated \$3,696.00. Scaling by
approximate training FLOPs $`F\approx 6T(P+Lsd)`$, where $`T`$ is the
token budget, $`P`$ the parameter count, $`L`$ the layer count, $`s`$
the sequence length, and $`d`$ the hidden width, and assuming equal
effective throughput per GPU gives a PolicyLong estimate of \$1,858.82.
````
