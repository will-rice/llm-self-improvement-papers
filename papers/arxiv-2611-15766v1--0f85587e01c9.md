###### Abstract

Self-evolving visual agents keep what they learn as skills: key frames
with step notes on a task’s steps and their order. On-policy
self-distillation (OPSD) is now a popular way to train such skills into
a vision-language model that matches, on its own answers, a teacher that
also reads the written order. At deployment, frames often go missing or
show an unusual order, as when a person or a website reorders the steps.
Existing internalization methods keep any order they store in the
frame-reading weights: trained on full frames, the model guesses when
they are missing, and trained with frames withheld, it stores the order,
so memory can override what it sees. We propose PriorSplit, which fills
in missing frames from an order kept outside the model, with no training
beyond OPSD. It _counts which of two neighboring steps comes first in
training episodes_, and the model checks if both asked steps are in
view: if so, it _answers from the frames_, and otherwise the counts
answer. Extensive experiments on synthetic skills with five models and
on step-tagged order questions in new episodes of seen web, cooking and
egocentric tasks demonstrate that, with frames missing, PriorSplit
outperforms every compared method at OPSD’s training cost. With
Qwen3-VL-4B on synthetic skills, it also stays close to OPSD on unusual
orders, where methods that gain most by storing the order fall behind.

## 1 Introduction

Self-evolving agents turn past runs into reusable
skills \[[49](#bib.bib17)\] and increasingly internalize them, so a
skill helps after leaving the context window \[[23](#bib.bib2),
[61](#bib.bib13), [43](#bib.bib14)\]. The agent must then answer from
what it _remembers_ and what it _sees_. For a visual agent, a skill is a
keyframe sequence with step notes on how a task unfolds on a
website \[[9](#bib.bib23), [59](#bib.bib24)\] or in egocentric videos of
cooking and other tasks \[[27](#bib.bib43), [46](#bib.bib46)\].
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) \[[57](#bib.bib1)\] suits this: a
teacher given privileged information supervises the student’s own
outputs, so the student matches it without that information.

At test time, frames fail the student in two ways. They go missing when
context budgets truncate long skills, retrieval returns some of
them \[[54](#bib.bib57)\] or a mid-task agent has not seen later steps.
They contradict the usual order when a person reorders steps or a
website changes its flow. An internalized skill should decide per
question whether its usual order or the frames answer.

Figure 2: Two ways frames fail. PriorSplit lets the counts decide in (a)
and the frames in (b). Steps X, Y and U. (a) Frames missing: an
evidence-only reader guesses. (b) Frames contradict: a shared-weight
reader follows its stored order (prior bleeding).

As
Fig. [1](#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
and
Fig. [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
show, existing internalization methods fall into two groups, and neither
escapes a choice between guessing and trusting memory over the frames.
In the evidence-only group, such as our OPSD
baseline \[[57](#bib.bib1)\], the student’s frames explain the whole
training signal, so its weights need not store the order. On synthetic
skills with Qwen3-VL-4B, its students reach $`98.8\%`$ with all six
frames but $`45.8\%`$ on two options without them. In the shared-weight
group, the student answers from less context, so its weights absorb the
skill \[[35](#bib.bib3), [53](#bib.bib4), [23](#bib.bib2),
[47](#bib.bib5)\] and the stored order sits where frames are read. On
the same skills, students gaining more than ten short-side points (zero
or two of six frames shown) lose $`6.5`$ to $`14.5`$ points on conflict
items, whose frames contradict the usual order. The stored prior’s part
of this loss is prior bleeding
(Section [3](#S3 "3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
This raises an intriguing question: can a skill fill in missing frames
from its order without storing it in the frame-reading weights?

An ideal method should (i) answer from the usual order without decisive
frames, which evidence-only methods cannot, as their weights hold almost
no order. It should also (ii) keep that order from overriding frames
that decide the answer, which shared-weight students fail to do once
they store much order, as their loss does not ask to switch it off.

The answer is positive: PriorSplit keeps the order outside the
frame-reading weights. To meet (i), it _counts_ the privileged orders
the OPSD teacher already sees, and the counts answer when a named step
is out of view. To meet (ii), the OPSD reader, trained on full frame
windows, checks per question if both named steps are in view. If they
are, it _reads the frames_ alone, so the prior can override them only on
a check miss, as
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Switch and counts). ‣ 4.5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")(a)
quantifies. Both parts add no training beyond OPSD. This follows Occam’s
razor: counting the order takes no training, so it need not live in the
frame-reading weights. Like the parameter-free shortcut of
ResNet \[[12](#bib.bib59)\], what need not be learned bypasses the
weights, which only read the frames.

Our main contributions are as follows.

- •
  We revisit skill internalization with OPSD and reveal prior bleeding:
  an order stored in the frame-reading weights overrides frames that
  contradict it. Our log-odds model identifies this stored prior from
  frame-free scores.
- •
  We propose PriorSplit, which adds no training beyond OPSD: counted
  orders outside the reader answer when its check finds an asked step
  out of view. We derive this switch from a Bayes posterior with the
  counts as its prior, and an identity of its routes predicts the
  conflict loss.
- •
  PriorSplit leads every compared method with frames missing at OPSD’s
  training budget, on synthetic skills with five models, new episodes of
  seen web and cooking tasks and order questions of seen egocentric
  tasks.

## 2 Related Work

Skills for self-evolving agents. Agents distill experience into reusable
skills retrieved by context \[[49](#bib.bib17)\].
AtlasVA \[[44](#bib.bib48)\] uses a visual skill memory as shaping
rewards, and EgoProceVQA \[[18](#bib.bib50)\] asks key-step questions
about egocentric procedures. MMSkills \[[56](#bib.bib51)\] builds
state-card and keyframe skills from agent trajectories and checks them
live at inference. ContractSkill \[[24](#bib.bib12)\] turns web-agent
skills into explicit contracts that are verified and locally repaired
outside the model. Others internalize skills. SKILL0 \[[23](#bib.bib2)\]
removes the full skill from context over training,
Skill0.5 \[[61](#bib.bib13)\] internalizes general skills and keeps
task-specific ones in context, and several use a skill as privileged
teacher context in on-policy distillation \[[43](#bib.bib14),
[52](#bib.bib35), [48](#bib.bib36)\]. UCOB \[[40](#bib.bib41)\] picks
skill-conditioned or skill-free views as a local teacher, and
LatentSkill \[[55](#bib.bib42)\] maps textual skills to weights with a
hypernetwork. Chen _et al_. \[[7](#bib.bib18)\] find repeated experience
internalization can collapse. These methods store the skill in the
weights that read the context, at a cost we measure for ordered visual
skills.

On-policy and context distillation. Distillation \[[13](#bib.bib32)\]
with privileged information \[[41](#bib.bib38)\] lets a student learn
from a teacher that sees more. Context distillation \[[35](#bib.bib3)\]
drops the prompt from the student, on-policy distillation trains on the
student’s own samples \[[1](#bib.bib19)\], on-policy context
distillation \[[53](#bib.bib4)\] combines the two, and
OPSD \[[57](#bib.bib1)\] conditions the teacher on privileged reference
solutions. Self-distillation serves continual
learning \[[32](#bib.bib15)\] and is studied for
overconfidence \[[31](#bib.bib16)\], GATES \[[36](#bib.bib40)\] gates
distillation by tutor consensus, and Imagine-OPD \[[6](#bib.bib52)\]
gives the teacher zoomed views. Closest to us, NCA \[[47](#bib.bib5)\]
adds a consistency regularizer because an internalized student degrades
when the context returns, and cross-world
distillation \[[37](#bib.bib10)\] matches the teacher’s belief change
across two visual worlds differing in answer-critical evidence. Unlike
PriorSplit, they keep prior and evidence in shared weights.

Contrasting contexts and replacing priors. Context-aware
decoding \[[33](#bib.bib6), [42](#bib.bib29)\], prior-aware
decoding \[[10](#bib.bib34)\] and contextual
calibration \[[58](#bib.bib7)\] contrast outputs on full and reduced
inputs to remove prior beliefs, and knowledge-conflict studies ask when
context beats parametric knowledge \[[51](#bib.bib30),
[60](#bib.bib31)\]. Vision-language models default to the image, but a
few attention heads let memorized knowledge override
it \[[19](#bib.bib49)\]. PhD \[[22](#bib.bib60)\] tests whether
misleading text or counter-common-sense images pull answers away from
what the image shows. Our conflict items ask the same of step order.
Logit arithmetic with experts \[[21](#bib.bib25), [20](#bib.bib37)\]
mixes models at inference, bias-only branches \[[8](#bib.bib26),
[5](#bib.bib28), [26](#bib.bib27)\] join them in training, and
prior-shift correction replaces a classifier’s training
prior \[[28](#bib.bib8), [25](#bib.bib9)\]. Task graphs learned from
egocentric video give structured step priors \[[29](#bib.bib44)\].
Video-LLMs separate spatial and temporal tokens to curb action-scene
hallucination \[[2](#bib.bib55)\], and multi-image models soften the
causal mask between images or calibrate logits with attention to reduce
position bias \[[39](#bib.bib56), [50](#bib.bib58)\]. Selective
classification \[[11](#bib.bib47)\] abstains at low confidence, and
mixtures of experts \[[16](#bib.bib53), [30](#bib.bib54)\],
Octopus \[[38](#bib.bib61)\] and a long-video agent \[[54](#bib.bib57)\]
route by a trained gate, a trained decoding selector or a prompted
memory pick. PriorSplit trains no gate and routes each question by its
reader’s own check.

Subsequence OPSD (its student sees a random subsequence of the window),
its all-pair variants, skill-shrinking curricula \[[23](#bib.bib2)\] and
prior distillation (which distills the order into the reader) apply the
natural remedy. The student answers from less context, so its weights
store the order. This assumes the stored prior does not interfere with
the frames, which may fail when they contradict the usual order. In
Table [1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.2 Limitations of Shared-Weight Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"),
every such student gains on the short side (zero or two of six frames)
and loses on conflict items, interval questions whose order departs from
the canonical one, and the loss grows with the gain. Trained five times
longer, subsequence OPSD and prior distillation do the same and stay
below PriorSplit on both
(Table [S10](#A2.T10 "Table S10 ‣ B.7 Tag-free Mind2Web ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (What stored scores identify). ‣ 3.2 Limitations of Shared-Weight Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
separates the stored prior’s part, prior bleeding, from worse reading.

A reader scores a question on the canonical pair $`X`$ before $`Y`$ by
its canonical log-odds $`z(V)=\log p(X\mid q,V)-\log p(Y\mid q,V)`$,
with $`\delta`$ and $`\mathrm{vis}(V)`$ as in
Section [4.1](#S4.SS1 "4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents").

###### Theorem 1 (What stored scores identify).

For a reader with canonical log-odds
$`z(V)=e(V)+\pi(1-\rho\,\mathrm{vis}(V))`$, evidence term $`e`$, stored
prior $`\pi`$, switch quality $`\rho\in[0,1]`$ and
$`\kappa=(1-\rho)\pi`$, let $`Z_{F},Z_{N},Z_{C}`$ be $`z`$ on random
frame-free, visible non-conflict and conflict items of one $`\delta`$,
with $`P(z=0)=0`$ on each. If $`\mathbb{E}[e]=0`$ without frames and the
margin toward the gold has one law on both visible sets, (i)
$`\pi=\mathbb{E}Z_{F}`$, (ii) the conflict accuracy is
$`P(Z_{N}>2\kappa)`$, between $`P(Z_{N}>2\pi)`$ and $`P(Z_{N}>0)`$, and
(iii) with the prior off on visible items and no tie at $`e=0`$ it is
$`P(Z_{N}>\kappa)`$.

By part (i), OPSD stores at most $`0.38`$ nats of frame-free prior per
$`\delta`$ on four synthetic backbones. As
Fig. [4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.2 Limitations of Shared-Weight Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
shows, its frame-free log-odds are wide and symmetric with mean
$`-0.01`$ nats, against $`0.90`$ nats for prior distillation. By part
(ii), $`31`$ of the $`34`$ student cases there with a band over five
points wide are consistent with $`{\rho=0}`$, weights that never switch
the prior off. Training asks no more: with a canonical majority, the
cross-entropy on visible items is least with the prior not switched off
(Proposition [S9](#Thmsprop9 "Proposition S9 (The loss does not ask for a switch). ‣ A.7 Training loss and prior shift ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
_Once shared-weight students store much order in the frame-reading
weights, they lose conflict accuracy, and the loss grows with the
short-side gain._

### 3.3 Limitations of Two Simpler Fixes

Counting adjacent step pairs in the privileged orders gives an order
prior with no training, and two simple ways to combine it with the
reader each give up one side
(Table [4](#S5.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 5.4 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Added to the reader’s scores, the counts are outweighed by those scores
without decisive frames, so reader plus prior reaches $`54.7\%`$ on the
short side, near OPSD’s $`51.1\%`$. Written into the prompt, the counts
override the frames, and the base model then answers $`9.1\%`$ of
conflict items correctly. _PriorSplit therefore keeps the prior outside
the reader and combines the two only at inference, where a check of the
named steps decides which one answers._

## 4 Methodology

Figure 5: PriorSplit. The counted prior answers when the check finds a

### 4.1 Problem Formulation

As
Fig. [5](#S4.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
shows, PriorSplit keeps the prior out of the reader and trains nothing
beyond OPSD. A visual skill $`s`$ is a canonical sequence of units
$`u_{1},\dots,u_{L}`$, each a keyframe with a short step note. An
episode shows a window of $`K`$ units in a presented order $`S`$, the
canonical order under order noise of per-skill strength $`\delta`$. A
question $`q`$ names two canonically adjacent units $`X`$ and $`Y`$ and
asks which comes first in $`S`$. At test time the context $`V`$ is an
order-preserving subsequence of $`S`$ with $`m\geq 0`$ frames. We write
$`\mathrm{vis}(V)=1`$ when both named units are in $`V`$, and
$`a_{s}=P(X\text{ before }Y\text{ in }S)`$ for the canonical order $`X`$
before $`Y`$. A conflict item is a visible question with $`Y`$ first in
$`S`$.

###### Definition 1 (Oracle reference).

This reference answers by the presented order when both named units are
in view and by the prior otherwise:
$`P(y^{\ast}=X\mid V)=\mathbf{1}[X\text{ before }Y\text{ in }V]`$ if
$`\mathrm{vis}(V)=1`$, and $`a_{s}`$ otherwise.

Definition [1](#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1 (Oracle reference). ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
is a posterior. Let $`T(V)`$ record $`\mathrm{vis}(V)`$ and, when both
named units are in view, which of them comes first in $`V`$. Bayes’ rule
in log-odds splits the posterior into a frame term and the skill prior,

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\logit P(y^{\ast}{=}X\mid T)=\underbrace{\log\frac{P(T\mid y^{\ast}{=}X)}{P(T\mid y^{\ast}{=}Y)}}_{\Lambda(T)}+\logit a_{s}.
``` |  | (1) |

###### Proposition 1 (Bayes reader).

Let $`\mathrm{vis}(V)`$ be independent of the presented order of the
named pair. (i) $`\Lambda(T)`$ is $`+\infty`$ if $`X`$ comes first in
$`V`$, $`-\infty`$ if $`Y`$ comes first in $`V`$, and $`0`$ if
$`\mathrm{vis}(V)=0`$, so
Definition [1](#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1 (Oracle reference). ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
is the posterior of
Eq. ([1](#S4.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
(ii) With $`v=P(\mathrm{vis}(V)=1)`$, its most probable answer is right
with probability $`A^{\ast}=v+(1-v)\max(a_{s},1-a_{s})`$, and no rule
that uses the frames through $`T`$ is right more often.

The frame term is all or nothing, so the Bayes reader never averages
frames and prior: it switches between them. The condition is exact with
no frame and with full windows, and
Section [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
proves the general form.

### 4.3 Counted Order Prior

Eq. ([1](#S4.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"))
needs $`a_{s}`$, which the OPSD reader barely stores
(Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Limitations of Evidence-Only Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")),
so we estimate it from the privileged orders. Let $`n`$ episodes hold an
adjacent pair $`(X,Y)`$, with $`X`$ first in $`n_{XY}`$ of them. We
model these orders as independent given $`\theta=P(X\text{ first})`$ and
place a uniform prior on $`\theta`$. The posterior is
$`\mathrm{Beta}(n_{XY}+1,\,n-n_{XY}+1)`$, and its mean, the probability
that $`X`$ comes first in the next episode, is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{p}_{s}(X\text{ first})=\mathbb{E}[\theta\mid n_{XY},n]=\frac{n_{XY}+1}{n+2},
``` |  | (2) |

clipped to $`[0.02,0.98]`$, so rare pairs stay near $`1/2`$. Every
symmetric prior $`\mathrm{Beta}(c,c)`$ puts this mean on the side of the
larger count, and only this side reaches an answer
(Lemma [S2](#Thmsprop2 "Lemma S2 (Direction of the counted prior). ‣ A.1 Setting and two lemmas ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
The generator’s orders are independent but not identically distributed,
the case that
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Switch and counts). ‣ 4.5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")(b)
bounds. A frame-free adapter fitted to the counts changes short-side
accuracy by $`-0.7`$ points
(Table [S9](#A2.T9 "Table S9 ‣ B.7 Tag-free Mind2Web ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).

### 4.4 Check and Switch

The check asks $`E`$ once per named unit $`u`$ whether $`u`$ is shown in
the same context (wordings in
Table [S3](#A2.T3 "Table S3 ‣ B.3 PriorSplit ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Its log-odds $`\ell_{u}`$ is the log ratio of summed yes and no
probabilities at the first answer token, and
$`g=\mathbf{1}[\ell_{X}>0]\,\mathbf{1}[\ell_{Y}>0]`$. No visibility
label trains the check, and its threshold is zero everywhere without
tuning. On the real settings, development data chose only its wording,
frame rendering, tag list and number of next-step check questions
(Section [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 PriorSplit ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
PriorSplit answers by

|     |                                                     |     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       s(y)=g\log p_{E}(y\mid q,V)+(1-g)\log\bar{p}_{s}(y)
       ```                                                  |     | (3) |

for each option $`y`$, with ties to the option named first. For
exchangeable orders,
Eq. ([1](#S4.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"))
given the counts holds with the unclipped count of
Eq. ([2](#S4.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 4.3 Counted Order Prior ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"))
in place of $`a_{s}`$
(Section [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Under
Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Bayes reader). ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")’s
condition,
Eq. ([3](#S4.E3 "Equation 3 ‣ 4.4 Check and Switch ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"))
is this posterior with $`g`$ for $`\mathrm{vis}(V)`$ and the reader’s
answer for the unbounded frame term $`\Lambda`$. For next-step
questions, $`\bar{p}_{s}`$ counts which option follows the named step,
whose presented successor is the gold, and $`g=1`$ when the check finds
the named step and another shown step after it
(Section [B.5](#A2.SS5 "B.5 Mind2Web skills and other backbones ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
The check adds at most two reader passes. With the shared prompt encoded
once they cost the time of $`1.0`$ answer passes per synthetic question
and $`0.3`$ on 32-frame windows
(Section [B.3](#A2.SS3 "B.3 PriorSplit ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
A probe can replace this check on order questions with Qwen3-VL-4B
readers: per seed, an L2-regularized logistic regression on the frozen
reader’s final hidden state at the check prompt, fitted on training
episodes and labeled by the shown steps. Development data choose
regularization and threshold.

### 4.5 Theoretical Analysis

###### Theorem 2 (Switch and counts).

(a) Let the check miss a share $`\alpha`$ of some visible questions and
pass a share $`\beta`$ of some out of view, with $`A_{E}`$ and
$`A_{\bar{p}}`$ the accuracies of the reader and of the prior on the
misrouted ones. Then PriorSplit is below the reader by exactly
$`\alpha(A_{E}-A_{\bar{p}})\leq\alpha`$ on the first and below the prior
by $`\beta(A_{\bar{p}}-A_{E})`$ on the second. (b) Let the $`n_{p}`$
counted orders of a pair $`p`$ be independent given their windows and
the test item, with mean canonical probability
$`\bar{q}_{p}>\frac{1}{2}`$. On a frame-free item of $`p`$, canonical
with probability $`a_{s}\geq\frac{1}{2}`$ and named by a fair coin, the
prior and PriorSplit with a correct check are right with probability at
most $`a_{s}`$ and at least
$`a_{s}-(2a_{s}-1)\exp\big(-2n_{p}(\bar{q}_{p}-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}\big)`$.

###### Corollary 1 (Regret to the oracle reference).

On a set of questions with visible share $`v`$, let $`R_{\mathrm{vis}}`$
be the reader’s accuracy on the visible ones. Let $`P_{\mathrm{inv}}`$
and $`a^{\ast}`$ be the accuracies of the counted prior and of the
oracle reference on the others, and $`L_{\alpha}`$, $`L_{\beta}`$ the
two losses of
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Switch and counts). ‣ 4.5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")(a).
The accuracies $`A`$ of PriorSplit and $`A^{\ast}`$ of the oracle
reference satisfy

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle A^{\ast}-A={}`$ | $`\displaystyle\underbrace{v(1-R_{\mathrm{vis}})}_{\text{reading}}+\underbrace{vL_{\alpha}+(1-v)L_{\beta}}_{\text{check}}`$ |  | (4) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\underbrace{(1-v)(a^{\ast}-P_{\mathrm{inv}})}_{\text{counting}}.`$ |  |  |

With a correct check, under the conditions of
Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (Bayes reader). ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
and of
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Switch and counts). ‣ 4.5 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")(b),
the expected regret on a pair is at most
$`v(1-R_{\mathrm{vis}})+(1-v)(2a_{s}-1)\exp\big(-2n_{p}(\bar{q}_{p}-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}\big)`$.

Each part of PriorSplit owns one term, and the counting bound decays
exponentially in $`n_{p}`$. A correct check keeps the prior off every
visible item, the case $`\rho=1`$
(Proposition [S3](#Thmsprop3 "Proposition S3 (Frontier). ‣ A.2 Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Conflict accuracy is the worst visible accuracy of a shared-weight
reader favoring the usual order
(Proposition [S10](#Thmsprop10 "Proposition S10 (Prior shift on visible items). ‣ A.7 Training loss and prior shift ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
On development data, part (a) predicts the test conflict loss within its
interval on $`5`$ of $`5`$ sets
(Table [S1](#A1.T1 "Table S1 ‣ A.8 Numerical checks ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Proposition [S6](#Thmsprop6 "Proposition S6 (Tolerable check errors). ‣ A.4 Theorem (a) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
gives the check errors below which PriorSplit beats both parts. Proofs
are in
Section [A](#A1 "Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents").

## 6 Conclusion

An internalized skill must answer when frames are missing or contradict
its usual order. On synthetic skills with Qwen3-VL-4B, OPSD stores
almost no order, and every tested subsequence variant stores it in the
same weights and loses conflict accuracy. PriorSplit *counts* privileged
orders outside the reader and *reads* the frames when its check finds
the steps. With frames missing, it leads every compared method at OPSD’s
training budget on synthetic skills, seen web and cooking tasks and seen
egocentric order questions, and it loses conflict accuracy only on check
misses.

### A.1 Setting and two lemmas

Generator. An episode of skill $`s`$ draws a window length $`K`$
(uniform on $`\{4,\dots,8\}`$ for training and the held-out library,
fixed on each test cell), a window start uniform over the canonical

A question names a canonically adjacent pair, $`X`$ before $`Y`$ in the
canonical order, and $`x`$ is the option named first, a fair coin under
the generator. The gold $`y^{\ast}`$ comes first in the presented order
$`S`$, and a conflict item is a visible item with $`y^{\ast}=Y`$.

Readers and PriorSplit. A reader answers $`X`$ if its canonical log-odds
$`z(V)`$ is positive, $`Y`$ if it is negative and $`x`$ if $`z=0`$.
PriorSplit uses the counts $`n_{XY}`$ and $`n`$ of

###### Lemma S1 (Order noise).

Let $`p_{j}`$ be the probability that the canonical pair at window
offset $`j`$ is presented swapped. Then $`p_{0}=\delta`$ and
$`p_{j}=\delta(1-p_{j-1})`$, so $`0\leq p_{j}\leq\delta`$ for every
$`\delta\in[0,1]`$ and the canonical probability $`a_{j}=1-p_{j}`$ is at
least $`1-\delta`$. For $`\delta\leq\tfrac{1}{2}`$,
$`a_{j}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}`$, with equality only for
$`\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}`$ and $`j=0`$. For $`\delta<1`$ the $`p_{j}`$
converge to $`\delta/(1+\delta)`$.

*Proof.* Swaps do not overlap and move every unit by at most one place,
so the pair at offset $`j`$ is swapped exactly when the swap at position
$`j`$ fires. That swap is tried only if position $`j`$ was not consumed
by a swap at $`j-1`$, and then fires with probability $`\delta`$, which
gives the recursion. Since $`1-p_{j-1}\in[0,1]`$,
$`0\leq p_{j}\leq\delta`$. For $`\delta\leq\tfrac{1}{2}`$ this gives
$`a_{j}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}`$. Equality needs $`\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}`$ and
$`p_{j}=\delta`$, that is $`p_{j-1}=0`$ for $`j\geq 1`$, which fails
because $`p_{j-1}\geq\delta(1-\delta)>0`$. The map
$`p\mapsto\delta(1-p)`$ is a contraction with factor $`\delta<1`$ and
fixed point $`\delta/(1+\delta)`$. ∎

The pair $`(u_{1},u_{2})`$ sits only at offset $`0`$, so at
$`\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}`$ its canonical probability is $`\tfrac{1}{2}`$,
and results that need a strict majority hold on the order noise grid for
every other pair.

###### Lemma S2 (Direction of the counted prior).

$`\bar{p}_{s}(X\text{ first})>\tfrac{1}{2}`$ if and only if
$`n_{XY}>n/2`$, and $`\bar{p}_{s}(X\text{ first})=\tfrac{1}{2}`$ if and
only if $`n_{XY}=n/2`$, including $`n=0`$. The same holds for the
posterior mean $`(n_{XY}+c)/(n+2c)`$ under every symmetric prior
$`\mathrm{Beta}(c,c)`$ with $`c>0`$. The switch uses only the argmax of
$`\log\bar{p}_{s}`$, so neither the prior strength $`c`$ nor the
clipping changes an answer of PriorSplit.

*Proof.* For every $`c>0`$, $`(k+c)/(n+2c)>\tfrac{1}{2}`$ if and only if
$`2k+2c>n+2c`$, that is $`k>n/2`$, and equality likewise.
Eq. ([2](#S4.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 4.3 Counted Order Prior ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"))
is the case $`c=1`$. Clipping to $`[0.02,0.98]`$ is nondecreasing and
fixes $`\tfrac{1}{2}`$. ∎

### A.2 Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (What stored scores identify). ‣ 3.2 Limitations of Shared-Weight Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")

Family. Fix an evidence term $`e(V)`$, any real function of the context
and the question, and a prior shape $`\psi_{q}`$, a real function of the
question such as $`\logit\bar{p}_{s}`$ or a constant. The shared-weight
family $`R(\lambda,\rho)`$ has
$`z(V)=e(V)+\lambda\psi_{q}(1-\rho\,\mathrm{vis}(V))`$ with
$`\lambda\geq 0`$ and $`\rho\in[0,1]`$, and $`\mu=(1-\rho)\lambda`$ is
the part of the prior strength that reaches visible items. Let $`F`$,
$`C`$ and $`N`$ be sets of frame-free items, visible conflict items and
visible non-conflict items, with margin toward the gold $`M=e`$ on $`N`$
and $`M=-e`$ on $`C`$. Items with $`\psi_{q}=e_{q}=0`$ tie for every
$`\lambda`$ and are left out. On every other item a tie, $`z=0`$, occurs
at one value of $`\lambda`$ or $`\mu`$ at most, the accuracy statements
below hold at every value without a tie, and $`\mathbb{E}A_{F}`$,
$`A_{C}`$ and $`A_{N}`$ denote the displayed sums at every value.

1.  (A1.1)
    Given its question, the presented order of a frame-free item is
    independent of $`e`$, and its gold is canonical with probability
    $`a_{q}`$ under the law that drew the items. At $`m=0`$, $`e`$ is a
    function of the question, so this holds for any law of the presented
    order.
2.  (A1.2)
    $`\psi_{q}>0`$ and $`a_{q}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}`$ on every item
    considered.
3.  (A1.3)
    Symmetric reading: for each $`\delta`$, the margin has the same law
    on the visible conflict and on the visible non-conflict items of
    that $`\delta`$.
4.  (A1.4)
    Normalization: $`\mathbb{E}[e\mid F,\delta]=0`$, so the stored
    strength is the mean frame-free log-odds and any canonical bias of
    frame-free evidence is stored prior.

The fair naming coin makes this bias zero for a reader whose frame-free
evidence depends on the naming order and the words but not on which
option is canonical.

###### Lemma S5 (Routes).

Split a set of questions with any number of options into the routes A
(visible, $`g=1`$), B (visible, $`g=0`$), C (not visible, $`g=0`$) and D
(not visible, $`g=1`$). Let $`v`$ be the visible share, $`\alpha`$ the
share of B among visible questions (miss rate), $`\beta`$ the share of D
among the others (false-yes rate), and $`R_{k}`$ and $`P_{k}`$ the
accuracies of the reader alone and of the counted prior alone on route
$`k`$. Then PriorSplit has accuracy
$`v[(1-\alpha)R_{A}+\alpha P_{B}]+(1-v)[(1-\beta)P_{C}+\beta R_{D}]`$.
On any set of visible questions with miss rate $`\alpha^{c}`$ it is
below the reader alone by $`\alpha^{c}(R_{B}-P_{B})\leq\alpha^{c}`$,
with $`R_{B}`$ and $`P_{B}`$ taken on the missed questions of that set.
For two options and a prior that is strictly canonical on every pair,
$`n_{XY}>n/2`$ in
Eq. ([2](#S4.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 4.3 Counted Order Prior ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")),
this loss is $`\alpha^{c}R_{B}`$ on visible conflict items and
$`-\alpha^{c}(1-R_{B})\leq 0`$ on the other visible items, so those
cannot lose.

*Proof.* On routes A and D PriorSplit outputs the reader’s answer and on
B and C the prior’s, so its accuracy on route $`k`$ is $`R_{k}`$ or
$`P_{k}`$, and the total is the sum weighted by the route shares
$`v(1-\alpha)`$, $`v\alpha`$, $`(1-v)(1-\beta)`$ and $`(1-v)\beta`$. On
a set of visible questions only A and B occur, where the reader alone
has accuracy $`(1-\alpha^{c})R_{A}+\alpha^{c}R_{B}`$ and PriorSplit
$`(1-\alpha^{c})R_{A}+\alpha^{c}P_{B}`$, a difference of
$`\alpha^{c}(R_{B}-P_{B})\leq\alpha^{c}`$ since $`R_{B}\leq 1`$ and
$`P_{B}\geq 0`$. A strictly canonical two-option prior answers $`X`$ on
every pair, wrong on every conflict item and right on every visible
non-conflict item, so $`P_{B}`$ is $`0`$ on the first and $`1`$ on the
second, which gives the two losses. ∎

In

Proposition [S7](#Thmsprop7 "Proposition S7 (Counted prior). ‣ A.5 Theorem (b) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")(a).
Since $`\bar{q}_{p}\geq 1-\delta_{s}`$, the bound tends to $`0`$ as
$`n_{p}`$ grows for every $`\delta_{s}<\tfrac{1}{2}`$. ∎

### A.7 Training loss and prior shift

Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (What stored scores identify). ‣ 3.2 Limitations of Shared-Weight Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
measures how far the prior stays on in view. Three results add why
training leaves it on and what conflict accuracy measures.

###### Proposition S8 (Full windows leave the prior unconstrained).

Let a loss be
$`\mathcal{L}(w)=\mathbb{E}_{(q,V)\sim\mathcal{D}}\,\ell\big(p_{w}(\cdot\mid q,V),q,V\big)`$
for weights $`w`$ and a training law $`\mathcal{D}`$ with
$`\mathrm{vis}(V)=1`$ almost surely, as for a student that sees the full
window. Then $`\mathcal{L}(w)=\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime})`$ whenever
$`p_{w}`$ and $`p_{w^{\prime}}`$ agree almost surely under
$`\mathcal{D}`$, whatever their log-odds on contexts with
$`\mathrm{vis}(V)=0`$. Given such a $`V`$ the gold is the named unit
that comes first in $`V`$
(Section [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Proposition ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")),
so a target that follows the presented order carries no information on
the order probability of the pair beyond $`V`$.

*Proof.* $`\mathcal{L}`$ integrates under $`\mathcal{D}`$ a function of
the values of $`p_{w}`$ on contexts drawn from $`\mathcal{D}`$. The gold
is a function of $`V`$ and hence independent of every other variable
given $`V`$. ∎

The loss of OPSD has this form: its student sees the full window and
matches the teacher on its own samples
(Section [B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2 Training ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")),
and on a full window the teacher’s privileged order is a function of
$`V`$. The proposition leaves the frame-free prior
$`\pi=\mathbb{E}Z_{F}`$ of
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (What stored scores identify). ‣ 3.2 Limitations of Shared-Weight Internalization ‣ 3 Revisiting Skill Internalization ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
to the base model and the optimizer, and OPSD keeps at most $`0.38`$
nats of it per $`\delta`$
(Section [A.8](#A1.SS8 "A.8 Numerical checks ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).

###### Proposition S9 (The loss does not ask for a switch).

On visible items let a student have canonical log-odds $`z=e+\kappa`$
with a fixed evidence term $`e`$ and a leaked prior
$`\kappa\in\mathbb{R}`$. Let the gold be canonical on a share
$`a\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1)`$ of them, and let $`M`$ be the margin toward the
gold, $`e`$ on non-conflict and $`-e`$ on conflict items. The
cross-entropy to the gold on visible items is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\kappa)`$ | $`\displaystyle=a\,\mathbb{E}_{N}\log\big(1+\exp(-M-\kappa)\big)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+(1-a)\,\mathbb{E}_{C}\log\big(1+\exp(\kappa-M)\big),`$ |  |

with expectations over the non-conflict and the conflict items, assumed
finite. (a) $`\mathcal{L}`$ is strictly convex with one minimizer. Under
(A1.3),
$`\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(0)=-(2a-1)\,\mathbb{E}\,\sigma(-M)\leq 0`$ with
$`\sigma`$ the logistic function, so the minimizer is nonnegative, and
positive if $`a>\tfrac{1}{2}`$. (b) If $`e`$ is a calibrated
log-likelihood ratio,
$`P(\text{gold canonical}\mid e)=\sigma(e+\logit a)`$, then
$`\kappa=\logit a`$ minimizes $`\mathcal{L}`$, also among all functions
of $`e`$. With $`a>\tfrac{1}{2}`$ and the frame-free prior at the same
value $`\pi=\logit a`$ this is $`\rho=0`$. (c) With $`\kappa>0`$ a
conflict item of margin $`M>0`$ pays at most $`(\exp\kappa-1)\exp(-M)`$
more cross-entropy than with $`\kappa=0`$, and its answer is wrong if
$`M<\kappa`$ and right if $`M>\kappa`$.

*Proof.* (a)
$`\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(\kappa)=-a\,\mathbb{E}_{N}\,\sigma(-(M+\kappa))+(1-a)\,\mathbb{E}_{C}\,\sigma(-(M-\kappa))`$,
and $`\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}(\kappa)>0`$ since $`\sigma^{\prime}>0`$
and $`a`$ and $`1-a`$ are positive. Both terms are nonnegative, the
first grows without bound as $`\kappa\to-\infty`$ and the second as
$`\kappa\to+\infty`$, so $`\mathcal{L}`$ has one minimizer. By (A1.3)
the two expectations agree at $`\kappa=0`$, which gives
$`\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(0)`$, negative if $`a>\tfrac{1}{2}`$. The
minimizer is thus not below $`0`$, and not at $`0`$ if
$`\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(0)<0`$. (b) The cross-entropy is a proper score,
so given $`e`$ it is minimized by the true log-odds $`e+\logit a`$,
which lie in the family at $`\kappa=\logit a`$. (c) The difference is
$`\log\frac{1+\exp(\kappa-M)}{1+\exp(-M)}\leq\log\big(1+(\exp\kappa-1)\exp(-M)\big)\leq(\exp\kappa-1)\exp(-M)`$.
A conflict item has $`z=\kappa-M`$ and is right if $`z<0`$ and wrong if
$`z>0`$. ∎

Reading. The proposition stands for training with a target close to the
gold, as the OPSD teacher sees the privileged order. A student’s
evidence $`e`$ is finite where the frame term $`\Lambda`$ of
Eq. ([1](#S4.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 4.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"))
is not, so the slope of (a) is not zero for $`a>\tfrac{1}{2}`$. A
student also trained on contexts with $`\mathrm{vis}(V)=0`$, as in
subsequence OPSD, the SFT variants and prior distillation, is driven
there toward the log-odds of the order probability. By (a) the loss on
visible contexts is least with that prior not switched off, and by (b)
with all of it on for a calibrated reader. By (c) the loss charges a
conflict item of margin $`M`$ at most $`(\exp\kappa-1)\exp(-M)`$ while
its answer flips once $`0<M<\kappa`$, the items that
$`\mathcal{C}_{\rho}`$ of
Proposition [S3](#Thmsprop3 "Proposition S3 (Frontier). ‣ A.2 Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
counts. Bleeding is thus a mismatch between an average proper score and
conflict accuracy and not a failure to optimize, in line with
Table [S10](#A2.T10 "Table S10 ‣ B.7 Tag-free Mind2Web ‣ Appendix B Implementation details ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents"),
where five times the steps do not restore conflict accuracy. On full
windows alone the slope of (a) vanishes as reading saturates and
Proposition [S8](#Thmsprop8 "Proposition S8 (Full windows leave the prior unconstrained). ‣ A.7 Training loss and prior shift ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
leaves the frame-free log-odds free, in line with the small frame-free
prior of OPSD.

###### Proposition S10 (Prior shift on visible items).

Let a share $`\eta`$ of the visible test items be conflict items,
whatever the share in training. (a) Without ties, a reader of the family
$`R(\lambda,\rho)`$ with constant shape,
$`\kappa=(1-\rho)\lambda\psi\geq 0`$ on visible items and (A1.3) has
visible accuracy $`A(\eta)=(1-\eta)P(M>-\kappa)+\eta P(M>\kappa)`$. It
is nonincreasing in $`\eta`$ with slope $`-P(-\kappa<M\leq\kappa)`$, its
minimum over $`\eta\in[0,1]`$ is the conflict accuracy $`P(M>\kappa)`$,
and at $`\eta=1`$ it is the bleeding $`P(0<M\leq\kappa)`$ below the same
reader with $`\kappa=0`$. (b) For two options and a strictly canonical
prior, PriorSplit minus its reader alone is
$`(1-\eta)\,\alpha_{N}(1-R_{B}^{N})-\eta\,\alpha_{C}R_{B}^{C}`$ on
visible items, with $`\alpha_{N}`$ and $`\alpha_{C}`$ the miss shares on
non-conflict and conflict items and $`R_{B}^{N}`$, $`R_{B}^{C}`$ the
reader’s accuracies on the missed ones (route B of
Lemma [S5](#Thmsprop5 "Lemma S5 (Routes). ‣ A.4 Theorem (a) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")).
Its absolute value is at most $`\max(\alpha_{N},\alpha_{C})`$ at every
$`\eta`$, and $`0`$ with a correct check.

*Proof.* (a) By
Proposition [S3](#Thmsprop3 "Proposition S3 (Frontier). ‣ A.2 Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
a non-conflict item is right if and only if $`M>-\kappa`$ and a conflict
item if and only if $`M>\kappa`$, with one law of $`M`$ on both by
(A1.3). The difference of the two probabilities is the slope, and
$`P(M>0)-P(M>\kappa)`$ is the bleeding of
Proposition [S4](#Thmsprop4 "Proposition S4 (What stored scores identify, full form). ‣ A.2 Theorem ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents").
(b)
Lemma [S5](#Thmsprop5 "Lemma S5 (Routes). ‣ A.4 Theorem (a) ‣ Appendix A Proofs ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
gives the loss $`\alpha_{C}R_{B}^{C}`$ on conflict items and the gain
$`\alpha_{N}(1-R_{B}^{N})`$ on the others, two terms of opposite sign. ∎

By (a) the conflict accuracy of a shared-weight reader is its worst
visible accuracy over the share of conflict items, and a reader with the
prior off in view is flat in that share. This is the sense of criterion
(ii) of
Section [1](#S1 "1 Introduction ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")
when a person or a website departs from the usual order more often than
in training. By (b) PriorSplit stays within its miss shares of its
reader at every share. Its prior is a table, so a shift of the order
itself is met by new counts, as on held-out skills and after a drift
(Section [C.3](#A3.SS3 "C.3 Held-out skills and training budget ‣ Appendix C Further results ‣ Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents")),
in the manner of prior-shift correction \[[28](#bib.bib8),
[25](#bib.bib9)\].

### B.2 Training

Trained methods start from Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct with LoRA rank $`64`$,
alpha $`128`$ and dropout $`0.05`$. Training uses $`200`$ steps of $`8`$
prompts from one episode stream, or one pass over the training pool on
HoloAssist ($`616`$ steps). It clips gradients at $`0.1`$ but not the
OPSD divergence, draws one sample per prompt at temperature $`1.0`$ and
top $`p=0.95`$ with at most $`32`$ new tokens, and keeps the last
checkpoint. Development data set the shared learning rate to
$`5\times 10^{-5}`$ against OPSD’s default $`5\times 10^{-6}`$. The OPSD
teacher has its adapter disabled and receives the full window and
privileged order text. The student sees the full window and matches the
teacher on its own samples with OPSD’s forward KL
divergence \[[57](#bib.bib1)\]. OPCD diverges at the shared rate and
takes $`5\times 10^{-6}`$ from $`\{10^{-6},5\times 10^{-6}\}`$ by best
mean short and interval development accuracy. The same criterion chooses
$`5\times 10^{-5}`$ for CW-OPD from its prescribed $`2\times 10^{-6}`$
and the shared rate.

### B.3 PriorSplit

1: base model $`\theta_{0}`$, consumed episodes $`\mathcal{D}`$,

3: $`n\leftarrow`$ number of episodes of $`s`$ in $`\mathcal{D}`$ that

4: $`n_{XY}\leftarrow`$ number of those with $`X`$ before $`Y`$

5:
$`\bar{p}_{s}(X\text{ first})\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}\big((n_{XY}+1)/(n+2)\big)`$

6: for each $`u\in\{X,Y\}`$ do

7:
  $`\ell_{u}\leftarrow\log p_{E}(\text{yes}\mid u,V)-\log p_{E}(\text{no}\mid u,V)`$

8: end for

9: $`g\leftarrow\mathbf{1}[\ell_{X}>0]\,\mathbf{1}[\ell_{Y}>0]`$

10: if $`g=1`$ then

11:   return $`\argmax_{y}\log p_{E}(y\mid q,V)`$

12: else

13:   return $`\argmax_{y}\log\bar{p}_{s}(y)`$

14: end if

### B.4 Baselines

Every trained method runs in our trainer with the OPSD loss of the
official code and shares with OPSD the teacher, the episodes, the

### B.5 Mind2Web skills and other backbones
````
