###### Abstract

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) achieve remarkable success on diverse
multimodal understanding and generation tasks, yet they still struggle
to effectively leverage language-based chain-of-thought reasoning for
image generation. As a result, existing reasoning-augmented UMMs often
fail to inherit the test-time scaling and fine-grained controllability
observed in large language models. We attribute this failure to a
fundamental modality mismatch: reasoning is performed in a discrete
token space, whereas image generation operates in a continuous space. To
bridge this gap, we introduce a Latent Reasoning framework in Continuous
space (LARC), which alleviates the token-level bottleneck by reasoning
over continuous hidden states. Specifically, the training framework
consists of two stages: (i) curriculum supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and
(ii) information-gain reinforcement learning (RL). First, the curriculum
SFT stage gradually converts token-level reasoning into latent
reasoning. Subsequently, the information-gain RL stage self-evolves
latent reasoning without ground truth supervision. Experiments across
diverse text-to-image generation benchmarks show that LARC consistently
improves generation quality, prompt following, and compositional
fidelity. Notably, LARC achieves state-of-the-art performance on GenEval
with a 7%p gain over BAGEL and demonstrates clear test-time scaling
behavior, outperforming token-based generation under both parallel and
sequential test-time scaling strategies.

## 1 Introduction

Recent advances in generative models have reshaped visual content
creation, achieving high quality and fine-grained controllability in
text-to-image synthesis, image editing, and instruction-following
generation \[[49](#bib.bib49), [46](#bib.bib50), [50](#bib.bib51),
[47](#bib.bib54), [57](#bib.bib55)\]. However, generation-only models
still struggle with complex semantics that require language-level
planning, such as attribute binding \[[2](#bib.bib61), [13](#bib.bib63),
[48](#bib.bib64)\] or world modeling \[[18](#bib.bib60),
[19](#bib.bib65)\]. Within this landscape, unified multimodal models
(UMMs) \[[66](#bib.bib24), [68](#bib.bib22), [43](#bib.bib27),
[64](#bib.bib23), [6](#bib.bib21)\], have emerged as a compelling
paradigm which integrates an image-generation module into large language
models (LLMs) that contain rich textual semantics.

Despite their success, prior UMMs fail to scale at test time: unlike in
LLMs, increasing the reasoning budget does not yield the smooth
performance gains observed on LLM benchmarks
(Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")).
We argue that this failure stems from a fundamental _modality mismatch_:
reasoning is performed in a _discrete_ token space, whereas image
generation operates in a _continuous_ latent space. Since discrete
chains struggle to encode the dense and spatially-grounded information
that image synthesis demands \[[35](#bib.bib69), [11](#bib.bib70)\],
reasoning collapses into surface-level prompt manipulation, which is
precisely the narrow regime in which existing methods provide gains.
This observation motivates our central claim: to extend the benefits of
reasoning _beyond_ this regime, reasoning itself must operate in a
continuous space.

To this end, we introduce LARC (Latent Reasoning in Continuous space), a
framework that mitigates the modality gap by aligning text and image
reasoning in a continuous latent space. Building on
Coconut \[[20](#bib.bib9)\], which performs latent reasoning in LLMs
without explicit tokens, we extend the idea to multimodal generation.
While we introduce reinforcement learning (RL) on the latent reasoning
system, RL training faces two challenges: (i) Coconut-style reasoning is
deterministic, whereas policy-gradient methods require stochastic
rollouts; and (ii) latent reasoning traces lack an explicit evaluator,
making it difficult to assess whether they are useful for generation. We
address both challenges with a two-stage recipe. Stage 1 employs
curriculum supervised fine-tuning that progressively replaces
token-level reasoning with continuous hidden states, thereby injecting
controlled stochasticity and restoring the rollout diversity required
for policy gradients. Then, Stage 2 applies reinforcement learning
driven by an _information-gain reward_, which quantifies how much each
latent step improves the predictive distribution over the target image,
together with a _collapse-prevention reward_ that preserves trajectory
diversity. Together, these constitute a self-evolving loop that requires
neither ground-truth chains nor external evaluators.

In experiments, we instantiate LARC on BAGEL \[[6](#bib.bib21)\] with 1M
interleaved samples drawn from FLUX-Reason \[[12](#bib.bib10)\],
WEAVE \[[10](#bib.bib11)\], and ScaleEdit \[[3](#bib.bib12)\]. On three
text-to-image benchmarks—GenEval \[[16](#bib.bib13)\],
DPG-Bench \[[22](#bib.bib14)\], and OneIG-Bench \[[1](#bib.bib15)\]—LARC
surpasses both the base UMMs and recent reasoning-augmented variants by
substantial margins, e.g., $`+7.6`$ absolute points on OneIG-Bench over
Uni-CoT \[[43](#bib.bib27)\]. More importantly, to our knowledge, LARC
is the first reasoning-augmented UMM that exhibits clear test-time
scaling: under budget forcing, doubling the latent reasoning compute
yields monotonic gains across all three benchmarks.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

- •
  We identify a discrete–continuous _modality mismatch_ between
  reasoning and generation as the root cause of the limited gains and
  absent test-time scaling in current reasoning-augmented UMMs, and
  argue that reasoning must operate in a continuous space.
- •
  We propose LARC, the first framework to perform reasoning in the same
  continuous latent space as image generation within a unified
  multimodal model, enabling test-time scaling in UMMs.
- •
  We design a self-evolving training recipe that (i) extends
  Coconut-style latent reasoning with controlled stochasticity to make
  it amenable to RL, and (ii) introduces an _information-gain_ reward
  coupled with a _collapse-prevention_ reward, eliminating the need for
  ground-truth latent chains or an external critic.

### 2.2 Interleaved Latent Reasoning

To enable continuous-space reasoning compatible with policy-gradient
optimization, we formulate the reasoning trajectory as an alternating
sequence of text segments and continuous latent steps, both generated by
the UMM backbone. Formally, given an input context
$`\mathbf{c}=(c_{1}\cdots c_{T})`$ consisting of a text prompt and
optionally a source image, the model produces a reasoning trajectory
followed by a target image $`\mathbf{x}`$. Following
Coconut \[[20](#bib.bib9)\] and subsequent latent reasoning
work \[[25](#bib.bib1)\], the model alternates between two modes during
trajectory generation: the _language mode_ and the _latent mode_. In
language mode, the model emits text tokens through stochastic sampling,
exposing the trajectory to the gradient signal of policy optimization.
In the latent mode, the model emits a continuous reasoning step that
bypasses text decoding, preserving the rich semantic content of internal
representations.

Specifically, the UMM backbone first processes the input embedding
$`E_{T}=[e(c_{1}),\cdots,e(c_{T})]`$ to produce the last hidden state
$`H_{T}=\text{Transformer}(E_{T})`$, where $`e`$ is the token embedding
function. Once the model produces the special token $`<`$latent$`>`$ at
position $`t`$, the mode turns latent and the embedding of the next
position is taken to be $`h_{t}`$ the hidden state at position $`t`$ in
the last transformer layer. The mode returns to language after the fixed
steps, at which point the trajectory ends and the image module is
invoked. Concretely, with $`E_{t}`$ denoting the input embedding
sequence up to position $`t`$ and $`h_{t}=H_{t}[t,:]`$ the corresponding
hidden state, the next-position embeddings is

|                                                          |                           |                                                                     |     |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|                                                          | $`\displaystyle E_{t+1}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[E_{t};h_{t}]&\text{if latent mode,}\\ |
| [E_{t};e(x_{t+1})]&\text{if language mode,}\end{cases}`$ |                           |
|                                                          | $`\displaystyle H_{t+1}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\text{Transformer}(E_{t+1}).`$                      |     |

At inference, the mode alternation proceeds until the model emits the
\<eos\> token, with each \<latent\> token marking the transition from a
language-mode span to the next latent-mode step. After termination, the
full trajectory of hidden states $`\mathbf{h}(\tau)`$ is read by the
image module through cross-attention to produce the target image
$`\mathbf{x}`$. The resulting reasoning trajectory naturally takes the
form

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       \tau=(s_{1},l_{1},s_{2},l_{2},\cdots,s_{N},l_{N}),
       ```                                                 |     |

where $`s_{n}`$ denotes the $`n`$-th language-mode span and $`l_{n}`$
denotes the $`n`$-th latent-mode hidden state, each consisting of
multiple tokens. Here, the trajectory length $`N`$ and the length of
each language-mode span $`|s_{n}|`$ are determined by when the model
issues \<eos\> and \<latent\>, respectively, rather than fixed by an
external schedule.

### 2.3 Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning with Curriculum Learning

As observed in prior work \[[20](#bib.bib9)\], introducing all $`N`$
latent steps from the beginning of training is unstable, since the
latent stream lacks any direct supervision while the model has yet to
learn the basic reasoning format. To progressively guide the model
toward reasoning in latent space, we perform supervised fine-tuning
(SFT) under a curriculum that gradually converts token-level reasoning
into latent reasoning. We therefore decompose this stage into a
curriculum that activates latent steps progressively, allowing each
newly introduced latent step to build upon the trained prefix.

Data construction. For SFT with a curriculum, we collect 1M interleaved
chain-of-thought (CoT) samples, providing the input context
$`\mathbf{c}`$, a step-by-step reasoning chain, and the target image
$`\mathbf{x}`$. We process each reasoning chain by inserting the special
token $`<`$latent$`>`$ at its step boundaries, marking the positions
where latent steps are introduced.

Curriculum learning. To stabilize training, we adopt a curriculum
learning that exposes the model to deeper interleaving over
phase \[[20](#bib.bib9)\]. We first suppress all latent steps and train
on the text-only trajectory $`\tau^{(0)}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{N})`$,
establishing the reasoning format under standard next-token prediction.
Subsequently, in phase $`k`$ for $`k=1,\cdots,N-1`$, we activate only
the first $`k`$ latent steps, ensuring that each newly introduced latent
is built on top of the stable prefix. The final phase activates all
latent steps, with the learning rate reduced at each phase transition.

Training loss. We optimize the model $`\pi_{\theta}`$ with a objective
that combines a standard next-token prediction loss
$`\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}(\cdot)`$ over text segments and a
flow-matching loss $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}(\cdot)`$ on the target
image,

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}^{(k)}=-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\pi_{\theta}\left(s_{n}|\mathbf{c},s_{<n},l_{<\min(n,k+1)}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}\left(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c},s_{1:N},l_{1:k}\right),
``` |  |

where $`s_{<n}`$ and $`l_{<\min(n,k+1)}`$ denote the text segments and
the active latent steps preceding $`s_{n}`$ at phase $`k`$,
respectively. Note that we propagate the gradient through the latent
steps without explicit supervision target. This implicit supervision
allows the latent steps to encode useful representations automatically,
imposing no external bias.

### 2.4 Stage 2: Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning

Building on the latent reasoning capability established in Stage 1, we
further refine the latent of reasoning trace through reinforcement
learning (RL), guiding the model toward latent thoughts that are
demonstrably useful for image generation. While supervised fine-tuning
(SFT) effectively guides the model to produce interleaved trajectories,
it remains fundamentally limited at refining the latent as it depends
highly on the data quality, even the reasoning chain does not have
ground-truth latent annotations. In this stage, we therefore adapt
self-rewarding RL with an intrinsic reward derived from the UMM itself,
yielding a fully self-evolving framework without any external critic.

Information-Gain reward. Motivated by prior work \[[24](#bib.bib31)\],
we define the reward as the Information Gain that a reasoning trace
provides to a frozen reference model, measuring how much the latent
reasoning improves the prediction of the target image. Concretely,
letting $`\pi_{\text{ref}}`$ denote the model after Stage 1 and marginal
density of its image module $`p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}|\cdot)`$,
the reward for a sampled trajectory $`\tau`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{\text{IG}}(\tau;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\triangleq\log p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\left(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c},h(\tau)\right)-\log p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\left(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}\right),
``` |  | (1) |

where $`p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\left(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c},h(\tau)\right)`$
is the predictive density when the trace is included and
$`p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\left(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}\right)`$ is the
reasoning-free baseline of the same model.
Equation [1](#S2.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 2.4 Stage 2: Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ 2 LARC: Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")
assigns reward exactly when the trace raises likelihood of the target
image in reference model, and zero reward when the trace carries no
information beyond the context $`c`$.

Specifically, the Information-Gain reward $`r_{\text{RG}}`$ is the
per-sample form of conditional mutual information, with
$`\mathbb{E}[r_{\text{IG}}]=I_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\left(\mathbf{x};h(\tau)|\mathbf{c}\right)\geq 0`$
under the joint distribution. Maximizing $`r_{\text{IG}}`$ is therefore
equivalent to maximizing the mutual information between the latent
reasoning trace and the target image, with the bare context $`c`$
playing the role of the marginal baseline. However, since the image
module of UMM does not provide direct access to log-likelihoods, we
thereby estimate $`r_{\text{IG}}`$ as a paired difference of conditional
flow-matching losses under shared noise,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{\text{IG}}(\tau;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\;\approx\;\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\;-\;\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\!\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c},h(\tau)\right),
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\xi)`$
denotes the conditional flow-matching loss of the reference image module
under conditioning $`\xi`$, with the two evaluations sharing the same
noise sample for sample-wise constant cancellation. We provide the full
derivation in
Appendix [C.2](#A3.SS2 "C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").

Total reward. To prevent the latent collapse where all $`N`$ latent
steps within a trajectory converge into identical vectors, we add an
inter-step regularization for latent diversity,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{\text{div}}(\tau)=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}\left(1-\cos(l_{n},l_{n^{\prime}})\right),
``` |  |

where $`\cos(\cdot,\cdot)`$ denotes the cosine similarity between two
latent steps, $`l_{n}`$ is the $`n`$-th latent-mode hidden state in
trajectory $`\tau`$, and $`N`$ is the number of latent steps. The total
reward used for policy optimization is then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(\tau;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\;=\;r_{\text{IG}}(\tau;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\;+\;\lambda_{\text{div}}\cdot r_{\text{div}}(\tau).
``` |  | (3) |

Policy optimization. We optimize the policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$ through
reinforcement learning with the following objective:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\max_{\pi_{\theta}}\;\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{c})}\left[R(\tau;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\right]\right].
``` |  | (4) |

We adopt GRPO \[[54](#bib.bib3)\] as the optimization algorithm, which
estimates advantages from group rollouts and removes an explicit value
model. Its simplicity and stability allow a single set of
hyperparameters to transfer across all benchmarks. For more optimization
details, please see
Appendix [A](#A1 "Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").

### 3.2 Text Generation Experiments

While the previous section established the benefits of latent reasoning
for image generation and editing, an open question is whether such
continuous reasoning also benefits text generation tasks that share the
same UMM backbone. We address this question by evaluating LARC on
perception-heavy benchmarks (MME, MMB) and a visual reasoning benchmark
(MIRA), comparing against the base model BAGEL and Uni-CoT, a recent UMM
with discrete token-level reasoning.

As shown in
Table [5](#S3.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 3.1 Image Generation Experiments ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models"),
LARC improves over both BAGEL and Uni-CoT across all benchmarks. The
most pronounced gain appears on MIRA ($`7.5\rightarrow 11.7`$), a
benchmark requiring visual chain-of-thought reasoning that explicitly
demands multi-step intermediate inference. The substantial margin over
Uni-CoT, which performs reasoning through discrete tokens, supports our
hypothesis that latent reasoning provides richer intermediate
representations than its discrete counterpart. We attribute this to the
fact that continuous latent steps preserve information that
discretization through a fixed vocabulary inevitably collapses, an
effect amplified in tasks where reasoning must integrate fine-grained
visual cues. Gains on MME and MMB further confirm that the continuous
reasoning capability transfers positively to perception-heavy tasks
without sacrificing general visual understanding. This indicates that
the additional latent reasoning supervision introduced during training
does not degrade the original visual perception ability of the UMM
backbone, an important property for unified models that serve both
generation and understanding.

### 3.3 Test-time Scaling

As shown in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models"),
we investigate the effect of latent reasoning at test-time. LARC
exhibits monotonically increasing performance on image generation, image
editing, and visual reasoning as the latent budget grows, a behavior
that is absent in prior reasoning-augmented UMMs. Furthermore, LARC
consistently outperforms best-of-N sampling under matched compute
budgets across all three tasks. We attribute this scaling behavior to
continuous latent reasoning operating in the same space as image
generation, allowing additional compute to refine useful representations
rather than producing redundant discrete tokens.

## 4 Related Work

### 4.1 Test-time Scaling in LLMs

Test-time scaling is a model behavior where additional inference compute
improves task performance in large language models
(LLMs) \[[41](#bib.bib75), [32](#bib.bib6)\]. Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
prompting \[[65](#bib.bib38)\] has emerged as a primary driver of
test-time scaling in LLMs by decomposing problems into intermediate
textual reasoning steps. Subsequent work has internalized this
capability through supervised fine-tuning \[[73](#bib.bib39)\] and
reinforcement learning \[[54](#bib.bib3)\]. Inference-time search
methods further expand the reasoning space through
tree-based \[[70](#bib.bib40)\] and
trajectory-based \[[15](#bib.bib41)\] exploration. Recognizing that
natural language fails to encapsulate all forms of
reasoning \[[31](#bib.bib71)\], recent paradigms shift reasoning
directly into latent space \[[20](#bib.bib9), [34](#bib.bib42),
[25](#bib.bib1)\]. Despite these advances, prior efforts to transfer
test-time scaling to UMM image generation \[[64](#bib.bib23),
[68](#bib.bib22), [5](#bib.bib25), [6](#bib.bib21), [43](#bib.bib27)\]
have relied on discrete reasoning tokens and failed to exhibit the
predictable scaling observed in language reasoning. We attribute this
failure to the modality mismatch between discrete reasoning tokens and
continuous image generation. Motivated by this observation, we propose
LARC, which performs reasoning entirely in continuous space and becomes
the first UMM to achieve test-time scaling for image generation.

### 4.2 Image Generation Models

Image generation has emerged as one of the most influential research
directions in modern deep learning, advancing along two complementary
paradigms. Diffusion models \[[21](#bib.bib47), [56](#bib.bib48)\] have
become the dominant paradigm for text-to-image generation, synthesizing
images through iterative denoising in systems such as Stable
Diffusion \[[49](#bib.bib49)\], DALL-E 2 \[[46](#bib.bib50)\], and
Imagen \[[50](#bib.bib51)\]. In parallel, autoregressive approaches
predict visual tokens from discrete vocabularies established by
vector-quantized tokenizers \[[62](#bib.bib52), [9](#bib.bib53)\],
scaling from DALL-E \[[47](#bib.bib54)\] to recent transformer-based
models including LlamaGen \[[57](#bib.bib55)\] and
VAR \[[59](#bib.bib56)\]. Building on these advances, unified multimodal
models (UMMs) integrate visual understanding and generation within a
single architecture, typically extending a pretrained LLM with image
tokenization to inherit strong language priors and enable cross-task
knowledge transfer \[[61](#bib.bib62)\]. Early work pursues fully
discrete tokenization with next-token prediction:
Chameleon \[[58](#bib.bib57)\] pioneers an early-fusion design trained
from scratch, and Emu3 \[[64](#bib.bib23)\] scales this paradigm across
understanding and generation. Hybrid formulations combine autoregressive
text decoding with diffusion-based image generation:
Show-o \[[67](#bib.bib58), [68](#bib.bib22)\] uses discrete diffusion
for images, and Transfusion \[[74](#bib.bib59)\] applies continuous
diffusion over image patches. A separate line of work decouples visual
representations for the two tasks: Janus \[[66](#bib.bib24)\] and
Janus-Pro \[[5](#bib.bib25)\] introduce separate visual encoders under a
shared transformer backbone, and BAGEL \[[6](#bib.bib21)\] extends
decoupling to the transformer itself through a
Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts architecture \[[36](#bib.bib67),
[55](#bib.bib68)\] combined with rectified flow \[[38](#bib.bib74)\] for
image generation. Our work is orthogonal to these architectural
advances, introducing a reasoning capability that operates over an
existing UMM backbone, with BAGEL being adopted as our base models.

### 4.3 Reinforcement Learning in LLMs

Reinforcement learning has long served as the standard machinery for
policy optimization. Trust-region methods such as
TRPO \[[51](#bib.bib43)\] and its first-order successor
PPO \[[53](#bib.bib44)\] stabilize policy updates through constrained or
clipped objectives. More recent work adapts policy optimization to
language modeling. GRPO \[[54](#bib.bib3)\] eliminates the value network
by estimating advantages from group rollouts, and follow-up
methods \[[37](#bib.bib46), [71](#bib.bib8), [40](#bib.bib45)\] further
improve this group-relative formulation. These advances have powered
substantial capability gains in LLMs, ranging from instruction-following
in InstructGPT \[[42](#bib.bib5)\] to answer-verifiable reasoning in
DeepSeek-R1 \[[17](#bib.bib4)\], where GRPO drives chain-of-thought
reasoning at scale. However, for reasoning in image generation, no
well-defined ground-truth reasoning trajectory exists. This precludes
direct supervision over the reasoning chain and motivates the RL-based
formulation adopted in LARC. We instantiate this formulation with GRPO,
which is widely used in reinforcement learning tasks, to train reasoning
chains using outcome-level rewards. This bypasses the need for
chain-level annotations standard in text-based CoT, yet absent in our
setting.

## 5 Conclusion

We introduced LARC, a latent reasoning framework that performs reasoning
in continuous space for unified multimodal models (UMMs). By
representing reasoning steps as continuous hidden states rather than
discrete tokens, LARC alleviates the modality gap that has prevented
prior reasoning-augmented UMMs from inheriting the test-time scaling of
large language models. Our two-stage pipeline pairs curriculum
supervised fine-tuning with a self-evolving reinforcement learning stage
driven by an information-gain reward, requiring no external critic.
Instantiated on BAGEL with 1M interleaved samples, LARC achieves
state-of-the-art text-to-image performance with a 7%p gain over BAGEL
and is the first reasoning-augmented UMM to exhibit clear test-time
scaling for image generation. This finding bridges a long-standing
modality gap in UMMs and opens a path toward UMMs that match the
inference-time scalability of modern reasoning LLMs.

## Broader Impact Statement

LARC advances the reasoning capability of unified multimodal models,
with the goal of producing more controllable and inference-time-scalable
image generation systems. We discuss the broader impacts below.

#### Positive impacts.

By enabling test-time scaling for image generation, LARC allows users to
allocate additional inference compute to obtain higher-fidelity outputs,
particularly for prompts that require fine-grained compositional
reasoning such as attribute binding and spatial layout. This
controllability supports applications in design assistance,
accessibility tools for visual communication, education, and scientific
visualization, where prompt fidelity directly impacts utility. The
latent reasoning paradigm also offers a unified mechanism for
transferring reasoning capability across modalities, potentially
reducing the need for modality-specific reasoning pipelines and lowering
the engineering cost of building multimodal systems.

#### Potential risks.

As with other text-to-image generation systems, LARC inherits risks
common to large generative models, including the potential for
generating misleading or harmful imagery, amplifying biases present in
pretraining data, and being misused for the production of deceptive
content such as misinformation or non-consensual depictions. The
improved prompt-following fidelity demonstrated in our experiments
increases generation quality across legitimate uses, and also amplifies
these dual-use concerns, since outputs that more faithfully reflect
adversarial prompts are correspondingly more capable of producing
targeted harmful content. We do not introduce new training data or new
modalities beyond those already established in BAGEL and the public
datasets we use, so risks specific to dataset provenance are inherited
rather than newly created.

#### Mitigation and responsible deployment.

We recommend that downstream applications of LARC combine the model with
established safeguards, including content moderation classifiers,
watermarking of generated images, prompt-level safety filters, and
auditing of training data for licensing and bias. For research
deployments, releasing only model weights without bundled deployment
infrastructure helps preserve the ability of downstream practitioners to
apply context-appropriate safety measures. We will release LARC under a
license that prohibits use for generating disinformation, non-consensual
intimate imagery, or content targeting protected groups, and we
encourage the community to develop evaluation protocols specifically for
reasoning-augmented generative models, where the relationship between
reasoning fidelity and harmful output remains understudied.

#### Environmental considerations.

Training LARC requires reinforcement learning over a
multi-billion-parameter UMM backbone, which incurs non-trivial compute
and associated carbon footprint. We mitigate this by initializing from a
pretrained BAGEL checkpoint rather than training from scratch, and by
using a curriculum that converges in fewer steps than naive joint
training of all latent reasoning steps. Future work on
parameter-efficient adaptation of latent reasoning, such as
adapter-based or low-rank variants, would further reduce the resource
cost of deploying this paradigm at scale.

## Appendix A Implementation Details

### A.1 Training Details

We adopt BAGEL \[[6](#bib.bib21)\] as the base model and perform
two-stage learning, namely (1) Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) with
curriculum and (2) self-evolving Reinforcement Learning (RL). In Stage
1, we freeze the vision tokenizer and jointly train the understanding
expert and the generation expert of the UMM backbone. We follow the
curriculum schedule described in
Section [2.3](#S2.SS3 "2.3 Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning with Curriculum Learning ‣ 2 LARC: Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models"),
where the number of activated latent steps is increased from $`0`$ to
$`N`$ over $`N{+}1`$ phases with the learning rate decayed at every
phase transition. In Stage 2, we initialize the policy $`\pi_{\theta}`$
from $`\mathcal{M}_{\text{SFT}}`$ and freeze a copy of the same model as
the reference $`\pi_{\text{ref}}`$. Only the understanding expert is
updated during RL, since the generation expert is reused for reward
estimation through the shared-noise flow-matching loss. Detailed
hyperparameters are summarized in
Table [9](#A1.T9 "Table 9 ‣ A.1 Training Details ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").
All training experiments are conducted with 8$`\times`$ NVIDIA A100s,
taking approximately 300 GPU hours in Stage 1 and 96 GPU hours in Stage
2.

### A.2 Evaluation Details

We evaluate LARC across three categories of benchmarks following the
official protocol of each benchmark. For inference, we apply greedy
sampling and generate one image per prompt unless specified otherwise.

Text-to-Image generation. We evaluate the models on
GenEval \[[16](#bib.bib13)\], DPG-Bench \[[22](#bib.bib14)\], and
OneIG-Bench \[[1](#bib.bib15)\]. GenEval automatically verifies
compositional properties of generated images via an object-detection
pipeline, reporting accuracy across six sub-tasks, e.g., single object,
two objects, counting, colors, position, and attribute binding.
DPG-Bench evaluates 1,065 dense prompts through an MLLM-as-a-Judge
protocol, providing scores across global, entity, attribute, relation,
and other categories. OneIG-Bench spans multiple dimensions of
text-to-image (T2I) evaluation, including prompt-image alignment, text
rendering, and reasoning, with scores partitioned by prompt type (NP,
T&P) and prompt length (short, medium, long). Specifically, we report
alignment score for OneIG-Bench.

Image editing. We evaluate the models on CompBench \[[27](#bib.bib16)\],
which features 3,200 complex instruction-guided editing tasks built
through an MLLM-human collaborative framework. Specifically, we report
multi-object editing tasks in
Table [4](#S3.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 3.1 Image Generation Experiments ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").
Following the official protocol, we report local CLIP-Text (LC-T) score
between the predicted foreground and the description, local CLIP-Image
(LC-I) similarity between the predicted foreground and the ground-truth
image, along with pixel-level metrics PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

Multimodal understanding. We evaluate on MME \[[14](#bib.bib17)\],
MMBench \[[39](#bib.bib18)\], MMMU \[[72](#bib.bib19)\], and
MIRA \[[75](#bib.bib20)\]. MME measures perception and cognition across
14 sub-tasks, while MMBench and MMMU benchmark broad multimodal
reasoning across diverse disciplines. MIRA targets visual
chain-of-thought reasoning with 546 carefully curated problems spanning
20 task types, where successful reasoning requires generating
intermediate visual representations.

For API-based baselines, we report results using the model version
gpt-4o-2024-08-06 for GPT-4o \[[23](#bib.bib30)\]. We adopt
VLMEvalKit \[[7](#bib.bib37)\] as the unified evaluation toolkit for
multimodal understanding tasks, and use the official codebase released
by each benchmark for T2I generation and image editing evaluation.

## Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning

### C.1 Why Self-Rewarding RL?

In this section, we discuss why standard reward-based reinforcement
learning (RL) paradigms are not directly applicable to latent reasoning
in UMMs, motivating the self-rewarding formulation adopted in LARC.

Inapplicability of preference-based methods. Direct Preference
Optimization (DPO) \[[45](#bib.bib32)\] and its diffusion variants such
as Diffusion-DPO \[[63](#bib.bib33)\] and CaPO \[[33](#bib.bib73)\]
optimize the policy from paired preference data $`(\tau^{w},\tau^{l})`$,
where $`\tau^{w}`$ is preferred over $`\tau^{l}`$. This paradigm
presumes the existence of a meaningful preference order between
trajectories. In our setting, however, each trajectory $`\tau`$ contains
continuous latent steps $`h(\tau)`$ that lie outside any
human-interpretable space, ruling out direct human annotation over the
reasoning trace. External reward models for image
generation \[[69](#bib.bib34), [29](#bib.bib35), [28](#bib.bib66)\] also
operate on the rendered image $`x`$ rather than the latent chain,
providing no signal that distinguishes between two latent traces that
yield comparable images. A recent study \[[60](#bib.bib36)\] further
reports that DPO-based optimization in autoregressive image generation
tends to overfit to in-domain preference distributions, limiting
generalization beyond the curated preference set. These observations
indicate that preference-based methods are structurally misaligned with
the latent reasoning regime, which lacks both pairwise preference labels
and a natural notion of pairwise dominance over hidden states.

Limitations of critic-based methods. Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO) \[[52](#bib.bib7)\] sidesteps the preference assumption but
introduces a separate value network that estimates the expected return
at each position, which roughly doubles the trainable parameters and
adds non-trivial training overhead at the scale of UMMs. More
fundamentally, fitting an accurate critic over the latent reasoning
trajectory requires a dense external supervision signal, which is
precisely what is absent in our setting. Without ground-truth latent
annotations, the critic must rely on an external image-quality estimator
or a learned reward model, both of which inherit the same evaluation gap
described above.

Self-rewarding via information gain. We point out two requirements that
the optimization framework must satisfy. (1) The reward should be
derivable from the model itself, since no external evaluator can
faithfully assess latent reasoning traces. (2) The policy gradient
estimator should avoid an additional value network to keep training
tractable on top of the UMM backbone. In LARC, the information-gain
reward satisfies the first requirement by reusing the frozen reference
$`\pi_{\text{ref}}`$ obtained from Stage 1, scoring each trajectory
through the change in target-image likelihood. Additionally,
GRPO \[[54](#bib.bib3)\] satisfies the second by replacing the value
network with group-relative advantages estimated from multiple rollouts.

### C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward

We provide the full derivation of the Information-Gain reward
$`r_{\text{IG}}`$ introduced in
Section [2.4](#S2.SS4 "2.4 Stage 2: Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ 2 LARC: Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").
Recall that the reward is defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{\text{IG}}(\tau\,;\,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\triangleq\log p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{c},h(\tau))-\log p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{c}),
``` |  | (5) |

where $`h(\tau)`$ denotes the sequence of latent hidden states produced
along trajectory $`\tau`$, and $`\mathbf{x}`$ is the target image.
Taking the expectation of
Equation [5](#A3.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")
under the joint distribution $`(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c},\tau)`$ recovers
the conditional mutual information,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,r_{\text{IG}}\,\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,\log\frac{p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{c},h(\tau))}{p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{c})}\,\right]=I_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\!\left(\mathbf{x}\,;\,h(\tau)\mid\mathbf{c}\right)\;\geq\;0.
``` |  | (6) |

Maximizing $`r_{\text{IG}}`$ is therefore equivalent to maximizing the
mutual information between the latent reasoning trace and the target
image, with the bare context $`c`$ playing the role of the marginal
baseline. The non-negativity in
Equation [6](#A3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")
follows from Jensen’s inequality applied to the log-ratio, which
guarantees that the expected reward is bounded below by zero regardless
of the policy.

Since the generation expert of UMM does not provide closed-form access
to the marginal density $`p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}\mid\cdot)`$,
we approximate the log-likelihood through the conditional flow-matching
objective. Following the rectified flow formulation of
BAGEL \[[6](#bib.bib21)\], the negative log-likelihood admits the bound

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
-\log p_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x}\mid\xi)\;=\;\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\xi)+C(\mathbf{x}),
``` |  | (7) |

where $`\xi`$ denotes the conditioning variable,
$`\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}`$ is the conditional
flow-matching loss evaluated under $`\pi_{\text{ref}}`$, and
$`C(\mathbf{x})`$ is a sample-wise constant that depends only on the
target image and the noise schedule. Substituting
Equation [7](#A3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")
into
Equation [5](#A3.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")
for both terms yields

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle r_{\text{IG}}(\tau\,;\,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\big[\,-\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c},h(\tau))-C(\mathbf{x})\,\big]-\big[\,-\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})-C(\mathbf{x})\,\big]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\,-\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c},h(\tau)).`$ |  | (8) |

Crucially, the constant $`C(\mathbf{x})`$ cancels exactly when the two
flow-matching losses are evaluated under the same noise sample
$`\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)`$ and the same time step
$`t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]`$, since both terms inherit the identical
perturbation. We therefore implement the reward as a paired evaluation
under shared randomness, as detailed in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").
This shared-noise estimator yields an unbiased and low-variance
approximation of
Equation [5](#A3.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models"),
requiring no additional reward model and reusing the frozen
$`\pi_{\text{ref}}`$ that is already available from Stage 1.

1: Frozen reference $`\pi_{\text{ref}}=\mathcal{M}_{\text{SFT}}`$,
dataset $`\mathcal{D}`$, hyperparameters $`G`$,
$`\epsilon_{\text{clip}}`$, $`\beta`$, $`\lambda_{\text{div}}`$

2: Initialize $`\pi_{\theta}\leftarrow\pi_{\text{ref}}`$

3: for each RL step do

4:   $`\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\leftarrow\pi_{\theta}`$

5:   Sample $`(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})\sim\mathcal{D}`$ and rollout
$`\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot\mid\mathbf{c})`$

6:   for $`i=1,\ldots,G`$ do

7:
   $`r_{\text{IG}}(\tau_{i})\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})-\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^{\pi_{\text{ref}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c},h(\tau_{i}))`$
$`\triangleright`$ Shared noise

8:
   $`R_{i}\leftarrow r_{\text{IG}}(\tau_{i})+\lambda_{\text{div}}\cdot r_{\text{div}}(\tau_{i})`$

9:   end for

10:   $`A_{i}\leftarrow(R_{i}-\mu_{R})/(\sigma_{R}+\varepsilon)`$ for
$`i=1,\ldots,G`$

11:   Update $`\theta`$ by maximizing
$`\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)`$ $`\triangleright`$ See

12: end for

13: return $`\mathcal{M}_{\text{LARC }}\leftarrow\pi_{\theta}`$

### C.3 Policy Optimization

We instantiate the policy gradient optimization in
Equation [4](#S2.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 2.4 Stage 2: Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ 2 LARC: Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models")
using GRPO \[[54](#bib.bib3)\], which estimates advantages from group
rollouts and removes the need for a separate value network. For each
context $`c`$ sampled from $`\mathcal{D}`$, we draw $`G`$ trajectories
$`\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}`$ from the old policy
$`\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}`$, compute the total reward $`R_{i}`$ for
each rollout, and normalize within the group to obtain the advantage
$`A_{i}`$. The policy is then updated by maximizing the clipped
surrogate objective

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\min\!\Big(\rho_{i}(\theta)A_{i},\;\mathrm{clip}(\rho_{i}(\theta),1-\epsilon_{\text{clip}},1+\epsilon_{\text{clip}})A_{i}\Big)-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\!\left[\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}\right]\right],
``` |  | (9) |

where
$`\rho_{i}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(\tau_{i}\mid\mathbf{c})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\tau_{i}\mid\mathbf{c})}`$
is the importance ratio. The full training procedure is summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ C.2 Derivation of the Information-Gain Reward ‣ Appendix C Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models").

Table [12](#A4.T12 "Table 12 ‣ D.2 Additional Ablation Study ‣ Appendix D Additional Experimental Results ‣ Latent Reasoning in Continuous Spacefor Unified Multimodal Models"),
removing the diversity reward ($`\lambda_{\text{div}}{=}0`$) leads to a

## Appendix E Limitations and Future Work

While LARC demonstrates strong empirical gains and clear test-time
scaling on various generation tasks, several directions remain open for
future exploration. First, our framework is currently instantiated on
UMMs whose image module is a flow-matching head, which generates the
image in continuous space. Extending the same principle to UMMs with
discrete space generation or other generative heads is a natural next
step. Second, although LARC enables effective test-time scaling, the
interleaved latent reasoning trajectory introduces additional
computation at inference, leading to latency overhead compared to the
base UMM. Reducing this overhead through efficient decoding strategy,
distillation, or adaptive latent allocation is a promising research
direction. We leave these extensions to future work.
