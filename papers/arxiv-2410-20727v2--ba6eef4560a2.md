###### Abstract

Recent advances in aligning large language models with human preferences
have corroborated the growing importance of best-of-$`N`$ distillation
(BOND). However, the iterative BOND algorithm is prohibitively expensive
in practice due to the sample and computation inefficiency. This paper
addresses the problem by revealing a unified game-theoretic connection
between iterative BOND and self-play alignment, which unifies seemingly
disparate algorithmic paradigms. Based on the connection, we establish a
novel framework, WIN rate Dominance (WIND), with a series of efficient
algorithms for regularized win rate dominance optimization that
approximates iterative BOND in the parameter space. We provide provable
sample efficiency guarantee for one of the WIND variants with the
squared loss objective. The experimental results confirm that our
algorithm not only accelerates the computation, but also achieves
superior sample efficiency compared to existing methods.

Keywords: Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), preference
optimization, matrix game, sample efficiency

## 1 Introduction

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) to align with human preferences
has become a critical challenge in artificial intelligence to ensure the
safety of their deployment. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback
(RLHF) has emerged as a dominant approach, significantly improving LLM
performance as demonstrated by InstructGPT ([Ouyang et al.,
2022](#bib.bib37)) and subsequent works. RLHF combines reward modeling
to quantify human preferences and RL fine-tuning to adjust the LLM’s
output distribution, enhancing desired responses while suppressing
unfavorable ones. While RLHF has shown promising results, it comes with
significant extra post-training cost, and the aligned LLM may exhibit
performance degeneration due to the alignment tax ([Askell et al.,
2021](#bib.bib2); [OpenAI, 2023](#bib.bib36)).

Alternatively, best-of-$`N`$ (BoN) sampling has emerged as a simple and
surprisingly effective technique to obtain high-quality outputs from an
LLM ([Stiennon et al., 2020](#bib.bib44)). In BoN sampling, multiple
samples are drawn from an LLM, ranked according to a specific attribute,
and the best one is selected. This simple approach can improve model
outputs without the need for extensive fine-tuning, offering a
potentially more efficient path to alignment. Building upon the success
of BoN sampling, a few works explored the iterative variants of this
approach ([Dong et al., 2023](#bib.bib15); [Sessa et al.,
2024](#bib.bib42)). Iterative BoN applies the BoN sampling and selection
process repeatedly, potentially leading to even better alignments with
human preferences.

However, BoN incurs significant computational overhead due to making
multiple inference calls to generate one output, especially when the
number of calls is high. To mitigate the high inference cost of
(iterative) BoN, [Sessa et al. (2024)](#bib.bib42) proposed a
distillation algorithm, best-of-$`N`$ distillation (BOND), to train a
new model emulating the output of iterative BoN. However, this approach
also has a high training cost, due to the need of collecting multiple
samples in each round of distillation, leading to a major bottleneck for
wider adoption. Given the growing importance and significance of the
iterative BoN approach, it raises new questions about its theoretical
properties, practical implementation, and relationship to established
methods like RLHF. In this paper, we delve into the theoretical
foundations and practical applications of iterative BoN sampling for LLM
alignment. We address the following question:

_What are the limiting points of iterative BoN, and can we design faster
algorithms to find them?_

### 1.1 Contributions

We provide comprehensive answers to these questions through the
following key contributions.

- •
  We introduce a general algorithmic framework for iterative BoN
  distillation, possibly with a slow-moving anchor, and uncover its
  limiting point corresponds to the Nash equilibrium of a (regularized)
  two-player min-max game optimizing the logarithm of the expected win
  rate. This offers a fresh game-theoretic interpretation that is
  unavailable before.
- •
  We show that the WIN rate Dominance (WIND) policy, which has a higher
  chance of winning against any other policy, solves the minmax game of
  win rate introduced in RLHF ([Swamy et al., 2024](#bib.bib45); [Munos
  et al., 2023](#bib.bib33)), and approximates the iterative BoN’s
  limiting point.
- •
  We propose a novel algorithm framework, WIND, to find the win rate
  dominance policy with flexible loss configurations, and demonstrate it
  exhibits improved sample and computational efficiency compared to
  prior work, while maintaining provable convergence guarantees.
- •
  We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the performance of WIND,
  demonstrating competitive or better performance against
  state-of-the-art alignment methods such as J-BOND across various
  benchmarks, highlighting its efficiency especially in the sampling
  process and training cost.

### 1.2 Related work

#### RLHF and LLM alignment.

Reinforcement Learning from human feedback (RLHF) is an effective
approach to train AI models to produce outputs that align with human
value and preference ([Christiano et al., 2017](#bib.bib12); [Stiennon
et al., 2020](#bib.bib44); [Nakano et al., 2021](#bib.bib34)). Recently,
RLHF has become the most effective approach to align language
models ([Ouyang et al., 2022](#bib.bib37); [Bai et al.,
2022](#bib.bib4)). The famous InstructGPT ([Ouyang et al.,
2022](#bib.bib37)) approach eventually led to the groundbreaking ChatGPT
and GPT-4 ([OpenAI, 2023](#bib.bib36)). A variety of RLHF methods have
been proposed, including direct preference optimization ([Rafailov
et al., 2024](#bib.bib40)) and many other variants ([Zhao et al.,
2023](#bib.bib59); [Yuan et al., 2023b](#bib.bib56); [Azar et al.,
2024](#bib.bib3); [Meng et al., 2024](#bib.bib29); [Xu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib50); [Ethayarajh et al., 2024](#bib.bib16); [Tang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib46)), to name a few, which directly learns from the
preference data without RL fine-tuning. Furthermore, value-incentive
preference optimization ([Cen et al., 2024](#bib.bib10)) has been
proposed to implement the optimistic (resp. pessimistic) principle for
online (resp. offline) RLHF in a practical way with theoretical
guarantees.

#### RLHF via self-play.

Another line of RLHF methods investigate self-play optimization for
unregularized and regularized two-player win rate games,
respectively ([Swamy et al., 2024](#bib.bib45); [Munos et al.,
2023](#bib.bib33)). [Wu et al. (2024b)](#bib.bib49) introduced a
scalable self-play algorithm for win rate games, enabling efficient
fine-tuning of LLMs, see also [Rosset et al. (2024)](#bib.bib41); [Zhang
et al. (2024)](#bib.bib58); [Gao et al. (2024)](#bib.bib18); [Huang
et al. (2024)](#bib.bib22) among others.

#### Best-of-$`N`$ and BOND.

BoN has empirically shown impressive reward-KL trade-off ([Nakano
et al., 2021](#bib.bib34); [Gao et al., 2023](#bib.bib17)), which has
been theoretically investigated by [Gui et al. (2024)](#bib.bib19) from
the perspective of win rate maximization. [Beirami et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib7) analyzed the KL divergence between the BoN policy and
the base policy, and [Yang et al. (2024a)](#bib.bib51) studied the
asymptotic properties of the BoN policy. The recent work [Gui et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib19) also proposed a method to use both best-of-$`N`$ and
worst-of-$`N`$ to train language models. [Sessa et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib42) introduced BOND and J-BOND to train language models
to learn BoN policies. [Amini et al. (2024)](#bib.bib1) proposed vBoN
which is equivalent to BOND. However, there is no existing work for
characterizing the properties of iterative BoN yet.

## 2 Preliminaries

### 2.1 RLHF: reward versus win rate

We consider the language model $`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot)`$ as a policy,
where $`\theta\in\Theta`$ denotes its parameters, and $`\Theta`$ the
compact parameter space. Given a prompt $`x\in\mathcal{X}`$, the policy
generates an answer $`y\in\mathcal{Y}`$ according to the conditional
distribution $`\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)`$. For notational simplicity, we
drop the subscript $`\theta`$ when it is clear from the context. We let
$`\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}`$ denote the simplex over $`\mathcal{Y}`$, and
$`\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}}`$ denote the space of policies as
follows:

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}}\coloneqq\big\{\pi=[\pi(\cdot|x)]_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\mid\pi(\cdot|x)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}},\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}\big\}.
``` |  |

In practice, RLHF optimizes the policy model against the reward model
while staying close to a reference model $`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$.
There are two metrics being considered: reward and win rate.

#### Reward maximization.

Suppose there is a reward model
$`r(x,y):\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\mathbb{R}`$, which produces
a scalar reward given a prompt $`x`$ and a response $`y`$. RLHF aims to
maximize the KL-regularized value function, given a reference model
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle V_{\text{rm}}(\pi)=\underset{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi(\cdot|x)}{\mathbb{E}}[r(x,y)]-\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right),`$ |  | (1) |

where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi_{1}\|\pi_{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho}\left[\textnormal{KL}\left(\pi_{1}(\cdot|x)\|\pi_{2}(\cdot|x)\right)\right]
``` |  |

is the KL divergence between policies $`\pi_{1}`$ and $`\pi_{2}`$, with
$`\rho`$ being the distribution of prompts. Here, $`\beta\geq 0`$ is a
hyperparameter that balances the reward and the KL divergence. Without
loss of generality, we assume $`\mathrm{supp}(\rho)`$ is
$`{\mathcal{X}}`$ throughout the paper.

#### Win rate maximization.

Another scheme of RLHF aims to maximize the KL-regularized win rate
against the reference model ([Gui et al., 2024](#bib.bib19)). Given a
reward model $`r`$, a preference model
$`P_{x}:{\mathcal{Y}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\{0,1/2,1\}`$ can be
defined as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
P_{x}(y,y^{\prime})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\,\,\text{ if $r(x,y)>r(x,y^{\prime})$,}\\
1/2,\,\,\text{ if $r(x,y)=r(x,y^{\prime})$,}\\
0,\,\,\text{ if $r(x,y)<r(x,y^{\prime})$.}\end{cases}
``` |  | (2) |

Given a policy pair $`\pi,\pi^{\prime}`$, the win rate of $`\pi`$ over
$`\pi^{\prime}`$ is given as ([Swamy et al., 2024](#bib.bib45))

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle P(\pi\succ\pi^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\underset{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi(\cdot|x),\atop y^{\prime}\sim\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|x)}{{\mathbb{E}}}P_{x}(y,y^{\prime})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho}\pi^{\top}(\cdot|x)P_{x}(\cdot,\cdot)\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|x).`$ |  | (3) |

The KL-regularized win rate maximization objective is defined as ([Gui
et al., 2024](#bib.bib19)):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle V_{\text{wr}}(\pi)\coloneqq P(\pi\succ\pi_{\textnormal{ref}})-\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right).`$ |  | (4) |

The win rate maximization is better aligned with evaluation metrics
adopted in common benchmarks, and further, can be carried out without
explicit reward models as long as the preference model $`P_{x}`$ is
well-defined.

### 2.2 Best-of-$`N`$ distillation

Best-of-$`N`$ (BoN) is a simple yet strong baseline in RLHF. Given a
reward model $`r`$ and a prompt $`x`$, BoN samples $`n`$ i.i.d.
responses $`y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n}`$ from the policy $`\pi(\cdot|x)`$ and
select the response

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
y=\arg\max_{1\leq i\leq n}r(x,y_{i}),\quad y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\sim\pi(\cdot|x)
``` |  |

with the highest reward. We call $`\pi^{(n)}`$ the BoN policy which
selects the sample with the highest reward given $`n`$ samples i.i.d.
drawn from $`\pi`$. [Gui et al. (2024)](#bib.bib19) shows that for any
fixed small $`\beta>0`$, $`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}^{(n)}`$
(approximately) maximizes $`V_{\text{wr}}(\cdot)`$ for properly chosen
$`n`$. While BoN is widely used in practice ([Beirami et al.,
2024](#bib.bib7); [Gao et al., 2023](#bib.bib17); [Wang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib47)), yet it can be quite expensive in terms of the
inference cost for drawing $`n`$ samples. Hence, BoN distillation (BOND)
([Sessa et al., 2024](#bib.bib42)) is developed to approximate the BoN
policy $`\pi^{(n)}`$ through fine-tuning from some reference policy
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(\cdot|x)`$, which can be updated iteratively
via an exponential moving average ([Sessa et al., 2024](#bib.bib42)).

## 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View

In this section, we present a game-theoretic understanding of iterative
BoN, which allows us to connect it to existing game-theoretic RLHF
approaches under a win rate maximization framework.

### 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving

#### Iterative BoN.

Due to the success of BoN sampling, its iterative version has also been
studied ([Dong et al., 2023](#bib.bib15); [Sessa et al.,
2024](#bib.bib42)), where BoN is performed iteratively by using a moving
anchor as the reference policy. To understand its property in
generality, we introduce the iterative BoN method in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Iterative BoN. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
that encapsulates iterative BoN methods with or without a moving
reference model, which we call the mixing and no-mixing case,
respectively.

1:  Input: reference policy $`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$, iterate number
$`T`$, Best-of-$`N`$ parameter $`n`$, boolean value Mixing.

2:  Optional: mixing rates $`\alpha_{1}>0,\alpha_{2}\geq 0`$ such that
$`\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq 1`$.

3:  Initialization: $`\pi_{0}\leftarrow\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$.

4:  for $`t=0,1,\cdots,T-1`$ do

5:   $`\pi_{t}^{(n)}\leftarrow\textrm{Best-of-$N$}(\pi_{t},n)`$.

6:   if Mixing then

7:
   $`\pi_{t+1}\propto(\pi_{t}^{(n)})^{\alpha_{1}}\pi_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}^{1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}`$;

8:   else if not Mixing then

9:    $`\pi_{t+1}\leftarrow\pi_{t}^{(n)}`$.

10:   end if

11:  end for

12:  Return $`\pi_{T}`$.

demonstrates these two cases. In the mixing case, we obtain new policies
by mixing the BoN policy $`\pi_{t}^{(n)}`$, $`\pi_{t}`$ and

#### Game-theoretic perspective.

We show that iterative BoN is implicitly solving the following game.
Define a preference matrix $`\overline{P}_{x}`$ of size
$`|{\mathcal{Y}}|\times|{\mathcal{Y}}|`$ at $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$ by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\overline{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\,\,\text{ if $r(x,y)\geq r(x,y^{\prime})$,}\\
0,\,\,\text{ if $r(x,y)<r(x,y^{\prime})$.}\end{cases}
``` |  | (5) |

Define further
$`f_{\beta}:\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathcal{Y}}\times\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}`$
as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle f_{\beta}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\underset{x\sim\rho,\atop y\in\pi(\cdot|x)}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\log\underset{y^{\prime}\in\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|x)}{\mathbb{E}}\overline{P}_{x}(y\succeq y^{\prime})\big]-\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right).`$ |  | (6) |

We introduce the following symmetric two-player log-win-rate game:

|     |                                                               |     |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \begin{cases}\pi_{1}=\arg\max_{\pi}f_{\beta}(\pi,\pi_{2}),&\\
       \pi_{2}=\arg\max_{\pi}f_{\beta}(\pi,\pi_{1}).&\end{cases}
       ```                                                            |     | (7) |

Let $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$ be a Nash equilibrium of the
above log-win-rate

|  |  |  |  |  |

and $`(\pi_{\infty},\pi_{\infty})`$ is a Nash equilibrium of the

game ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ Game-theoretic perspective. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
when:

1.  1.
    (no-mixing) $`\alpha_{1}=1,\alpha_{2}=0`$ for $`\beta=0`$;
2.  2.
    (mixing) $`\alpha_{1}=\frac{\eta}{(1+\beta\eta)(n-1)}`$,
    $`\alpha_{2}=\frac{n-1-\eta}{(1+\beta\eta)(n-1)}`$ for any
    $`\beta,\eta>0`$.

In the no-mixing case, we can show that the limiting policy obtained by
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Iterative BoN. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
converges to the equilibrium of the unregularized log-win-rate game. In
the mixing case, we show that with a proper choice of the mixing rates,
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Iterative BoN. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
solves the regularized log-win-rate game. To the best of our knowledge,
this is the first game-theoretic understanding of iterative BoN using a
general preference model.

### 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance

The $`\log`$-win-rate
game ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ Game-theoretic perspective. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is a non-zero-sum game that may be challenging to optimize: the function
$`f_{\beta}`$ is not convex-concave, the Nash equilibria may not be
unique, and the $`\log`$ term introduces nonlinearity, which induces
difficulty in estimation. Therefore, we seek a good alternative to the
$`\log`$-win-rate game that maintains its core properties while being
more amenable to optimization.

Specifically, we now consider the following alternative two-player
win-rate game:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\max_{\pi}\min_{\pi^{\prime}}P(\pi\succ\pi^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle-\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right)+\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi^{\prime}\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right),`$ |  | (9) |

which eliminates the nonlinearity in the reward, and has been recently
studied by [Swamy et al. (2024)](#bib.bib45); [Wu et al.
(2024b)](#bib.bib49) for $`\beta=0`$ and [Munos et al.
(2023)](#bib.bib33) for $`\beta>0`$.

The following proposition guarantees the game
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is well-defined and is equivalent to the following fixed point problem:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}\pi^{\star}_{\beta}&\in\arg\max_{\pi}P(\pi\succ\pi^{\star}_{\beta})-\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right)\\
&=\underset{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi(\cdot|x),\atop y^{\prime}\sim\pi^{\star}_{\beta}(\cdot|x)}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left[P_{x}(y,y^{\prime})-\beta\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)}\right].\end{split}
``` |  | (10) |

###### Proposition 1 (existence of $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$).

$`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ exists and
$`(\pi^{\star}_{\beta},\pi^{\star}_{\beta})`$ is the Nash equilibrium of
the minmax
game ([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
Moreover, when $`\beta>0`$,
$`(\pi^{\star}_{\beta},\pi^{\star}_{\beta})`$ is the unique Nash
equilibrium.

#### Win rate dominance.

The fixed-point equation
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
identifies a policy with a higher winning probability against any other
policy. For $`\beta=0`$, $`\pi^{\star}_{0}`$ satisfies
$`P(\pi\succ\pi^{\star}_{0})\leq 1/2`$ for any $`\pi`$, ensuring it
outperforms other policies. When $`\beta>0`$, the KL divergence term
encourages $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ to remain close to
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$ while maintaining a high win rate. We term
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
the Win rate Dominance (WIND) optimization problem.

### 3.3 Connecting iterative BoN with WIND

Due to the monotonicity of $`\log(\cdot)`$, it is natural to postulate
that the win rate game and the log-win-rate game underpinning iterative
BoN are connected. We establish the novel relationship rigorously, which
allows a unifying game-theoretic view for many existing algorithms. We
define a constant $`c_{\beta}\in(0,+\infty]`$ related to
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$ as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle c_{\beta}\coloneqq\min_{x\in{\mathcal{X}},\atop y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\backslash{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\left\{\frac{\sum_{y^{\star}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y^{\star}|x)}{4\max\left\{\log\frac{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)}{\underset{y^{\star}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}{\max}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y^{\star}|x)},0\right\}}\right\},`$ |  | (11) |

where
$`{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)\coloneqq\argmax_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}r(x,y)`$
is the set of optimal responses for each $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$. We now
demonstrate the relationship between the equilibria set of the
log-win-rate game $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$ and the win-rate
game $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$.

###### Theorem 2 (relationship between two games (informal)).

Let
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\in\text{relint}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}}\right)`$
and $`n\geq 2`$ in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Iterative BoN. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").
Then

- •
  When $`\beta=0`$, $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$ is also a
  solution to
  ([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"));
- •
  When $`\beta\in(0,c_{\beta})`$ where $`c_{\beta}`$ is defined in
  ([11](#S3.E11 "Equation 11 ‣ 3.3 Connecting iterative BoN with WIND ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
  for all $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$
  satisfies

  |  |  |  |  |
  |----|----|----|----|
  |  | $`\displaystyle\left\|\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}(\cdot|x)-\pi^{\star}_{\beta}(\cdot|x)\right\|_{1}\leq 4(|{\mathcal{Y}}|-|{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)|)\exp\left(\frac{-\sum_{y^{\star}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y^{\star}|x)}{4\beta}\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\beta\rightarrow 0.`$ |  | (12) |

Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (relationship between two games (informal)). ‣ 3.3 Connecting iterative BoN with WIND ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
shows when $`\beta=0`$, both games have the solution
$`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{0}`$. For small positive $`\beta`$, the
$`\ell_{1}`$ distance between the solutions of the two games is bounded
by a term that decreases exponentially with $`1/\beta`$. We verify
Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (relationship between two games (informal)). ‣ 3.3 Connecting iterative BoN with WIND ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
empirically on contextual bandits in
Section [5.1](#S5.SS1 "5.1 Contextual bandits ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").
This result provides theoretical justification for using iterative BoN
as an approximation to WIND, or vice versa, especially when $`\beta`$ is
small. More importantly, it paves a way for efficient algorithm to WIND,
bypassing the $`\log`$ operator in the win-rate game.

## 4 Faster WIND

Based on the understanding of the connection between the
$`\log`$-win-rate game and the win-rate game, in this section, we
propose a new sample-efficient algorithm for finding the WIND solution
in
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
which includes two ingredients: (i) identifying a memory-efficient,
exact policy optimization algorithm with linear last-iterate convergence
([Sokota et al., 2023](#bib.bib43)), and (ii) developing a series of
sample-efficient algorithms with flexible loss functions and finite-time
convergence guarantee. With slight abuse of terminology, we shall refer
to our algorithmic framework also as WIND.

### 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence

Recognizing that
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is an KL-regularized matrix game, there are many existing algorithms
that can be applied to find $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$. Nonetheless, it is
desirable to achieve fast last-iterate convergence with a small memory
footprint. This is especially important in LLM optimization, for the
memory efficiency. For example, extragradient algorithms (e.g.,
[Korpelevich (1976)](#bib.bib27); [Popov (1980)](#bib.bib39); [Cen
et al. (2021)](#bib.bib9))—although fast-convergent—are expected to be
expensive in terms of memory usage due to the need of storing an
additional extrapolation point (i.e., the LLM) in each iteration.

It turns out that the magnetic mirror descent algorithm in [Sokota
et al. (2023)](#bib.bib43), which is proposed to solve a variational
inequality formulation equivalent to
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
meets our consideration. We present a tailored version of this algorithm
in
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
and state its linear last-iterate convergence in

### 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm

We now derive practical sample-efficient methods for approximating the
exact update
([13](#S4.E13 "Equation 13In Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
of WIND in the parameter space $`\Theta`$ of the policy
$`\pi_{\theta}`$, $`\theta\in\Theta`$. For exposition, we use
$`\phi_{\theta}`$ to denote the logits before softmax, i.e.,

|     |                                                  |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \pi_{\theta}=\mathsf{softmax}\circ\phi_{\theta},
       ```                                               |     |

where $`\mathsf{softmax}(x)_{i}\coloneqq e^{x_{i}}/\sum_{j}e^{x_{j}}`$
is the softmax function.

We consider the existence of reward model approximation error, i.e., we
may use an inaccurate judger $`\widehat{P}_{x}`$, which is an
approximation of $`P_{x}`$. For example, instead of training a reward
model, we could use an LLM $`\widehat{P}`$ as a judger to directly judge
if response $`y`$ is better than $`y^{\prime}`$ given a prompt $`x`$,
and use $`\widehat{P}_{x}`$ as an approximation of $`P_{x}`$.

#### Algorithm derivation with the squared risk.

Let $`\theta_{t},\theta_{\text{ref}}`$ denote the parameters of
$`\pi^{(t)}`$ and $`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$ in
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
respectively. We rewrite the update
rule ([13](#S4.E13 "Equation 13In Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\phi_{\theta_{t+1}}(y|x)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\beta\eta}\phi_{\theta_{t}}(y|x)+\frac{\beta\eta}{1+\beta\eta}\phi_{\theta_{\textnormal{ref}}}(y|x)+\frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}P_{x}(y,y^{\prime})+Z_{t}(x)`$ |  | (15) |

for some function $`Z_{t}:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}`$. We
define a proxy
$`\varphi_{t}:{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}`$
using the empirical win-rate as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(x,y,y^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{1+\beta\eta}\phi_{\theta_{t}}(y|x)+\frac{\beta\eta}{1+\beta\eta}\phi_{\theta_{\textnormal{ref}}}(y|x)+\frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}\widehat{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})+Z_{t}(x).`$ |  | (16) |

Our main observation is that the update
([15](#S4.E15 "Equation 15 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
of $`\phi_{\theta_{t}}(y|x)`$ is approximating the conditional
expectation of $`\varphi_{t}`$, that is, for all $`(x,y)`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\psi_{t}(x,y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}[\varphi_{t}(x,y,y^{\prime})|x,y].
``` |  |

Furthermore, this conditional expectation has the lowest risk, due to
the following lemma:

###### Lemma 1 (Conditional mean minimizes the square loss).

For any two random variables $`u,v`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-g(u))^{2}\right]
``` |  | (17) |

for any function $`g`$. In particular, the equality holds if and only if
$`g(u)=\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)`$ almost everywhere on the support of the
distribution of $`u`$.

To invoke
Lemma [1](#Thmlm1 "Lemma 1 (Conditional mean minimizes the square loss). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
we assume the LLM is expressive enough, such that $`\psi_{t}`$ can be
represented by $`\phi_{\theta}`$:

###### Assumption 1 (expressive power).

For any $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$, there exists
$`\theta_{t+1}^{\star}\in\Theta`$ such that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}:\quad\phi_{\theta_{t+1}^{\star}}(y|x)=\psi_{t}(x,y).
``` |  | (18) |

Note that $`\mathrm{supp}(\rho)={\mathcal{X}}`$,
$`\mathrm{supp}(\pi^{(t)}(\cdot|x))={\mathcal{Y}}`$ for all
$`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$. Thus under
Assumption [1](#Thmasmp1 "Assumption 1 (expressive power). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
by
Lemma [1](#Thmlm1 "Lemma 1 (Conditional mean minimizes the square loss). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
we know that for all $`t`$, $`\theta_{t+1}^{\star}\in\Theta`$ satisfies
([18](#S4.E18 "Equation 18 ‣ Assumption 1 (expressive power). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
if and only if

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta_{t+1}^{\star}\in\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}R_{t}^{\textnormal{sq}}(\theta),
``` |  | (19) |

where we define the squared expected risk at the $`t`$-th iteration
$`R_{t}^{\textnormal{sq}}(\theta)`$ as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{t}^{\textnormal{sq}}(\theta)\coloneqq\underset{x\sim\rho,\atop y,y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\varphi_{t}(x,y,y^{\prime})-\phi_{\theta}(y|x)\right)^{2}\right].
``` |  |

In implementation, at each iteration $`t`$, we shall approximate
$`\theta_{t+1}^{\star}`$ by minimizing the empirical risk: we sample
$`x_{i}^{(t)}\sim\rho`$,
$`y_{i}^{(t)},{y_{i}^{\prime}}^{(t)}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x_{i}^{(t)})`$,
$`i\in[M]`$, and minimize the empirical risk
$`R^{\textnormal{sq}}_{t,M}(\theta)`$ defined as

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R_{t,M}^{\textnormal{sq}}(\theta)`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(\varphi_{t}(x_{i}^{(t)},y_{i}^{(t)},{y_{i}^{\prime}}^{(t)})-\phi_{\theta}(y_{i}^{(t)}|x_{i}^{(t)})\big)^{2}.`$ |  | (SQ) |

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle R_{t,M}^{\textnormal{nce}}(\theta)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{i}=1\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{i}^{\prime}=0\}}\right)\log\frac{\zeta_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{i})}{\zeta_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{i})+p}+\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{i}=0\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{i}^{\prime}=1\}}\right)\log\frac{p}{\zeta_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{i})+p}\Big],`$ |  | (NCE) |

where
$`v_{i}\sim\mathsf{Ber}(\widehat{P}_{x_{i}}(y_{i},y_{i}^{\prime}))`$,
$`v_{i}^{\prime}\sim\mathsf{Ber}(p)`$, $`p\in(0,1)`$ is a
hyperparameter, $`\zeta_{\theta}`$ is defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\zeta_{\theta}(x,y)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}\phi_{\theta}(y|x)-\frac{1}{\eta}\phi_{\theta_{t}}(y|x)-\beta\phi_{\theta_{\textnormal{ref}}}(y|x)-\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}Z_{t}(x),`$ |  |

and $`\mathbbm{1}_{\{A\}}`$ is the indicator function that equals 1 if
$`A`$ is true and 0 otherwise.

When we use the regression
objective ([SQ](#S4.Ex10 "Equation SQ ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
our WIND algorithm shares a similar form to SPPO ([Wu et al.,
2024b](#bib.bib49)). However, WIND differs from SPPO in the following
aspects: (i) we solve the regularized game with the KL regularization
term
$`\beta D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi^{\prime}\|\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\right)`$.
This term is crucial in practice and is also considered in other
iterative BOND methods ([Dong et al., 2023](#bib.bib15); [Sessa et al.,
2024](#bib.bib42)); (ii) our sampling scheme is more sample-efficient:
in SPPO, for each $`x_{i}`$, they sample $`K`$ responses
$`\{y_{i,j}\}_{j\in[K]}`$ to estimate
$`\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x_{i})}[P_{x_{i}}(y_{i,j},y^{\prime})]`$
by $`\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}P_{x_{i}}(y_{i,j},y_{i,k})`$ for each
$`j\in[K]`$. On the other hand,
Lemma [1](#Thmlm1 "Lemma 1 (Conditional mean minimizes the square loss). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
implies estimating the conditional mean with multiple samples is
unnecessary and for each $`x_{i}`$, sampling two responses $`y_{i}`$ and
$`y_{i}^{\prime}`$ is enough; (iii) we allow different risk functions
beyond the squared loss.

### 4.3 Convergence analysis

We provide a finite-sample complexity guarantee for
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
when the risk $`R_{t,M}=R_{t}^{\textnormal{sq}}`$ is the squared loss.
Our results could be easily extended to other risks. We define the
reward model approximation error $`\delta_{P}`$ as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{P}\coloneqq\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}},y,y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left|P_{x}(y,y^{\prime})-\widehat{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})\right|.
``` |  | (20) |

We require the following assumptions to prove the convergence of
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").
The first assumes $`\phi_{\theta}`$ is differentiable and $`\Theta`$,
$`Z_{t}`$ is bounded.

###### Assumption 2 (differentiability and boundedness).

The parameter space $`\Theta`$ is compact, $`\phi_{\theta}(y|x)`$ is
differentiable w.r.t. $`\theta`$ for any
$`(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}`$, and $`Z_{t}`$ in
([15](#S4.E15 "Equation 15 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is uniformly bounded, i.e., $`\exists Z\geq 0`$ such that
$`|Z_{t}(x)|\leq Z`$ for all $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$ and
$`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$.

Assumption [2](#Thmasmp2 "Assumption 2 (differentiability and boundedness). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
guarantees the (uniform) boundedness of $`\phi_{\theta}`$. Especially,
there exists $`L_{0}>0`$ such that for any
$`\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta`$ and
$`(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle|\phi_{\theta}(y|x)-\phi_{\theta^{\prime}}(y|x)|\leq L_{0}.`$ |  | (21) |

Assumption [2](#Thmasmp2 "Assumption 2 (differentiability and boundedness). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
also guarantees there exist $`L,C>0`$ such that for all
$`x\in{\mathcal{X}},y,y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}},\theta\in\Theta`$ and
$`t`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\|\nabla_{\theta}\left[\left(\varphi_{t}(x,y,y^{\prime})-\phi_{\theta}(y|x)\right)^{2}\right]\right\|_{2}\leq L
``` |  | (22) |

and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left(\varphi_{t}(x,y,y^{\prime})-\phi_{\theta}(y|x)\right)^{2}\leq C.
``` |  | (23) |

The next assumption controls the concentrability coefficient, which is
commonly used in the RL literature, see [Yuan et al.
(2023a)](#bib.bib55); [Munos (2003)](#bib.bib30); [Munos
(2005)](#bib.bib31); [Munos and Szepesvári (2008)](#bib.bib32); [Yang
et al. (2024b)](#bib.bib52) for example.

###### Assumption 3 (concentrability coefficient).

For
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
there exists finite $`C_{\pi}>0`$ such that for all $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$
and $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(\cdot|x)}\left[\left(\frac{\pi^{\star}_{\beta}(y|x)}{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)}\right)^{2}\right]\leq C_{\pi}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(\cdot|x)}\left[\left(\frac{\pi_{\theta_{t+1}}(y|x)}{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)}\right)^{2}\right]\leq C_{\pi}.`$ |  |

We define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle C_{1}\coloneqq\exp\left(\frac{2}{\beta}\left(\delta_{P}+\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}L_{0}+1\right)\right)C_{\pi}.`$ |  | (24) |

We also assume for every $`t`$, the expected risk $`R_{t}`$ and the
empirical risk $`R_{t,N}`$ both satisfy Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL)
condition, which has been proven to hold for over-parameterized neural
networks including transformers ([Liu et al., 2022](#bib.bib28); [Wu
et al., 2024a](#bib.bib48); [Yang et al., 2024c](#bib.bib53)).

###### Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition).

For all $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$, risk $`R_{t}`$ and empirical risk
$`R_{t,M}`$ both satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition with parameter
$`\mu>0`$, i.e., for all $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$ and $`\theta\in\Theta`$,
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla_{\theta}R_{t}(\theta)\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\mu\left(R_{t}(\theta)-R_{t}(\theta_{t+1}^{\star})\right)
``` |  |

and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla_{\theta}R_{t,N}(\theta)\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\mu\left(R_{t,M}(\theta)-R_{t,M}(\theta_{t+1})\right).
``` |  |

###### Remark 2 (Assumption [4](#Thmasmp4 "Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment") is satisfied with linear function approximation).

We consider a special case where
$`\phi_{\theta}(y|x)=\phi(x,y)^{\top}\theta`$ for all
$`(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}`$, where $`\phi(x,y)`$ are
the feature maps. If for all $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}\big[\phi(x,y)\phi(x,y)^{\top}\big]\geq\frac{\mu}{2}
``` |  |

and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\phi(x_{i}^{(t)},y_{i}^{(t)})\phi(x_{i}^{(t)},y_{i}^{(t)})^{\top}\geq\frac{\mu}{2},
``` |  |

then it is straightforward to verify that $`R_{t}`$ and $`R_{t,M}`$ are
both $`\mu`$-strongly convex, which indicates
Assumption [4](#Thmasmp4 "Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
holds ([Karimi et al., 2016](#bib.bib25)).

The following theorem gives the convergence of
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").

###### Theorem 4 (Convergence of Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

Let $`\theta_{0}=\theta_{\textnormal{ref}}`$ and $`\eta\in(0,\beta]`$ in
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").
Under Assumption
[1](#Thmasmp1 "Assumption 1 (expressive power). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),[2](#Thmasmp2 "Assumption 2 (differentiability and boundedness). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),[3](#Thmasmp3 "Assumption 3 (concentrability coefficient). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),[4](#Thmasmp4 "Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
for any $`T\in{\mathbb{N}}`$ and $`\delta\in(0,1)`$, with probability at
least $`1-\delta`$,
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
satisfies:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi^{\star}_{\beta}\|\pi_{\theta_{T}}\right)`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{1+\beta\eta}\right)^{T}D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi^{\star}_{\beta}\|\pi_{\theta_{0}}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\beta}\delta_{P}+\frac{2(1+\beta\eta)}{\beta\eta}\sqrt{C_{1}C_{r}\log\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}\sqrt{\frac{2L^{2}\log M}{\mu(M-1)}+\frac{C+2L^{2}/\mu}{M}},`$ |  | (25) |

where $`C_{r}`$ is an absolute constant, $`C_{1}`$, $`L`$,
$`\delta_{P}`$, $`C`$, $`\mu`$ are defined in
([24](#S4.E24 "Equation 24 ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
([22](#S4.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
([20](#S4.E20 "Equation 20 ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
([23](#S4.E23 "Equation 23 ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
Assumption [4](#Thmasmp4 "Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
respectively.

Theorem [4](#Thmthm4 "Theorem 4 (Convergence of Algorithm ). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
indicates that, assuming no model approximation error, the total sample
complexity for
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
to reach $`\varepsilon`$-accuracy is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
2MT=\widetilde{O}\left(\left(\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\beta\eta}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\mu}+C\right)C_{1}C_{r}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).
``` |  |

In contrast with SPPO ([Wu et al., 2024b](#bib.bib49)), which only
ensures average-iterate convergence without quantifying sample
efficiency, our method has stronger theoretical guarantees, offering
last-iterate convergence and explicit sample complexity bounds.

### 5.2 LLM alignment

We follow the experiment setup in [Wu et al. (2024b)](#bib.bib49) and
Snorkel¹¹ 1
[https://huggingface.co/snorkelai/Snorkel-Mistral-PairRM-DPO](https://huggingface.co/snorkelai/Snorkel-Mistral-PairRM-DPO).
We use Llama-3-8B-Instruct²² 2
[https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct)
as the base pretrained model for baseline comparisons. For fair
comparison, we chose the same prompt dataset UltraFeedback ([Cui et al.,
2023](#bib.bib14)) and round splits, and the same Pair-RM framework
([Jiang et al., 2023](#bib.bib23)) for the preference model as in [Wu
et al. (2024b)](#bib.bib49) and Snorkel. The learning rate is set to be
$`5\times 10^{-7}`$. In each iteration, we generate answers from
$`20000`$ prompts in the UltraFeedback dataset to train the model. The
global training batch size is $`64`$ ($`4`$ per device $`\times`$ $`16`$
GPUs). Our experiments are run on $`16`$ A$`100`$ GPUs, where each has
$`40`$ GB memory. We modify the per-device batch size and gradient
accumulation steps in SPPO GitHub repository³³ 3
[https://github.com/uclaml/SPPO](https://github.com/uclaml/SPPO) while
keeping the actual training batch size, to avoid out-of-memory error.

#### Baselines and Benchmarks.

We consider two baselines: SPPO ([Wu et al., 2024b](#bib.bib49)) and a
variant of J-BOND ([Sessa et al., 2024](#bib.bib42)). Here we follow the
exact same setting in their repository of the SPPO paper to reproduce
SPPO results, with the only change being that we use different
computation devices.

We consider 4 major evaluation benchmarks: GSM8k, HellaSwag, MMLU and
MT-Bench. They evaluated the following capability:

- •
  GSM8k ([Cobbe et al., 2021](#bib.bib13)) evaluates the mathematical
  reasoning at a grade school level.
- •
  HellaSwag ([Zellers et al., 2019](#bib.bib57)) measures the
  commonsense reasoning by letting language models select a choice to
  finish a half-complete sentence.
- •
  MMLU ([Hendrycks et al., 2020](#bib.bib21)) is a large-scale benchmark

## 6 Conclusion

This work establishes a unified game-theoretic framework that connects
iterative BoN with existing game-theoretic RLHF approaches. We present
WIND, a sample-efficient efficient algorithm for win rate dominance
optimization with finite-sample guarantees, which provides an
accelerated alternative to iterative BOND. Empirical validation on
multiple public benchmarks demonstrates the effectiveness and efficiency
of our approach compared to several state-of-the-art methods. In the
future, it is interesting to explore schemes that incorporate
exploration in the game-theoretic framework when the payoff matrix is
accessed using bandit feedbacks ([Yang et al., 2025](#bib.bib54)).

## Appendix A Other Objectives for Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

In this section we give some possible alternative objectives for
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
by utilizing different variational forms.

#### $`f`$-divergence objectives:

We could use $`f`$-divergence $`D_{f}`$ as the objective function. For a
convex function $`f`$, $`D_{f}`$ is defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle D_{f}(P||Q)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[f\left(\frac{P}{Q}\right)\right].`$ |  | (27) |

Let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U=(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad V=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if $r(x,y)>r(x,y^{\prime})$,}\\
0,\text{ if $r(x,y)<r(x,y^{\prime})$,}\\
z\sim Ber(1/2),\text{ if $r(x,y)=r(x,y^{\prime})$,}\end{cases}
``` |  |

Then $`V|U`$ is a function of $`y^{\prime}`$ and
$`P_{V|U}=\mathsf{Ber}(\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}}P_{x}(y,y^{\prime}))`$.
Further define

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\zeta_{\theta}(x,y)=\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}\phi_{\theta}(y|x)-\frac{1}{\eta}\phi_{\theta_{t}}(y|x)-\beta\phi_{\theta_{\textnormal{ref}}}(y|x)-\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}Z_{t}(x).
``` |  | (28) |

We let $`Q_{V|U}=\mathsf{Ber}(\zeta_{\theta}(y|x))`$, then by solving

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta_{t+1}=\arg\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{U}D_{f}(P_{V|U}||Q_{V|U}),
``` |  | (29) |

we could approximate the update
rule ([15](#S4.E15 "Equation 15 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

In particular, when $`f(x)=x\log x`$, we have $`D_{f}=D_{\text{KL}}`$,
and
([29](#A1.E29 "Equation 29 ‣ 𝑓-divergence objectives: ‣ Appendix A Other Objectives for Algorithm ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
becomes

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\theta_{t+1}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}\mathbb{E}_{v\sim P_{V|U}}\log\frac{P_{V|U}(v)}{Q_{V|U}(v)}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}\mathbb{E}_{v\sim P_{V|U}}[-\log Q_{V|U}(v)].`$ |  | (30) |

We could approximate the above objective by sampling
$`x_{i}\sim\rho,y_{i},y_{i}^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x_{i})`$
($`i\in[M]`$) and minimizing the empirical risk:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\theta_{t+1}=\arg\min_{\theta}R_{t,M}^{\textnormal{kl}}(\theta)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{i}=1\}}\log\zeta_{\theta}(x,y)+\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{i}=0\}}\log(1-\zeta_{\theta}(x,y))\right],`$ |  | (31) |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
v_{i}=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if $r(x_{i},y_{i})>r(x_{i},y_{i}^{\prime})$,}\\
0,\text{ if $r(x_{i},y_{i})<r(x_{i},y_{i}^{\prime})$,}\\
z\sim Ber(1/2),\text{ if $r(x_{i},y_{i})=r(x_{i},y_{i}^{\prime})$.}\end{cases}
``` |  | (32) |

For other $`f`$-divergence objectives, we may not be able to get rid of
the unknown $`P_{V|U}`$ on the RHS of
([29](#A1.E29 "Equation 29 ‣ 𝑓-divergence objectives: ‣ Appendix A Other Objectives for Algorithm ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
but
([29](#A1.E29 "Equation 29 ‣ 𝑓-divergence objectives: ‣ Appendix A Other Objectives for Algorithm ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
could provide a gradient estimator for the objective that allows us to
optimize $`\theta`$ by stochastic gradient descent.

#### Noise contrastive estimation (NCE) objectives.

We could use NCE ([Gutmann and Hyvärinen, 2010](#bib.bib20)) as
objectives. NCE is a method to estimate the likelihood of a data point
by contrasting it with noise samples. Let $`D_{\theta}`$ be the
discriminator (parameterized by $`\theta`$) that distinguishes the true
data from noise samples. The NCE objective is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim P_{\textnormal{data}}}[-\log D_{\theta}(z)]+\mathbb{E}_{z\sim P_{\textnormal{noise}}}[-\log(1-D_{\theta}(z))],
``` |  | (33) |

where the optimal solution of
([33](#A1.E33 "Equation 33 ‣ Noise contrastive estimation (NCE) objectives. ‣ Appendix A Other Objectives for Algorithm ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle D_{\theta^{\star}}(z)=\frac{P_{\textnormal{data}}(z)}{P_{\textnormal{data}}(z)+P_{\textnormal{noise}}(z)}.`$ |  |

In our case, we let $`P_{\textnormal{data}}=P_{V|U}`$ and
$`P_{\textnormal{noise}}=\mathsf{Ber}(p)`$ where $`p\in(0,1)`$ is a
hyperparameter. We also let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math

``` |  |

where $`\zeta_{\theta}`$ is defined in

|  | $`\displaystyle\theta_{t+1}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,y\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}\mathbb{E}_{v\sim P_{V|U}}\left[-\mathbbm{1}_{\{v=1\}}\log\frac{\zeta_{\theta}(x,y)}{\zeta_{\theta}(x,y)+p}-\mathbbm{1}_{\{v=0\}}\log\frac{p}{\zeta_{\theta}(x,y)+p}\right]`$ |  |  |

### B.1 Proof of Proposition [1](#Thmprop1 "Proposition 1 (existence of 𝜋^⋆_𝛽). ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

We first prove the case when $`\beta=0`$. This part of proof is inspired
by [Swamy et al. (2024, Lemma 2.1)](#bib.bib45). Let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi_{1}\coloneqq\arg\max_{\pi}\min_{\pi^{\prime}}P(\pi\succ\pi^{\prime}),\quad\pi_{2}\coloneqq\arg\min_{\pi^{\prime}}\max_{\pi}P(\pi\succ\pi^{\prime}),`$ |  |

i.e., $`(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})`$ is a Nash equilibrium of
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
(which is guaranteed to exist since the policy space is compact). Then
$`\forall\pi,\pi^{\prime}`$ and $`\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, we have:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{1,x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi^{\prime}_{x}\geq\pi_{1,x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{2,x}\geq\pi_{x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{2,x},
``` |  |

which is equivalent to

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
(\pi^{\prime}_{x})^{\top}P_{x}^{\top}\pi_{1,x}\geq\pi_{2,x}^{\top}P_{x}^{\top}\pi_{1,x}\geq\pi_{2,x}^{\top}P_{x}^{\top}\pi_{x}.
``` |  |

Note that

|     |                       |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       P_{x}+P_{x}^{\top}=J,
       ```                    |     | (36) |

where $`J\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{Y}}|\times|{\mathcal{Y}}|}`$ is the
matrix of all ones. This gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
-(\pi^{\prime}_{x})^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{1,x}\geq-\pi_{2,x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{1,x}\geq-\pi_{2,x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{x},
``` |  |

i.e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
(\pi^{\prime}_{x})^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{1,x}\leq\pi_{2,x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{1,x}\leq\pi_{2,x}^{\top}P_{x}\pi_{x},\,\,\forall\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}},\,\,\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}.
``` |  |

This implies $`(\pi_{2},\pi_{1})`$ is also a Nash equilibrium of
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
Then by the interchangeability of Nash equilibrium strategies for
two-player zero-sum games ([Nash, 1951](#bib.bib35)),
$`(\pi_{1},\pi_{1})`$ and $`(\pi_{2},\pi_{2})`$ are both the Nash
equilibria of
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
which indicates that $`\pi_{1},\pi_{2}`$ are both the solutions of
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

Next we prove the case when $`\beta>0`$. When $`\beta>0`$, due to the
strong concavity-convexity of the minmax
problem ([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
there exists a unique Nash equilibrium
$`(\pi_{1}^{\star},\pi_{2}^{\star})`$ of it. Moreover, it is
straightforward to compute that $`(\pi_{1}^{\star},\pi_{2}^{\star})`$
satisfies the following relation:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}:\quad\begin{cases}\pi_{1}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\propto\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\circ\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}P_{x}\pi_{2}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\right),\\
\pi_{2}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\propto\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\circ\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\beta}P_{x}^{\top}\pi_{1}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\right),\end{cases}
``` |  | (37) |

where we use $`\circ`$ to denote the element-wise product of two
vectors.

Again, using
([36](#A2.E36 "Equation 36 ‣ B.1 Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}:\quad\begin{cases}\pi_{1}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\propto\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\circ\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}P_{x}\pi_{2}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\right),\\
\pi_{2}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\propto\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\circ\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}P_{x}\pi_{1}^{\star}(\cdot|x)\right),\end{cases}
``` |  |

which implies $`(\pi_{2}^{\star},\pi_{1}^{\star})`$ is also a Nash
equilibrium of
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
By the uniqueness of the Nash equilibrium we immediately know that
$`\pi_{1}^{\star}=\pi_{2}^{\star}`$. Letting
$`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}=\pi_{1}^{\star}=\pi_{2}^{\star}`$, we have
$`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$
satisfies ([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

On the other hand, if $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ is the solution of
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
then $`(\pi^{\star}_{\beta},\pi^{\star}_{\beta})`$ satisfies
([37](#A2.E37 "Equation 37 ‣ B.1 Proof of Proposition ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
and thus is a Nash equilibrium of
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
In addition, by the uniqueness of
([9](#S3.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we deduce that
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
has a unique solution.

#### Step 1: show convergence for the no-mixing case.

for any policy $`\pi`$, $`\pi^{(n)}`$ has the expression

Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Iterative BoN. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

|     |                                                        |     |

|     |
       ``` math

       ```                                                     |     |

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|

``` math

``` |  |

Then for any $`y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)`$ and
$`y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}`$, we have
$`\overline{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})=1`$. In addition,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x):\quad\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{\text{ref},x}`$ | $`\displaystyle=1,`$ |  |

0,&\textnormal{otherwise}.\end{cases}`$ |  | (42) |

#### Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case.

|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{t+1}\log\pi_{0}(y|x)+(n-1)\alpha_{1}\sum_{i=0}^{t}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i}\log\left(\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{t-i,x}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+(1-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{t+1})\log\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)+c_{x}^{\prime\prime}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\log\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)+(n-1)\alpha_{1}\sum_{i=0}^{t}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i}\log\left(\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{t-i,x}\right)+c_{x}^{\prime\prime}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\log\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)+\frac{\eta}{1+\eta\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{1+\beta\eta}\right)^{i}\log\left(\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{t-i,x}\right)+c_{x}^{\prime\prime},`$ |  | (45) |

where $`c_{x},c_{x}^{\prime},c_{x}^{\prime\prime}`$ are constants that
depend on $`x`$, and the second equality makes use of
([41](#A2.Ex33 "In Step 1: show convergence for the no-mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
and the last equality follows from our choice of
$`\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}`$.

Note that for all $`\pi\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x):\quad\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{x}`$ | $`\displaystyle=1,`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\setminus{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x):\quad\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{x}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq 1.`$ |  |

For each $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, we let
$`y_{1}(x)\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)`$ such that
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y_{1}|x)=\max_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)`$.
For notation simplicity, when it does not cause confusion, we simply
write $`y_{1}(x)`$ as $`y_{1}`$.
([45](#A2.Ex40 "In Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
indicates that for all $`y\in{\mathcal{Y}}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\pi_{t+1}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{t+1}(y|x)}\right)=\log\left(\frac{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)}\right),\,\,\text{if }y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x),`$ |  | (46) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\pi_{t+1}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{t+1}(y|x)}\right)=\log\left(\frac{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)}\right)+\frac{\eta}{1+\eta\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{1+\beta\eta}\right)^{i}\log\left(\frac{1}{\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\pi_{t-i,x}}\right),\,\,\text{if }y\notin{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x).`$ |  | (47) |

Especially,
([47](#A2.E47 "Equation 47 ‣ Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
indicates that the ratio $`\frac{\pi_{t+1}(y|x)}{\pi_{t+1}(y_{1}|x)}`$
is decreasing with $`t`$ for all $`y\notin{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)`$.
Since it has a lower bound 0, we have that the ratio
$`\frac{\pi_{t+1}(y|x)}{\pi_{t+1}(y_{1}|x)}`$ converges as
$`t\rightarrow\infty`$ for all $`y\notin{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)`$.
Therefore,
([46](#A2.E46 "Equation 46 ‣ Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
together with
([47](#A2.E47 "Equation 47 ‣ Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
implies that
$`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{t+1}`$
exists. To see that $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$ is a solution to
([38](#A2.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ 2nd item ‣ Theorem 5 (solution to iterative BoN (formal)). ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we make use of the following lemma.

###### Lemma 2.

For any sequence $`\{a_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}`$ in $`\mathbb{R}`$ where
$`a_{t}\leq 0`$ for all $`t`$ and
$`a\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}a_{t}`$ exists ($`a`$ can be
$`-\infty`$), for any $`\alpha\in(0,1)`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}a_{t-i}=\frac{a}{1-\alpha}.
``` |  | (48) |

###### Proof of Lemma [2](#Thmlm2 "Lemma 2. ‣ Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").

If $`a=-\infty`$, then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}a_{t-i}\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}a_{t}=-\infty.
``` |  |

If $`a>-\infty`$, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}a_{t-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}a+\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}(\underbrace{a_{t-i}-a}_{e_{t-i}})=\frac{1-\alpha^{t+1}}{1-\alpha}a+\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}e_{t-i},
``` |  |

thus we only need to verify that
$`\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}e_{t-i}=0`$.

For any $`\epsilon>0`$, there exists $`N\in{\mathbb{N}}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall t\geq N:\quad\left|\sum_{i=N}^{t}\alpha^{i}e_{t-i}\right|\leq\frac{\alpha^{N}}{1-\alpha}b\leq\epsilon/2,
``` |  |

where $`b=\max_{i\geq N}|e_{i}|`$, and $`b<\infty`$ because $`e_{t}`$
converges to 0. We fix $`N`$ and choose $`T`$ such that for all
$`t\geq T`$, we have

|     |                                                |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha^{i}a_{t-i}\leq\epsilon/2.
       ```                                             |     |

Then for all $`t\geq T`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha^{i}e_{t-i}\right|`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha^{i}e_{t-i}\right|+\left|\sum_{i=N+1}^{t}\alpha^{i}e_{t-i}\right|\leq\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon.`$ |  |

This completes the proof. ∎

Let $`t\rightarrow\infty`$ on both sides of
([45](#A2.Ex40 "In Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
and by
Lemma [2](#Thmlm2 "Lemma 2. ‣ Step 2: show convergence of the mixing case. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}(y|x)\propto\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)\frac{1}{\beta}\log\left(\overline{P}_{x}(y,:)\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta,x}\right).`$ |  | (49) |

Note that for any $`x`$, the strongly-concave problem

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\max_{\pi_{x}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\log\left(\underset{y^{\prime}\sim\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}(\cdot|x)}{\mathbb{E}}\overline{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})\right)-\beta\textnormal{KL}(\pi_{x}||\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(\cdot|x))
``` |  |

has a unique solution $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta,x}`$. Therefore,
$`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$ is a solution to
([38](#A2.E38 "Equation 38 ‣ 2nd item ‣ Theorem 5 (solution to iterative BoN (formal)). ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
By
Remark [3](#Thmrmk3 "Remark 3. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
we know that
$`(\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta},\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta})`$ is a
Nash equilibrium of the log-win-rate
game ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ Game-theoretic perspective. ‣ 3.1 Iterative BoN as game solving ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

#### Step 3: bound the distance between $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ and $`\overline{\pi}^{\star}_{\beta}`$.

We let $`\pi^{(0)}=\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}`$ in
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

|  |  |  |  |

|  |  |  |  |

For any $`\pi\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}}`$, we have

we know that

$`y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\setminus{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)`$, and thus

$`\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{(t)}`$ exists and is the solution to

Also note that by

and the decreasing property of $`\pi^{(t)}(y|x)`$ for all

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}},\forall y\neq{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x):\quad\left(P_{x}(y_{1},:)-P_{x}(y,:)\right)\pi_{x}^{(t-i)}`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x).`$ |  |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|

Then by

we know that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}},\forall y\neq{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x):\quad\log\left(\frac{\pi_{\beta}^{\star}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{\beta}^{\star}(y|x)}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4\beta}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x),`$ |  |

which indicates that for all
$`y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\setminus{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)`$,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi_{\beta}^{\star}(y|x)`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\pi_{\beta}^{\star}(y_{1}|x)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4\beta}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4\beta}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)\right),`$ |  | (56) |

which gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\setminus{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\beta}^{\star}(y|x)\leq(|{\mathcal{Y}}|-|{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)|)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4\beta}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}(x)}\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x)\right).
``` |  |

Combining the above relation with
([51](#A2.E51 "Equation 51 ‣ Step 3: bound the distance between 𝜋^⋆_𝛽 and 𝜋̄^⋆_𝛽. ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),

``` |  |

and

from which we can see that
([40](#A2.E40 "Equation 40 ‣ 2nd item ‣ Theorem 5 (solution to iterative BoN (formal)). ‣ B.2 Proofs of Theorem and Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
holds.

### B.3 Proof of Theorem [3](#Thmthm3 "Theorem 3 (Linear last-iterate convergence of Algorithm , ( ) ). ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

To start with, we reformulate
problem ([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
as a monotone variational inequality (VI) problem. We first define the
operator
$`F_{x}:\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{Y}}|}`$
for all $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$ as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{x}(\pi_{x})\coloneqq-P_{x}\pi_{x}-\beta\log\pi_{\text{ref},x},\,\,\forall\pi_{x}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}.
``` |  | (61) |

We also let

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
h:\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,h(p)\coloneqq\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}
``` |  | (62) |

denote the negative entropy. The following lemma gives the VI form of
WIND.

###### Lemma 3.

Assume $`\beta>0`$ and
$`\pi^{(0)},\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\in\text{relint}(\Delta^{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{Y}})`$.
Then
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is equivalent to the following monotone VI problem:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\underset{x\sim\rho}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle F_{x}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x})+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{\beta,x}^{\star}),\pi_{x}-\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}\right\rangle\right]\geq 0,\,\,\forall\pi\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}},
``` |  | (63) |

where for all $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, $`F_{x}`$ is monotone and
1-Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the $`\ell_{1}`$-norm.

###### Proof of Lemma [3](#Thmlm3 "Lemma 3. ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").

By the proof of
Proposition [1](#Thmprop1 "Proposition 1 (existence of 𝜋^⋆_𝛽). ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
we know that when $`\beta>0`$ and
$`\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}\in\text{relint}(\Delta^{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{Y}})`$,
we have
$`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}\in\text{relint}(\Delta^{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{Y}})`$.
By the optimality condition, $`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ satisfies
([10](#S3.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ 3.2 Self-play and win rate dominance ‣ 3 A Unified Game-Theoretic View ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
if and only if

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f^{\star}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta}),\pi-\pi^{\star}_{\beta}\right\rangle\geq 0,\,\,\forall\pi,`$ |  | (64) |

where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{\star}(\pi)\coloneqq\underset{x\sim\rho}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\left\langle\pi_{x},\,-P_{x}\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\beta\log\pi_{\text{ref},x}+\beta\log\pi_{x}\right\rangle\big].
``` |  |

By
([61](#A2.E61 "Equation 61 ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
and
([62](#A2.E62 "Equation 62 ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we have
([64](#A2.E64 "Equation 64 ‣ Proof of Lemma . ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
equivalent to
([63](#A2.E63 "Equation 63 ‣ Lemma 3. ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
To see the monotonicity of $`F_{x}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left\langle F_{x}(\pi_{x})-F_{x}(\pi_{x}^{\prime}),\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime}\right\rangle`$ | $`\displaystyle=(\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime})^{\top}P_{x}(\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime})^{\top}\frac{1}{2}(P_{x}+P_{x}^{\top})(\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime})^{\top}\frac{1}{2}J(\pi_{x}-\pi_{x}^{\prime})=0,`$ |  | (65) |

where $`J\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{Y}}|\times|{\mathcal{Y}}|}`$ is the
matrix of all ones.

Furthermore, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}},\forall p,q\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}:\quad\left\|F_{x}(p)-F_{x}(q)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|P_{x}(p-q)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|p-q\right\|_{1},`$ |  | (66) |

where the second inequality follows from the fact that each entry of
$`P_{x}`$ only take its value in $`\{0,1/2,1\}`$. We finish up by noting
([66](#A2.E66 "Equation 66 ‣ Proof of Lemma . ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
indicates that $`F_{x}`$ is 1-Lipschitz with respect to
$`\ell_{1}`$-norm. ∎

We use the following proximal mirror descent ascent rule ([Sokota
et al., 2023](#bib.bib43); [Pattathil et al., 2023](#bib.bib38)) to
solve the monotone VI
problem ([63](#A2.E63 "Equation 63 ‣ Lemma 3. ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi^{(t+1)}=\arg\min_{\pi}\underset{x\sim\rho}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left\langle F_{x}(\pi^{(t)}_{x}),\pi_{x}\right\rangle+\beta h(\pi_{x})+\frac{1}{\eta}B_{h}(\pi_{x},\pi^{(t)}_{x})\bigg],`$ |  | (67) |

where $`\eta>0`$ is the learning rate, and the Bregman distance
$`B_{h}:\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\times\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}`$
is generated from the negative entropy $`h`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
B_{h}(p,q)\coloneqq h(p)-h(q)-\left\langle\nabla h(q),p-q\right\rangle=\text{KL}(p||q).
``` |  |

It’s straightforward to verify that the analytical solution of
([67](#A2.E67 "Equation 67 ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is
([13](#S4.E13 "Equation 13In Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
in
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment").
Note that the negative entropy $`h`$
(c.f. ([62](#A2.E62 "Equation 62 ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")))
is 1-strongly convex on $`\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}`$ with respect to the
$`\ell_{1}`$-norm ([Beck, 2017](#bib.bib6), Example 5.27). Furthermore,
Lemma [3](#Thmlm3 "Lemma 3. ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
shows $`F_{x}`$ is 1-Lipschitz with respect to $`\ell_{1}`$-norm. With
these facts, the theorem follows directly from [Sokota et al. (2023,
Theorem 3.4)](#bib.bib43):

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}:\quad\textnormal{KL}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta}(\cdot|x)||\pi^{(t)}(\cdot|x))\leq\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta\beta}\right)^{t}\textnormal{KL}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta}(\cdot|x)||\pi^{(0)}(\cdot|x)).`$ |  |

The relation
([14](#S4.E14 "Equation 14 ‣ Theorem 3 (Linear last-iterate convergence of Algorithm , ( ) ). ‣ 4.1 Exact policy optimization with last-iterate linear convergence ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
can be deduced from the above relation by taking the expectation over
$`x\sim\rho`$ on both sides.

### B.4 Proof of Lemma [1](#Thmlm1 "Lemma 1 (Conditional mean minimizes the square loss). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

To start with, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad\,\,\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-g(u))^{2}\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[\left((v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))+(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))\right)^{2}\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))\right]+\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))^{2}\right].`$ |  | (68) |

We use $`F(u)`$, $`F(u,v)`$ and $`F(v|u)`$ to denote the distribution of
$`u`$, the joint distribution of $`u,v`$ and the distribution of $`v`$
conditioned on $`u`$, resp. Then the cross term

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))\right]`$ | $`\displaystyle=\int_{(u,v)}(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))dF(u,v)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\int_{u}\left(\int_{v}v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)dF(v|u)\right)(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))dF(u)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=0,`$ |  | (69) |

where the last relation follows from the fact that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\int_{v}v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)dF(v|u)=\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)=0.
``` |  |

Combining
([69](#A2.Ex70 "In B.4 Proof of Lemma ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
and
([68](#A2.Ex68 "In B.4 Proof of Lemma ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-g(u))^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{u}\left[(\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)-g(u))^{2}\right]\geq\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[(v-\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u))^{2}\right],`$ |  |

and the equality holds if and only if $`g(u)=\mathbb{E}_{v}(v|u)`$
almost everywhere on the support set of $`F(u)`$.

### B.5 Proof of Theorem [4](#Thmthm4 "Theorem 4 (Convergence of Algorithm ). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

We first introduce the three-point property of the Bregman
divergence ([Sokota et al., 2023](#bib.bib43), Proposition D.1),
([Bauschke et al., 2003](#bib.bib5), Proposition 2.3):

###### Lemma 4 (three-point property of the Bregman divergence).

Let $`\psi:\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}`$ be a function
that’s differentiable on $`int(\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}})`$. Let
$`p,q\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}`$ and $`r,s\in int(\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}})`$.
Then the following equality holds:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{\psi}(r,s)+B_{\psi}(s,r)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla\psi(r)-\nabla\psi(s),r-s\right\rangle.`$ |  | (70) |
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{\psi}(p,r)`$ | $`\displaystyle=B_{\psi}(p,s)+B_{\psi}(s,r)+\left\langle\nabla\psi(s)-\nabla\psi(r),p-s\right\rangle.`$ |  | (71) |
|  | $`\displaystyle B_{\psi}(p,s)+B_{\psi}(q,r)`$ | $`\displaystyle=B_{\psi}(p,r)+B_{\psi}(q,s)+\left\langle\nabla\psi(r)-\nabla\psi(s),p-q\right\rangle.`$ |  | (72) |

To start with, we rewrite the update rule in
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\theta_{t+1}\leftarrow\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(\varphi_{t}(x_{i}^{(t)},y_{i}^{(t)},{y^{\prime}}^{(t)}_{i})-\phi_{\theta}(y_{i}^{(t)}|x_{i}^{(t)})\big)^{2}.`$ |  | (73) |

to a similar form as the update
rule ([67](#A2.E67 "Equation 67 ‣ B.3 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

#### Step 1: reformulate the update rule ([73](#A2.E73 "Equation 73 ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).

We let
$`\delta_{S}^{(t)},\delta_{P}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{X}}|\times|{\mathcal{Y}}|}`$
denote the statistical error and the model approximation error at the
$`t`$-th round, respectively:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}:\quad\delta_{S}^{(t)}(x,y)`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\phi_{\theta_{t+1}}(y|x)-\phi_{\theta_{t+1}^{\star}}(y|x),`$ |  | (74) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\delta_{P}^{(t)}(x,y)`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}\widehat{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}P_{x}(y,y^{\prime}).`$ |  | (75) |

We write
$`\delta_{S,x}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{X}}|},\delta_{P,x}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{X}}|}`$
as the shorthand of
$`\left(\delta_{S}^{(t)}(x,y)\right)_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}},\left(\delta_{P}^{(t)}(x,y)\right)_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}`$,
respectively.

The above
expression ([74](#A2.E74 "Equation 74 ‣ Step 1: reformulate the update rule (). ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
combined with
([18](#S4.E18 "Equation 18 ‣ Assumption 1 (expressive power). ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
gives

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi_{\theta_{t+1}}(y|x)`$ | $`\displaystyle\propto(\pi_{\theta_{t}}(y|x))^{\frac{1}{1+\beta\eta}}(\pi_{\textnormal{ref}}(y|x))^{\frac{\beta\eta}{1+\beta\eta}}\exp\left(\frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}\left(\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{\theta_{t}}(\cdot|x)}\widehat{P}_{x}(y,y^{\prime})+\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}\delta_{S}^{(t)}(x,y)\right)\right).`$ |  | (76) |

For notation simplicity, we let
$`\Pi\coloneqq\Delta^{{\mathcal{X}}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}`$ denote the whole
policy space. Note that the above relation is equivalent to

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{\theta_{t+1}}=\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\rho}\left[\left\langle\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x},\pi_{x}\right\rangle+\beta h(\pi_{x})+\frac{1}{\eta}B_{h}(\pi_{x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})\right],
``` |  | (77) |

where $`\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{Y}}|}`$ is
defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}:\quad\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x}\coloneqq\underbrace{-P_{x}\pi_{\theta_{t},x}-\beta\log\pi_{\text{ref},x}}_{=F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})\text{ by \eqref{eq:F_x}}}-\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}\delta_{S,x}^{(t)}-\delta_{P,x}^{(t)},
``` |  | (78) |

which could be seen as an approximation of
$`F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})`$. We let
$`\delta^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\mathcal{X}}||{\mathcal{Y}}|}`$ denote

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}}:\quad\delta^{(t)}_{x}\coloneqq\delta^{(t)}(x,\cdot)\coloneqq\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x}-F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})=-\frac{1+\beta\eta}{\eta}\delta_{S,x}^{(t)}-\delta_{P,x}^{(t)}.
``` |  | (79) |

The next step is to bound the distance between $`\pi_{\theta_{t}}`$ and
$`\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ utilizing the reformulated update
rule ([77](#A2.E77 "Equation 77 ‣ Step 1: reformulate the update rule (). ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
This part of our proof is inspired by [Sokota et al. (2023, Theorem
3.4)](#bib.bib43).

#### Step 2: bound $`D_{\textnormal{KL}}\left(\pi^{\star}_{\beta}\|\pi_{\theta_{t}}\right)`$.

By the first-order optimality condition we know that
([77](#A2.E77 "Equation 77 ‣ Step 1: reformulate the update rule (). ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
is equivalent to

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\langle\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x}+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x})+\frac{1}{\eta}(\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x})-\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})),\pi_{x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle\geq 0,\,\,\forall\pi\in\Pi,\,\,\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}},
``` |  | (80) |

Reorganizing the terms in
([80](#A2.E80 "Equation 80 ‣ Step 2: bound 𝐷_"KL"(𝜋^⋆_𝛽∥𝜋_𝜃_𝑡). ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left\langle\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x}+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}),\pi_{x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\eta}\left\langle\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})-\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}),\pi_{x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:three_point_2}}{=}\frac{1}{\eta}\left(-B_{h}(\pi_{x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})+B_{h}(\pi_{x},\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x})+B_{h}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})\right).`$ |  | (81) |

Let $`\pi=\pi^{\star}_{\beta}`$ in
([81](#A2.Ex74 "In Step 2: bound 𝐷_"KL"(𝜋^⋆_𝛽∥𝜋_𝜃_𝑡). ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
and reorganize the terms, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad\,\,B_{h}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x},\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq B_{h}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})-B_{h}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})+\eta\left\langle\widehat{F}^{(t)}_{x}+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=B_{h}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})-B_{h}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})+\eta\left\langle F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+\eta\left\langle\delta^{(t)}_{x},\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=B_{h}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})-B_{h}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x},\pi_{\theta_{t},x})+\eta\left\langle F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t},x})-F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+\eta\left\langle F_{x}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x})+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\delta^{(t)}_{x},\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{\theta_{t+1},x}\right\rangle,\,\,\forall x\in{\mathcal{X}},\,\,\forall\pi\in\Pi.`$ |  | (82) |

Note that for any $`\pi\in\Pi`$ and $`x\in{\mathcal{X}}`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left\langle F_{x}(\pi_{x})+\beta\nabla h(\pi_{x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{x}\right\rangle`$ | $`\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\langle F_{x}(\pi_{x})-F_{x}(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{x}\right\rangle}_{=0\text{ by~\eqref{eq:monotonicity}}}+\beta\left\langle\nabla h(\pi_{x})-\nabla h(\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}),\pi^{\star}_{\beta,x}-\pi_{x}\right\rangle`$ |  |  |

#### Step 3: bound the excess risk.

To bound the excess risk, we first introduce the concept of uniform
stability ([Bousquet and Elisseeff, 2002](#bib.bib8)). Suppose we have a
training dataset $`{\mathcal{D}}=\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{M}\}`$ where each
$`z_{i}`$ is sampled i.i.d. from some unknown distribution $`P`$ defined
on some abstract set $`{\mathcal{Z}}`$. Given $`{\mathcal{D}}`$, a
learning algorithm produces the decision rule
$`w_{M}=w_{M}({\mathcal{D}})=w_{M}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{M})\in{\mathcal{W}}`$,
where $`{\mathcal{W}}`$ is the set of all decision rules and is assumed
to be a closed subset of a separable Hilbert space. We use $`w_{M}`$ to
refer to both the algorithm and the decision rule. For the loss function
$`\ell:{\mathcal{Z}}\times{\mathcal{W}}\rightarrow[0,\infty)`$, we
define the risk and the empirical risk of $`w\in{\mathcal{W}}`$
respectively as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(w)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim P}\ell(z,w)\quad\text{and}\quad R_{M}(w)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\ell(z_{i},w).
``` |  | (96) |

###### Definition 1.

An algorithm $`w_{M}`$ is uniformly $`\gamma`$-stable, if for any
$`z,z^{\prime},z_{1},\cdots,z_{M}\in{\mathcal{Z}}`$ and any $`i\in[M]`$,
it holds that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|\ell(z,w_{M}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{M}))-\ell(z,w_{M}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{i-1},z^{\prime},z_{i+1},\cdots,x_{M}))|\leq\gamma.
``` |  | (97) |

We will also use the generalized Bernstein condition defined as follows:

###### Definition 2 (Assumption 1.1 in [Klochkov and Zhivotovskiy (2021)](#bib.bib26)).

Define $`{\mathcal{W}}^{\star}\coloneqq\argmin_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}R(w)`$
where $`{\mathcal{W}}`$ is a closed set. We say that
$`({\mathcal{W}},P,\ell)`$ satisfies the generalized Bernstein condition
if there exists some constant $`B>0`$ such that for any
$`w\in{\mathcal{W}}`$, there exists
$`w^{\star}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{\star}`$ that satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}_{z\in P}\left[(\ell(w,z)-\ell(w^{\star},z))^{2}\right]\leq B(R(w)-R(w^{\star})).
``` |  | (98) |

With the above two lemmas, we now introduce the following important
lemma that bounds the generalization error for uniformly stable
algorithms:

###### Lemma 5 (Theorem 1.1 in [Klochkov and Zhivotovskiy (2021)](#bib.bib26)).

Assume loss $`\ell`$ is bounded by $`C`$ on
$`{\mathcal{Z}}\times{\mathcal{W}}`$, and $`({\mathcal{W}},P,\ell)`$
satisfies the generalized Bernstein condition with the parameter
$`B`$ (c.f. Definition [2](#Thmdefinition2 "Definition 2 (Assumption 1.1 in ( ) ). ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")).
Let $`w`$ be a $`\gamma`$-stable
algorithm (c.f. Definition [1](#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1. ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
that returns $`w_{M}\in\argmin_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}R_{M}(w)`$ given the
training dataset $`{\mathcal{D}}`$. Then with probability at least
$`1-\delta`$, it holds that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R(w_{M})-\inf_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}R(w)\leq C_{r}\left(\gamma\log M+\frac{C+B}{M}\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right),
``` |  | (99) |

where $`C_{r}>0`$ is an absolute constant.

To proceed, we analyze the generalization error at the $`t`$-th iterate
of
Algorithm [3](#alg3 "Algorithm 3 ‣ Algorithm derivation with the squared risk. ‣ 4.2 Sample-efficient algorithm ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
for a fixed arbitrary $`t\in{\mathbb{N}}`$. We’ll let
$`\widehat{\theta}`$ denote $`\theta_{t+1}`$ and drop
superscript/subscript $`t`$ when this causes no confusion. For example,
we’ll simply write the update
rule ([73](#A2.E73 "Equation 73 ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{\theta}\leftarrow\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\varphi(x_{i},y_{i},{y^{\prime}}_{i})-\phi_{\theta}(y_{i}|x_{i})\right)^{2}.
``` |  |

For notation simplicity, we also let $`u_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})`$,
$`v_{i}=\varphi(x_{i},y_{i},{y^{\prime}}_{i})`$,
$`z_{i}=(u_{i},v_{i})\in{\mathcal{Z}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\times\mathbb{R}`$,
and let $`\phi_{\theta}(u_{i})`$ denote $`\phi_{\theta}(y_{i}|x_{i})`$
for all $`i\in[M]`$. Let $`{\mathcal{Z}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{X}}`$. Then
in our case, the loss function
$`\ell:{\mathcal{Z}}\times\Theta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}`$ has the
following form:

|     |                                                             |     |       |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \ell(z,\theta)\coloneqq\left(v-\phi_{\theta}(u)\right)^{2},
       ```                                                          |     | (100) |

where $`z=(u,v)\in{\mathcal{Z}}`$, and similar
as ([96](#A2.E96 "Equation 96 ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
our risk and empirical risk at the $`t`$-th itrerate satisfy:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall\theta\in\Theta:\quad R(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim P}\ell(z,\theta)\quad\text{and}\quad R_{M}(\theta)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\ell(z_{i},\theta),
``` |  | (101) |

where we let $`P`$ denote the distribution of $`z_{i}`$ ($`i\in[M]`$),
and we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta^{\star}=\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}R(\theta)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\theta}=\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}R_{M}(\theta).
``` |  | (102) |

Mote that
([22](#S4.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
implies that $`L(z,\theta)`$ is $`L`$-Lipschitz over $`\theta`$ for any
$`z\in{\mathcal{Z}}`$. Then by
Assumption [4](#Thmasmp4 "Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
and Remark 3 in [Kang et al. (2022)](#bib.bib24), we have that
$`(\Theta,P,\ell)`$ satisfies the generalized Bernstein condition with

|     |                       |     |       |
|-----|-----------------------|-----|-------|
|     |
       ``` math
       B=\frac{2L^{2}}{\mu}.
       ```                    |     | (103) |

Furthermore, Corollary 4 in [Charles and Papailiopoulos
(2018)](#bib.bib11) gives that, when
Assumption [2](#Thmasmp2 "Assumption 2 (differentiability and boundedness). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"),[4](#Thmasmp4 "Assumption 4 (Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). ‣ 4.3 Convergence analysis ‣ 4 Faster WIND ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")
hold, the empirical risk $`R_{M}`$ is $`\gamma`$-uniform stability
(c.f. Definition [1](#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1. ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
with

|     |                                 |     |       |
|-----|---------------------------------|-----|-------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \gamma=\frac{2L^{2}}{\mu(M-1)}.
       ```                              |     | (104) |

Substituting
([103](#A2.E103 "Equation 103 ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
and
([104](#A2.E104 "Equation 104 ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
into
([99](#A2.E99 "Equation 99 ‣ Lemma 5 (Theorem 1.1 in ( ) ). ‣ Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
we obtain that for any fixed $`t`$, with probability at least
$`1-\delta`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
R_{t}(\widehat{\theta})-R_{t}^{\star}\leq C_{r}\left(\frac{2L^{2}\log M}{\mu(M-1)}+\frac{C+2L^{2}/\mu}{M}\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right).
``` |  | (105) |

By the independence of the samples in different rounds we know that with
probability at least $`1-\delta`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall t\leq T-1:\quad R_{t}(\widehat{\theta})-R_{t}^{\star}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\left(\frac{2L^{2}\log M}{\mu(M-1)}+\frac{C+2L^{2}/\mu}{M}\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{1-(1-\delta)^{1/T}}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\left(\frac{2L^{2}\log M}{\mu(M-1)}+\frac{C+2L^{2}/\mu}{M}\right)\log\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right).`$ |  | (106) |

#### Step 4: put everything together.

Let

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\xi\coloneqq\sqrt{C_{1}C_{r}\left(\frac{2L^{2}\log M}{\mu(M-1)}+\frac{C+2L^{2}/\mu}{M}\right)\log\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}+\frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}\delta_{P}.
``` |  | (107) |

Then
([107](#A2.E107 "Equation 107 ‣ Step 4: put everything together. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment")),
([106](#A2.Ex116 "In Step 3: bound the excess risk. ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
and
([95](#A2.Ex114 "In Step 2: bound 𝐷_"KL"(𝜋^⋆_𝛽∥𝜋_𝜃_𝑡). ‣ B.5 Proof of Theorem ‣ Appendix B Proofs ‣ Faster WIND: Accelerating Iterative Best-of-𝑁 Distillation for LLM Alignment"))
together give the desired result.
````
