###### Abstract

Human beings solve complex problems through critical thinking, where
reasoning and evaluation are intertwined to converge toward correct
solutions. However, most existing large language models (LLMs) treat the
reasoning and verification as separate processes: they either generate
reasoning without explicit self-checking or rely on external verifiers
to detect errors post hoc. The former lacks immediate feedback, while
the latter increases system complexity and hinders synchronized
learning. Motivated by human critical thinking, we propose Stepwise
Think-Critique (STC), an end-to-end trainable framework in which a
single LLM emits a structured, step-level critique inline with each
reasoning step. STC is trained with reinforcement learning that
complements reasoning rewards with a critique-consistency reward derived
from final-answer correctness, jointly optimizing reasoning correctness
and critique reliability. On five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, STC
improves Pass@1 by 7.2% over the 1.5B base model and attains 67.4%
step-level critique F1, surpassing seven external process reward models
evaluated at their per-dataset oracle thresholds—a step toward LLMs with
built-in critical thinking.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities
in solving complex reasoning tasks. Techniques such as Chain of Thought
(CoT) prompting [Wei et al. (2022)](#bib.bib17) and reinforcement-based
reasoning approaches (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5)
enable models to decompose problems into multi-step reasoning
trajectories, leading to improved performance in domains such as
mathematical problem-solving, program synthesis, and complex
question-answering [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5); [Yu et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib20); [Chen et al. (2025)](#bib.bib1).

Despite these advances, a fundamental limitation persists: current
approaches lack an intrinsic, on-the-fly mechanism to assess the
correctness of intermediate reasoning steps. Such a mechanism is highly
desirable because i) in domains such as mathematics, science, and
finance, users require transparent judgments on whether each reasoning
step is correct; ii) integrated self-critique allows models to adapt
their reasoning trajectories dynamically, enabling self-assessment
during inference; and iii) stepwise evaluation could provide dense
feedback, facilitating more effective policy optimization.

Figure 1: Paradigms of reasoning and verification in LLMs. (a) _Pure
reasoning_: a model generates multi-step reasoning and answers without
assessment. (b) _Post-hoc verification_: reasoning is produced first and
then critiqued by an external verifier (e.g., process reward model),
which provides delayed feedback and increases system complexity. (c)
_Stepwise Think-Critique (ours)_: a single model outputs interleaved
reasoning and critique at each step, enabling timely self-assessment and
improved interpretability.

Most existing reasoning models [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5); [OpenAI
(2025)](#bib.bib13) focus solely on generating reasoning and final
answers, as illustrated in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM") (a).
Some studies have explored post-hoc verification, where an external
verifier evaluates the correctness of the reasoning process (see
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM") (b)).
For example, process reward models (PRMs) have been developed to
evaluate intermediate reasoning steps produced by a policy model [Zhang
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib24); [Zou et al. (2025)](#bib.bib28); [Khalifa
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8); [Xiong et al. (2025)](#bib.bib19). Although
such methods can detect errors, the post-hoc nature (i.e., the
separation of reasoning and verification) prevents timely feedback, and
maintaining separate reasoning policy and verifier models increases
system complexity, hindering seamless deployment in real-world
pipelines.¹¹ 1 In deployment, passing trajectories between models
requires additional scheduling, memory management, and fault tolerance
burdens, which increases system fragility.

The ability to interleave reasoning and evaluation is a hallmark of
human problem solving. Humans naturally engage in critical thinking,
continuously reflecting on intermediate conclusions, assessing their
validity, and adjusting subsequent steps accordingly. This process
allows humans not only to reach correct solutions more efficiently but
also to produce reasoning traces that are interpretable and trustworthy.
Inspired by this, we propose a novel framework called Stepwise
Think-Critique (STC). As illustrated in
Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM") (c),
unlike existing paradigms that separate reasoning and verification, our
method unifies them. At each step of problem solving, the model not only
generates the next reasoning step but also the justification for its
correctness. We instantiate our framework within an LLM that interleaves
reasoning and self-critique steps. The model is trained with RL using a
hybrid objective that combines reasoning rewards (based on the final
outcomes), critique-consistency rewards (encouraging the model’s
self-assessment over the final answer to align with the ground-truth
correctness), and format rewards for structured outputs. STC endows the
model with reasoning and critiquing capabilities.

Our work makes the following contributions:

- •
  We propose Stepwise Think-Critique (STC), a framework in which a
  single LLM emits a structured, step-level critique _inline with_ each
  reasoning step, eliminating the post-hoc reasoner–verifier split.
- •
  We design an end-to-end RL training scheme that jointly optimizes
  reasoning and critique within a single model: a critique-consistency
  reward derived from final-answer correctness is propagated only to
  critique tokens via masking, for accurate credit assignment.
- •
  On five math benchmarks, STC improves Pass@1 by 7.2% and attains 67.4%
  step-level critique F1 on average, outperforming seven external
  process reward models evaluated at their per-dataset oracle
  thresholds.

Our unified framework eliminates the requirement of an external verifier
and enables on-the-fly self-critique. This represents a crucial step
towards building LLMs with built-in critical thinking, bridging the gap
between human-like problem solving and reliable machine reasoning. At
inference time, we can optionally enable the critiquing for enhanced
interpretability—providing judgments on where and why mistakes occur—or
disable it to reduce token usage.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 RL for LLM Reasoning

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a central paradigm for eliciting
multi-step reasoning—“thinking”—in LLMs, with works such as
DeepSeek-R1 [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5), DAPO [Yu et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib20), and REINFORCE++ [Hu (2025)](#bib.bib6) reinforcing
sequences of reasoning actions that lead to correct solutions.

Actor–critic methods like VAPO [Yue et al. (2025)](#bib.bib21) use a
value model to estimate expected return but do not explicitly assess the
correctness of reasoning steps or provide interpretable feedback.
Existing RL frameworks thus lack intrinsic, fine-grained self-assessment
capabilities.

A separate line of work studies LLM _self-correction_, where an explicit
refinement pass consumes a response-level critique
signal—Self-Refine [Madaan et al. (2023)](#bib.bib12),
Self-Verification [Weng et al. (2023)](#bib.bib18), CRITIC [Gou et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib4) (tool-augmented), and SCoRe [Kumar et al.
(2024)](#bib.bib9). DeepSeek-R1 [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5) shows
reflection-style “wait” tokens can emerge mid-trajectory from
outcome-only RL, while such reflection is unstructured and unsupervised.

_STC pursues a different objective_: equipping a single model with an
intrinsic, consistency-supervised, step-level critique capability,
directly enabling error localization and interpretability without an
external verifier.

### 2.2 Process Reward Modelling

Process reward models (PRMs) provide another perspective on stepwise
evaluation, focusing on the intermediate reasoning steps rather than
solely on the final answer [Zhong et al. (2025)](#bib.bib27). By
offering finer-grained feedback, PRMs could improve credit assignment,
help identify local errors.

Designs of PRMs have evolved along multiple axes. Early approaches adopt
a discriminative format, where a classification head predicts step-level
correctness scores [Lightman et al. (2023)](#bib.bib10); [Zhang et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib24). More recent works embrace generative approaches,
producing natural language judgments for each reasoning step [Zhang
et al. (2024)](#bib.bib23); [Zheng et al. (2025)](#bib.bib26); [Khalifa
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8); [Zhao et al. (2025)](#bib.bib25).
ThinkPRM [Khalifa et al. (2025)](#bib.bib8), for instance, generates a
verification chain-of-thought (CoT) that critiques and justifies each
step post hoc, improving interpretability and error localization.

Beyond scoring individual steps, PRMs are also applied during policy
optimization, where they provide step-level reward signals that guide
model updates. Despite their utility, most PRMs are trained offline on
curated datasets and remain frozen, which can make them brittle under
distribution shifts. PRIME [Cui et al. (2025)](#bib.bib2) mitigates this
limitation by deriving implicit process rewards from outcome labels
through SFT, while TANGO [Zha et al. (2025)](#bib.bib22) jointly trains
a generator and PRM via RL. Nevertheless, these methods rely on separate
models, which adds complexity, communication overhead, and memory usage.
_In contrast, STC integrates the critic into a single model, avoiding
extra communication and deployment overhead._

## 3 Stepwise Think-Critique (STC)

### 3.1 Overview of STC

We introduce Stepwise Think-Critique (STC), a unified framework that
interleaves reasoning and critique within an output sequence. Unlike
prior paradigms that either perform pure reasoning or rely on external
post-hoc verification, STC equips a single model with the ability to
generate reasoning steps and simultaneously assess their correctness.

Formally, given an input problem $`x`$, the model generates a sequence
that alternates between reasoning step $`\{r_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$ and
critique step $`\{c_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$, where $`T`$ denotes the number of
steps that varies for different sequences. As illustrated in
Figure [2](#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"),
each reasoning step $`r_{t}`$ advances the solution, while the
corresponding critique $`c_{t}`$ provides a natural-language judgment
and score for assessing the correctness of $`r_{t}`$. The final solution
is produced in the last reasoning step, followed by its associated
critique.

We first train the model with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on our
synthesized reasoning–critique data, enabling it to learn the
interleaved reasoning-critique patterns and to acquire basic critique
ability (for cold start). We then further train the model with RL using
reasoning reward, critique-consistency reward, and format reward, to
boost critical thinking capability.

### 3.2 Interleaved Reasoning-Critiquing

For an LLM, to enable interleaved generation, we design instruction
formats that explicitly encourage the model to alternate between
reasoning and critique. Specifically, the prompt specifies the required
structure:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
r_{1}\rightarrow c_{1}\rightarrow r_{2}\rightarrow c_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow r_{T}\rightarrow c_{T}.
``` |  | (1) |

Reasoning steps are partitioned according to the generated \n\n. Each
critique step is expressed in natural language and consists of two
parts: (i) a concise justification, and (ii) a binary correctness label,
where $`1`$ denotes “Correct” and $`0`$ denotes “Incorrect.” The model
outputs critiques using the following tagged format:
\<critic\>{*justification*}\</critic\> and a binary judgment in the
format \<score\>{*1 or 0*}\</score\>. This structured format allows the
model to explain errors in context while keeping the output compact and
informative.

During inference, through prompting alone, we support two modes:

- •
  Compact mode: reasoning only, omitting critiques to save tokens.
- •
  Full mode: reasoning and critique are interleaved, providing detailed
  step-level judgment, enabling error localization and enhancing
  interpretability.

This dual-mode design offers a flexible trade-off between efficiency and
interpretability, allowing users to select the mode that best suits
their needs.

### 3.3 Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

We begin with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to equip the model with
interleaved reasoning-critique generation. We synthesize SFT data by
sampling reasoning trajectories $`(r_{1},\ldots,r_{T})`$ from the base
model on training problems, then prompting a strong LLM (e.g., GPT-5) to
produce stepwise critiques $`c_{n}=(j_{n},s_{n})`$ following the format
in
§[3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Interleaved Reasoning-Critiquing ‣ 3 Stepwise Think-Critique (STC) ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"),
where $`j_{n}`$ is a natural-language justification and
$`s_{n}\in\{0,1\}`$ a binary correctness label. To remove unreliable
supervision, a rule-based verifier $`V`$ checks the final answer
$`r_{T}`$, and we discard trajectories where the strong LLM’s score on
$`r_{T}`$ disagrees with $`V`$. We then fine-tune the model on the
resulting $`\mathcal{D}=\{(x,\tau)\}`$ with
$`\tau=(r_{1},c_{1},\ldots,r_{T},c_{T})`$ by minimizing
$`\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,\tau)\sim\mathcal{D}}\log\pi_{\theta}(\tau\mid x)`$.

### 3.4 RL with Joint Reasoning and Critiquing

While SFT provides initial alignment, it is limited by data coverage and
generalization. We therefore further post-train the model using Grouped
Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) with designed rewards.

#### 3.4.1 Reward Design

We define rewards at three levels:

*Reasoning reward:* We assign reward based on whether the answer is
correct or not:

|     |                                               |     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       R_{\text{reason}}(\tau)=\mathbbm{1}[r_{T}=y],
       ```                                            |     | (2) |

where $`y`$ is the ground-truth answer. $`\mathbbm{1}[\cdot]`$ denotes
indicator function.

*Critique-consistency reward (Critique reward):* Since no step-level
ground-truth critiques are available during training, we derive
supervision from the correctness of the final answer. Let
$`z\in\{0,1\}`$ be the true correctness indicator of the final answer
$`r_{T}`$, i.e.,

|     |                         |     |     |
|-----|-------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       z=\mathbbm{1}[r_{T}=y].
       ```                      |     | (3) |

Recall that $`s_{T}`$ denotes the binary label predicted by the critique
for the final answer (i.e., reasoning step $`r_{T}`$). We then define
the critique reward w.r.t. the final answer as

|     |                                              |     |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       R_{\text{crit}}(c_{T})=\mathbbm{1}[s_{T}=z],
       ```                                           |     | (4) |

which *encourages the model to produce critiques consistent with the
actual correctness of the final answer*. *Similar to the RL mechanism
that the final reasoning reward can drive the learning of intermediate
reasoning steps [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5), our critique reward
drives the learning of intermediate critic steps, which naturally
penalizes false judgments that do not lead to correct final critique.*

*Format reward:* To ensure structural validity, we require that each
reasoning step is followed by a well-formed critique (i.e., both
justification and binary label). Let $`v_{n}\in\{0,1\}`$ be an indicator
of whether the $`n`$-th reasoning–critique pair is correctly formatted.
The format reward is defined as the fraction of valid pairs among all
$`T`$ reasoning steps:

|     |                                                         |     |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       R_{\text{format}}(\tau)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{n=1}^{T}v_{n},
       ```                                                      |     | (5) |

where $`R_{\text{format}}(\tau)\in[0,1]`$ encourages the model to
consistently produce structurally correct interleaved outputs.

#### 3.4.2 Optimization

We leverage GRPO to update model $`\pi_{\theta}`$ to maximize expected
advantages. For an input problem $`x`$, the policy model samples a group
of $`G`$ individual trajectories $`\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{G}`$. *To ensure
correct credit assignment, we perform separate optimization for the
critique parts.*

Specifically, the critique advantage for the $`k`$-th trajectory is
computed by normalizing the group-level critique-consistency rewards:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{\text{crit}}^{(k)}=\frac{R_{\text{crit}}^{(k)}-\mathrm{mean}\!\left(\{R_{\text{crit}}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{G}\right)}{\mathrm{std}\!\left(\{R_{\text{crit}}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{G}\right)}.
``` |  | (6) |

The gradients from critique-consistency rewards are only back-propagated
to critique tokens within $`\{c_{n}^{(k)}|n=1,2,\cdots,T\}`$ for
accurate credit assignment. Therefore, the critique-consistency
advantage for the $`t`$-th token in $`\tau_{k}`$ is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}_{\text{crit}}^{k,t}=A_{\text{crit}}^{(k)}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\!\left[t\in\text{Critique}(\tau_{k})\right],
``` |  | (7) |

where $`t\in\text{Critique}(\tau_{k})`$ denotes that the $`t`$-th token
belongs to critique tokens.

Considering the critiques would influence the next step reasoning, the
reasoning reward and format reward are used to optimize all the tokens.
Similar to
([6](#S3.E6 "In 3.4.2 Optimization ‣ 3.4 RL with Joint Reasoning and Critiquing ‣ 3 Stepwise Think-Critique (STC) ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")),
we obtain reasoning advantage $`A_{\text{reason}}^{(k)}`$ and format
advantage $`A_{\text{format}}^{(k)}`$, respectively.

The combined advantage is then:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{A}_{k,t}=\lambda_{\text{crit}}\alpha\mathcal{A}_{\text{crit}}^{k,t}+\lambda_{\text{reason}}A_{\text{reason}}^{(k)}+\lambda_{\text{format}}A_{\text{format}}^{(k)},
``` |  | (8) |

where $`\lambda_{\text{crit}}`$, $`\lambda_{\text{reason}}`$,
$`\lambda_{\text{format}}`$ control contributions;
$`\alpha=\sum_{k}|\tau_{k}|/\sum_{k}|\tau_{k}^{\text{crit}}|`$ undoes
the dilution from GRPO’s token-level averaging
(Eq. [9](#A1.E9 "In Full GRPO objective. ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")
in Appendix A), since $`\mathcal{A}_{\text{crit}}^{k,t}`$ is supported
on only $`|\tau_{k}^{\text{crit}}|`$ tokens.

We optimize $`\mathcal{A}_{k,t}`$ via DAPO-style token-level GRPO [Yu
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20) with a KL constraint to a reference policy
(Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px4 "Full GRPO objective. ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"),
Eq. [9](#A1.E9 "In Full GRPO objective. ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")).

This joint objective encourages accurate reasoning, faithful critiques,
and well-structured outputs, enabling the model not only to solve
problems but also to verify and explain its reasoning process.

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

#### 4.1.1 SFT Data Construction

We construct the SFT dataset by sampling 10k base-model rollouts on the
training pool, generating stepwise critiques with GPT-5, and filtering
for structural integrity and answer-critique consistency, yielding 5,056
interleaved samples ($`\sim`$51% retention). Length statistics and the
full filtering pipeline are detailed in
Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1 "SFT data construction. ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM").

#### 4.1.2 Implementation Details

We use DS-Qwen-1.5B [Guo et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5) as the 1.5B-parameter
base model and train on DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset [Luo et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib11). Hyperparameters, max response lengths, and the
system prompt are in
Appendix [A](#A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2 "Hyperparameters and lengths. ‣ Appendix A Implementation Details ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM").

#### 4.1.3 Evaluation Protocol

We evaluate both the reasoning and critique capability of models.
Evaluations are performed with a maximum response length of 8,192 tokens
for reasoning and 4,096 tokens for critique by default.

For reasoning quality, we report Pass@1 (P@1 for short) and Pass@8.

For critique quality, we evaluate both the answer level and the process
level. At the answer level, correctness is directly verifiable from the
final solution (see
Eq. [4](#S3.E4 "In 3.4.1 Reward Design ‣ 3.4 RL with Joint Reasoning and Critiquing ‣ 3 Stepwise Think-Critique (STC) ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")).
At the process level, because step-level human labels are unavailable,
we use a strong LLM judge (GPT-5-mini) to produce pseudo labels for
stepwise critiques.

We report three metrics: Correct Accuracy (Corr.), Error Accuracy
(Err.), and their harmonic mean F1; higher is better for all
($`\uparrow`$). Corr. measures how well the critic accepts truly correct
items, Err. measures how well it rejects truly incorrect items.
Following [Zheng et al. (2025)](#bib.bib26), we use F1 to assess the
overall critic capability. This metric penalizes degenerate solutions
that achieve high correct accuracy but fail to detect errors, or vice
versa. Detailed metric definitions (including step-level and
answer-level) are provided in
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Evaluation Protocol Details ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM").

#### 4.1.4 Evaluation Datasets

We evaluate our framework on multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks:
AIME24, MATH-500, AMC23, Minerva, and OlympiadBench. These datasets vary
in problem complexity and problem styles, providing a comprehensive
testbed.

### 4.2 Results

We evaluate STC on three axes: intrinsic reasoning quality
(§[4.2.1](#S4.SS2.SSS1 "4.2.1 Reasoning Performance ‣ 4.2 Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")),
critique quality

#### 4.2.1 Reasoning Performance

Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")
reports reasoning accuracy across all benchmarks. i) SFT reduces average
Pass@1 accuracy by 2.1% relative to the base model. We attribute this
drop to enabling stepwise critique, which shifts the model’s output
distribution and temporarily degrades reasoning performance.
ii) Subsequent RL training recovers and surpasses this gap: *STC (full)*
outperforms *STC-SFT* by 9.3% at Pass@1 and 8.5% at Pass@8 on average,
and also exceeds the base DS-Qwen-1.5B by 7.2% Pass@1, showing that the
inline-critic objective is compatible with strong reasoning.

##### Reliability of the GPT-5-mini judge.

Since human-annotated step-level labels are unavailable, we employ
GPT-5-mini as an automatic judge for process-level evaluation, which may
introduce noise. To assess its reliability, we compare GPT-5-mini
judgments with verifiable answer-level ground truth on a subset,
obtaining an agreement rate of about 90%. This suggests that GPT-5-mini
provides reasonably reliable pseudo ground truth for stepwise critique
evaluation.

To further validate the reliableness of GPT-5-mini step labels, we use a
stronger (more expensive) LLM, Claude Opus 4.7 (with maximum reasoning
budget and no truncation), as a reference and re-evaluate the AIME24
labeled corpus. Treating Claude as the gold standard, GPT-5-mini
achieves a balanced F1 of 87.3% (Corr. 88.3%, Err. 86.4%, raw agreement
88.1%). To balance performance and cost, we use GPT-5-mini as the judge.
More analysis can be found in

## 5 Conclusion

We presented Stepwise Think-Critique (STC), a unified framework that
interleaves reasoning and self-critique within a single model. Across
multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks, STC demonstrates strong
reasoning and critiquing capabilities and produces more interpretable
process traces. These results support STC as a practical step toward
LLMs with built-in critical thinking, bridging the gap between
human-like problem solving and reliable machine reasoning.

We hope this work motivates further research on critical-thinking
mechanisms that jointly improve transparency, reliability, and reasoning
quality.

## 6 Limitations

While STC demonstrates promising results on mathematical reasoning,
several limitations remain.

##### Scale and single-seed evaluation.

We validate the framework on a single 1.5B-parameter base model
(DS-Qwen-1.5B) under a fixed random seed; multi-seed experiments were
not run due to compute cost (one full SFT+RL run requires $`{\sim}5`$
days on 16$`\times`$40GB A100). Extension to 7B/14B+ models and to
multimodal settings is left as future work.

##### Domain coverage.

Our experiments cover only text-only English mathematical reasoning
across five benchmarks. We do not evaluate STC on code synthesis,
agentic / tool-use tasks, multilingual reasoning, multimodal
(vision+math) problems, or open-ended generation. While the framework
itself is domain-agnostic, our claims about “critical thinking” should
be read narrowly as referring to step-level error detection in
mathematical chain-of-thought.

##### Critic reliability.

STC’s step-level balanced F1 of 67.4% leaves substantial room for
improvement, especially on Minerva (52.7% F1) where critic reliability
is weakest.

##### Step granularity vs. PRMs.

STC’s \n\n-delimited atomic steps are substantially finer than the
paragraph-level granularity of most external PRMs. The PRM comparison
(Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Stepwise critique as a dense reward. ‣ 4.2.2 Critique Performance ‣ 4.2 Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"))
therefore operates at different base rates across systems and should be
read as evidence that STC reaches a strong calibration-free operating
point, not as a direct step-by-step claim over PRMs in their native
granularity.

## 7 Ethical Considerations

STC is a methodology study evaluated on standard public mathematical
reasoning benchmarks; it does not introduce new user-facing systems,
sensitive datasets, or task domains beyond those already established in
the LLM reasoning literature. Nevertheless, we identify a concrete risk
that warrants disclosure.

Critique reliability. As shown in
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")
of the main manuscript, STC’s error-detection accuracy on the answer
level remains well below 100% (averaging 42.6%); a positive critique
should therefore not be interpreted as a correctness guarantee,
particularly in high-stakes settings such as education, tutoring, or
scientific computing where users may over-trust an explicit score-$`1`$
signal.

We do not foresee misuse pathways unique to STC beyond those already
common to LLM-based mathematical assistants.

## Appendix A Implementation Details

##### SFT data construction.

We randomly select 10k samples and generate one reasoning rollout per
sample using the base model. We then filter out samples with reasoning
token length larger than 8,192, and use GPT-5 to generate critiques for
each reasoning step. Next, we apply a three-stage filter to ensure: (i)
structural integrity (reasoning and critique blocks are strictly
paired); (ii) answer–critique consistency with the ground-truth critic.
This yields 5,056 high-quality samples (about 51% retention). For each
retained sample, we interleave reasoning and critique steps to construct
a unified trace. The dataset spans a wide range of complexities with
token lengths up to 15,130: about 75% of samples fall between 2,048 and
8,192 tokens, 24% exceed 8,192 tokens, and 10% exceed 10,240 tokens,
providing sufficient depth for complex multi-step reasoning.

##### Hyperparameters and lengths.

SFT uses batch size 64 for 550 steps. RL with GRPO uses group size
$`G{=}8`$, batch size 32, $`\lambda_{\text{format}}{=}0.05`$, and trains
for $`{\sim}1200`$ steps total (two-stage schedule below). Maximum
response lengths are 8,192 tokens for reasoning and 4,096 tokens for
critique. The system prompt is shown in
Table [11](#A3.T11 "Table 11 ‣ C.6 More Qualitative Examples ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")
(highlighted in orange).

##### RL training schedule.

For RL training, we use a two-stage schedule with different
hyperparameter settings. In Stage 1, we set $`\lambda_{crit}=0`$,
$`\lambda_{reason}=1`$, and $`\lambda_{format}=0.05`$, and train for
about 450 steps to improve reasoning ability and adherence to the
reasoning–critique format. In Stage 2, we alternate between two settings
every 40 steps for approximately 450 steps:
$`(\lambda_{reason},\lambda_{format},\lambda_{crit})=(1,0.05,0)`$ and
$`(0,0.05,1)`$. This stage is designed to improve both reasoning ability
and critique capability. We alternate these two objectives to mitigate
gradient conflict between reasoning and critique optimization.

##### Full GRPO objective.

The GRPO objective maximizes the expected normalized advantage under a
KL constraint to the reference policy:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tfrac{1}{\sum_{k}|\tau_{k}|}\!\!\sum_{k=1}^{G}\sum_{t=1}^{|\tau_{k}|}\!\Big(\mathcal{L}_{k,t}^{\mathrm{clip}}`$ |  | (9) |
|  | $`\displaystyle{}-\beta\,D_{\rm KL}\!\big(\pi_{\theta}(\tau_{k}|x)\,\|\,\pi_{\rm ref}(\tau_{k}|x)\big)\Big)\bigg],`$ |  |  |

where the expectation is taken over
$`(x,a)\sim\mathcal{D},\;\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}(\cdot\mid x)`$,
and
$`\mathcal{L}_{k,t}^{\mathrm{clip}}=\min\!\big\{\rho_{k,t}\mathcal{A}_{k,t},\;\mathrm{clip}(\rho_{k,t},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\mathcal{A}_{k,t}\big\}`$;
$`\rho_{k,t}=\frac{\pi_{\theta}({\tau}_{k,t}\mid x,{\tau}_{k,<t})}{{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}({\tau}_{k,t}\mid x,{\tau}_{k,<t})}`$;
$`\epsilon`$ and $`\beta`$ are hyperparameters. Here, similar to
DAPO [Yu et al. (2025)](#bib.bib20), we use token-level policy gradient
loss to optimize the model, where longer sequences can have more
influence.

## Appendix B Evaluation Protocol Details

We evaluate both the reasoning and critique capability of models.
Evaluations are performed with a maximum response length of 8,192 tokens
for reasoning and 4,096 tokens for critique by default.

For problem solving capability, we evaluate model reasoning performance
using Pass@1 (P@1 for short) and Pass@8, which capture single-attempt
accuracy as well as success rates across multiple sampled attempts.

For critique capability, we evaluate models at both the final answer
level and the reasoning-step level. i) For the final answer, the
correctness of the critique is directly verifiable (see discussion for
Critique-consistency reward in
§[3.4.1](#S3.SS4.SSS1 "3.4.1 Reward Design ‣ 3.4 RL with Joint Reasoning and Critiquing ‣ 3 Stepwise Think-Critique (STC) ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")).
ii) For intermediate reasoning steps, however, no human-annotated ground
truth exists. Therefore, we employ a strong LLM (GPT-5-mini) as an
automatic judge to assess the correctness of the model’s stepwise
critiques. We denote the pseudo ground-truth critic labels for a
trajectory as $`z^{g}=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{T})`$. Note that we use
GPT-5-mini instead of GPT-5 here in considering that GPT-5-mini is much
faster than GPT-5 while they achieve similar agreement rate with
verifiable critics when evaluated at answer level.

##### Sampling protocol for critique evaluation.

To bound LLM-judge cost (GPT-5-mini step labels are billed per token;
per-trajectory labeling cost is dominated by the $`\sim`$250 atomic
steps in each trajectory), we evaluate critique on a fixed sample of 240
STC trajectories per benchmark: the first 30 prompts of each dataset
$`\times`$ 8 STC rollouts per prompt. This sample is reused for all PRM
comparisons
(Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Stepwise critique as a dense reward. ‣ 4.2.2 Critique Performance ‣ 4.2 Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"))
and all critic tables
(Tables [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"), [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Full mode vs. compact mode. ‣ 4.2.1 Reasoning Performance ‣ 4.2 Results ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")).
AIME24 has exactly 30 prompts and is therefore fully covered; AMC23 (83
prompts), MATH-500 (500), Minerva (272), and OlympiadBench (675) are
each represented by their first 30 prompts. Reasoning (Pass@1/Pass@8) is
evaluated on each benchmark’s full prompt set. Extending critique
evaluation to full benchmark coverage is a straightforward follow-up
bounded only by LLM-judge cost.

Since our work is (to our knowledge) the first to unify stepwise
reasoning and critique in a single model, there is no off-the-shelf
evaluation metric. Previous PRM benchmarks and evaluation metrics [Zheng
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib26) are designed for post-hoc verification and
are not suitable for our evaluation since they assume a separate
verifier with access to full trajectories and gold step labels, while
our setting evaluates a jointly generated step-and-critique process.

Motivated by [Zheng et al. (2025)](#bib.bib26), we design task-aligned
metrics that measure correct accuracy (Corr.), error accuracy (Err.),
and their harmonic mean (F1).

Correct Accuracy (Corr.). Correct accuracy evaluates critic predictions
on instances whose ground-truth critic label is 1 (correct).

At the answer level, this measures the fraction of truly correct final
answers that are validated as correct by the critic. Formally, letting
$`s_{T_{j}}`$ denote the critic’s final prediction for sample $`j`$ and
$`z_{T_{j}}`$ its label,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\text{Corr.}=\frac{\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}(s_{T_{j}}=1\land z_{T_{j}}=1)}{\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}(z_{T_{j}}=1)}.
``` |  | (10) |

Note that each sample may have a different number of steps $`T_{j}`$.

At the process level, this measures the fraction of reasoning steps that
are correct according to pseudo ground-truth and are predicted as
correct by the critic. Letting $`s_{t}^{(j)}`$ denote the critic’s
prediction at step $`t`$ for sample $`j`$, we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\text{Corr.}=\frac{\sum_{j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{j}}\mathbf{1}(s_{t}^{(j)}=1\land z_{t}^{(j)}=1)}{\sum_{j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{j}}\mathbf{1}(z_{t}^{(j)}=1)}.
``` |  | (11) |

Error Accuracy (Err.). Similarly, error accuracy evaluates critic
predictions on instances whose ground-truth critic label is 0
(incorrect).

At the answer level, this measures the fraction of incorrect final
answers that are correctly rejected by the critic. Formally,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\text{Err.}=\frac{\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}(s_{T_{j}}=0\land z_{T_{j}}=0)}{\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}(z_{T_{j}}=0)}.
``` |  | (12) |

At the process level, this measures the fraction of reasoning steps that
are incorrect according to pseudo ground-truth and are predicted as
incorrect by the critic. Formally,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\text{Err.}=\frac{\sum_{j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{j}}\mathbf{1}(s_{t}^{(j)}=0\land z_{t}^{(j)}=0)}{\sum_{j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{j}}\mathbf{1}(z_{t}^{(j)}=0)}.
``` |  | (13) |

F1. Since critique evaluation is inherently imbalanced (correct
instances often dominate), we adopt the harmonic mean of correct
accuracy and error accuracy for overall critic capability evaluation:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\text{Balanced F1}=\frac{2\cdot\text{Corr.}\cdot\text{Err.}}{\text{Corr.}+\text{Err.}}.
``` |  |

This metric penalizes degenerate solutions that achieve high correct
accuracy but fail to detect errors, or vice versa.

emits $`R`$ reasoning tokens and $`C`$ critique tokens inline within a
single 1.5B model, costing $`\approx 2N_{R}(R{+}C)`$ FLOPs. Empirically,
on the 240 STC trajectories from AIME24 (30 prompts $`\times`$ 8
rollouts), $`C/R\approx 0.44`$.

AIME24 (30 problems) and AMC23 (83 problems) are small benchmarks of the

### C.5 Discussion on Stepwise Critique as Dense Rewards

Alternatively, we leverage stepwise critique judgments as dense rewards
to further improve learning of the reasoning process.

Concretely, the critique model produces a binary decision
$`s_{n}^{(k)}\in\{0,1\}`$ at each reasoning step $`n`$ of trajectory
$`\tau_{k}`$, indicating whether the reasoning step is correct or not.
We obtain step-level advantages by summing the normalized step rewards
of its following steps:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
A_{k}^{dense,n}=\sum_{n^{\prime}\geq n}\frac{{s_{n^{\prime}}^{(k)}}-\mathrm{mean}\!\left(\mathcal{S}\right)}{\mathrm{std}\!\left(\mathcal{S}\right)},
``` |  | (14) |

where
$`\mathcal{S}=\{{s_{n}^{(k)}}|n=1,2,\cdots,T^{(k)},k=1,2,\cdots,G\}`$
denotes the set including all step critique rewards within the group.

The stepwise reasoning advantage for the $`t`$-th token is

|     |                                                             |     |      |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{A}^{\text{dense}}_{k,t}=A_{k}^{\text{dense},s(t)},
       ```                                                          |     | (15) |

where $`s(t)`$ denotes the identification of the reasoning step index
that the $`t`$-th token belongs to.

The overall advantage for the $`t`$-th token of the trajectory
$`\tau_{k}`$ is then augmented by this dense critique signal as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}_{k,t}}=\mathcal{A}_{k,t}+\lambda_{\text{dense}}\mathcal{A}^{\text{dense}}_{k,t},
``` |  | (16) |

where $`\lambda_{\text{dense}}`$ balances the contribution of dense
stepwise feedback ($`\lambda_{\text{dense}}=0.5`$).

This extension transforms the optimization from relying solely on sparse
trajectory-level rewards to incorporating fine-grained, step-level
supervision. By providing dense reward shaping, the model receives
immediate reward signals for fine-grained optimization.

Finally, the GRPO objective maximizes the expected normalized advantage
under a KL constraint to the reference policy:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tfrac{1}{\sum_{k}|\tau_{k}|}\!\!\sum_{k=1}^{G}\sum_{t=1}^{|\tau_{k}|}\!\Big(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k,t}^{\mathrm{clip}}`$ |  | (17) |
|  | $`\displaystyle{}-\beta\,D_{\rm KL}\!\big(\pi_{\theta}(\tau_{k}|x)\,\|\,\pi_{\rm ref}(\tau_{k}|x)\big)\Big)\bigg],`$ |  |  |

where the expectation is taken over
$`(x,a)\sim\mathcal{D},\;\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}(\cdot\mid x)`$,
and
$`\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k,t}^{\mathrm{clip}}=\min\!\big\{\rho_{k,t}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}_{k,t}},\;\mathrm{clip}(\rho_{k,t},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\widetilde{\mathcal{A}_{k,t}}\big\}`$;
$`\rho_{k,t}=\frac{\pi_{\theta}({\tau}_{k,t}\mid x,{\tau}_{k,<t})}{{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}({\tau}_{k,t}\mid x,{\tau}_{k,<t})}`$;
$`\epsilon`$ and $`\beta`$ are hyperparameters.

We implemented this and
Table [10](#A3.T10 "Table 10 ‣ Inference compute and memory. ‣ C.3 More Comparisons of STC and External PRMs ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")
shows the results. Compared with plain STC, dense reward supervision
shifts average Pass@1 by $`+1.2\%`$ and average Pass@8 by $`-0.5\%`$.

We thus thought our existing implementation does not achieve a robust
gain. More effective reward shaping may be needed to unlock the
potential of stepwise critique as a dense reward, which we leave as
future work.
````

$`3{,}000\,\mathrm{pc}`$.” GPT-5-mini further flags steps 4 onward as
incorrect because the model continues to apply the wrong distance
(“Arithmetic using the incorrect distance is based on the wrong
conversion”), and the final boxed value as inconsistent with the correct
distance modulus $`\approx 27.39`$. The model arrives at the wrong final
answer 12.39 (ground truth 27.39).

## Appendix D Additional Discussion

### D.1 Computation Cost

We validate STC on a 1.5B model. We set the maximum response length as
8,192 tokens for reasoning and 4,096 tokens for critique. Running 1200
RL steps requires approximately 5 days on 16$`\times`$40GB A100 GPUs.

Our goal is to establish the core mechanism; the framework is
model-agnostic and extendable to other models. Due to limited resources,
we did not extend the validation to larger scale or multimodal
experiments. We hope this work motivates further research on
critical-thinking mechanisms.

### D.2 About Reward Hacking

One may wonder whether our scheme faces reward hacking. Actually, we did
not observe evidence of reward hacking in extensive inspection.

First, as shown in
Table [6](#A3.T6 "Table 6 ‣ C.1 Reliability of GPT-5-mini as a Judge ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"),
STC identifies 21% of final answers as incorrect, rather than collapsing
to predicting all answers as either correct or incorrect. Similarly, STC
identifies 23% of steps as incorrect on average, close to the pseudo
ground-truth ratio of 19.1% (see
Table [7](#A3.T7 "Table 7 ‣ C.1 Reliability of GPT-5-mini as a Judge ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM")).

Second, as shown in
Table [11](#A3.T11 "Table 11 ‣ C.6 More Qualitative Examples ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Stepwise Think-Critique: Interleaved Reasoning and Self-Critique in a Single LLM"),
our critic correctly flags a bogus alternative computation that the
model floats mid-trace, providing valuable information rather than
merely stylistic output.

### D.3 Inference Cost

The full mode interleaves critiques with reasoning and consumes
additional tokens at inference time. Naive deployment without exposing
the compact-mode fallback could increase cost, latency, and energy use
in user-facing applications. We recommend defaulting to compact mode and
enabling full mode only when interpretability is required.
