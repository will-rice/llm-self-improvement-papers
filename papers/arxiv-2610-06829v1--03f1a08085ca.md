###### Abstract

Open-source web agents are now strong enough to execute realistic
browser tasks, but training them with reinforcement learning still
depends on weak supervision: binary task success is too sparse for
credit assignment, while frontier-language-model judges are too
expensive to call at every step and cannot be assumed available at
deployment. We introduce CLIFT, a training and test-time scaling method
built around conformal self-verification. During training, the agent
answers natural-language verification questions about its own rollouts;
a Compositional Conformal Certifier keeps only question signals whose
URL-conditional evidence agrees with a training-time judge, assigns
signed trust weights through polarity-aware lift, and blends the
resulting verifier score into per-step rewards in a way that never
subtracts from the judge baseline. At test time, the same certified bank
is frozen and reused as structured evidence for Conformal Trajectory
Selection (CTS): the agent samples a greedy rollout and one or more
diverse retries, the self-verifier summarises each URL trace, and a
conservative majority-vote rule chooses whether to swap away from the
current incumbent without calling any external judge. This single
mechanism supports three settings. On WebArena Infinity, CLIFT achieves
state-of-the-art performance among open-source web agents. On
VisualWebArena, a bank trained with the open model transfers to GPT-5.5
at test time and reaches state-of-the-art performance under the
canonical harness. On Online Mind2Web, without training an agent on the
benchmark, translating the certified question bank improves a live-web
agent in zero-shot evaluation. Together these results position conformal
self-verification as a way to turn costly judge feedback into a reusable
training signal and a judge-free test-time scaling signal.

## 1 Introduction

Open-source web agents have reached the point where they can operate
realistic browser interfaces, but the verifier remains the bottleneck
for improving them with reinforcement learning. A binary success signal
is easy to deploy but too sparse for credit assignment: when all
rollouts in a GRPO group ([Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)) receive the
same outcome, the advantage estimate collapses. A frontier language
model judge ([Bai et al., 2022](#bib.bib5); [Liu et al.,
2023](#bib.bib19)) provides richer feedback, but it is costly during
training and cannot be assumed available at test time. This tension is
especially visible in web-agent benchmarks: WAI ([Zhou,
2026](#bib.bib48)) uses programmatic per-app verifiers, VWA ([Koh
et al., 2024](#bib.bib15)) uses outcome-based URL/DOM checks, and
live-web suites such as OM2W ([Xue et al., 2025](#bib.bib41)) depend on
noisy LLM judges. The policy is often not the only limiting object; the
way we score and select its rollouts can dominate the observed success
rate.

CLIFT asks whether the judge’s feedback can be turned into a reusable
verifier that the agent carries with itself, rather than a separate
process reward model ([Lightman et al., 2024](#bib.bib18); [Wang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib37)) or a post-hoc best-of-$`N`$ scorer ([Cobbe et al.,
2021](#bib.bib8); [Snell et al., 2025](#bib.bib33)). CLIFT maintains a
bank of natural-language verification questions about web-agent
behaviour and certifies which questions are reliable at which URL
states. The same bank is used in two places
(Figure [2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling")).
During training, certified bank evidence is blended with the comparative
judge to produce a denser per-step reward and URL-stratified advantages.
At test time, the trained agent runs a greedy trajectory plus diverse
retries; the frozen certified bank summarizes each URL trace; and a
conservative trajectory selector keeps one emitted trajectory per task.
The common object is not a new model but a calibrated verifier: a set of
questions, URL scopes, polarity signs, and conformal trust weights that
can be reused after training.

Figure 1: CLIFT concept. Vanilla GRPO broadcasts one trajectory-scalar
reward; CLIFT scores each visited URL with verification questions and
inherits the URL score to actions taken there. Full pipeline:
Figure [2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling").

The certifier is the part that makes this more than prompt engineering.
Each verification question carries a URL scope and a polarity sign
indicating whether YES is evidence of progress or failure. A Mondrian
adaptive conformal tracker ([Gibbs and Candès, 2021](#bib.bib12); [Vovk,
2012](#bib.bib35)) estimates whether that question separates high- and
low-judge rollouts at the relevant URL granularity. Certified questions
receive signed trust weights. During training this gives a verifier
reward that can only add evidence on top of the judge baseline,
preventing the reward-collapse failure of earlier linear blends. During
test-time scaling, the same signed evidence is frozen and presented to a
comparative selector over two completed trajectories. In both cases, the
question bank is a compact interface between expensive judge feedback
and the agent’s own rollouts.

We evaluate this idea in three complementary ways. On WAI, where
programmatic verifiers provide dense training supervision, CLIFT
improves a trainable open Gemma-4 agent and reaches the strongest public
9-app score we are aware of; the point is both training and CTS on the
same open backbone. On VWA, where the evaluation is more outcome-based,
we use the bank created during Gemma-4 training as a test-time verifier
for GPT-5.5, showing that the learned verification knowledge transfers
to a stronger closed backbone and reaches state-of-the-art performance
under the canonical harness. On OM2W, we push transfer further: no agent
is trained on OM2W; instead, the certified question bank is translated
and recalibrated, then used by CTS to improve live-web test-time
scaling. These three stories support the same claim: conformal
self-verification is useful both as a training signal and as a reusable,
judge-free test-time scaling signal. Related work is discussed after the
empirical section
(§[6](#S6 "6 Related work ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling")).

## 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories

Figure 2: CLIFT overview. _Training:_ rollouts are scored by a
comparative judge and by a self-verifier that queries a polarity-tagged
question bank. The CCC certifies question weights by URL tier, yielding
a signed verifier score that is asymmetrically added to the judge reward
and then converted into URL-stratified token advantages. _Test-time
scaling:_ the trained agent emits a greedy rollout and diverse retries;
the frozen certified bank produces per-URL evidence for each pairwise
comparison, and a conservative repeated-vote rule decides whether to
keep the incumbent or swap.

CLIFT is a GRPO-family policy-gradient method ([Shao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib29)) that changes how rollouts are scored and reused. It
turns natural-language verification questions into a conformally
certified URL-aware verifier, uses that verifier to add non-negative
per-step evidence during training, and freezes the same bank for CTS at
test time. Thus both halves of
Figure [2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling")
share one object: questions with URL scopes, polarity signs, and
certified trust weights.

### 2.1 The Conformal Verifier

A verification question (VQ) is a YES/NO check on the agent’s behaviour.
Each question $`q`$ has a URL pattern $`\pi_{q}`$ and polarity
$`\sigma_{q}\in\{-1,0,+1\}`$ indicating progress, failure, or ambiguity.
At training and test time, the agent’s base model answers applicable
questions with the task adapter detached. Answers at URL state $`u`$ are
aggregated into

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
R^{VQ}_{i}(u)=\!\!\!\sum_{q\in Q^{\mathrm{cert}}(u)}\!\!w^{q}_{\tau^{\star}(u)}\cdot\mathbb{1}\!\big[y^{q}_{i}{=}\mathrm{YES}\big]\;\in\;[-1,+1],
``` |  | (1) |

where $`Q^{\mathrm{cert}}(u)`$ contains certified in-scope questions and
$`w^{q}_{\tau^{\star}(u)}`$ is the most-specific certified trust weight.
Positive weights indicate progress, negative weights indicate failure,
and an empty set gives $`R^{VQ}_{i}(u)=0`$.

#### Certification.

The same question can be reliable on one app and useless on another, so
the CCC keeps a Mondrian-ACI tracker ([Vovk, 2012](#bib.bib35); [Gibbs
and Candès, 2021](#bib.bib12)) for each question and URL tier
$`\tau\in\{\text{global},\text{host},\text{URL-path}\}`$. The
non-conformity score is polarity-aware:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle s_{t}^{q,\tau}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}\!\left[\,\mathrm{pred}_{q}(y^{q}_{t})\neq z_{t}\,\right],\quad z_{t}=\mathbb{1}[\hat{R}^{\mathrm{judge}}_{t}>\theta_{j}],`$ |  | (2) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{pred}_{q}(\mathrm{YES})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}[\sigma_{q}{=}{+}1],\quad\mathrm{pred}_{q}(\mathrm{NO})=\mathbb{1}[\sigma_{q}{=}{-}1].`$ |  |  |

Positive questions conform when YES agrees with a high judge score;
negative questions conform when YES agrees with a low score. Neutral
questions do not certify. A tier $`\tau`$ of question $`q`$ is certified
when

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
n^{q}_{t,\tau}\geq n_{\min}\;\,\wedge\;\,\alpha^{q}_{t,\tau}<\alpha^{\star}\;\,\wedge\;\,\big|\mathrm{lift}^{q}_{t,\tau}\big|>\mathrm{lift}_{\min}\;\,\wedge\;\,\sigma_{q}\neq 0,
``` |  | (3) |

where $`\mathrm{lift}^{q}_{t,\tau}`$ is the mean judge-score difference
between YES and NO examples at tier $`\tau`$; $`n_{\min}`$,
$`\alpha^{\star}`$, and $`\mathrm{lift}_{\min}`$ are calibration
hyperparameters reported with the experimental setup. Certified
questions receive

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
w^{q}_{\tau}=\sigma_{q}\cdot\big|\mathrm{lift}^{q}_{t,\tau}\big|\cdot\max\!\Big(0,\,1-\tfrac{\alpha^{q}_{t,\tau}}{\alpha^{\star}}\Big)\;\in\;[-1,+1],
``` |  | (4) |

with $`w^{q}_{\tau}=0`$ for uncertified tiers. At URL $`u`$ we query the
most specific certified tier, falling back URL-path $`\to`$ host $`\to`$
global.

#### Bank growth.

The bank starts from a judge-grounded seed set of VQs, each tagged with
a polarity. At periodic training intervals, a CEGAR-style loop ([Clarke
et al., 2000](#bib.bib7)) proposes a small batch of new URL-scoped
questions separating top and bottom rollouts. New questions enter
uncertified. A sanity veto demotes polarity to neutral when observed
lift contradicts the tag:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma_{q}\leftarrow 0\quad\text{if}\quad n_{q}\geq n_{\mathrm{veto}}\;\wedge\;\mathrm{sgn}\!\big(\mathrm{lift}^{q}\big)\neq\sigma_{q}^{\mathrm{LLM}}\;\wedge\;\big|\mathrm{lift}^{q}\big|>\delta.
``` |  | (5) |

The same lift estimate therefore certifies questions and catches
polarity mistakes.

### 2.2 Training: Per-step Rewards and URL-stratified Advantages

For each task, one comparative judge call scores a GRPO group of $`G`$
rollouts and returns trajectory scores $`R^{\mathrm{judge}}_{i}`$ and
per-step scores $`r^{i,\mathrm{judge}}_{t}`$. In parallel, the
self-verifier answers bank questions for each rollout and URL state. The
training reward is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{r}^{i}_{t}\;=\;r^{i,\mathrm{judge}}_{t}\;+\;\beta\cdot\max\!\big(0,\,R^{VQ}_{i}(u^{i}_{t})\big),
``` |  | (6) |

where $`\beta`$ controls the verifier contribution. Since the verifier
term is clipped at zero,
$`\tilde{r}^{i}_{t}\geq r^{i,\mathrm{judge}}_{t}`$: certified evidence
can strengthen the judge signal, but cannot drag it down.

#### URL-stratified per-token advantage.

We discount step rewards into returns
$`G^{i}_{t}=\sum_{\ell=t}^{T_{i}-1}\gamma^{\ell-t}\,\tilde{r}^{i}_{\ell}`$
with discount $`\gamma`$, then group-normalise within each
*(rollout-group, step-position, URL-state)* cell:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{A}^{i}_{t}=\frac{G^{i}_{t}-\bar{G}_{u^{i}_{t}}}{\sigma_{G,u^{i}_{t}}+\varepsilon}.
``` |  | (7) |

Advantages are broadcast to tokens over tool-call spans; tokens outside
those spans receive zero gradient. The GRPO update is unchanged, but
credit assignment becomes URL-local.

#### Training-time judge.

The judge sees compact traces with human-readable actions; opaque
browser-use object strings collapse judge variance. One prompt sees all
$`G`$ rollouts and returns trajectory scores, step scores, and
rationales for CEGAR. The judge is training-only and is not called by
CTS.

### 2.3 Test Time: CTS (Conformal Trajectory Selection)

At deployment the external training judge is dropped. The policy emits
candidate rollouts under a small budget $`K`$: a greedy incumbent
$`\rho_{A}`$ and one or more diverse retries $`\rho_{B}`$. CLIFT then
uses the frozen bank and base self-verifier in a pairwise selector to
decide whether to keep the incumbent or swap to the retry, avoiding
brittle step-wise selection. For $`K{>}2`$, the same selector is applied
as a tournament chain that maintains one incumbent and still emits a
single trajectory.

For each rollout $`\rho\in\{\rho_{A},\rho_{B}\}`$, let
$`U(\rho)=(u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{T_{\rho}})`$ be its sequence of
distinct URL states (consecutive duplicates collapsed). The verifier
produces a per-state evidence record at every $`u_{t}\in U(\rho)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
E_{t}(\rho)=\Big\{\,\big(q,\;w^{q}_{\tau^{\star}(u_{t})},\;\mathrm{lift}^{q}_{\tau^{\star}(u_{t})},\;y^{q}(\rho,u_{t})\big)\;:\;q\in Q^{\mathrm{cert}}(u_{t}),\;\pi_{q}\sim u_{t}\,\Big\},
``` |  | (8) |

where $`y^{q}(\rho,u_{t})`$ is the verifier’s YES probability on the
partial trajectory. We keep this as structured evidence rather than a
single scalar.

The evidence sheets, action traces, and final states are passed to the
self-verifier in a comparative prompt, which outputs A (keep greedy), B
(swap), or TIE. This uses the bank as structured evidence instead of a
hand-designed URL-trace scalar.

To prevent verifier variance from causing harmful swaps, each pairwise
comparison uses a small odd number $`m`$ of independent self-verifier
calls and applies a conservative majority rule:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{CTS}(\rho_{A},\rho_{B})=\begin{cases}\rho_{B}&\text{if at least }\lceil(m+1)/2\rceil\text{ calls vote B},\\
\rho_{A}&\text{otherwise (including ties and majority-A votes)}.\end{cases}
``` |  | (9) |

The asymmetric default is the safety valve: ties and uncertainty keep
$`\rho_{A}`$. If the bank-conditioned comparison is above chance, then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{SR}(\mathrm{CTS})]\;\geq\;\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{SR}(\rho_{A})]-\epsilon(m,\delta),
``` |  | (10) |

with $`\epsilon`$ decreasing as the majority vote becomes more reliable.
Here $`m`$ is the number of verifier votes in a pairwise comparison,
whereas $`K`$ denotes the rollout budget reported in the result tables.
CTS swaps only when evidence repeats; otherwise it defaults to greedy.

Training and test time therefore share one abstraction: per-URL-state
CCC-certified bank evidence. Training aggregates it into rewards and
advantages; CTS aggregates it through a comparative selector. The bank
is frozen after training but remains a reusable representation of what
the judge taught the agent to notice.

## 3 Experimental Design

We evaluate CLIFT as both a training method and a test-time scaling
method.

| Benchmark | Training role | Eval role           |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| WAI       | train Gemma-4 | in-domain + board   |
| VWA       | train bank    | transfer to GPT-5.5 |
| OM2W      | none          | zero-shot transfer  |

Table 2: Experimental roles. OM2W supplies no reported policy-training
data.

WAI is the primary training setting: its self-hosted SaaS-style apps
expose programmatic verifiers that can support online RL and
public-board comparison. VWA is the controlled transfer setting: its
Reddit, Shopping, and Classifieds environments use outcome-based URL/DOM
checks, so we test whether a bank learned while training an open model
remains useful when applied to GPT-5.5 at inference. OM2W is the
live-web transfer setting: it contains 300 public-domain tasks judged by
an LLM rubric, and no reported policy is trained on OM2W data.

### 3.1 Task synthesis and bank construction

Training tasks are synthesized without reading evaluation tasks. WAI
seeds come from app descriptions; VWA seeds come from SynthAgent ([Wang
et al., 2026](#bib.bib38)) site exploration. Two frontier LLMs expand
the seeds, and a shared filter removes malformed, duplicate, hardcoded,
pure-lookup, and trivial-navigation tasks. The reported checkpoint
trains on $`1{,}886`$ WAI tasks and $`1{,}272`$ VWA tasks across $`16`$
LoRA shards. OM2W is excluded from policy training and used only for
bank transfer and CTS.
Appendix [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 CLIFT Training Setup ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling")
gives CLIFT training details.

### 3.2 Per-benchmark protocols

The three benchmarks probe complementary properties of the same verifier
bank: WAI tests whether certified self-verification can *train* a
stronger open agent, VWA tests whether the trained-on-open bank
*transfers* to a frozen closed backbone, and OM2W tests whether the same
bank *recalibrates* for live-web zero-shot selection.

WAI. We train Gemma 4 31B + LoRA with the full URL-path / host / global
CCC hierarchy. The comparison isolates three effects: the base
browser-use backbone, vanilla GRPO with the same comparative judge but
no certified bank, and CLIFT with certified step-level feedback. We then
apply CTS to the trained checkpoint to measure whether the frozen bank
gives an additional no-regression test-time selection gain on the same
in-domain tasks.

VWA. We use the bank learned during open-model training as structured
evidence for a stronger closed backbone. GPT-5.5 is not updated and sees
the same completed-task interface; the only added component is CTS over
finished trajectories. This setting isolates whether the bank stores
transferable web-interaction knowledge rather than merely improving the
Gemma-4 policy that produced it.

OM2W. We translate and recalibrate the WAI/VWA bank for live-web
domains, then run CTS directly at inference. Reported rows use upstream
Gemma-4 or GPT-5.5 weights with no OM2W-specific LoRA. Question
translation rewrites benchmark-specific URL patterns into the live-web
equivalents, drops predicates that no longer apply, and adds
OM2W-specific failure-mode questions for bot-block pages, hallucinated
extraction, partial-filter application, and wrong-final-answer stops.
The recalibration pass scores each translated or new question on a
held-out OM2W rollout sample using the same lift-based certifier as
training, so any reported gain comes from cross-benchmark transfer of
the certified question taxonomy plus the calibration recipe, not from
policy training on OM2W.

the setting with the most direct supervision to the setting with no

improves an open trainable web agent; VWA asks whether the bank learned
during open-model training remains useful for a stronger closed model;
OM2W asks whether the same bank can be translated and recalibrated for
live-web zero-shot selection.

The cross-benchmark result also clarifies what transfers. The policy

into structured evidence for a new live-web environment. Because the
bank is just a list of URL-scoped, polarity-tagged questions with
calibrated trust weights, recalibration on a new domain only requires a
sample of judge-scored rollouts; no policy update is needed to begin
selecting trajectories. Detailed bank construction and transfer tables

The decomposition further shows that the gain is not explained simply by
additional interaction or inference-time search. Vanilla GRPO improves

policy during training, while CTS intervenes selectively at test time
when the certified bank provides sufficient evidence to prefer an
alternative rollout.

## 5 Discussion and future work

CLIFT’s failures are intended to be visible. On WAI and VWA, many URL
states accumulate enough observations for signed trust weights; on OM2W,
the noisier live-web judge leaves more questions uncertified. This
refusal is a diagnostic rather than a silent collapse: it says the
current judge, state partition, or question bank does not support
reliable distillation, and keeps noisy verifier evidence out of both the
training reward and the CTS selector.

The study is still bounded. We train one open-weight backbone and rely
on a frontier judge during training and bank construction; if that judge
is systematically biased, calibration can detect disagreement only
through observed data. The transfer results show that the bank can help
stronger closed-source agents, but not that one bank is universal across
all web domains. Stronger open web agents also carry misuse risk, so
deployment should retain rate limits and domain-specific safeguards.

A natural next step is to replace natural-language questions with
executable browser-state assertions written by a coding agent. The agent
would learn not only to act on the web, but also to write compact checks
that can be executed during rollouts and reused for test-time selection,
reducing dependence on a separate self-verification bank while
preserving auditability.

## 6 Related work

CLIFT sits at the intersection of web-agent benchmarks, RL credit
assignment, test-time search, and reliable verifier-based selection. The
closest systems usually improve one component of this loop: stronger web
interfaces for agents, denser reward signals for training, or calibrated
decision rules for deployment. CLIFT instead studies how one certified
verification bank can connect these components, carrying judge feedback
from online RL into test-time trajectory selection.

#### Web agents and test-time search.

Language agents combine reasoning, browser actions, and tools ([Nakano
et al., 2021](#bib.bib21); [Yao et al., 2023b](#bib.bib45); [Shinn
et al., 2023](#bib.bib30); [Schick et al., 2023](#bib.bib27); [Qin
et al., 2024](#bib.bib24)); search methods add deliberation or tree
expansion at inference time ([Wei et al., 2022](#bib.bib39); [Yao
et al., 2023a](#bib.bib44); [Zhou et al., 2024a](#bib.bib47); [Snell
et al., 2025](#bib.bib33); [Song et al., 2024](#bib.bib34)); and modern
web, software, desktop, and embodied benchmarks stress realistic
interfaces ([Zhou et al., 2024b](#bib.bib49); [Koh et al.,
2024](#bib.bib15); [Deng et al., 2023](#bib.bib10); [Yao et al.,
2022](#bib.bib43); [Shridhar et al., 2021](#bib.bib31); [Yang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib42); [Xie et al., 2024](#bib.bib40)). CLIFT differs from
prompt-level search because its selector reuses a certified per-URL bank
learned during RL training, not just more samples.

#### RL credit assignment and process rewards.

GRPO and related RLVR methods improve LLMs from scalar outcomes
([Schulman et al., 2017](#bib.bib28); [Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib29);
[DeepSeek-AI, 2025](#bib.bib9); [Qi et al., 2025](#bib.bib23)), while
agent variants refine credit assignment through stratification, anchor
states, success rates, or Monte-Carlo values ([Zhu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib50); [Feng et al., 2025](#bib.bib11); [Chen et al.,
2025](#bib.bib6); [Kazemnejad et al., 2025](#bib.bib14)).
Process-verifier and rich-feedback methods provide denser supervision
([Cobbe et al., 2021](#bib.bib8); [Lightman et al., 2024](#bib.bib18);
[Wang et al., 2024](#bib.bib37); [Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib13);
[Madaan et al., 2023](#bib.bib20); [Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)),
and LLM judges provide it for open-ended generations ([Bai et al.,
2022](#bib.bib5); [Liu et al., 2023](#bib.bib19); [Zheng et al.,
2023](#bib.bib46); [Rafailov et al., 2023](#bib.bib26)). CLIFT keeps
GRPO but changes the reward object: judge feedback is augmented by a
certified self-verifier and normalised by URL state.

#### Conformal reliability and specification mining.

Conformal prediction gives finite-sample reliability guarantees ([Vovk
et al., 2005](#bib.bib36); [Angelopoulos and Bates, 2021](#bib.bib3);
[Gibbs and Candès, 2021](#bib.bib12); [Vovk, 2012](#bib.bib35)), with
recent adaptations to language modeling, decision making, off-policy
prediction, LLM-agent routing, and LLM judging ([Quach et al.,
2024](#bib.bib25); [Lekeufack et al., 2024](#bib.bib17); [Kuipers
et al., 2024](#bib.bib16); [Si et al., 2026](#bib.bib32); [Badshah
et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)). Those works calibrate outputs, policies, or
judgments; CLIFT calibrates the *verification questions* themselves,
using URL scope, polarity, and a Mondrian-ACI trust weight. A
CEGAR-style loop ([Clarke et al., 2000](#bib.bib7)) turns judge
rationales into reusable specifications for both training and test-time
selection.

## 7 Conclusion

CLIFT turns expensive judge feedback into a reusable verifier for web
agents: conformally certified URL-state evidence densifies training
reward, then the frozen bank supports judge-free Conformal Trajectory
Selection. Across WAI, VWA, and OM2W, the same bank helps train an open
model, transfers to a stronger closed model, and recalibrates for
live-web selection without policy fine-tuning. A trajectory score is
consumed once; a certified bank can be inspected, recalibrated, and
reused.

The bank is the durable artefact behind all three results. Each
certified question carries a URL scope, a polarity sign, and a trust
weight derived from observed lift, with provenance back to the judge
rationale that introduced it. This makes the bank small enough to ship
with the agent, auditable when a result looks off, and recoverable: when
a question’s lift collapses on a new distribution, the certifier
withholds trust rather than amplifying noise. Together these properties
give one knob, namely what the judge taught the agent to notice, that
persists from training reward to test-time selection, from open backbone
to closed backbone, and from sandboxed verifiers to live-web judges, and
that stronger future judges can grow without discarding what earlier
rounds taught.

### A.2 CLIFT Training Setup

This subsection documents the training setup for the reported CLIFT
checkpoint, organised into the four moving parts of the system: the
backbone and the task pool that drives RL rollouts; the compute budget
and shard layout; the per-rollout reward and verification loop; and the
certification rule that turns the running bank into the deployment
object loaded by CTS at test time. The concrete hyperparameter values
used by all four stages are listed separately in
§[A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Reported CLIFT Hyperparameters ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling").

#### Backbone and shard layout.

The reported CLIFT checkpoint trains Gemma-4-31B-it with a rank-8 LoRA
adapter. We use $`16`$ single-node shards, each with its own browser
workers, reward pipeline, and LoRA adapter. After training, the
per-shard adapters are averaged element-wise and merged into the base
model for evaluation. OM2W is excluded from policy training entirely; we
use it only for bank transfer and CTS at evaluation.

#### Seed pool.

The training pool is synthesised from scratch without reading any
evaluation task. WAI seeds ($`186`$ tasks) are drawn from each
application’s schema and user-facing documentation; VWA seeds ($`206`$
tasks) come from a separate SynthAgent crawl that probes the three
Docker environments via header-only navigation. Both seed sets describe
what the apps *can do*, not what the public benchmarks *ask the agent to
do*.

#### Frontier-LLM expansion.

Two frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.5) expand each seed into a
draft pool of roughly $`20{\times}`$ candidates per seed. Each expansion
call sees only the seed’s schema or crawl trace and a small list of
formatting constraints; it never sees the public benchmark task list,
evaluator code, or any reference answer.

#### Four-stage filter.

A shared filter then removes (i) *malformed* tasks that fail to parse or
execute against a sandbox replay, (ii) *near duplicates* under
embedding-based dedup at cosine $`0.92`$, (iii) *hardcoded* tasks whose
answers are already present verbatim in the seed schema, and
(iv) *trivial-navigation* tasks that resolve in a single URL change
without any UI interaction or form input. What remains is the
$`1{,}886`$ WAI and $`1{,}272`$ VWA training tasks used for all reported
runs.

#### Train/eval isolation.

At no point during synthesis, filtering, sharding, or RL training did
the pipeline read WAI, VWA, or OM2W evaluation task descriptions,
evaluation HTML/DOM snapshots, or evaluation reward signals. The eval
suites are first loaded only after all training runs and adapter
averaging are complete, and only by the read-only evaluator.

#### Compute resources.

Training ran as shard-parallel single-node jobs on H200 machines; at
peak we used eight nodes, each with $`8{\times}`$H200 GPUs, and
completed $`16`$ shard jobs across the WAI and VWA training pools.
Within each node, resources were split between vLLM serving, LoRA
optimization, and browser-rollout processes. Closed-backbone VWA and
OM2W evaluations used GPT-5.5 API inference for rollouts and CTS
candidates, but did not fine-tune the closed model.

#### Rollout and verification loop.

Each prompt produces a GRPO group of $`G=8`$ browser rollouts. One
comparative batch judge call scores the group at step level and supplies
reasoning for CEGAR. In parallel, the base Gemma-4 model answers
verification questions with the LoRA adapter detached, so verifier
answers come from the same backbone without using the trained task
adapter.

#### Certification, reward, and deployment object.

For each question, CCC maintains global, host, and URL-path Mondrian-ACI
trackers with target $`\alpha^{\star}=0.10`$ and lift-based trust
weights; certification uses $`n_{\min}=50`$ and
$`\mathrm{lift}_{\min}=0.02`$. Certified verifier evidence is added
through the asymmetric per-step blend, so it can support the judge
reward but cannot subtract from it. The resulting URL-stratified returns
are written to token advantages over tool-call spans, and the frozen
certified bank is the deployment object reused by CTS at test time.

### A.3 Reported CLIFT Hyperparameters

The method section uses symbolic quantities; the concrete values used
for the reported runs are: judge threshold $`\theta_{j}=0.5`$; GRPO
group size $`G=8`$; certification target $`\alpha^{\star}=0.10`$;
minimum certification count $`n_{\min}=50`$; minimum lift
$`\mathrm{lift}_{\min}=0.02`$; CEGAR interval $`M=60`$ training steps;
CEGAR batch size 3 to 5 questions; reward blend $`\beta=0.15`$; return
discount $`\gamma=0.95`$; CTS vote count $`m=3`$ with self-verifier
temperature $`0.7`$; greedy incumbent temperature $`0`$; diverse retry
temperature $`1.0`$; and seed-bank sizes $`281`$ WAI questions and
$`89`$ VWA questions before filtering and certification.

This appendix tracks the construction, certification, and transfer of
the verification banks that drive both training and test-time selection.
For each benchmark we report the seed bank size, the size of the
candidate bank after CEGAR-driven growth across training shards, the
certified subset retained by the conformal certifier, and (for OM2W
only) the subset that survives filtering and rewriting for live-web
reuse. The same bank object is read by the training reward
(§[2.2](#S2.SS2 "2.2 Training: Per-step Rewards and URL-stratified Advantages ‣ 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling"))
and by CTS at test time
(§[2.3](#S2.SS3 "2.3 Test Time: CTS (Conformal Trajectory Selection) ‣ 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling"));
the numbers below describe how that object grows and shrinks at each
stage.

#### WAI and VWA training banks.

The WAI training bank starts with $`281`$ seed verification questions
derived from app schemas and user documentation; the VWA training bank
starts with $`89`$ seed questions from SynthAgent site exploration of
the three Docker environments. Both banks grow through the CEGAR
refinement loop, in which the judge proposes $`3`$ to $`5`$ new
questions every $`M{=}60`$ training steps targeting rollouts that the
existing bank fails to discriminate, and shrink through certification:
questions whose lift falls below $`0.02`$ at all tiers, or whose
polarity is contradicted by observed lift, are demoted to neutral or
dropped. After cross-shard merging, the WAI candidate bank totals
$`992`$ unique questions, of which $`44`$ retain non-zero trust weights
at one or more URL tiers and form the certified WAI bank used by the
training reward and by CTS at test time. The VWA candidate bank totals
$`1{,}565`$ unique questions with $`21`$ certified at the end of
training. The $`36`$ WAI and $`48`$ VWA questions reported in
Table [7](#A1.T7 "Table 7 ‣ OM2W transfer setting. ‣ A.4 Bank Details and OM2W Transfer ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling")
are the portion of each bank that the filter/rewrite stage judged
general enough to reuse on live-web OM2W: certified questions kept as-is
($`7`$ WAI, $`10`$ VWA) plus rewritten variants of certified or
promising uncertified questions ($`29`$ WAI, $`38`$ VWA). The remainder
stay in their original training banks because their URL patterns or DOM
references are app- or environment-specific and do not transfer.

To make the bank concrete, we walk through six representative certified
questions drawn from the merged WAI and VWA banks, using the same
prompt-style format as the agent and judge templates above. Each block
shows the question identifier, the URL pattern at which it applies (\* =
global; the bank also contains per-host and per-URL-path refinements
such as \*google.com\* and \*reddit.com/r/\* that share the same text

judge-scored observations $`n`$ that supported certification, followed

#### Q.1: Reaching content-bearing pages (WAI, $`w{=}{+}0.54`$).

The single strongest signal in the bank. Rollouts that visit a page
containing task-relevant content are far more likely to receive a high
judge score than rollouts that loiter on app homepages or navigation
menus, and this pattern holds across all 9 WAI apps.

> Identifier: made_meaningful_progress_toward_goal
> URL pattern: \*
> Trust weight: $`w=+0.54`$, observations: $`n=82`$.
> Did the agent reach at least one page or state that contains
> information directly relevant to answering or completing the task (not
> just the app homepage or navigation menus)?

#### Q.2: Constructive non-destructive behaviour (WAI, $`w{=}{+}0.54`$).

A two-part check that distinguishes "did nothing harmful" from "did
something productive". Rollouts that take destructive shortcuts (delete,
cancel, revoke) without a constructive counterpart fail this question
even when the final URL looks plausible.

> Identifier: avoided_destructive_unintended_actions
> URL pattern: \*
> Trust weight: $`w=+0.54`$, observations: $`n=82`$.
> Did the agent both (a) avoid performing destructive actions (delete,
> cancel, revoke) not required by the task AND (b) take at least one

#### Q.3: Source-grounded final answer (WAI, $`w{=}{+}0.27`$).

Targets a common failure mode in information-retrieval tasks: emitting
an answer without ever opening the page that actually contains the
requested fact. Certified at high $`n`$, so the verifier has stable
evidence at the global tier.

> Identifier: extracted_info_from_correct_page
> URL pattern: \*
> Trust weight: $`w=+0.27`$, observations: $`n=520`$.
> For information-retrieval tasks, did the agent extract its final
> answer from the page that actually contains the requested information?

#### Q.4: End-state coherence (WAI, $`w{=}{+}0.26`$).

A simple URL coherence check that catches "wandered off" rollouts in
which the agent ends on an unrelated section of the app. Cheap to answer
for the base verifier (it only needs the final URL and the task
description) and stable across apps.

> Identifier: final_page_matches_task_context
> URL pattern: \*
> Trust weight: $`w=+0.26`$, observations: $`n=238`$.
> Is the final URL or page the agent ended on relevant to the task
> objective rather than an unrelated section of the app?

#### Q.5: Active search behaviour (VWA, $`w{=}{+}0.20`$).

A VWA-specific predicate. Distinguishes agents that submit a query and
then act on the results from agents that issue a search and freeze on
the results page without clicking through. Especially discriminative on
the Classifieds and Shopping environments.

> Identifier: used_search_and_acted_on_results
> URL pattern: \*
> Trust weight: $`w=+0.20`$, observations: $`n=66`$.
> Did the agent both submit a search query AND subsequently take a
> meaningful action on the results (e.g., clicking a result link rather
> than just observing the search page)?

#### Q.6: Navigation depth (VWA, $`w{=}{+}0.13`$).

Many VWA tasks require going past the search-results page to a specific
listing or product detail page to extract a price, image, or
description. This question rewards depth of navigation rather than
breadth and is the highest-trust VWA question whose URL pattern is
already general enough to translate to live-web OM2W without rewriting.

> Identifier: visited_target_detail_page
> URL pattern: \*
> Trust weight: $`w=+0.13`$, observations: $`n=200`$.
> Did the agent navigate to a specific item detail page (product page,
> listing page, or submission page) relevant to the task, beyond the
> search results page?

These six illustrate three properties the certifier rewards: the
question must discriminate between high- and low-judge rollouts
(positive lift), it must be answerable from the trajectory by the base
self-verifier (not too low-level for the LoRA-detached Gemma-4 to read
off), and its YES/NO must align with the judge’s separation under the
chosen polarity. Questions that fail these tests, e.g., ones that ask
about UI elements specific to a single page or that the base model
cannot reliably answer, are demoted to neutral or dropped by the
polarity-veto rule of
Eq. [5](#S2.E5 "In Bank growth. ‣ 2.1 The Conformal Verifier ‣ 2 CLIFT: Conformal Lift-calibrated Introspective Filtering of Trajectories ‣ CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling").

#### Transferred question taxonomy.

The filter/rewrite stage keeps general web-interaction predicates where
possible and removes benchmark-specific app names or URL patterns when
they do not transfer. The expansion stage adds questions for live-web
failure modes observed on OM2W, including bot-block pages, timeout or
wandering behaviour, hallucinated extraction, partial-filter
application, search-vs-detail-page confusion, and wrong-final-answer
stops. The resulting 132-question bank uses the same schema as the
training-time bank, so CTS loads it without code changes.

#### Recalibration.

Each question is scored on a stratified pass/fail rollout sample using
the base Gemma-4 verifier with logprob-extracted YES probabilities. Lift
is computed as the pass/fail separation of the YES probability.
Questions whose absolute lift is below the discrimination floor remain
in the bank with zero weight; they can be re-certified later on a
different rollout sample. Among the 100 certified OM2W questions, 75
have positive lift and 25 have negative lift, so the selector receives
both progress evidence and failure-mode evidence.
````
