# Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation

Aryan Das Affiliation: IIT Kanpur    Vinay Kumar Verma Affiliation: IIT
Kanpur    Aakansha Mishra Affiliation: Amazon

###### Abstract

Self-distillation methods for large language models with an exponential
moving average (EMA) teacher already retain a history of student
updates. Muon nevertheless stores a separate momentum buffer, increasing
persistent training memory. We propose Muon with Reused Teacher
(Muon-RT), which reuses the teacher–student displacement to eliminate
momentum buffers for hidden weight matrices. We show that this
displacement encodes an exponentially weighted history of applied
updates and smooths temporally uncorrelated noise under a local model.
Muon-RT removes its radial component, normalizes it spectrally, and
mixes it with the current gradient after Newton–Schulz (NS)
orthogonalization. Under explicit regularity and alignment assumptions,
we establish linear contraction of an expected objective-gap bound
toward a residual error level. With Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT)
on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Muon-RT improves average accuracy over AdamW by
2.6 points with 42% less persistent memory, and over momentum Muon by
5.4 points with 27% less persistent memory. With Self-Distillation
Policy Optimization (SDPO) on Olmo-3-7B-Instruct, it improves average
accuracy over AdamW by 2.0 points.

## 1 Introduction

Figure 1: Muon-RT with SDFT on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. (a) Average accuracy
over 2 datasets against memory per GPU. (b) Gain over AdamW in points.

Self-distillation ([Shenfeld et al., 2026](#bib.bib4); [Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)) provides an effective approach to adapting large
language models (LLMs) by using privileged information to elicit
supervision from the model’s existing capabilities. The student learns
from its own generated responses by matching a teacher’s predictions
conditioned on additional context. This formulation supports skill
acquisition and policy improvement while helping preserve previously
learned capabilities. Such retention is an advantage over conventional
fine-tuning, which can cause forgetting ([Biderman et al.,
2024](#bib.bib19); [Zheng et al., 2025a](#bib.bib25)), a recognized
challenge when updating LLMs ([Shi et al., 2025](#bib.bib38); [Zheng et
al., 2025b](#bib.bib39)). Within this family, Self-Distillation
Fine-Tuning (SDFT) uses demonstrations to guide the acquisition of new
skills ([Shenfeld et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)), while Self-Distillation
Policy Optimization (SDPO) extends the approach to on-policy
post-training using feedback from successful attempts ([Hübotter et al.,
2026](#bib.bib9)). In this settings, the teacher is maintained as an
exponential moving average (EMA) of student weights, following
approaches developed in semi-supervised and self-supervised
learning ([Tarvainen and Valpola, 2017](#bib.bib1); [Grill et al.,
2020](#bib.bib2); [Caron et al., 2021](#bib.bib3)). The teacher
therefore evolves alongside the student, providing an averaged model
whose predictions guide subsequent learning.

Optimizing EMA-based self-distillation incurs substantial memory costs
because training must retain student and teacher parameters alongside
optimizer state. Adam [Kingma and Ba (2014)](#bib.bib58) and
AdamW ([Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019](#bib.bib6)) store two running
moment estimates per parameter. Muon ([Jordan et al., 2024](#bib.bib54))
reduces this overhead by maintaining one momentum buffer per hidden
weight matrix and approximately orthogonalizing update directions using
Newton–Schulz iterations. Muon and its variants have been used to train
Kimi K2 ([Bai et al., 2025](#bib.bib53)) and GLM-4.5 ([Team et al.,
2025](#bib.bib63)). Nevertheless, optimizing self-distillation with Muon
still requires three persistent states for each hidden matrix: student
weights, teacher weights, and momentum. AdamW requires four, including
its two moment buffers. For example Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, momentum Muon
and AdamW require 103.2 GB and 129.0 GB of GPU memory for persistent
states, respectively, compared with 75.4 GB for Muon-RT
(Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
Existing memory-efficient optimizers address state storage through
low-rank projection, quantization, or shared statistics ([Zhao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib7); [Li et al., 2023](#bib.bib15); [Zhang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib13); [Zhu et al., 2025](#bib.bib31)). The teacher offers
another opportunity: reusing history already maintained by
self-distillation to remove a separate optimizer buffer. We therefore
ask:

Can the teacher’s existing parameter trajectory replace Muon’s momentum
buffer reducing persistent memory while improving task accuracy in
self-distillation?

Why could the teacher replace momentum? In SDFT and SDPO, the teacher is
not fixed; its weights continuously follow the evolving student through
an EMA. Unlike momentum, which averages gradients, the teacher averages
weights. The momentum-like information therefore lies in the
_teacher–student displacement_, not the teacher itself. As the student
progresses, this displacement records an exponentially weighted history
of applied updates
(Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),
smoothing noisy steps while preserving persistent directions
(Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
Under gradient descent, this history is proportional to gradient
momentum; under Muon, it captures previously orthogonalized updates. As
optimization settles, the displacement shrinks as the teacher catches up
with the student. Although this cannot recover AdamW’s second-moment
statistics, however, it can replace Muon’s momentum buffer.

Even though this approach appears simple, reusing the teacher–student
displacement in Muon is not straightforward. First, Muon orthogonalizes
its updates, so the displacement averages past optimizer outputs rather
than raw gradients. Using it directly as momentum would feed these
outputs back into the optimizer. Second, mixing the displacement with
gradients before Newton–Schulz leaves its influence uncontrolled, as
Newton–Schulz can amplify weak singular directions. This distinction
matters in self-distillation, where the teacher and learning targets
continuously evolve with the student. While momentum averages gradients
from outdated targets, the displacement captures the updates actually
applied to the student, providing a different source of historical
guidance.

We therefore propose Muon with Reused Teacher (Muon-RT), which replaces
the dedicated momentum buffer with information from the existing EMA
teacher. For each hidden weight matrix, Muon-RT orthogonalizes the
current gradient without momentum and separately computes the
teacher–student displacement. It removes the component parallel to the
student weights and scales the remainder by an estimate of its largest
singular value. The two directions are then combined after
orthogonalization using a small fixed mixing coefficient. This preserves
current gradient information while adding historical guidance without
changing the training objective. Only one small vector per matrix is
retained to warm-start power iteration across successive optimization
steps. The method applies wherever a parameter-aligned EMA teacher is
already maintained, including the studied SDFT and SDPO settings and
mean-teacher training ([Tarvainen and Valpola, 2017](#bib.bib1)).
Independent teachers lacking this alignment fall outside its scope. Our
contributions are:

- •
  We establish that the teacher–student displacement stores a discounted
  history of applied updates, is proportional to gradient momentum under
  gradient descent, and reduces variance under a local model with a
  fixed direction and temporally uncorrelated noise.
- •
  We introduce Muon-RT, eliminating hidden-matrix momentum buffers
  through normalized teacher–student displacement added after
  orthogonalization. Under regularity, bounded objective drift, and
  alignment assumptions, we establish linear contraction of an expected
  objective-gap bound toward a residual error level.
- •
  With SDFT, Muon-RT improves average accuracy over AdamW by 2.6 points
  with 42% less persistent memory, and over momentum Muon by 5.4 points
  with 27% less
  (Figure [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
  With SDPO, it gains 2.0 points over AdamW. Muon-RT ranks first among
  Muon variants across both hosts’ six settings.

## 2 Preliminaries

##### Self-distillation with an EMA-teacher:

Let $`S`$ and $`T`$ denote the student and teacher LLMs, with policies
$`\pi_{S}`$ and $`\pi_{T}`$ parameterized by $`\theta_{S}`$ and
$`\theta_{T}`$, respectively. Both share the same architecture and
initialization. For a prompt $`x`$, the student samples a completion
$`y\sim\pi_{S}(\cdot\mid x)`$. The teacher evaluates the same tokens
using privileged context $`c`$: an expert demonstration in
SDFT ([Shenfeld et al., 2026](#bib.bib4)), or rollout feedback,
potentially including a successful sibling response, in SDPO ([Hübotter
et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)). At token position $`j`$, their next-token
distributions are

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
p_{S,j}=\pi_{S}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<j}),\quad p_{T,j}=\pi_{T}(\cdot\mid x,c,y_{<j}).
``` |  | (1) |

The student minimizes the token-level distillation objective

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}(\theta_{S})=\mathbb{E}_{x,y,c}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{|y|}\mathcal{D}\!\left(p_{S,j},\operatorname{sg}[p_{T,j}]\right)\right],
``` |  | (2) |

where the expectation uses student-generated completions and their
associated teacher contexts, $`\operatorname{sg}`$ stops teacher
gradients, and $`\mathcal{D}`$ denotes the chosen divergence. SDFT
formulates reverse KL but favors forward KL empirically, while SDPO
employs Jensen–Shannon or reverse KL. To stabilize distillation, the
EMA-teacher is updated after each student optimization step:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\theta_{T}\leftarrow\tau\theta_{T}+(1-\tau)\theta_{S},\qquad\tau\in[0,1).
``` |  | (3) |

Our method assumes only that the EMA-teacher is explicitly maintained
and parameter-wise aligned with the student.

##### Muon Optimizer:

Muon updates each student hidden weight matrix
$`\mathbf{W}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{A\times B}`$, while embeddings, output
heads, norms, and biases typically use AdamW ([Jordan et al.,
2024](#bib.bib54); [Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019](#bib.bib6); [Liu et
al., 2025](#bib.bib5)). Given gradient
$`\mathbf{G}=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_{S}}\mathcal{L}(\theta_{S})`$ and
momentum coefficient $`\mu`$, Muon maintains
$`\mathbf{M}\leftarrow\mu\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{G}`$ and orthogonalizes the
Nesterov direction:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{O}^{\mathrm{mom}}=\mathrm{NS}_{K}(\mathbf{G}+\mu\mathbf{M}).
``` |  | (4) |

Here, $`\mathrm{NS}_{K}`$ applies $`K`$ quintic Newton–Schulz iterations
from
$`\mathbf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{Z}/(\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\varepsilon)`$,
with $`\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{G}+\mu\mathbf{M}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{X}_{r+1}=\big(\kappa_{1}\mathbf{I}+\kappa_{2}\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{X}_{r}^{\top}+\kappa_{3}(\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{X}_{r}^{\top})^{2}\big)\mathbf{X}_{r}.
``` |  | (5) |

The fixed coefficients approximately equalize nonzero singular values,
yielding an approximate polar factor ([Jordan et al.,
2024](#bib.bib54)). With decoupled weight decay, the update is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{W}_{S}\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda)\mathbf{W}_{S}-\eta c_{A,B}\mathbf{O}^{\mathrm{mom}}.
``` |  | (6) |

where $`\eta`$ is the learning rate, $`\lambda`$ is weight decay, and
$`c_{A,B}=0.2\sqrt{\max(A,B)}`$ approximately matches AdamW’s update
RMS ([Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib5)). Combined with the EMA-teacher, Muon
maintains 3 persistent states per hidden matrix: student weights
$`\mathbf{W}_{S}`$, teacher weights $`\mathbf{W}_{T}`$, and momentum
$`\mathbf{M}`$. We do not count the gradient $`\mathbf{G}`$ as a
persistent state: it is recomputed at every step and freed after the
update, and every optimizer needs it in the same way. During a step,
Muon therefore holds 4 matrix-sized tensors, and between steps it keeps
3.

## 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization

![Refer to caption](2610.41104v1/method.png)

Figure 2: Overview of Muon-RT. (a) Each hidden matrix maintains student
weights $`\mathbf{W}_{S}`$ and EMA-teacher weights $`\mathbf{W}_{T}`$,
eliminating Muon’s momentum buffer $`\mathbf{M}`$. (b) Newton–Schulz
approximately orthogonalizes the gradient $`\mathbf{G}`$ into
$`\mathbf{O}`$. The radial component of the teacher–student displacement
$`\mathbf{W}_{T}-\mathbf{W}_{S}`$ is removed to obtain $`\mathbf{H}`$,
which is spectrally normalized into $`\mathbf{P}`$. The update combines
both directions as $`\mathbf{U}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{O}+\alpha\mathbf{P}`$.
(c) The EMA-teacher tracks the student’s parameter trajectory with a
temporal lag.

We first characterize the information retained by the EMA-teacher,
derive the proposed update, analyze memory efficiency and sharded
implementation, and present convergence guarantees. Throughout, we
consider a student hidden weight matrix
$`\mathbf{W}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{A\times B}`$ and its corresponding
teacher matrix $`\mathbf{W}_{T}`$. Each matrix is processed
independently.
Figure [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
illustrates the proposed framework.

### 3.1 The Teacher Stores Applied Updates

Muon keeps a momentum buffer as large as each hidden matrix, while the
EMA-teacher already stores a history of the same trajectory. If this
history can take the role of momentum, the buffer becomes unnecessary.
We therefore first characterize what the teacher stores and why it
cannot simply replace momentum. Let $`\mathbf{U}^{(s)}`$ denote the
applied update direction at step $`s`$, with
$`\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)}=\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}-\eta c_{A,B}\mathbf{U}^{(s)}`$,
assuming constant learning rate $`\eta`$ and zero weight decay.
Unrolling the EMA update in
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In Self-distillation with an EMA-teacher: ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),
initialized with $`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(0)}=\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(0)}`$, gives
(Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)}-\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\tau\eta c_{A,B}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}\mathbf{U}^{(s-1-j)}.`$ |  | (7) |

Thus, the teacher lags behind the student, with its displacement
encoding a discounted history of applied update directions. Unlike
Muon’s momentum, which accumulates raw gradients, this history contains
optimizer outputs that have already been orthogonalized under standard
Muon. Directly using this displacement as momentum would therefore mix
historical updates with current gradients, potentially reinforcing stale
directions as the self-distillation target evolves. Moreover, combining
both signals before Newton–Schulz entangles their contributions through
nonlinear orthogonalization, making their relative influence difficult
to control. Muon-RT instead processes the two signals separately and
combines them after normalization, explicitly controlling the historical
contribution through a mixing weight.

### 3.2 The Muon-RT Update

##### Gradient branch.

We eliminate Muon’s momentum buffer and orthogonalize the current
student gradient
$`\mathbf{G}=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_{S}}\mathcal{L}(\theta_{S})`$:

|     |                                         |     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbf{O}=\mathrm{NS}_{K}(\mathbf{G}).
       ```                                      |     | (8) |

This corresponds to Muon with $`\mu=0`$. Since the EMA-teacher and its
distillation targets evolve during training, accumulated gradients may
lag behind the current objective
(Section [4.5](#S4.SS5 "4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

##### Teacher branch.

Let $`\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{W}_{T}-\mathbf{W}_{S}`$ denote the
teacher–student displacement. We remove its component parallel to the
student weights:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{D}-\frac{\langle\mathbf{D},\mathbf{W}_{S}\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}}{\|\mathbf{W}_{S}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\varepsilon}\mathbf{W}_{S}.
``` |  | (9) |

This suppresses radial changes associated with parameter scaling,
including weight decay, while retaining the remaining trajectory
information. We normalize the residual by its estimated largest singular
value:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{P}=\frac{\mathbf{H}}{\max(\widehat{\sigma}_{\max}(\mathbf{H}),\varepsilon)}.
``` |  | (10) |

This brings its spectral norm approximately to one, comparable to the
orthogonalized gradient $`\mathbf{O}`$. We estimate $`\sigma_{\max}`$
using $`K_{\mathrm{PI}}`$ power iterations, warm-started from the
previous right singular vector, retaining only one vector of length
$`B`$ per matrix.

##### Mixing after orthogonalization.

We combine the orthogonalized gradient and normalized teacher
directions:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{U}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{O}+\alpha\mathbf{P},`$ |  | (11) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{S}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda)\mathbf{W}_{S}-\eta c_{A,B}\mathbf{U}.`$ |  | (12) |

Here, $`\alpha\in[0,1]`$ controls the teacher contribution, with
$`\|\alpha\mathbf{P}\|_{2}\leq\alpha`$ under exact spectral
normalization and an approximate bound with power iteration. Setting
$`\alpha=0`$ recovers momentum-free Muon. The EMA-teacher is updated
afterward using
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In Self-distillation with an EMA-teacher: ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

1: Student $`\theta_{S}`$, EMA-teacher $`\theta_{T}`$, loss
$`\mathcal{L}`$, mixing weight $`\alpha`$, EMA decay $`\tau`$, learning
rate $`\eta`$, weight decay $`\lambda`$

2: Compute student gradients $`\nabla_{\theta_{S}}\mathcal{L}`$

3: for each Muon-optimized hidden matrix $`\ell`$ do

4:
  $`\mathbf{G}_{\ell}\leftarrow\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_{S,\ell}}\mathcal{L}`$

5:   $`\mathbf{O}_{\ell}\leftarrow\mathrm{NS}_{K}(\mathbf{G}_{\ell})`$

6:
  $`\mathbf{D}_{\ell}\leftarrow\mathbf{W}_{T,\ell}-\mathbf{W}_{S,\ell}`$

7:   Compute $`\mathbf{H}_{\ell}`$ using
Eq. ([9](#S3.E9 "In Teacher branch. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))

8:   Estimate $`\widehat{\sigma}_{\max}(\mathbf{H}_{\ell})`$ by power
iteration

9:   Compute $`\mathbf{P}_{\ell}`$ using
Eq. ([10](#S3.E10 "In Teacher branch. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))

10:
  $`\mathbf{U}_{\ell}\leftarrow(1-\alpha)\mathbf{O}_{\ell}+\alpha\mathbf{P}_{\ell}`$

11:   Update $`\mathbf{W}_{S,\ell}`$ in place via
Eq. ([12](#S3.E12 "In Mixing after orthogonalization. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))

12:   Release temporary matrix buffers

13: end for

14: Update remaining student parameters with AdamW

15: $`\theta_{T}\leftarrow\tau\theta_{T}+(1-\tau)\theta_{S}`$

### 3.3 Memory and Sharded Implementation

Muon-RT reduces the persistent states of each Muon-optimized hidden
matrix from $`\{\mathbf{W}_{S},\mathbf{W}_{T},\mathbf{M}\}`$ to
$`\{\mathbf{W}_{S},\mathbf{W}_{T}\}`$, eliminating the momentum buffer.
Including gradient storage $`\mathbf{G}`$, this reduces the matrix-sized
tensors from 4 to 3. Importantly, $`\mathbf{O}`$, $`\mathbf{D}`$,
$`\mathbf{H}`$, $`\mathbf{P}`$, and $`\mathbf{U}`$ are temporary
computational tensors, not additional parameters or persistent states.
As summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Mixing after orthogonalization. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"),
they are computed one matrix at a time, with buffers reused or released
after updating $`\mathbf{W}_{S}`$ in place. Only a power-iteration
vector of length $`B`$ per matrix is retained for warm-starting. Thus,
the temporary workspace scales with the processed matrix size rather
than requiring additional model-wide buffers. We report measured
persistent memory in
Section [4.4](#S4.SS4 "4.4 Memory and Compute ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
and detailed accounting in
Appendix [A.6](#A1.SS6 "A.6 Memory Projection ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation").

We use Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP) ([Zhao et al.,
2023](#bib.bib21)), with matching sharding layouts for student and
EMA-teacher parameters. The displacement
$`\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{W}_{T}-\mathbf{W}_{S}`$ is computed locally, while
the Frobenius inner products for radial projection are all-reduced
across ranks. Full matrices $`\mathbf{G}`$ and $`\mathbf{H}`$ are
temporarily gathered for Newton–Schulz orthogonalization and power
iteration, respectively. Each rank then updates only its corresponding
shard of $`\mathbf{W}_{S}`$, followed by the sharded EMA update. With
consistent global reductions and power-iteration initialization, the
sharded implementation matches the unsharded computation up to numerical
differences
(Appendix [A.4](#A1.SS4 "A.4 Implementation Details ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

### 3.4 Theoretical Analysis

We now give the guarantees behind Muon-RT;
Appendix [A.2](#A1.SS2 "A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
contains the proofs and the full assumptions. All results hold for any
differentiable host loss with a parameter-aligned EMA-teacher.

###### Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory).

If
$`\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)}=\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}-\eta c_{A,B}\mathbf{U}^{(s)}`$,
$`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s+1)}=\tau\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)}+(1-\tau)\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)}`$
and $`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(0)}=\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(0)}`$, then
Eq. ([7](#S3.E7 "In 3.1 The Teacher Stores Applied Updates ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
holds for every $`s\geq 1`$.

###### Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging).

Let
$`\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}=(1-\tau)\sum_{j<s}\tau^{j}\mathbf{U}^{(s-1-j)}`$
be the rescaled displacement. If locally
$`\mathbf{U}^{(s)}=\mathbf{d}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(s)}`$ with a fixed
direction $`\mathbf{d}`$ and zero-mean, temporally uncorrelated noise of
variance $`\sigma_{\xi}^{2}`$, then
$`\mathbb{E}[\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}]=(1-\tau^{s})\mathbf{d}`$ and
the variance of $`\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}`$ is
$`\frac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}(1-\tau^{2s})\sigma_{\xi}^{2}`$.

Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
shows that the teacher holds a smoothed estimate of persistent motion,
with the noise variance reduced by a factor of up to
$`(1-\tau)/(1+\tau)`$. The closer $`\tau`$ is to 1, the more stable the
teacher direction is than any single step.

###### Theorem 1 (Convergence of Muon-RT).

Under A1–A4 with constant step $`\eta`$, $`\lambda{=}0`$ and $`c_{A,B}`$
absorbed into $`\eta`$, let $`\gamma_{R}=a_{R}-\frac{L\eta}{2}b_{R}`$.
If $`\gamma_{R}>0`$ and $`0<2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}<1`$, then
with $`q_{R}=1-2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{s+1}]\leq q_{R}\,\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{s}]+\tfrac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}+2\delta_{s}.
``` |  | (13) |

Hence $`\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{s}]`$ converges linearly at rate $`q_{R}`$ to
a neighborhood of size
$`O\big(\frac{L\eta\sigma_{R}^{2}}{\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\gamma_{R}}+\frac{\bar{\delta}}{\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}}\big)`$
when $`\delta_{s}\leq\bar{\delta}`$.

Proposition and Corollary in the
Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1 "A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
further shows where gain must come from and when the reuse of the
teacher parameters helps.

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Tasks and data. For fine-tuning with self-distillation, we follow

biology and materials subsets of SciKnowEval. In both settings, we use
the default splits and metrics of the original papers.

Baselines. For SDFT, we compare with SFT, SFT with re-invoked prior

For SDPO, we compare with Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO; [Shao et al., 2024](#bib.bib50)), on-policy GRPO and SDPO with
AdamW, as reported by [Hübotter et al. (2026)](#bib.bib9), and run SDPO
with Muon, MuonClip and Muon-RT. The training and evaluation details are

## 5 Related Work

Training on the model’s own samples against a teacher improves
adaptation ([Agarwal et al., 2024](#bib.bib10); [Ko et al.,
2024](#bib.bib40)), with further advances in self-distillation ([Xu et
al., 2024](#bib.bib42); [Wen et al., 2023](#bib.bib41)). Such approaches
can reduce forgetting relative to SFT ([Yang et al., 2024b](#bib.bib20);
[Shenfeld et al., 2025](#bib.bib36); [Chen et al., 2025](#bib.bib37)),
which often compromises prior capabilities ([Biderman et al.,
2024](#bib.bib19); [Zheng et al., 2025a](#bib.bib25); [Shi et al.,
2025](#bib.bib38)). In particular, SDFT ([Shenfeld et al.,
2026](#bib.bib4)) and SDPO ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)) use
privileged context with an EMA-teacher. These methods redesign the
objective but retain standard optimizers, keeping 2 model-sized
histories without exploiting their interaction. We preserve their
objectives and modify only the optimizer, improving accuracy with both
hosts.

Muon ([Jordan et al., 2024](#bib.bib54)) brings orthogonalized updates
to LLMs ([Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib5); [Bai et al., 2025](#bib.bib53)),
supported by analyses of matrix updates ([Amsel et al.,
2025](#bib.bib35); [Large et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)) and of its
convergence ([Li and Hong, 2025](#bib.bib55); [Shen et al.,
2025](#bib.bib56)). Other optimizers include SOAP ([Vyas et al.,
2024](#bib.bib12)), Sophia ([Liu et al., 2024](#bib.bib11)) and
Lion ([Chen et al., 2023](#bib.bib44)). Memory-efficient methods use
low-rank projections ([Zhao et al., 2024](#bib.bib7); [Hao et al.,
2024](#bib.bib32); [Zhu et al., 2025](#bib.bib31)), quantized or shared
states ([Li et al., 2023](#bib.bib15); [Dettmers et al.,
2023](#bib.bib45); [Zhang et al., 2025](#bib.bib13)), block
updates ([Luo et al., 2024](#bib.bib28); [Pan et al.,
2024](#bib.bib27)), or fused updates ([Lv et al., 2024](#bib.bib46);
[Malladi et al., 2023](#bib.bib16)). Others introduce slow
averages ([Pagliardini et al., 2025](#bib.bib14)) or examine optimizer
tuning ([Zhao et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)). These approaches redesign the
optimizer’s *own* state rather than reuse model copies already
maintained during training. Muon-RT eliminates Muon’s momentum state,
saving 27% of the measured memory at 7B and 25% of the projected memory
at 10T while improving accuracy.

EMA-teachers provide stable learning targets ([Tarvainen and Valpola,
2017](#bib.bib1); [Grill et al., 2020](#bib.bib2); [Caron et al.,
2021](#bib.bib3)), extending to representation learning ([Oquab et al.,
2024](#bib.bib34)) and EMA scaling ([Busbridge et al.,
2023](#bib.bib17); [Morales-Brotons et al., 2024](#bib.bib18)). Weight
averaging also improves optimization and model selection ([Polyak and
Juditsky, 1992](#bib.bib62); [Izmailov et al., 2018](#bib.bib61); [Zhang
et al., 2019](#bib.bib60); [Jang et al., 2024](#bib.bib33); [Ramé et
al., 2024](#bib.bib30); [Defazio et al., 2024](#bib.bib8)), and momentum
itself can be written as an average of iterates ([Defazio,
2020](#bib.bib57)) or mixed with the current gradient ([Ma and Yarats,
2019](#bib.bib59)). These approaches treat the average as a model, a
target or a dedicated buffer, without characterizing the information
encoded by its displacement from current weights. We show that this gap
stores applied updates, averages out noise, and provides a reusable
optimized direction.

## 6 Conclusion

We asked whether the EMA-teacher of self-distillation can replace Muon’s
momentum buffer. We showed that the teacher–student displacement is a
discounted sum of applied updates
(Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
that averages out their noise
(Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),
while the orthogonalized gradient is already optimal for a single step
(Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (No instantaneous alignment gain). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"));
the teacher thus adds temporal information rather than a better
instantaneous direction. On this basis, Muon-RT drops the momentum
buffer and adds the radial-free, spectrally normalized displacement
after orthogonalization with a bounded weight, with linear convergence
under the PL condition. Empirically, it reached the highest accuracy
among all optimizers with both SDFT and SDPO, converged faster with
SDFT, retained prior capabilities well above SFT and reduced memory. The
ablations show that gradient momentum hurts when the target moves,
whereas the teacher’s history helps when it enters after
orthogonalization and stays consistent with the student. Any method that
keeps a parameter-aligned EMA model can lend it to the optimizer.

## Limitations

Our experiments cover 7B-parameter models from 2 families and 4 tasks;
larger models and further domains are left for future work. The
convergence analysis relies on smoothness, PL and alignment assumptions
that are standard for non-convex optimization but whose constants are
not estimated for LLMs. Muon-RT applies when a parameter-aligned
EMA-teacher already exists, as in SDFT and SDPO; for methods without
one, a teacher kept only to replace momentum would not save memory.

## Ethics Statement

This work changes how a model is optimized, not what it is trained on,
and it uses public datasets (ToolAlpaca, SciKnowEval) under their
released licenses and splits. Models trained on science questions may
still produce wrong answers about chemical, biological or material
properties and should not be used for laboratory or safety decisions
without expert checks. Reducing optimizer memory lowers the hardware
needed for continual adaptation; we report our compute in
Appendix [A.5](#A1.SS5 "A.5 Hyperparameters and Compute ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation").
AI assistants were used only for language and writing refinement; the
authors checked all content and remain responsible for it.

### A.1 Theoretical Analysis

This section provides remaining theoretical analysis. The below
proposition shows where this gain must come from.

###### Proposition 1 (No instantaneous alignment gain).

Let $`\mathbf{Q}`$ be the polar factor of $`\mathbf{G}\neq\mathbf{0}`$.
Then
$`\mathbf{Q}\in\arg\max_{\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2}\leq 1}\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}`$,
with maximum $`\|\mathbf{G}\|_{*}`$. Hence any teacher direction with
$`\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}\leq 1`$ satisfies
$`\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{P}\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{Q}\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}`$.

So the Newton–Schulz direction is already optimal for the current
gradient, and the teacher branch contributes what a single gradient
cannot: temporal information. To make this precise, write $`F_{s}`$ for
the expected host loss at step $`s`$ (it moves with the teacher),
$`F_{s}^{\star}=\inf F_{s}`$ and
$`\Delta_{s}=F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-F_{s}^{\star}`$. We assume (A1)
$`L`$-smoothness, (A2) the PL condition
$`\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla F_{s}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\geq\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(F_{s}-F_{s}^{\star})`$ ([Karimi
et al., 2016](#bib.bib51)), (A3) bounded teacher drift
$`\sup_{\mathbf{W}}|F_{s+1}-F_{s}|\leq\delta_{s}`$, and (A4) alignment
$`\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{g}^{(s)},\mathbf{U}^{(s)}\rangle\geq a_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|^{2}`$
with second moment
$`\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{U}^{(s)}\|^{2}\leq b_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|^{2}+\sigma_{R}^{2}`$,
where $`\mathbf{g}^{(s)}=\nabla F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})`$.

The below corollary further shows when reuse of the teacher parameters
are faster:

###### Corollary 1 (When reuse is faster).

Let a baseline optimizer (e.g. momentum-free Muon) satisfy A1–A4 with
$`\gamma_{B}>0`$ at the same $`\eta`$. If $`\gamma_{R}>\gamma_{B}`$, the
Muon-RT bound contracts strictly faster ($`q_{R}<q_{B}`$); if, in
addition, its neighborhood term is smaller, it also reaches a lower
error floor.

Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (When reuse is faster). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
shows that reuse helps when the teacher branch raises the effective
descent coefficient, i.e. adds alignment with the true gradient over
time without inflating the update’s second moment.

###### Proof.

Substituting the local model
$`\mathbf{U}^{(s)}=\mathbf{d}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(s)}`$ of
Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
into the definition of $`\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}`$,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(1-\tau)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}\left(\mathbf{d}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(s-1-j)}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=(1-\tau)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}\right)\mathbf{d}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+(1-\tau)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(s-1-j)}.`$ |  | (17) |

Since

|     |                                              |     |      |
|-----|----------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       (1-\tau)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}=1-\tau^{s},
       ```                                           |     | (18) |

taking expectations gives the stated mean. After subtracting the mean,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}-\mathbb{E}[\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}]=(1-\tau)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(s-1-j)}.
``` |  | (19) |

Therefore,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}-\mathbb{E}[\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}]\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad=(1-\tau)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{j}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(s-1-j)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right].`$ |  | (20) |

All cross terms vanish by temporal uncorrelatedness, yielding

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}-\mathbb{E}[\overline{\mathbf{U}}^{(s)}]\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad=(1-\tau)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\tau^{2j}\sigma_{\xi}^{2}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad=(1-\tau)^{2}\frac{1-\tau^{2s}}{1-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{\xi}^{2}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}\left(1-\tau^{2s}\right)\sigma_{\xi}^{2},`$ |  | (21) |

which proves the variance statement of
Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation").
Letting $`s\rightarrow\infty`$ gives the stated limit. ∎

###### Remark 1.

Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
applies to the *raw, rescaled* EMA trajectory before the radial
projection and spectral normalization in Muon-RT. Since these operations
are nonlinear, the factor $`(1-\tau)/(1+\tau)`$ should not be
interpreted as an exact variance reduction for the final normalized
direction $`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$. Moreover, for $`\alpha>0`$ the applied
direction $`\mathbf{U}^{(s)}`$ itself contains $`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$, so
its noise is not exactly temporally uncorrelated; the lemma is exact for
$`\alpha=0`$ and a small-$`\alpha`$ approximation otherwise. The lemma
instead establishes that the state from which $`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$ is
constructed already contains a temporally smoothed estimate of
persistent parameter motion.

#### A.2.3 Instantaneous alignment is not sufficient to explain the gain

The following proposition is useful for interpreting the role of the EMA
branch.

###### Restatement (Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (No instantaneous alignment gain). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

Let
$`\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{L}_{G}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{G}^{\top}`$ be
a compact singular value decomposition of $`\mathbf{G}\neq\mathbf{0}`$
and define its exact polar factor

|     |                                                 |     |      |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{L}_{G}\mathbf{V}_{G}^{\top}.
       ```                                              |     | (22) |

Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\mathbf{Q}\in\arg\max_{\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2}\leq 1}\left\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}},}
``` |  | (23) |

and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{Q}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}=\|\mathbf{G}\|_{*},
``` |  | (24) |

where $`\|\cdot\|_{*}`$ denotes the nuclear norm. Therefore, for any
spectrally normalized teacher direction $`\mathbf{P}`$ satisfying
$`\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}\leq 1`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\left\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{P}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\left\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{Q}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}.
``` |  | (25) |

###### Proof.

By the duality between the nuclear and spectral norms,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\mathbf{G}\|_{*}=\max_{\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2}\leq 1}\left\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}.
``` |  | (26) |

For $`\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{L}_{G}\mathbf{V}_{G}^{\top}`$,
$`\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{2}=1`$, and

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{Q}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left(\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left(\mathbf{V}_{G}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{L}_{G}^{\top}\mathbf{L}_{G}\mathbf{V}_{G}^{\top}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\right)=\|\mathbf{G}\|_{*}.`$ |  | (27) |

Thus $`\mathbf{Q}`$ attains the spectral-norm-constrained maximum,
proving
Eqs. ([23](#A1.E23 "In Restatement (Proposition ). ‣ A.2.3 Instantaneous alignment is not sufficient to explain the gain ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))–([24](#A1.E24 "In Restatement (Proposition ). ‣ A.2.3 Instantaneous alignment is not sufficient to explain the gain ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
Since $`\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}\leq 1`$,
Eq. ([25](#A1.E25 "In Restatement (Proposition ). ‣ A.2.3 Instantaneous alignment is not sufficient to explain the gain ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
follows immediately. ∎

###### Remark 2.

Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (No instantaneous alignment gain). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
rules out an overly strong interpretation of Muon-RT. In the idealized
deterministic setting with an exact polar factor, the teacher branch
cannot improve the current gradient’s *linear* alignment beyond the
polar direction while remaining inside the same spectral-norm ball. Any
optimization advantage must therefore arise from temporal information,
stochastic denoising, finite Newton–Schulz approximation,
curvature-dependent second-order effects, or a combination of these
factors.

#### A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT

We now analyze the complete mixed direction of
Eq. ([11](#S3.E11 "In Mixing after orthogonalization. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),
written $`\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}`$ to distinguish it from baseline
directions,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{O}^{(s)}+\alpha\mathbf{P}^{(s)},\qquad\alpha\in[0,1),
``` |  | (28) |

where $`\mathbf{O}^{(s)}`$ is the momentum-free Muon branch and
$`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$ is the normalized EMA-trajectory branch.

The host objective may itself move because the teacher changes. With
$`\mathbf{W}`$ denoting the Muon-eligible matrix under analysis, we
therefore write the expected host objective as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{s}(\mathbf{W})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}}\left[\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)};\mathcal{B}\right)\right],
``` |  | (29) |

with

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{s}^{\star}=\inf_{\mathbf{W}}F_{s}(\mathbf{W}),\qquad\Delta_{s}=F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-F_{s}^{\star}.
``` |  | (30) |

We use the following standard local conditions over the region visited
during training.

1.  A1.
    Smoothness. Every $`F_{s}`$ is $`L`$-smooth with respect to the
    Frobenius norm:

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \begin{split}F_{s}(\mathbf{Y})\leq{}&F_{s}(\mathbf{X})+\left\langle\nabla F_{s}(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{X}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}\\
    &+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{X}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.\end{split}
    ``` |  | (31) |
2.  A2.
    PL condition. There exists $`\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}>0`$ such that

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla F_{s}(\mathbf{W})\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\geq\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\left(F_{s}(\mathbf{W})-F_{s}^{\star}\right)
    ``` |  | (32) |

    for all iterates under consideration.
3.  A3.
    Controlled teacher-induced objective drift. There exists
    $`\delta_{s}\geq 0`$ such that

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \sup_{\mathbf{W}}\left|F_{s+1}(\mathbf{W})-F_{s}(\mathbf{W})\right|\leq\delta_{s}.
    ``` |  | (33) |
4.  A4.
    Descent alignment and second moment. Let

    |     |                                                      |     |      |
    |-----|------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
    |     |
           ``` math
           \mathbf{g}^{(s)}=\nabla F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}).
           ```                                                   |     | (34) |

    Conditioned on the optimization history $`\mathcal{F}_{s}`$, the
    Muon-RT direction satisfies

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{(s)},\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\geq a_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},
    ``` |  | (35) |

    and

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\leq b_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\sigma_{R}^{2}.
    ``` |  | (36) |

The constants in A4 have a direct interpretation. $`a_{R}`$ measures
expected alignment with the current true descent direction, $`b_{R}`$
measures how much update energy grows with the gradient, and
$`\sigma_{R}^{2}`$ collects residual update energy that does not scale
with the gradient. Because $`\mathbf{O}^{(s)}`$ and $`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$
are normalized, this residual includes their gradient-independent
magnitude as well as stochastic noise, and $`a_{R}`$ is understood over
a region in which $`\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}`$ is bounded. A
conservative set of valid coefficients can be obtained from separate
branch bounds. In particular, if

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{g}^{(s)},\mathbf{O}^{(s)}\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\geq a_{O}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},
``` |  | (37) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{g}^{(s)},\mathbf{P}^{(s)}\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\geq a_{P}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},
``` |  | (38) |

and

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{O}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq b_{O}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\sigma_{O}^{2},`$ |  | (39) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{P}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq b_{P}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\sigma_{P}^{2},`$ |  | (40) |

where $`a_{P}`$ may be negative, then linearity of the inner product and
convexity of the squared norm imply

|     |                                     |     |      |
|-----|-------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       a_{R}=(1-\alpha)a_{O}+\alpha a_{P},
       ```                                  |     | (41) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}b_{R}&=(1-\alpha)b_{O}+\alpha b_{P},\\
\sigma_{R}^{2}&=(1-\alpha)\sigma_{O}^{2}+\alpha\sigma_{P}^{2}.\end{split}
``` |  | (42) |

These are conservative because they do not exploit any favorable
cancellation between $`\mathbf{O}^{(s)}`$ and $`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$.

###### Restatement (Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Muon-RT). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

Suppose A1–A4 hold and the Muon-RT student update is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)}=\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}-\eta\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}
``` |  | (43) |

with constant $`\eta>0`$, i.e.,
Eq. ([12](#S3.E12 "In Mixing after orthogonalization. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
with $`\lambda=0`$ and $`c_{A,B}`$ absorbed into $`\eta`$. Define the
effective descent coefficient

|     |                                                |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \boxed{\gamma_{R}=a_{R}-\frac{L\eta}{2}b_{R}.}
       ```                                             |     | (44) |

If

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{R}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}<1,
``` |  | (45) |

then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{s+1}]\leq q_{R}\,\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{s}]+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}+2\delta_{s},}
``` |  | (46) |

where

|     |                                                   |     |      |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       q_{R}=1-2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}\in(0,1).
       ```                                                |     | (47) |

Consequently, for any $`n\geq 1`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}]\leq{}&q_{R}^{n}\Delta_{0}\\
&+\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}q_{R}^{n-1-s}\left(\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}+2\delta_{s}\right).\end{aligned}}
``` |  | (48) |

If the objective drift is uniformly bounded by
$`\delta_{s}\leq\bar{\delta}`$, then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}]\leq{}&q_{R}^{n}\Delta_{0}\\
&+\frac{\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}+2\bar{\delta}}{1-q_{R}}\left(1-q_{R}^{n}\right).\end{aligned}}
``` |  | (49) |

In particular,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}]\leq\frac{L\eta\sigma_{R}^{2}}{4\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\gamma_{R}}+\frac{\bar{\delta}}{\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}}.}
``` |  | (50) |

For a fixed objective, $`\bar{\delta}=0`$.

###### Proof.

By $`L`$-smoothness of $`F_{s}`$ and
Eq. ([43](#A1.E43 "In Restatement (Theorem ). ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})\leq{}`$ | $`\displaystyle F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-\eta\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{(s)},\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{F}}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{U}_{R}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.`$ |  | (51) |

Conditioning on $`\mathcal{F}_{s}`$ and applying
Eqs. ([35](#A1.E35 "In item A4. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
and
([36](#A1.E36 "In item A4. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\leq`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-\eta a_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\left(b_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\sigma_{R}^{2}\right)`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-\eta\left(a_{R}-\frac{L\eta}{2}b_{R}\right)\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-\eta\gamma_{R}\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}.`$ |  | (52) |

Using the PL inequality in
Eq. ([32](#A1.E32 "In item A2. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\|\mathbf{g}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\geq 2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\left(F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)})-F_{s}^{\star}\right)=2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\Delta_{s}.
``` |  | (53) |

Substituting this into
Eq. ([52](#A1.E52 "In Proof. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})-F_{s}^{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\\
&\quad\leq q_{R}\Delta_{s}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{R}^{2}.\end{split}
``` |  | (54) |

It remains to account for the fact that the EMA-teacher changes the host
objective between steps. From A3,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{s+1}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})\leq F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})+\delta_{s}.
``` |  | (55) |

Moreover,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{split}F_{s+1}^{\star}&=\inf_{\mathbf{W}}F_{s+1}(\mathbf{W})\\
&\geq\inf_{\mathbf{W}}\left(F_{s}(\mathbf{W})-\delta_{s}\right)=F_{s}^{\star}-\delta_{s}.\end{split}
``` |  | (56) |

Combining
Eqs. ([55](#A1.E55 "In Proof. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
and
([56](#A1.E56 "In Proof. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Delta_{s+1}`$ | $`\displaystyle=F_{s+1}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})-F_{s+1}^{\star}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\leq F_{s}(\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s+1)})-F_{s}^{\star}+2\delta_{s}.`$ |  | (57) |

Taking total expectation in
Eq. ([54](#A1.E54 "In Proof. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
and applying
Eq. ([57](#A1.E57 "In Proof. ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
yields
Eq. ([46](#A1.E46 "In Restatement (Theorem ). ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

Repeatedly applying the recursion gives
Eq. ([48](#A1.E48 "In Restatement (Theorem ). ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
When $`\delta_{s}\leq\bar{\delta}`$, the geometric-series identity

|     |                                                           |     |      |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \sum_{s=0}^{n-1}q_{R}^{n-1-s}=\frac{1-q_{R}^{n}}{1-q_{R}}
       ```                                                        |     | (58) |

gives
Eq. ([49](#A1.E49 "In Restatement (Theorem ). ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
Finally, using

|     |                                          |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       1-q_{R}=2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}
       ```                                       |     | (59) |

and taking $`n\rightarrow\infty`$ yields
Eq. ([50](#A1.E50 "In Restatement (Theorem ). ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
∎

#### A.2.5 Comparison with a baseline optimizer

The previous result immediately gives a sufficient condition for a
faster convergence guarantee than AdamW, momentum Muon, or momentum-free
Muon, provided the corresponding baseline satisfies the same local
regularity assumptions.

###### Restatement (Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (When reuse is faster). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

Let a baseline optimizer $`B`$ satisfy A1–A4 at the same step size
$`\eta`$ with coefficients $`a_{B},b_{B},\sigma_{B}^{2}`$ and
objective-drift bound $`\bar{\delta}_{B}`$. Define

|     |                                                        |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \begin{split}\gamma_{B}&=a_{B}-\frac{L\eta}{2}b_{B},\\
       q_{B}&=1-2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{B}.\end{split}
       ```                                                     |     | (60) |

If

|     |                                  |     |      |
|-----|----------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \boxed{\gamma_{R}>\gamma_{B}>0,}
       ```                               |     | (61) |

then

|     |              |     |      |
|-----|--------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       q_{R}<q_{B},
       ```           |     | (62) |

so the Muon-RT bound contracts strictly faster per optimization step.

Furthermore, define the asymptotic upper-bound terms

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Phi_{R}=\frac{L\eta\sigma_{R}^{2}}{4\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\gamma_{R}}+\frac{\bar{\delta}_{R}}{\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}},
``` |  | (63) |

and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Phi_{B}=\frac{L\eta\sigma_{B}^{2}}{4\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\gamma_{B}}+\frac{\bar{\delta}_{B}}{\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{B}}.
``` |  | (64) |

If, in addition,

|     |                            |     |      |
|-----|----------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \boxed{\Phi_{R}<\Phi_{B},}
       ```                         |     | (65) |

then the Muon-RT convergence bound has both a faster transient
contraction and a strictly smaller asymptotic optimization neighborhood.

###### Proof.

Equation ([61](#A1.E61 "In Restatement (Corollary ). ‣ A.2.5 Comparison with a baseline optimizer ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
implies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}>2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{B},
``` |  | (66) |

and therefore

|     |                                                          |     |      |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \begin{split}q_{R}&=1-2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{R}\\
       &<1-2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\eta\gamma_{B}=q_{B}.\end{split}
       ```                                                       |     | (67) |

Hence the geometric transient $`q_{R}^{n}`$ decays more rapidly than
$`q_{B}^{n}`$. The second statement follows directly from
Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Muon-RT). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"):
$`\Phi_{R}`$ and $`\Phi_{B}`$ are the respective asymptotic upper bounds
obtained by taking the limit superior of each finite-time recursion. If
$`\Phi_{R}<\Phi_{B}`$, the Muon-RT bound approaches a smaller
optimization neighborhood. ∎

For the fixed-objective case $`\bar{\delta}_{R}=\bar{\delta}_{B}=0`$,
Eq. ([65](#A1.E65 "In Restatement (Corollary ). ‣ A.2.5 Comparison with a baseline optimizer ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
reduces to

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boxed{\frac{\sigma_{R}^{2}}{\gamma_{R}}<\frac{\sigma_{B}^{2}}{\gamma_{B}}.}
``` |  | (68) |

Thus the relevant quantity is not variance alone, but the ratio between
residual update energy and effective descent. This formalizes the
optimization mechanism suggested by
Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"):
a temporally smoothed trajectory is useful when the reduction in update
noise is large enough relative to any loss of instantaneous descent
alignment.

#### A.2.6 Iteration complexity

Because the branches are normalized, $`\sigma_{R}^{2}>0`$ and the bound
converges to the neighborhood $`\Phi_{R}`$ rather than to zero. From
Eq. ([49](#A1.E49 "In Restatement (Theorem ). ‣ A.2.4 Finite-time convergence of Muon-RT ‣ A.2 Theoretical Analysis: Proofs and Convergence ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),

|     |                                                          |     |      |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}]\leq q_{R}^{n}\Delta_{0}+\Phi_{R}.
       ```                                                       |     | (69) |

To reach $`\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}]\leq\Phi_{R}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}`$,
it is sufficient that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math

###### Remark 3 (What the theorem does and does not establish).

Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (When reuse is faster). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
supports two claims under explicit conditions: faster finite-time
contraction and a lower optimization-error bound. It does *not* claim
that Muon-RT universally reaches a better local minimum in an arbitrary
non-convex neural-network landscape. Higher downstream accuracy can be
consistent with the lower error bound and the temporal smoothing of the
raw trajectory discussed above, but the mapping from optimization loss
to task accuracy is data- and objective-dependent and is therefore
verified empirically rather than assumed by the theorem.

### A.3 Teacher Precision

Muon-RT reads the teacher–student displacement
$`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)}-\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}`$, so the teacher must
actually follow the EMA of
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In Self-distillation with an EMA-teacher: ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
With $`\tau{=}0.99`$, each step moves the teacher by $`1\%`$ of the
displacement, and the displacement is only $`0.1`$–$`0.3\%`$ of the
weights
(Appendix [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Learning-Rate Sensitivity and Training Diagnostics ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
The per-step change is therefore about $`10^{-5}`$ of a weight’s
magnitude, while bfloat16 can only represent relative changes larger
than about $`4\times 10^{-3}`$ (8 significand bits). In 16-bit storage
most EMA steps round to zero. We measured this on a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct
key projection matrix with 250 momentum-free Muon steps
(Figure [6](#A1.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ A.7 Learning-Rate Sensitivity and Training Diagnostics ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(a);
setup in
Appendix [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Learning-Rate Sensitivity and Training Diagnostics ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")):
the student moved by 2.15 in Frobenius norm, a 32-bit teacher by 1.37,
and a bfloat16 teacher by only 0.66, with 79% of its entries never
changing. A 16-bit displacement would thus reflect rounding rather than
the averaged trajectory that
Lemmas [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
and [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
describe. For the same reason, optimizer states such as Muon’s momentum
and AdamW’s moments are usually kept in 32 bits ([Liu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib5); [Li et al., 2023](#bib.bib15)); since the teacher
takes over the role of optimizer state, we store it in 32 bits as well,
and we do so for every optimizer we train in
Tables [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")–[3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"),
so that comparisons share the same teacher. The teacher’s forward pass
still runs in bfloat16, so the self-distillation loss is computed as in
SDFT. The 32-bit teacher adds 2 bytes per parameter (7.6 GB per GPU with
2 GPUs); the memory in
Figure [3](#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 4.2 Fine-Tuning with Self-Distillation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(d)
includes this cost.

Precision setting. Our precision differs from the original SDFT setting,
which keeps the student and the teacher in bfloat16. To make sure that
the gains of the Muon variants come neither from precision nor from
run-to-run randomness, we train every optimizer in the same setting: a
bfloat16 mixed-precision student with 32-bit master weights and a
teacher stored in 32 bits, with forward passes in bfloat16. In this
setting, SDFT and SDPO with AdamW reproduce their reported accuracy
within run-to-run variation, so the AdamW rows taken from [Shenfeld et
al. (2026)](#bib.bib4) and [Hübotter et al. (2026)](#bib.bib9) are
comparable with our runs. All memory values in the paper are for a
32-bit teacher
(Appendix [A.6](#A1.SS6 "A.6 Memory Projection ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

### A.4 Implementation Details

We build on the released SDFT code (TRL with colocated vLLM generation)
and replace the optimizer. Hidden matrices are all two-dimensional
weights inside the transformer blocks; embeddings, the output head,
normalization weights and query/key/value biases use AdamW with the same
learning rate, following [Liu et al. (2025)](#bib.bib5). Student and
teacher are sharded with FSDP2 using the same wrapping policy, so each
teacher shard matches a student shard. For every hidden matrix, the
inner products in
Eq. ([9](#S3.E9 "In Teacher branch. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
and the norms are reduced across shards; the gradient (in bfloat16) and
$`\mathbf{H}^{(s)}`$ are gathered, Newton–Schulz and power iteration run
on the full matrix on every rank, and each rank keeps its slice of
$`\mathbf{U}^{(s)}`$. The power-iteration vector of each matrix is
stored across steps (1 vector of length $`B`$, 4.5 MB in total for
Qwen2.5-7B); starting from a random vector, 8 iterations underestimate
$`\sigma_{\max}`$ by about 8%, and the warm start removes this error
within 3 steps. We verified on a small model with 2 GPUs that (i)
$`\alpha{=}0`$ is bit-identical to Muon with $`\mu{=}0`$, (ii) sharded
and unsharded updates are identical, and (iii) for a single matrix the
update matches an exact singular-value reference to $`5\times 10^{-7}`$,
with $`\|\mathbf{P}^{(s)}\|_{2}{=}1`$.

### A.8 Attention Logits and QK-Clip

Growing attention logits can destabilize training with Muon, which
motivated QK-Clip ([Bai et al., 2025](#bib.bib53); [Wortsman et al.,
2024](#bib.bib22); [Dehghani et al., 2023](#bib.bib24)). We measured the
maximum pre-softmax logit of every head (after rotary embeddings) on 24
evaluation prompts. The base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct already reaches about
4,800, and 26–28 heads exceed the threshold of 100 used by [Bai et al.
(2025)](#bib.bib53); these are attention-sink heads driven by the query
and key biases ([Xiao et al., 2024](#bib.bib23)). After SDFT, the global
maximum stays within $`\pm 1\%`$ of the base value for AdamW, Muon and
Muon-RT alike, the largest heads grow by at most 13–20%, and the norms
of the query and key weights change by less than 0.05%. Clipping at 100
would therefore rescale about 27 heads that are large by design. Our
MuonClip baseline instead uses per-head thresholds of $`1.1\times`$ the
largest logit observed in the first 10 steps. Because logits do not
grow, QK-Clip is not needed in this setting. With Muon-RT, it is also
harmful
(Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(d)).
In the tool-use run with clipping, 8 clips occur, each on 1 head, with
factors down to 0.56; right after the first clip (step 20, factor 0.90),
the mean
$`\|\mathbf{H}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}/\|\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}`$
over all hidden matrices rises 12% above the run without clipping, after
tracking it within 2% before. To isolate the effect, we take the query
matrix of layer 10 of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, scale the 128 rows of 1 head
in the student but not in the teacher, and form $`\mathbf{H}^{(s)}`$ and
$`\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$ as in
Eqs. ([9](#S3.E9 "In Teacher branch. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))–([10](#S3.E10 "In Teacher branch. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
A factor of 0.90 (0.56) creates a gap of 1.9% (8.0%) of the matrix norm,
against 0.1–0.3% from learning; radial removal changes it by under 1.5%,
97% (99%) of $`\|\mathbf{H}^{(s)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}`$ lies in the
clipped head, and $`-\mathbf{P}^{(s)}`$ restricted to that head has
cosine $`-1.00`$ with its weights. The update therefore shrinks the
clipped head further at every step. The offset decays by $`\tau`$ per
step, so a 1.9% gap falls below 0.2% only after about 225 steps. Scaling
the same query rows of the teacher by the same factor at every clip
keeps $`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)}-\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}`$ free of this offset;
in
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(d),
this variant reaches 67.3 on tool use and 70.1 on science.

### A.9 Training and evaluation.

Only the optimizer changes; each host method keeps its loss, batch size
and EMA settings same. For every optimizer, the student is trained in
bfloat16 mixed precision (bfloat16 compute, 32-bit master weights), and
the EMA-teacher is stored in 32 bits with its forward pass in bfloat16.
Appendix [A.3](#A1.SS3 "A.3 Teacher Precision ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
shows that this precision does not affect accuracy. We train Muon,
MuonClip and Muon-RT ($`\alpha{=}0.1`$) with learning rates
$`5{\times}10^{-5}`$ and $`3{\times}10^{-5}`$ and report the better run.
Following [Shenfeld et al. (2026)](#bib.bib4), we report the best
checkpoint under greedy decoding and measure prior capabilities on
HellaSwag, HumanEval, IFEval, MMLU, TruthfulQA, and Winogrande. For
SDPO, we report the best avg@16 within 1 and 5 hours of
training ([Hübotter et al., 2026](#bib.bib9)).

### A.10 Empirical Checks of the Theory

Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.4 Memory and Compute ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
tests each theoretical statement with a matching measurement.

Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"):
the teacher stores applied updates.
Eq. ([7](#S3.E7 "In 3.1 The Teacher Stores Applied Updates ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
predicts the size of $`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)}-\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}`$ from
the learning rate and the size of the applied updates alone: it lies
between the sum of their norms (fully aligned updates) and the root of
the sum of their squares (uncorrelated updates). The measured
displacement follows this range closely, with correlation 0.97
(Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.4 Memory and Compute ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(a)).
It peaks near step 107, after the learning rate peaks, exactly as an EMA
of applied updates should. Without normalization, the measured size lies
inside this range at every step after step 20, at 1.2–1.7 times the
uncorrelated bound, so the applied updates share a persistent component
(Appendix [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Learning-Rate Sensitivity and Training Diagnostics ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).

Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2 (Temporal averaging). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"):
the teacher averages out noise. On a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct weight matrix
trained with noisy gradients of known mean, the current orthogonalized
gradient loses the mean direction as gradient noise grows (cosine 0.87
to 0.11), while the teacher branch keeps it (0.99 to 0.75;
Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.4 Memory and Compute ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(b)).

Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1 (No instantaneous alignment gain). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"):
the gain is temporal. During training, the 2 branches are nearly
orthogonal, with $`|\cos(\mathbf{O}^{(s)},\mathbf{P}^{(s)})|<0.015`$
after the first steps
(Appendix [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Learning-Rate Sensitivity and Training Diagnostics ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
The teacher branch thus adds directions that the current gradient does
not contain, as the proposition requires.

Post-Newton–Schulz mixing bounds the teacher. Mixed before
Newton–Schulz, even $`\alpha{=}0.02`$ changes the update by more than
its own norm, because Newton–Schulz lifts the teacher’s weak directions
to unit singular value
(Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.4 Memory and Compute ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(c)).
Mixed after Newton–Schulz, as in Muon-RT, the change grows linearly with
$`\alpha`$, as
Eq. ([11](#S3.E11 "In Mixing after orthogonalization. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))
guarantees.

Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Convergence of Muon-RT). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")
and
Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (When reuse is faster). ‣ A.1 Theoretical Analysis ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"):
faster convergence. Under the PL condition, a smaller gradient norm
means a smaller optimality gap. At the same learning rate, Muon-RT has
the lowest gradient norm from step 40 onward
(Figure [4](#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.4 Memory and Compute ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(d))
and the lowest self-distillation KL for most of training
(Figure [3](#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 4.2 Fine-Tuning with Self-Distillation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(c)),
and it reaches its best accuracy earlier
(Figure [3](#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 4.2 Fine-Tuning with Self-Distillation ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(a)).
These observations are consistent with the corollary. We also check its
condition directly by estimating the constants of A4 on the
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct weight matrix with gradients of known mean
(Appendix [A.7](#A1.SS7 "A.7 Learning-Rate Sensitivity and Training Diagnostics ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")):
at every noise level, the teacher branch is better aligned with the mean
gradient than the orthogonalized gradient (up to 6.6 times) and has a
smaller second moment, so $`a_{R}>a_{B}`$ and $`b_{R}<b_{B}`$, which
gives $`\gamma_{R}>\gamma_{B}`$ for any step size. The single-batch
cosine between gradient and teacher direction stays small (0.03 at the
highest noise), so the near-zero cosines measured in training do not
contradict this condition.

### A.11 Ablations and Analysis

We ablate each component of Muon-RT with SDFT on both tasks
(Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")):
the optimizer state it replaces, the teacher weight $`\alpha`$, the
construction of the teacher branch, and its combination with logit
clipping. All ablations follow the setting of
Table [1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"),
with $`\alpha{=}0.1`$ unless varied, and every block includes the full
method as reference.

Which history helps. Gradient history and teacher history have opposite
effects
(Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(a)).
Standard momentum reduces accuracy by 0.7–6.2 points for both Muon and
MuonClip on both tasks, whereas the teacher branch raises the accuracy
of momentum-free Muon by 5.1 and 2.8 points. Orthogonalization
normalizes each update, but with momentum consecutive updates share a
direction for about $`1/(1-\mu){=}20`$ steps and add up instead of
partly cancelling, so the weights move further at the same learning
rate. In addition, self-distillation targets move with the teacher at
every step, so an average of past gradients points partly toward targets
that no longer apply. The teacher branch, by contrast, records the
updates that were actually applied
(Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1 (EMA trajectory). ‣ 3.4 Theoretical Analysis ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")),
and the bounded mix adds this history without letting it override the
current gradient.

Teacher weight. $`\alpha{=}0.1`$ is the best value on both tasks
(Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(b)).
Both smaller and larger values reduce accuracy, confirming that the
teacher should guide, not replace, the gradient direction
(Eq. ([11](#S3.E11 "In Mixing after orthogonalization. ‣ 3.2 The Muon-RT Update ‣ 3 Momentum-Free Muon Optimization ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation"))).
The same weight is optimal for both tasks and is used without
modification for SDPO, so $`\alpha`$ requires no per-task tuning.

Teacher branch. Every modification to the teacher branch reduces
accuracy
(Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(c)),
by 1.7–2.6 points for mixing before Newton–Schulz up to 5.0 points for a
cosine gate. The gradient and displacement are nearly orthogonal
($`|\cos|\approx 0.004`$), causing the cosine gate to effectively switch
the teacher off. On the tool-use task, even the weaker variants
outperforms the momentum-free Muon (70.5–72.5 Vs. 69.1), indicating that
the teacher’s history is beneficial in every form; however, across both
tasks, the bounded post-Newton–Schulz formulation extracts the most from
it.

QK-Clip. Logit clipping helps Muon (69.1 to 73.2 on tool use) but hurts
Muon-RT
(Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.5 Ablations and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")(d);
71.1 to 66.0 at the same learning rate). QK-Clip rescales a head’s query
weights in the student only, so each clip introduces an offset into
$`\mathbf{W}_{T}^{(s)}-\mathbf{W}_{S}^{(s)}`$ that is not learning
motion. On a real query matrix, a single clip by a factor 0.9 creates a
gap 6–19 times larger than the learning displacement, this gap is
retained by radial removal and accounts for 97% of $`\mathbf{H}^{(s)}`$
(Appendix [A.8](#A1.SS8 "A.8 Attention Logits and QK-Clip ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Muon-RT: Reusing the Teacher’s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation")).
As a result, the teacher branch continues shrinking the clipped head at
full weight $`\alpha`$ for over 200 steps; in training, the first clip
raises the mean displacement over all matrices by 12% within a single
step. Applying the same clip to the teacher removes this offset and
recovers 1.3 points on tool use (67.3), but both tasks remain below
Muon-RT without clipping (67.3 and 70.1): each clip also perturbs the
teacher’s attention patterns and therefore the self-distillation target.
In any case, clipping is unnecessary here, as logits stay within 1% of
their initial maximum for every optimizer.
````
