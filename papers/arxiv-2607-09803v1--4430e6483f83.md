# Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation

Ingrid Petrova    Luan Vejsiu Affiliation: European University of Tirana
Email: [ingrid.petrova@uet.edu.al](mailto:)

###### Abstract

Large autoregressive language models exhibit a self-correction blind
spot: they reliably fix identical errors when attributed to an external
source yet fail to fix the same errors in their own outputs. Prior work
has documented this phenomenon empirically, through controlled error
injection, error-depth decompositions, RL-based verifier–corrector
training, and intrinsic self-verification, but offers no formal model of
why generating a token suppresses the ability to detect its error, no
quantitative activation condition for correction markers, and no
convergence guarantee for reinforcement-learning-based self-correction.
We close these gaps with SPARC, a spectral-algebraic theory of
self-correction in autoregressive generation. We define the
error-propagation operator as the product of per-step attention
Jacobians on the residual stream and prove that the blind spot arises if
and only if the spectral radius of this operator is at least one. We
derive a sharp activation threshold, given as a function of the spectral
radius, that a correction marker must exceed, recovering the 89.3%
blind-spot reduction observed with a simple “Wait” marker. We further
prove that RL-based verifier–corrector training converges at a rate
proportional to the squared coupling strength over the square root of
the number of samples if and only if the verifier–corrector coupling
matrix has spectral norm below one, and that this criterion is invariant
across residual-stream autoregressive modalities, unifying text LLMs and
autoregressive image and video generation. Experiments across four
backbones and a visual autoregressive probe validate every theorem, with
spectral predictions matching measured blind-spot rates within 3.2%
RMSE.

## 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have become transformative across natural
language processing, yet they remain unreliable: they make mistakes,
follow unproductive reasoning paths, and fail to revise errors in their
own outputs ([Tsui, 2025](#bib.bib1); [Kumar et al., 2025](#bib.bib11)).
Self-correction — the ability of a model to detect and revise errors in
its own generation — is a prerequisite for trustworthy deployment. A
striking empirical finding, however, is the _Self-Correction Blind
Spot_: LLMs can identify and correct an error when it is attributed to
an external source (a user, a tool) but systematically fail to correct
the identical error when it appears in their own output ([Tsui,
2025](#bib.bib1)). Across fourteen open-source models the blind-spot
rate averages 64.5%, and even a simple prompt-level intervention —
appending the token “Wait” — reduces it by 89.3%, suggesting the
capability is latent but suppressed during standard decoding.

A wave of recent work has refined this picture. [Li (2025)](#bib.bib3)
decompose self-correction into detectability and correctability and
propose the Error Depth Hypothesis, observing that correction success is
non-monotonic in how deeply an error is embedded in the reasoning trace.
[Ma et al. (2025)](#bib.bib4) show that reinforcement learning (RL) —
not supervised fine-tuning (SFT) — is what installs usable
self-correction, via a two-stage verifier–corrector pipeline (S²R). [Lee
et al. (2025)](#bib.bib2) train an intrinsic verifier (ReVISE) and
expose a refinement-instability regime in which self-refinement degrades
output quality. Iterative self-feedback ([Madaan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib18); [Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib23)), RL-based
self-correction training ([Kumar et al., 2024](#bib.bib10)), and
confidence-aware verification ([Li et al., 2024](#bib.bib12); [Liu et
al., 2024a](#bib.bib16)) all improve self-correction empirically but
rest on the same foundational question that none of them answers
formally: _why does generating a token suppress the ability to detect
its error?_

#### Limitations of prior work.

All existing accounts are empirical or phenomenological. There is (i) no
formal model tying the decoding dynamics to the suppression of error
detection; (ii) no quantitative condition predicting when a correction
marker will succeed or fail; (iii) no convergence guarantee or
sample-complexity bound for RL-based verifier–corrector training; and
(iv) no theory that connects the blind spot in text LLMs to the
structurally identical error-propagation dynamics of autoregressive
image and video generation, where a large body of recent work confronts
the same error-propagation problem without a unifying account ([Yu et
al., 2024](#bib.bib31); [Zhou et al., 2026d](#bib.bib39); [Zhou et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib40); [Song et al., 2026](#bib.bib41); [Zhou et al.,
2025](#bib.bib42); [Zhou and Shen, 2026](#bib.bib43); [Zhou et al.,
2026c](#bib.bib44); [Zhou and Long, 2023](#bib.bib45); [Wang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib46); [Wang et al., 2025](#bib.bib47); [Wang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib48)).

#### Our approach.

We build SPARC (Spectral Propagation Analysis for Reasoning Correction),
a spectral-algebraic theory that formalizes the blind spot as a property
of the _error-propagation operator_ $`F_{T}`$, defined as the product of
per-step attention Jacobians on the residual stream. The theory is
organized around three objects: $`F_{T}`$, whose spectral radius
$`\rho(F_{T})`$ governs error amplification; the _correction
sensitivity_ $`\kappa`$ of a marker; and the _verifier–corrector
coupling matrix_ $`C`$, whose spectral norm governs RL stability.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Self-Correction and Self-Refinement in LLMs

The empirical study of LLM self-correction begins with iterative
self-feedback: Self-Refine ([Madaan et al., 2023](#bib.bib18)) iterates
a critic-and-revise loop, while Reflexion ([Shinn et al.,
2023](#bib.bib23)) augments agents with verbal reinforcement.
Confidence-aware analyses ([Li et al., 2024](#bib.bib12); [Liu et al.,
2024a](#bib.bib16)) show intrinsic self-correction is bounded by the
model’s self-knowledge, and alignment-based reasoners ([Wang et al.,
2023](#bib.bib25)) improve step-level correction. The Self-Correction
Bench of [Tsui (2025)](#bib.bib1) isolates the _blind spot_ — the gap
between external and internal error correction — through controlled
error injection. [Li (2025)](#bib.bib3) decompose the
accuracy-correction paradox via the Error Depth Hypothesis, and Socratic
self-refinement ([Shi et al., 2025](#bib.bib22)) and synergistic
training-inference co-design ([Zeng et al., 2025](#bib.bib33)) push
activation further. Our work departs from these empirical accounts by
providing the first formal spectral model of _why_ the blind spot arises
and _when_ activation succeeds.

### 2.2 Reinforcement Learning for Self-Correction

A second line installs self-correction via RL. SCoRe ([Kumar et al.,
2024](#bib.bib10)) trains self-correction through multi-turn RL, and S²R
([Ma et al., 2025](#bib.bib4)) decouples a verifier from a corrector
with a two-stage RL pipeline, demonstrating that RL — not SFT — is what
makes self-correction usable. ReVISE ([Lee et al., 2025](#bib.bib2))
learns an intrinsic verifier and exposes a refinement-instability
regime. Surveys of RL for LLMs ([Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib17); [Kumar
et al., 2025](#bib.bib11)) and weakness-driven problem synthesis ([Liang
et al., 2025](#bib.bib13)) confirm that outcome feedback is the active
ingredient, echoing the training-data analysis of [Tsui
(2025)](#bib.bib1). Value-bonus exploration ([Wahab et al.,
2026](#bib.bib24)) and intrinsic verifier training ([Lee et al.,
2025](#bib.bib2)) further refine the RL toolbox. None of these works
provides convergence rates, sample-complexity bounds, or a sharp
stability condition;
Theorem [7](#Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
fills this gap. RLHF foundations ([Ouyang et al., 2022](#bib.bib19);
[Bai et al., 2022](#bib.bib38)) and direct preference optimization
([Rafailov et al., 2023](#bib.bib21)) provide the policy-gradient
substrate our analysis builds on.

### 2.3 Autoregressive Generation and Error Propagation

A third line studies error propagation in autoregressive generation,
both textual and visual. Local and global decoding ([Gareev et al.,
2024](#bib.bib7)) and hierarchical skip decoding ([Zhu et al.,
2024](#bib.bib37)) analyze how decoding choices affect error propagation
in text. For visual autoregressive generation, randomized generation
([Yu et al., 2024](#bib.bib31)), speculative decoding ([Yue et al.,
2026](#bib.bib32); [Xiang et al., 2026](#bib.bib26); [Zhou et al.,
2026a](#bib.bib36)), diffusion-correction hybrids ([Chung et al.,
2024](#bib.bib6)), multi-resolution models ([Liu et al.,
2024b](#bib.bib15)), retrieval augmentation ([Qi et al.,
2025](#bib.bib20)), and non-autoregressive variants ([Yang et al.,
2019](#bib.bib27); [Zhao et al., 2024](#bib.bib34)) all confront the
same token-level error-propagation dynamics. Vision-representation
compression for video generation ([Zhou et al., 2026d](#bib.bib39)),
multi-subject in-context image generation ([Zhou et al.,
2026b](#bib.bib40)), entropy-guided RPO for autoregressive image
generation ([Song et al., 2026](#bib.bib41)), holistic
complex-instruction image-generation benchmarks ([Zhou et al.,
2025](#bib.bib42)), training acceleration for autoregressive video
generation ([Zhou and Shen, 2026](#bib.bib43)), condition-error
refinement in autoregressive image generation with diffusion loss ([Zhou
et al., 2026c](#bib.bib44)), cross-modal alignment for text-guided
inpainting ([Zhou and Long, 2023](#bib.bib45)), locality-aware dynamic
rescue for text-to-image dLLMs ([Wang et al., 2026](#bib.bib46)),
complex-instruction image-editing benchmarks ([Wang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib47)), and memory-augmented state-space defect recognition
([Wang et al., 2024](#bib.bib48)) all face error propagation in
autoregressive visual decoding but lack a unifying theoretical account.
Multimodal LLM surveys ([Yin et al., 2023](#bib.bib30); [Yang et al.,
2023](#bib.bib28)) and visual token pruning ([Huang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib8)) provide architectural context.

### 2.4 Theoretical Analyses of Attention and Decoding

A growing theoretical literature analyzes attention dynamics:
thermodynamic isomorphisms ([Kim, 2026](#bib.bib9)), gated attention
with forget gates ([Lin et al., 2025](#bib.bib14)), and self-cognition
studies ([Chen et al., 2024](#bib.bib5)) examine structural properties
of transformers. Code-based self-verification ([Zhou et al.,
2023](#bib.bib35)) and thought-template distillation ([Yang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib29)) give empirical proxies for the theoretical quantities
we formalize. SPARC complements these by giving the first spectral
criterion — $`\rho(F_{T})<1`$ — that certifies self-correction
capability, made predictively checkable via the Hutchinson estimator
(Corollary [12](#Thmtheorem12 "Corollary 12 (Hutchinson Estimability). ‣ 3.7 Corollaries ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).

## 3 The SPARC Theory

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Let $`G_{\theta}`$ be an autoregressive generator emitting tokens
$`x_{1},\ldots,x_{T}`$ by sampling
$`x_{t}\sim p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{<t})`$ with residual-stream hidden
states $`h_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`$. A _self-correction event_ at step
$`\tau`$ is a transition $`h_{\tau}\mapsto h_{\tau}+\Delta_{\tau}`$
induced by a correction marker (e.g., the token “Wait”) intended to let
the model detect and revise an error in $`\{x_{t}\}_{t\leq\tau}`$. The
Self-Correction Blind Spot ([Tsui, 2025](#bib.bib1)) is the empirical
fact that, under standard SFT decoding, $`G_{\theta}`$ fails to perform
this revision on its own outputs while succeeding on identical external
inputs.

We seek formal answers to four questions: (Q1) Under what spectral
condition does the blind spot arise? (Q2) What is the sharp threshold a
correction marker must exceed? (Q3) Under what condition does RL-based
verifier–corrector training provably install self-correction, and at
what rate? (Q4) Is the criterion invariant across autoregressive
modalities?

### 3.2 Preliminaries and Notation

Modern autoregressive generators — text LLMs and autoregressive
image/video models — share the residual-stream update
$`h_{t}=h_{t-1}+f_{\theta}(h_{t-1},x_{<t})`$, where $`f_{\theta}`$ is a
stack of self-attention and MLP blocks with a residual connection. This
residual form is the structural basis of cross-modal invariance
(Theorem [8](#Thmtheorem8 "Theorem 8 (Cross-Modal Invariance). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).
For a matrix $`A`$ with eigenvalues $`\{\lambda_{i}\}`$,
$`\rho(A)=\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}|`$ is the spectral radius; by the Gelfand
formula $`\rho(A)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|A^{k}\|^{1/k}`$, so $`\rho(A)<1`$
iff $`A^{k}\to 0`$, i.e., the linear map is asymptotically contractive.

### 3.3 Definitions

###### Definition 1 (Error-Propagation Operator).

For an autoregressive generator $`G_{\theta}`$ with residual states
$`\{h_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$, the _error-propagation operator_ over horizon
$`T`$ is

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
F_{T}\;=\;\prod_{t=1}^{T}J_{t},\qquad J_{t}\;=\;\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial h_{t-1}}\;=\;I+\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial h_{t-1}}.
``` |  | (1) |

$`F_{T}`$ maps a perturbation $`\delta h_{0}`$ at step $`0`$ to the
induced perturbation $`\delta h_{T}=F_{T}\,\delta h_{0}`$ at step $`T`$.

###### Definition 2 (Self-Correction Blind Spot).

$`G_{\theta}`$ exhibits the *blind spot* on horizon-$`T`$ traces iff
there exists an error perturbation $`\delta h_{0}\neq 0`$ that
$`G_{\theta}`$ can identify when injected externally but cannot revise
when generated internally:
$`\exists\,\delta h_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ext}},\;\delta h_{0}\notin\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{int}}`$,
where $`\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ext}}`$ is the external-error detection set
and $`\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{int}}`$ the internal-error revision set.

###### Definition 3 (Correction Sensitivity).

A correction marker $`m`$ induces a perturbation $`\Delta_{m}`$ on the
residual stream. Its *correction sensitivity* is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\kappa(m)\;=\;\frac{\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}}{\|h_{\tau}\|_{2}+\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}}\;\in\;[0,1],
``` |  | (2) |

the relative magnitude of the marker-induced perturbation at the
injection point $`\tau`$.

###### Definition 4 (Verifier–Corrector Coupling Matrix).

For an RL self-correction system with verifier $`V_{\phi}`$ and
corrector $`R_{\psi}`$, the *coupling matrix*
$`C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}`$ has entries
$`C_{ij}=\langle\nabla_{\phi}\mathbb{E}[V_{\phi}],\,\nabla_{\psi}\mathbb{E}[R_{\psi}]\rangle\cdot\mathbf{1}[\text{shared parameters}]`$,
the inner product of verifier and corrector gradient flows restricted to
shared parameters. $`\|C\|_{2}`$ measures how strongly corrector updates
interfere with verifier stability.

### 3.4 Assumptions

###### Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness).

There exists $`L<\infty`$ such that $`\|J_{t}\|_{2}\leq L`$ for all
$`t\in[1,T]`$ and all prompts. *Justification:* attention and MLP blocks
are Lipschitz on bounded inputs (softmax is 1-Lipschitz; MLP activations
are bounded).

###### Assumption 2 (Residual-Stream Form).

$`h_{t}=h_{t-1}+f_{\theta}(h_{t-1},x_{<t})`$ with $`f_{\theta}`$
differentiable. *Justification:* holds for all transformer-based
autoregressive generators, text and visual.

###### Assumption 3 (Non-Degenerate Spectral Radius).

The leading eigenvalue of $`F_{T}`$ is simple (multiplicity 1).
*Justification:* generic condition; holds with probability 1 under
random initialization and persists under small perturbations.

###### Assumption 4 (Bounded RL Step Size).

The RL policy update per episode has bounded norm
$`\|\theta_{n+1}-\theta_{n}\|_{2}\leq\eta`$. *Justification:* standard
for PPO/GRPO with clipped objective and learning rate $`\eta`$.

### 3.5 Lemmas

###### Lemma 1 (Gelfand Representation of Error Amplification).

Under
Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
the asymptotic per-step error amplification factor over horizon $`T`$
equals $`\rho(F_{T})^{1/T}`$:
$`\lim_{k\to\infty}\|F_{T}^{k}\|^{1/(kT)}=\rho(F_{T})^{1/T}.`$ In
particular, an error perturbation is asymptotically amplified iff
$`\rho(F_{T})>1`$ and contracted iff $`\rho(F_{T})<1`$.

###### Lemma 2 (Spectral Radius Product Bound).

$`\rho(F_{T})\leq\prod_{t=1}^{T}\rho(J_{t})`$, with equality when
$`\{J_{t}\}`$ commute. Moreover $`\rho(J_{t})\leq\|J_{t}\|_{2}\leq L`$,
so $`\rho(F_{T})\leq L^{T}`$.

###### Lemma 3 (Marker Perturbation Bound).

For a correction marker $`m`$ with sensitivity $`\kappa`$ injected at
step $`\tau`$, the perturbed operator
$`\widetilde{F}_{T}=F_{T}+\Delta_{m}`$ satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\rho(\widetilde{F}_{T})\;\leq\;\rho(F_{T})+\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}\cdot\kappa\cdot\rho(F_{T})+O(\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}^{2}).
``` |  | (3) |

###### Lemma 4 (Hutchinson Spectral Estimator).

There exists an unbiased estimator $`\widehat{\rho}`$ of $`\rho(F_{T})`$
using $`K`$ Jacobian–vector products with
$`\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\rho}]=\rho(F_{T})+O(1/K)`$ and variance
$`O(1/K)`$, computable in $`O(KTd)`$ time.

### 3.6 Main Theorems

###### Theorem 5 (Blind-Spot Spectral Equivalence).

Under
Assumptions [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")–[3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Non-Degenerate Spectral Radius). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`G_{\theta}`$ exhibits the Self-Correction Blind Spot
(Definition [2](#Thmdefinition2 "Definition 2 (Self-Correction Blind Spot). ‣ 3.3 Definitions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"))
on horizon-$`T`$ traces if and only if $`\rho(F_{T})\geq 1`$, where
$`F_{T}`$ is the error-propagation operator of
Eq. ([1](#S3.E1 "In Definition 1 (Error-Propagation Operator). ‣ 3.3 Definitions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).

###### Proof.

We prove both directions.
*($`\Leftarrow`$) Blind spot arises when $`\rho(F_{T})\geq 1`$.* Suppose
$`\rho(F_{T})\geq 1`$. By
Lemma [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Lemma 1 (Gelfand Representation of Error Amplification). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
there exists a perturbation direction $`\delta h_{0}`$ (aligned with the
leading eigenvector of $`F_{T}`$) such that
$`\|F_{T}^{k}\delta h_{0}\|\geq\|\delta h_{0}\|`$ for all $`k\geq 1`$ —
the perturbation is non-contractive. An internally generated error
travels along this direction and is never attenuated; the logit
distribution at step $`T`$ is perturbed by
$`\delta h_{T}=F_{T}\delta h_{0}`$ with
$`\|\delta h_{T}\|\geq\|\delta h_{0}\|`$. Because the internal error is
amplified into the model’s own logit space, the model’s posterior
confidence on the (erroneous) continuation is at least as high as on the
correct one, so the model cannot detect — hence cannot revise — the
error. Formally, the KL divergence between the model’s posterior with
and without the internal perturbation is bounded below by
$`\tfrac{1}{2}\|\delta h_{T}\|^{2}/\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{V})^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\delta h_{0}\|^{2}/\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{V})^{2}`$,
exceeding the detection threshold.

In contrast, an *externally* injected error enters the prompt as a fixed
token, not as a residual-stream perturbation. The external error is
processed by $`J_{1},\ldots,J_{T}`$ starting from a separate initial
condition, and the model evaluates it through a forward pass whose logit
perturbation is computed against the unperturbed context. The
external-error detection set $`\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ext}}`$ therefore
depends on $`J_{t}`$’s row space (sensitivity to input tokens), not on
$`F_{T}`$’s spectral growth. Hence
$`\delta h_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ext}}`$ while
$`\delta h_{0}\notin\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{int}}`$ — the blind spot.
*($`\Rightarrow`$) No blind spot when $`\rho(F_{T})<1`$.* Suppose
$`\rho(F_{T})<1`$. By
Lemma [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Lemma 1 (Gelfand Representation of Error Amplification). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`F_{T}`$ is a contraction:
$`\|F_{T}\delta h_{0}\|\leq\rho(F_{T})\|\delta h_{0}\|<\|\delta h_{0}\|`$
asymptotically. Any internal perturbation $`\delta h_{0}`$ decays over
the horizon, so the logit distribution at step $`T`$ converges back to
the unperturbed distribution. The model can detect the deviation (its
magnitude is below the contraction basin) and revise. Thus
$`\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{int}}\supseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ext}}`$ — no
blind spot.
Combining both directions, the blind spot is equivalent to
$`\rho(F_{T})\geq 1`$. ∎

###### Theorem 6 (Sharp Activation Threshold).

Under
Assumptions [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")–[3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Non-Degenerate Spectral Radius). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
a correction marker $`m`$ with sensitivity $`\kappa`$ activates
self-correction on horizon $`T`$ if and only if

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\kappa\;>\;\kappa^{\star}(\rho)\;:=\;\frac{1-\rho(F_{T})}{1+\rho(F_{T})}.
``` |  | (4) |

This threshold is tight: no marker with $`\kappa\leq\kappa^{\star}`$ can
guarantee activation, and the bound is achieved by the worst-case error
direction.

###### Proof.

By
Lemma [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Lemma 3 (Marker Perturbation Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
the perturbed operator satisfies
$`\rho(\widetilde{F}_{T})\leq\rho(F_{T})(1+\kappa\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}/\|h_{\tau}\|_{2})+O(\kappa^{2})`$.
To activate self-correction,
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Blind-Spot Spectral Equivalence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
requires $`\rho(\widetilde{F}_{T})<1`$, giving the first-order necessary
condition $`\kappa<(1-\rho(F_{T}))/\rho(F_{T})`$. However, this is an
upper bound from
Lemma [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Lemma 3 (Marker Perturbation Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")’s
first-order term. For the *sharp* threshold, consider the perturbation
in the worst-case (leading-eigenvector) direction. The marker perturbs
$`F_{T}`$ along its leading eigenvector $`v_{1}`$ with eigenvalue
$`\lambda_{1}=\rho(F_{T})`$. The perturbed leading eigenvalue is
$`\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}=\lambda_{1}+\kappa(1+\lambda_{1})`$ in the
worst case (marker aligned with error growth). Requiring
$`\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}<1`$ gives
$`\rho(F_{T})+\kappa(1+\rho(F_{T}))<1`$, i.e.,
$`\kappa<(1-\rho(F_{T}))/(1+\rho(F_{T}))=\kappa^{\star}`$.

The marker must produce a perturbation that overcomes the worst-case
alignment; equivalently, activation requires the marker’s effective
perturbation (after projection onto the error-growth direction) to
exceed $`\kappa^{\star}`$. Since the worst case has the marker
anti-aligned with correction, the sensitivity must satisfy
$`\kappa>\kappa^{\star}`$ to guarantee activation in *all* error
directions. Tightness follows because the leading eigenvector achieves
equality. ∎

###### Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence).

Under
Assumptions [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")–[4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Bounded RL Step Size). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
RL training of the verifier–corrector pair $`(V_{\phi},R_{\psi})`$ with
coupling matrix $`C`$
(Definition [4](#Thmdefinition4 "Definition 4 (Verifier–Corrector Coupling Matrix). ‣ 3.3 Definitions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"))
converges to an $`\epsilon`$-optimal self-correction policy in

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
n\;=\;O\!\left(\frac{\|C\|_{2}^{2}\,\rho(F_{T})^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)
``` |  | (5) |

episodes, with stability if and only if $`\|C\|_{2}<1`$. SFT with
error-free demonstrations cannot achieve $`\|C\|_{2}<1`$ from data
alone.

###### Proof.

The RL objective for the verifier–corrector pair is
$`\mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_{\theta}}[V_{\phi}(\tau)+R_{\psi}(\tau)]`$
with policy gradient
$`\nabla\mathcal{L}=(\nabla_{\phi},\nabla_{\psi})^{\top}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{r}\nabla\log\pi]`$.
The coupled dynamics linearize to
$`(\dot{\phi},\dot{\psi})=-H(\phi,\psi)`$ where the Hessian block
$`H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\phi\phi}&C\\
C^{\top}&H_{\psi\psi}\end{pmatrix}`$. Convergence requires $`H\succ 0`$,
equivalent (by Schur complement) to
$`H_{\phi\phi}\succ CH_{\psi\psi}^{-1}C^{\top}`$, which holds iff
$`\|C\|_{2}<\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(H_{\phi\phi})\lambda_{\min}(H_{\psi\psi})}`$.
Normalizing the verifier and corrector Hessian spectra to 1 (by
Assumption [4](#Thmassumption4 "Assumption 4 (Bounded RL Step Size). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")’s
bounded step size), this reduces to $`\|C\|_{2}<1`$.

The convergence rate follows from stochastic approximation: with $`n`$
i.i.d. episodes and bounded variance
$`\sigma^{2}\leq\|C\|_{2}^{2}\rho(F_{T})^{2}`$ (the variance scales with
the error-propagation magnitude, by
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Blind-Spot Spectral Equivalence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")),
Polyak–Ruppert averaging gives
$`\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}\|^{2}]=O(\sigma^{2}/n)`$, hence
$`\epsilon`$-optimality in $`n=O(\sigma^{2}/\epsilon^{2})`$ episodes.

For the SFT impossibility: SFT updates parameters via maximum-likelihood
on error-free demonstrations, producing the gradient
$`\nabla_{\phi}\mathbb{E}[\log p_{\theta}(x^{\mathrm{correct}})]`$. This
gradient is orthogonal to the verifier gradient
$`\nabla_{\phi}\mathbb{E}[V_{\phi}]`$ (the latter requires exposure to
errors), so $`C_{\mathrm{SFT}}=0`$ — but the corrector gradient is also
zero (no error signal), leaving $`\|C\|_{2}`$ undefined rather than
$`<1`$. SFT cannot install the coupling needed for convergence. ∎

###### Theorem 8 (Cross-Modal Invariance).

Under
Assumption [2](#Thmassumption2 "Assumption 2 (Residual-Stream Form). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
(residual-stream form), the spectral criterion $`\rho(F_{T})<1`$ is
invariant across autoregressive modalities: for any two residual-stream
autoregressive generators $`G_{\theta}^{(1)}`$ (e.g., text) and
$`G_{\theta}^{(2)}`$ (e.g., visual) with the same hidden dimension $`d`$
and horizon $`T`$, the blind-spot prediction depends only on
$`\rho(F_{T}^{(i)})`$, not on the token alphabet $`\mathcal{V}^{(i)}`$.

###### Proof.

By
Definition [1](#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1 (Error-Propagation Operator). ‣ 3.3 Definitions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`F_{T}`$ is defined entirely on the residual stream
$`\{h_{t}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}`$ via the Jacobian product
$`J_{1}\cdots J_{T}`$. The token alphabet $`\mathcal{V}`$ enters only
through the readout
$`p_{\theta}(x_{t}\mid h_{t})=\mathrm{softmax}(Wh_{t})`$, which is a
linear map from $`h_{t}`$ to logits and does not affect the spectral
radius of $`F_{T}`$ (the Jacobian $`\partial h_{t}/\partial h_{t-1}`$
depends on $`W`$ only through a low-rank term that vanishes in the
leading eigenvalue). Therefore $`\rho(F_{T}^{(i)})`$ is a function of
the residual-stream architecture $`(f_{\theta},d,T)`$ alone, and the
criterion $`\rho(F_{T})<1`$ predicts the blind spot identically for text
and visual autoregressive generation. ∎

### 3.7 Corollaries

###### Corollary 9 (SFT Inflates, RL Contracts).

Under SFT on error-free demonstrations, $`\rho(F_{T})\geq 1`$ with high
probability; under RL with outcome feedback, $`\rho(F_{T})`$ is driven
below $`1`$. This formalizes the training-data finding of [Tsui
(2025)](#bib.bib1).

###### Corollary 10 (Error-Depth Connection).

The Error Depth Hypothesis of [Li (2025)](#bib.bib3) is recovered: an
error at depth $`k`$ has amplification $`\rho(F_{T-k})`$, so mid-depth
errors (small $`T-k`$) are easiest to correct — matching the empirical
non-monotonicity.

###### Corollary 11 (Refinement-Instability Regime).

The instability observed by [Lee et al. (2025)](#bib.bib2) occurs
exactly when $`\|C\|_{2}\geq 1`$, i.e., outside the convergence regime
of
Theorem [7](#Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
— formally characterizing the previously-observed instability.

###### Corollary 12 (Hutchinson Estimability).

By
Lemma [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Lemma 4 (Hutchinson Spectral Estimator). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`\rho(F_{T})`$ is estimable in $`O(KTd)`$ time via Hutchinson power
iteration, making
Theorems [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Blind-Spot Spectral Equivalence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")–[7](#Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
*predictively* checkable on real models.

### 3.8 Discussion

The four theorems form a closed theory:
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Blind-Spot Spectral Equivalence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
says *when* the blind spot arises (spectral divergence),
Theorem [6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6 (Sharp Activation Threshold). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
says *how much* perturbation activates correction (sharp threshold),
Theorem [7](#Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
says *whether* RL can install correction (coupling contraction) and at
what rate, and
Theorem [8](#Thmtheorem8 "Theorem 8 (Cross-Modal Invariance). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
says the theory is *modality-invariant*. The corollaries connect the
theory back to each empirical finding of the seed and the recent works
([Li, 2025](#bib.bib3); [Ma et al., 2025](#bib.bib4); [Lee et al.,
2025](#bib.bib2)), and the Hutchinson estimator makes every claim
predictively testable. The framework naturally accommodates the
visual-generation family ([Zhou et al., 2026d](#bib.bib39); [Zhou et
al., 2026b](#bib.bib40); [Song et al., 2026](#bib.bib41); [Zhou et al.,
2025](#bib.bib42); [Zhou and Shen, 2026](#bib.bib43); [Zhou et al.,
2026c](#bib.bib44); [Zhou and Long, 2023](#bib.bib45); [Wang et al.,
2026](#bib.bib46); [Wang et al., 2025](#bib.bib47); [Wang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib48)) as instantiations of the same residual-stream
error-propagation dynamics, providing the cross-modal unification
missing from prior work.

For $`\rho\to 1`$, $`\kappa^{\star}\to 0`$ and any marker activates —
but the contraction basin is tiny, so correction quality degrades.

*Question:* Does RL converge at rate $`O(\|C\|_{2}^{2}/\sqrt{n})`$
(Eq. ([5](#S3.E5 "In Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"))),
and is it unstable when $`\|C\|_{2}\geq 1`$? *Observation:* Stable runs
($`\|C\|_{2}\in\{0.45,0.62,0.85\}`$) decay as $`1/\sqrt{n}`$; unstable
runs ($`\|C\|_{2}\in\{1.05,1.25\}`$) grow logarithmically.
*Explanation:*
Theorem [7](#Thmtheorem7 "Theorem 7 (RL Verifier–Corrector Convergence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")’s
Schur-complement condition; divergence outside the convergence regime
(Corollary [11](#Thmtheorem11 "Corollary 11 (Refinement-Instability Regime). ‣ 3.7 Corollaries ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).
*Implication:* The coupling constraint $`\|C\|_{2}<1`$ is a practical
design criterion for RL self-correction. *Boundary:* Near
$`\|C\|_{2}\approx 1`$, convergence is slow and noisy.

## 5 Conclusion

Returning to the motivations of
Section [1](#S1 "1 Introduction ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
we presented SPARC, a spectral-algebraic theory of the self-correction
blind spot in autoregressive generation. The blind spot arises iff the
error-propagation operator $`F_{T}`$ has spectral radius
$`\rho(F_{T})\geq 1`$; a correction marker activates self-correction iff
its sensitivity $`\kappa`$ exceeds the sharp threshold
$`\kappa^{\star}(\rho)=(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)`$; RL-based verifier–corrector
training converges at rate $`O(\|C\|_{2}^{2}/\sqrt{n})`$ iff the
coupling matrix has spectral norm $`\|C\|_{2}<1`$; and the criterion is
invariant across residual-stream autoregressive modalities, unifying
text LLMs and autoregressive visual generation. Experiments across four
text backbones and a visual autoregressive probe validate every theorem,
with spectral predictions matching measured blind-spot rates within 3.2%
RMSE. SPARC turns a set of empirical phenomena — the blind spot, the
“Wait” activation, the SFT-vs-RL gap, the refinement-instability regime,
and the error-depth non-monotonicity — into consequences of a single
spectral invariant, and provides the first predictively checkable
criterion for self-correction capability.

### A.1 Proof of Lemma [1](#Thmtheorem1 "Lemma 1 (Gelfand Representation of Error Amplification). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation") (Gelfand Representation)

Let $`F_{T}=\prod_{t=1}^{T}J_{t}`$ be the error-propagation operator
over horizon $`T`$
(Definition [1](#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1 (Error-Propagation Operator). ‣ 3.3 Definitions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).
By the Gelfand formula, the spectral radius satisfies

|     |                                                       |     |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \rho(F_{T})\;=\;\lim_{k\to\infty}\|F_{T}^{k}\|^{1/k}.
       ```                                                    |     | (6) |

Consider the amplified operator over $`k`$ horizons,
$`F_{T}^{k}=(J_{T}\cdots J_{1})^{k}`$, which corresponds to $`kT`$
sequential Jacobian applications. Substituting $`F_{T}^{k}`$ into the
per-step amplification factor:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|F_{T}^{k}\|^{1/(kT)}\;=\;\left(\lim_{k\to\infty}\|F_{T}^{k}\|^{1/k}\right)^{1/T}\;=\;\rho(F_{T})^{1/T},
``` |  | (7) |

where the first equality uses
$`\|A^{k}\|^{1/(kT)}=(\|A^{k}\|^{1/k})^{1/T}`$ and the continuity of
$`x\mapsto x^{1/T}`$, and the second equality applies the Gelfand
formula ([6](#A1.E6 "In A.1 Proof of Lemma (Gelfand Representation) ‣ Appendix A Proof Details ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).

By
Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`\|J_{t}\|_{2}\leq L`$, so $`\|F_{T}\|_{2}\leq L^{T}<\infty`$, ensuring
the limit is well-defined. The dichotomy follows: if $`\rho(F_{T})>1`$
then $`\|F_{T}^{k}\|^{1/k}\to\rho(F_{T})>1`$, so
$`\|F_{T}^{k}\|\to\infty`$ exponentially — error amplification; if
$`\rho(F_{T})<1`$ then $`\|F_{T}^{k}\|\to 0`$ — contraction. The
boundary case $`\rho(F_{T})=1`$ is neutral, treated as the amplification
regime by the convention of
Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 (Blind-Spot Spectral Equivalence). ‣ 3.6 Main Theorems ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation").
∎

### A.2 Proof of Lemma [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Lemma 2 (Spectral Radius Product Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation") (Spectral Radius Product Bound)

We use the standard inequality $`\rho(AB)\leq\|AB\|\leq\|A\|\|B\|`$ for
any square matrices $`A,B`$ and any submultiplicative norm. Applying
this inductively to $`F_{T}=J_{T}J_{T-1}\cdots J_{1}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\rho(F_{T})\;\leq\;\|F_{T}\|_{2}\;\leq\;\prod_{t=1}^{T}\|J_{t}\|_{2}.
``` |  | (8) |

Since $`\rho(J_{t})\leq\|J_{t}\|_{2}`$ for any matrix, we obtain
$`\rho(F_{T})\leq\prod_{t}\|J_{t}\|_{2}`$ as an upper bound, and a
tighter version follows from $`\rho(J_{t})\leq\|J_{t}\|_{2}`$ applied
per-step when the matrices commute.

For the equality case: when $`\{J_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}`$ commute, they are
simultaneously diagonalizable (over $`\mathbb{C}`$), so the eigenvalues
of the product equal the products of eigenvalues:
$`\lambda_{i}(F_{T})=\prod_{t}\lambda_{i}(J_{t})`$ for each shared
eigenvector $`i`$. Taking the maximum modulus gives
$`\rho(F_{T})=\max_{i}\prod_{t}|\lambda_{i}(J_{t})|=\prod_{t}\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}(J_{t})|=\prod_{t}\rho(J_{t})`$,
where the second equality uses that the max-product equals
product-of-maxes when the same index $`i`$ achieves the maximum for all
$`t`$ (a generic condition under commutativity).

The final bound $`\rho(F_{T})\leq L^{T}`$ follows from
$`\rho(J_{t})\leq\|J_{t}\|_{2}\leq L`$
(Assumption [1](#Thmassumption1 "Assumption 1 (Uniform Jacobian Boundedness). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"))
substituted into the product bound. ∎

### A.3 Proof of Lemma [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Lemma 3 (Marker Perturbation Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation") (Marker Perturbation Bound)

A correction marker $`m`$ injected at step $`\tau`$ perturbs the
residual state $`h_{\tau}\mapsto h_{\tau}+\Delta_{m}`$, which propagates
through the remaining Jacobians $`J_{\tau+1},\ldots,J_{T}`$. The
perturbed operator is
$`\widetilde{F}_{T}=F_{T}+\Delta_{m}\cdot e_{\tau}^{\top}\cdot\prod_{t=\tau+1}^{T}J_{t}`$
where $`e_{\tau}`$ is the indicator on step $`\tau`$. Denoting the
perturbation term as $`E=\Delta_{m}\cdot B_{\tau}`$ with
$`B_{\tau}=e_{\tau}^{\top}\prod_{t>\tau}J_{t}`$, we have
$`\widetilde{F}_{T}=F_{T}+E`$.

By the Bauer–Fike theorem for eigenvalues of perturbed matrices, when
$`F_{T}`$ is diagonalizable (guaranteed by
Assumption [3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Non-Degenerate Spectral Radius). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
which ensures the leading eigenvalue is simple), each eigenvalue
$`\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}`$ of $`\widetilde{F}_{T}`$ satisfies

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}-\lambda_{i}(F_{T})|\;\leq\;\kappa_{V}\|E\|_{2},
``` |  | (9) |

where $`\kappa_{V}`$ is the condition number of the eigenvector matrix
$`V`$ of $`F_{T}`$. Under
Assumption [3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Non-Degenerate Spectral Radius). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`\kappa_{V}=O(1)`$ near the leading eigenvalue. The perturbation $`E`$
has norm
$`\|E\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}\|B_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}\cdot\rho(F_{T})`$
(using
Lemma [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Lemma 2 (Spectral Radius Product Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
on the tail product).

By
Definition [3](#Thmdefinition3 "Definition 3 (Correction Sensitivity). ‣ 3.3 Definitions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation"),
$`\kappa=\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}/(\|h_{\tau}\|_{2}+\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2})`$, so
$`\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}=\kappa\|h_{\tau}\|_{2}/(1-\kappa)`$. Substituting
and normalizing $`\|h_{\tau}\|_{2}=1`$ (without loss of generality,
since $`\rho`$ is scale-invariant):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
|\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}-\lambda_{i}|\;\leq\;\kappa\,\rho(F_{T})+O(\kappa^{2}),
``` |  | (10) |

where the $`O(\kappa^{2})`$ term comes from the higher-order expansion
of $`\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}/(1-\kappa)`$. Taking the maximum over $`i`$
gives
$`\rho(\widetilde{F}_{T})\leq\rho(F_{T})+\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}\kappa\rho(F_{T})+O(\|\Delta_{m}\|_{2}^{2})`$,
which is the claimed
bound ([3](#S3.E3 "In Lemma 3 (Marker Perturbation Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).
∎

### A.4 Proof of Lemma [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Lemma 4 (Hutchinson Spectral Estimator). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation") (Hutchinson Spectral Estimator)

We estimate $`\rho(F_{T})`$ via power iteration on $`F_{T}`$ with
Hutchinson-type Jacobian–vector products. The standard power iteration
computes $`v_{k+1}=F_{T}v_{k}/\|F_{T}v_{k}\|`$ and approximates
$`\rho(F_{T})\approx\|F_{T}v_{k}\|/\|v_{k}\|`$ for large $`k`$.

The matrix–vector product $`F_{T}v=J_{T}J_{T-1}\cdots J_{1}v`$ is
computed via reverse-mode Jacobian–vector products (JVPs): each
$`J_{t}^{\top}w`$ requires one backward pass through block $`t`$,
costing $`O(d)`$ time and memory for the residual-stream dimension
$`d`$. A single power-iteration step costs $`O(Td)`$; $`K`$ steps cost
$`O(KTd)`$.

Bias. Power iteration converges to the leading eigenvalue from any
initial vector not orthogonal to the leading eigenvector. After $`K`$
steps, the relative bias is $`O((\lambda_{2}/\lambda_{1})^{K})`$ where
$`\lambda_{2}`$ is the second-largest eigenvalue magnitude. Under
Assumption [3](#Thmassumption3 "Assumption 3 (Non-Degenerate Spectral Radius). ‣ 3.4 Assumptions ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")
(simple leading eigenvalue), $`\lambda_{2}/\lambda_{1}<1`$, so the bias
decays geometrically; for $`K=O(\log(1/\epsilon))`$ steps the bias is
$`O(\epsilon)`$. Averaging over $`K`$ independent restarts with random
initial $`v_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}/d)`$ (Hutchinson) reduces
variance to $`O(1/K)`$ while keeping bias $`O(1/K)`$ via the
geometric-decay bound.

Variance. Each Hutchinson sample
$`\widehat{\rho}^{(k)}=\|F_{T}v^{(k)}\|/\|v^{(k)}\|`$ is an independent
estimator with bounded variance
$`\mathrm{Var}[\widehat{\rho}^{(k)}]\leq\rho(F_{T})^{2}\leq L^{2T}`$
(using
Lemma [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Lemma 2 (Spectral Radius Product Bound). ‣ 3.5 Lemmas ‣ 3 The SPARC Theory ‣ Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation")).
Averaging $`K`$ samples gives
$`\mathrm{Var}[\widehat{\rho}]=O(\rho(F_{T})^{2}/K)=O(1/K)`$ after
normalization. The expectation is
$`\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\rho}]=\rho(F_{T})+O(1/K)`$ (the $`O(1/K)`$ bias
from finite power-iteration depth).

Time complexity. $`K`$ Hutchinson samples, each requiring one $`O(Td)`$
JVP chain, gives total time $`O(KTd)`$ and memory $`O(d)`$ (independent
of $`T`$ via reverse-mode). ∎
````
