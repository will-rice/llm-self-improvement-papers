###### Abstract.

Multi-agent learning is intrinsically harder, more unstable and
unpredictable than single agent optimization. For this reason, numerous
specialized heuristics and techniques have been designed towards the
goal of achieving convergence to equilibria in self-play. One such
celebrated approach is the use of dynamically adaptive learning rates.
Although such techniques are known to allow for improved convergence
guarantees in small games, it has been much harder to analyze them in
more relevant settings with large populations of agents. These settings
are particularly hard as recent work has established that learning with
fixed rates will become chaotic given large enough
populations \[[17](#bib.bib17), [9](#bib.bib9)\]. In this work, we show
that chaos persists in large population congestion games despite using
adaptive learning rates even for the ubiquitous Multiplicative Weight
Updates algorithm, even in the presence of only two strategies. At a
technical level, due to the non-autonomous nature of the system, our
approach goes beyond conventional period-three
techniques \[[29](#bib.bib29)\] by studying fundamental properties of
the dynamics including invariant sets, volume expansion and turbulent
sets. We complement our theoretical insights with experiments showcasing
that slight variations to system parameters lead to a wide variety of
unpredictable behaviors.

###### Key words and phrases: 

Nash equilibrium; Li-Yorke Chaos; Adaptive MWU; Turbulence sets.

## 1. Introduction

Arguably one of the most thorny problems on the intersection of learning
and games is the development of simple and practical algorithms that
converge to Nash equilibria. The problem in its full generality is known
to be intractable \[[18](#bib.bib18), [23](#bib.bib23)\], however,
recent developments in Machine Learning have lead to a revived interest
in the problem even in special classes of games. Unfortunately, the
additional scrutiny has only helped crystallize the severity of the
problem at hand through a diverse set of non-convergence, instability
results even in well motivated special classes of
games \[[31](#bib.bib31), [5](#bib.bib5), [19](#bib.bib19),
[2](#bib.bib2), [22](#bib.bib22), [28](#bib.bib28), [4](#bib.bib4),
[24](#bib.bib24), [6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7)\]. Worse yet, standard
online learning meta-algorithms, such as Multiplicative Weights Updates
(MWU), have been shown to exhibit chaos \[[33](#bib.bib33),
[13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15), [9](#bib.bib9),
[17](#bib.bib17)\], which has be shown to be rather common in more
complex games, e.g., as we increase the number of agents
 \[[21](#bib.bib21), [37](#bib.bib37), [9](#bib.bib9),
[17](#bib.bib17)\]. Given this proliferation of negative results, what
other approaches are left to explore?

An interesting hint can be found in some of the earliest AI work on the
subject. \[[42](#bib.bib42)\] established arguably one of the first
non-convergence results in the area, showing that gradient dynamics do
not suffice to achieve point-wise convergence even in the trivial case
of two agent, two strategy games. On the positive side, when those
dynamics are non-convergent, time-average convergence results can be
established. Building up on their work, \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] showed that
a modification of these standard dynamics where the agents can
dynamically update their step-size based on payoff cues from their
environment suffices to stabilize these dynamics in all $`2\times 2`$
games. Informally, the specific heuristic, Win or Learn Fast (WoLF), has
the agents increase their learning rate when they are "losing" in an
effort to escape from a non-promising region of the state space.
Unfortunately, WoLF requires that each agent has knowledge to a Nash
equilibrium strategy for themselves, which means it can only be applied
in small games. On the positive side, this first result has led to
several other more similar heuristics, with promising results in small
games \[[8](#bib.bib8), [11](#bib.bib11), [1](#bib.bib1),
[25](#bib.bib25), [26](#bib.bib26), [10](#bib.bib10)\]. While staying in
the realm of small games, such heuristics have been shown to robustly
improve the stability of other popular learning heuristics such as
Proximal Policy Optimization \[[38](#bib.bib38), [35](#bib.bib35)\]. On
the negative side, even for slightly larger games most positive results
are largely empirical in nature and effectively very little is known
about the behavior of such techniques in games with many agents. These
works raise our key motivating question:

Does the simple idea of judiciously increasing the learning rate to
“escape" barren regions of the state space scale to games with a large
number of agents? If not, what type of formal instability results can be
established?

Our approach. We focus on one of the most well studied class of large
population games, non-atomic congestion games \[[36](#bib.bib36),
[32](#bib.bib32)\] where the agents learn using the ubiquitous MWU
update rule \[[3](#bib.bib3), [20](#bib.bib20), [30](#bib.bib30)\].
Furthermore, given the recent works of \[[17](#bib.bib17),
[9](#bib.bib9)\], we focus on games with exactly two strategies/routes
and linear cost functions, since such settings are already sufficiently
hard for dynamics. Specifically, given fixed, non-adaptive learning
rates, MWU dynamics typically will bifurcate to chaotic behavior for a
large enough population size. Critically, however, these previous
results are based on the celebrated "Period three implies chaos"
methodology pioneered by Li-Yorke \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], which is only
applicable in autonomous, i.e., time-invariant systems. In contrast,
understanding the emergent behavior in our case will require totally
different techniques.

Our model will allow for a wide range of dynamically adaptive learning
rates. In particular, instead of having only two learning rates, fast
and slow, as, e.g., in \[[12](#bib.bib12)\], we will allow for a
continuous range of learning rates. Moreover, the decision to
increase/decrease the learning rate will be driven by a regret-like
measure \[[39](#bib.bib39)\]. Intuitively, each agent compares the
historical time-average performance of both routes available to them. If
both routes appear very similar to each other then the agents favor
large learning rates so as to reach sufficiently different
configurations where they can hopefully find informative payoff signals
to exploit. On the other hand, if one route is significantly better than
the other then the agents will take smaller but non-vanishing step sizes
so as to take advantage of such opportunities without overcorrecting.

Our main result. We prove that our class of dynamics exhibits Li-Yorke
chaotic behavior, implying the existence of an uncountable set of
initial conditions that become “scrambled" by the dynamics
(Theorem [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).
Formally, for any two initial conditions from this set, the trajectories
of the game dynamics come arbitrarily close to each other and move away
from each other infinitely often.

Figure 1. An assortment of bifurcation diagrams for different adaptive

Our techniques. Despite the intense recent interest in developing formal
arguments about chaos in game settings \[[9](#bib.bib9),
[17](#bib.bib17), [27](#bib.bib27), [34](#bib.bib34)\] proving chaos in
non-autonomous dynamical systems is a challenging endeavor, as the
departure from one-dimensional autonomous systems gives up from many
established equivalences among different notions of chaos, such as
volume expansion, topological entropy, and arbitrarily long periodic
points.

One possible approach to addressing this challenge would be to first
establish that the sequence of employed maps converges uniformly to a
fixed chaotic map, and then transfer the resulting scrambled set of the
limit system to the non-autonomous one.

Although this property is not true for our system, it suffices to use a
slightly relaxed version of it. We establish uniform convergence
guarantees for initialization sets that are confined to the interior of
the strategy space
(Lemma [10](#Thmlemma10 "Lemma 10. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")),
while tolerating the discrepancy between the varying-step and the
corresponding MWU map in the limit. To confirm the existence of an
uncountable scrambled set, we propose a novel connection between the
behavior of MWU maps with varying learning rates and symbolic dynamics.
Essentially, our proof strategy entails assigning every binary sequence
to a different initialization such that scrambled binary strings
correspond to scrambled initializations of the our non-autonomous
dynamical system (Proof of
Theorem [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

The first part of our analysis
(Section [3](#S3 "3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"))
involves enhancing previously existing results for the case of fixed
learning rate dynamics. Specifically, we present an explicit
construction of a _perpetual set_ $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$, i.e., a forward
invariant set where our dynamical system is surjective,
$`f_{a}(\mathcal{F}(a))=\mathcal{F}(a)`$, where $`f_{a}`$ represents the
MWU system with fixed learning rate $`a`$.
(Lemma [3](#Thmlemma3 "Lemma 3. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).
Next we further show that, even in the presence of agent volatility due
to their high learning rates, if their initial strategies lie within the
interior of the strategy space, their strategies will eventually be
absorbed into the perpetual set we have constructed
(Lemma [4](#Thmlemma4 "Lemma 4. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

In the case of fixed learning rate, we are able to quantify an even
stronger volume expansion result. Specifically, we show that the image
of any arbitrarily small neighborhood of the mixed equilibrium of our
game will converge exactly to the perpetual set we have constructed
(Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

Turning our attention to the adaptively dynamic learning rate
(Section [4](#S4 "4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")),
we show that thanks to $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$, while learning rate may vary
over iterations and initializations, there exists a set $`\Delta`$ –the
closure of all perpetual sets $`\mathcal{F}(a_{n})`$ –to which our
dynamics eventually will be absorbed
(Lemmas [5](#Thmlemma5 "Lemma 5 (Forward Invariance Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),[6](#Thmlemma6 "Lemma 6 (Absorption Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")),
provided the initialization set lies in the interior of solution space.
Leveraging this property, we show that despite the volatility due to
agents employing potentially high learning rates, our average
regret-like concepts converge for nearly all initial conditions (Lemmas
[7](#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),[8](#Thmlemma8 "Lemma 8. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),[9](#Thmlemma9 "Lemma 9. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).
It is noteworthy that this result requires a substantial degree of
additional technicality in contrast to its analogue for the fixed rate
regime \[[17](#bib.bib17)\].

Before developing our machinery for the symbolic dynamics reduction in
Section [5](#S5 "5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
our proof strategy includes an additional step of demonstrating that our
system does not collapse early in any fixed point or subspace of
strategies that lacks volume expansion, as this would eliminate chaos.
To achieve this, we rely on
Theorem [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
which guarantees that almost all neighborhoods of mixed equilibrium will
avoid this scenario by volume expanding and approximately covering
$`\mathcal{F}(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n})`$. Putting everything
together our main result about chaotic limit behavior follows.

## 2. Problem setup and preliminaries

### 2.1. Dynamical systems & Li Yorke chaos

A discrete autonomous $`m`$-dimensional dynamical system is described by
an equation $`x_{n+1}=f(x_{n})`$ for a function
$`f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}`$. In contrast a discrete
$`m`$-dimensional non-autonomous dynamical system is described by an
equation $`x_{n+1}=f(x_{n},n)`$ for a function
$`f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}`$. In both of these
cases we can view the $`x_{n}(x_{0})`$ iterate as a function of the
initialization $`x_{0}`$. We thus term the sequence
$`\{x_{n}(x_{0})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}`$ the orbit of the dynamical system
of $`x_{0}`$.

We call two initializations $`p,q`$ of a dynamical system scrambled if
$`\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}(p)-x_{n}(q)\|>0`$ and
$`\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}(p)-x_{n}(q)\|=0`$. Intuitively the orbits
of $`p,q`$ continuously alternate between moving apart and arbitrarily
close respectively. A set $`S`$ is scrambled if every pair $`p,q\in S`$
is scrambled. We are now ready to define the notion of Li Yorke chaos
for both autonomous and non-autonomous dynamical systems.

###### Definition 1.

An autonomous/non-autonomous dynamical system is called Li Yorke chaotic
if there is an uncountably infinite set $`S`$ that is scrambled.

A famous result for the case of one dimensional autonomous dynamical
systems provides a simple and easily verified sufficient condition for
chaos based on periodic orbits:

###### Theorem 1 (\[[29](#bib.bib29)\]).

Let $`J`$ be an interval and $`f:J\to J`$ be a continuous map of a
dynamical system. If $`f`$ has a period-three orbit, then it is Li-Yorke
chaotic.

### 2.2. Linear congestion games

In this work we consider the case of non-atomic two strategy linear
congestion games. In a non-atomic congestion game, there is a continuum
of agents each of which controls an infinitesimal fraction of the total
flow $`N`$. In each iteration, a fraction of agents $`x`$ choose the
first action and $`1-x`$ the second one, suffering costs
$`c_{1}(x)=\gamma Nx`$ and $`c_{2}(1-x)=\delta N(1-x)`$ respectively
where $`\gamma,\delta>0`$ are the linear cost coefficients of each
action respectively.

### 2.3. Multiplicative Weights Update

The dynamics of the MWU meta-algorithm correspond to an autonomous
dynamical system. Given the common learning rate of all agents
$`\eta>0`$, the MWU update is as follows

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                 |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle x_{n+1}=\frac{x_{n}e^{-\eta c_{1}(x_{n})}}{x_{n}e^{-\eta c_{1}(x_{n})}+(1-x_{n})e^{-\eta c_{2}(1-x_{n})}}=\frac{x_{n}}{x_{n}+(1-x_{n})e^{-\eta(c_{2}(1-x_{n})-c_{1}(x_{n}))}}`$ |     |

We can further simplify the MWU update rule by making the following
changes of variables

|     |                                                 |     |                                                  |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------- | --- | ------------------------------------------------ | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle a=\frac{N\eta}{\delta+\gamma}`$ |     | $`\displaystyle b=\frac{\delta}{\delta+\gamma}`$ |     |

where $`a`$ corresponds to a normalized learning rate that accounts for
the scale of $`c_{1}`$ and $`c_{2}`$ and $`(b,1-b)`$ is the Nash
equilibrium of the game. The simplified update rule can be expressed via
a parametric map $`f`$ over $`a`$ and $`b`$ that captures the whole
class of games we are interested in

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (1) |     |

````math
x_{n+1}=f(x_{n},a,b)=\frac{x_{n}}{x_{n}+(1-x_{n})\exp(a(x_{n}-b))}.
``` |  |

When $`a,b`$ are fixed we may skip them from the notation of MWU map and
use just $`f(x)`$.

### 2.4. Dynamic Learning Rate Model

Here we extend the MWU meta-algorithm to adaptive learning rates
$`\eta`$. The basis of our adapting dynamics is a notion of
pseudo-regret that the agents suffer when choosing the first action over
the second one. The definition captures the average difference of costs
between the two actions weighted by the learning rate at each iteration
$`\eta_{i}`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r_{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\eta_{i}[c_{1}(x_{i})-c_{2}(1-x_{i})].
``` |  |

Based on this calculation of pseudo-regret, the agents may seek to adapt
their learning rates $`\eta_{i}`$ in order to balance between
exploration and exploitation. When $`|r_{n}|`$ is big then it is clear
than one action dominates over the other so the agents seek to move in
the direction of improving costs but hopefully not too aggressively so
as not to overshoot, akin to a gradient-like dynamics. In contrast, when
$`|r_{n}|`$ is close to zero the average penalty of switching between
actions is small since they suffer the same weighted cost in average.
Thus there is an opportunity for the agents to increase their learning
rate to encourage exploration while the cost differences are low. To
hedge between exploration and exploitation as above, agents can pick
appropriate function $`h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}`$ that peaks at
$`0`$ and setting $`\eta_{n}=h(r_{n})`$.

This dynamics of adapting $`\eta_{i}`$ to the pseudo-regret can be
implemented as a 2-dimensional non-autonomous dynamical system with
$`x_{n}`$ and $`r_{n}`$ as its state variables:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (2) |  | $`\displaystyle x_{n+1}=f\left(x_{n},\frac{Ng(r_{n})}{\gamma+\delta},b\right)\quad r_{n+1}=\frac{nr_{n}+h(r_{n})(c_{1}(x_{n})-c_{2}(1-x_{n}))}{n+1}.`$ |  |

The system is non-autonomous in the 2-dimensional space as the update
rule of $`r_{n}`$ depends on $`n`$. We can always view $`d`$-dimensional
non-autonomous systems as autonomous systems in $`d+1`$ dimensions by
treating $`n`$ as an additional dimension. However, this does not really
simplify things as
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 ([]). ‣ 2.1. Dynamical systems & Li Yorke chaos ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
does not apply beyond a single dimension.

For simplicity, we rewrite the dynamics in terms of the normalized
learning rates $`a_{i}`$. Let $`a_{i}`$ be the normalized learning rates
corresponding to $`\eta_{i}`$ based on
[Eq. 1](#S2.E1 "In 2.3. Multiplicative Weights Update ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates").
We can then rewrite $`r_{n+1}`$ as

|     |                                                    |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       r_{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}[x_{i}-b].
       ```                                                 |     |

Similarly, given that $`\eta_{i}`$ and $`\alpha_{i}`$ are equivalent up
to game specific scale parameters, we can always find a $`g`$ such that
$`a_{n}=g(r_{n})`$ . Instead of working in terms of the cumbersome
dynamical system update rule
[Eq. 2](#S2.E2 "In 2.4. Dynamic Learning Rate Model ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
we will be be performing our analysis in terms of these simpler
equations

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle x_{n+1}=f(x_{n},a_{n},b)`$ |  | $`\displaystyle a_{n+1}=g\left(\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(x_{i}-b)}{n+1}\right).`$ |  |

In the following sections we will make some assumptions on $`g`$. We
will chose $`g`$ to be a continuous function bounded in
$`[a_{\min},a_{\max}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}`$. We will also assume that
the initialization $`a_{0}`$ is also a constant in
$`[a_{\min},a_{\max}]`$.

Throughout this work we will think of $`x_{n}`$ and $`a_{n}`$ as
functions of the initialization $`x_{0},a_{0}`$. Thus, the complete
notation of the $`n`$-th iterate would be $`x_{n}(x_{0},a_{0})`$ and
$`a_{n}(x_{0},a_{0})`$, however, when it does not hinder understanding
we will drop the explicit description of all the dependencies.

## 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates

Figure 2. The order of $`f_{min}(a)`$, $`x_{\max}(a)`$, $`b`$,
$`x_{\min}(a)`$, $`f_{max}(a)`$ in $`x`$-axis.

We start our analysis by providing a refined analysis in the regime of
fixed learning rate as in \[[17](#bib.bib17)\]. In the following we will
examine conditions of such systems under the assumption of $`a>4`$. Let
us study the local minima and maxima of $`f`$:

|     |                                                               |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{\min/\max}(a)=\frac{1}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{a}}
       ```                                                            |     |

Correspondingly we will denote $`f_{\max}(a)=f(x_{\max}(a),a,b)`$ and
$`f_{\min}(a)=f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)`$. Our first observation is connected
with the order of these values for high enough learning rate:

###### Lemma 1.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`a_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>a_{b}`$

|     |                                                   |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f_{\min}(a)<x_{\max}(a)<b<x_{\min}(a)<f_{\max}(a)
       ```                                                |     |

and $`f_{\min}(a)`$ is decreasing and $`f_{\max}(a)`$ is increasing.

Additionally, by construction, we can show that the interval
$`\mathcal{F}(a)=[f_{min}(a),f_{max}(a)]`$ consists a forward invariant
set for our dynamical system. In other words, if the MWU map with fixed
learning rate $`a`$ starts in a state that is within $`I`$, it will
remain in that set for all future times.

###### Lemma 2.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$ there is a $`s_{b}`$ such that
$`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ is forward invariant for all $`a>s_{b}`$, i.e.

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x\in\mathcal{F}(a)\Rightarrow f(x,a,b)\in\mathcal{F}(a).
       ```                                                       |     |

We can also prove that $`f`$ is surjective on $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ for
high enough learning rates.

###### Lemma 3.

For $`a>a_{b}`$, $`f`$ is surjective on $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$, i.e.
$`f(\mathcal{F}(a),a,b)=\mathcal{F}(a).`$

When $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ is both surjective and forward invariant as is
the case for $`a>a_{b},s_{b}`$, we will call $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ a
*perpetual* set. Actually, $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ is also an absorbing set
for all $`x\in(0,1)`$, in the following sense:

###### Lemma 4.

Let $`a>s_{b}`$,
$`x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]\Rightarrow\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\text{ s.t }\ x_{n}\in\mathcal{F}(a)`$,
for every $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$.

We now turn to the chaotic properties of $`f`$. Given that period-three
orbits do not carry over to the dynamic learning rate case, we choose to
study an alternative property of chaotic maps, namely volume expansion.
In autonomous maps, the existence of period-three orbits together with
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 ([]). ‣ 2.1. Dynamical systems & Li Yorke chaos ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
imply that there is an initialization set whose volume under the
dynamics expands quickly. Intuitively, the more volume this
initialization set covers the more chaotic the dynamic is. While
[Theorem 1](#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 ([]). ‣ 2.1. Dynamical systems & Li Yorke chaos ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
guarantees the existence of volume expansion, it does not quantify how
much volume it eventually covers. We prove that any interval around
$`b`$ is sufficient to eventually cover $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$:

###### Theorem 2.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`v_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>v_{b}`$ and any interval $`[\gamma,\delta]`$ :
$`\{b\}\subset[\gamma,\delta]\subset(0,1)`$, it holds that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\quad f^{n}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)=\mathcal{F}(a).
``` |  |

The proof of this theorem, which notably has not been established in any
prior work, relies on a novel argument that is based on the monotonicity
of $`f`$ on $`\mathcal{D}=[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$, the lack of
period-2 trajectories in $`\mathcal{D}`$ and the instability of fixed
point $`x=b`$.

The key takeaway for the next section is that we can focus our efforts
on analyzing the behavior of the non autonomous system in the interior
of $`(0,1)`$ with special attention to neighborhoods of $`b`$ that alone
can exhibit chaotic behavior. This is especially important because as we
will see our notion of pseudo-regret converges uniformly in closed
intervals in the interior of $`(0,1)`$ but not on $`[0,1]`$.

## 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems

Turning our attention to the dynamic learning rate setting, we will
refer to $`x_{n}(x_{0})`$ as the $`n`$-th iterate given the
initialization $`x_{0}`$, and $`a_{n}(x_{0})`$ the learning rate at the
same iteration, respectively.

Leveraging the existence of the perpetual set $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ in the
fixed rate case, we can construct a forward invariant absorbing set.
This set will be crucial in proving the Li-Yorke chaotic behavior for
the non-autonomous case. Intuitively, although the learning rate is
varying both among different initializations and iterations, there
exists a set which corresponds to the closure of all perpetual sets
$`\{\mathcal{F}(a_{n})\}`$, to which our dynamics are always absorbed.
This motivates the definition of set $`\Delta`$ as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta=\left[\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a),\max_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\max}(a)\right]
``` |  |

where $`a_{\min}`$ and $`a_{\max}`$ are the
$`\min_{x_{0}\in[0,1]}g(x_{0})`$ and $`\max_{x_{0}\in[0,1]}g(x_{0})`$
correspondingly.

###### Lemma 5 (Forward Invariance Property).

For all $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ we get that $`\Delta`$ is forward invariant,
i.e.

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle x_{n}(x_{0})\in\Delta\implies x_{n+1}(x_{0})\in\Delta.`$ |  |

More interestingly, we show that eventually, any subinterval of
$`(0,1)`$ will be absorbsed within $`\Delta`$.

###### Lemma 6 (Absorption Property).

For all $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ and $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$, there is
an $`n_{0}\geq 0`$ so that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall n\geq n_{0}\quad x_{n}([\gamma,\zeta])\subseteq\Delta.`$ |  |

The key observation for the proof of the aforementioned lemmas is the
fact that at any iteration $`x_{n}(x_{0})`$ will always come closer to
the perpetual absorbing set $`\mathcal{F}(a_{n}(x_{0}))`$ and thus to
its closure $`\Delta`$.

An immediate consequence of this lemma is the following corollary which
will be dominant element for the uniform convergence both of our
proposed *pseudo-regret* notion and the Césaro mean of the iterations.
Analytically, for a given subinterval $`[\gamma,\zeta]`$ of $`(0,1)`$,
we can always choose some $`\delta>0`$ such that
$`\Delta\cup[\gamma,\zeta]\subseteq(\delta,1-\delta)`$. Again such
choice of $`\delta`$ is possible because the attraction to $`\Delta`$ is
not eventual phenomenon – at each iteration,
$`\mathrm{dist}(x_{n}(x_{0}),\Delta)`$ will be either zero or always
decreasing . This argumentation is expressed by the following statement:

###### Corollary 1.

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$, then for every $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$,
there is an $`\delta>0`$ so that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall n\geq 0\quad x_{n}([\gamma,\zeta])\subseteq(\delta,1-\delta).`$ |  |

Having established
Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
we are now in a position to present our claims regarding the average
convergence for any initialization in $`(0,1)`$. Notably, while the
individual iterates of the system, as we shall demonstrate in the
ensuing section, exhibit chaotic characteristics, the iterate averages
display stabilization over time.

Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
indicates that for any $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, $`x_{n}(x_{0})`$ will remain
bounded away from the endpoints $`{0,1}`$. We now seek to connect how
the boundness away from $`{0,1}`$ affects the average iterate of a
trajectory. By induction on $`x_{n+1}=f(x_{n},a_{n},b)`$ we can prove
the following

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (3) |  |
``` math
x_{n}=\frac{x_{0}}{x_{0}+(1-x_{0})\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{i}-b)\right)}.
``` |  |

Observe that if we choose a $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, then $`x_{n}`$ is bounded
away from $`{0,1}`$ if and only if $`\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{i}-b)`$ is
bounded. Specifically we can prove:

###### Lemma 7.

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ and $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)=0.
``` |  |

Upon examination of the proof, a salient conclusion is that as $`x_{0}`$
moves farther away from the absorbing set $`\Delta`$ and closer to the
endpoints $`{0,1}`$, the aforementioned convergence rate becomes
increasingly slow, and fails in the case that $`x_{0}\in\{0,1\}`$,
precluding a blanket result for the closed interval $`[0,1]`$.
Conversely, if we restrict our attention to an arbitrary subinterval
$`[\gamma,\zeta]`$, bounded away from the endpoints, we can always
exploit the minimum convergence rate of this interval to derive a
uniform convergence bound for the learning rate

###### Lemma 8.

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$. The sequence of functions $`a_{n}(\cdot)`$ is
converging uniformly to the constant function $`a^{\star}(\cdot)=g(0)`$
in every interval $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$.

Having established that $`a_{n}(x_{0})`$ converges to $`a^{\star}=g(0)`$
for any $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, we are able to strengthen
Lemma [7](#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
demonstrating that its unweighted version of the Césaro mean (i.e. the
average iterate in the limit of infinite time) also converges to $`b`$.

###### Lemma 9.

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ and $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, then

|     |                                                             |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}(x_{0})=b.
       ```                                                          |     |

It is noteworthy that, despite this being equivalent to the guarantee
provided in prior work, e.g. \[[17](#bib.bib17)\], for the fixed
learning rate case, the machinery developed here for the adaptive rate
regime has been more complex and involved.

The next lemma will be essential in our proofs of chaos and examines the
relationship between $`(x_{n}(x_{0}),a_{n}(x_{0}))`$ and the MWU map
with a fixed learning rate $`f(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{\star},b)`$.

###### Lemma 10.

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$. For every $`k`$, $`\epsilon>0`$ and
$`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\forall n\geq n_{0}\ \max_{x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]}\lvert x_{n+k}(x_{0})-f^{k}(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{\star},b)\rvert\leq\epsilon.
``` |  |

We can intuitively think of this result as a form of uniform convergence
result for the sequence of MWU maps with varying learning rates to the
fixed learning rate map. We establish this result by showing that for
small changes in the learning rate, i.e.,
$`a\in(a_{n}(x_{0})-\tilde{\epsilon},a_{n}(x_{0})+\tilde{\epsilon})`$,
the sensitivity of $`f(x_{n}(x_{0}),a,b)`$ is independent of the choice
of $`x_{0}`$. It is thus not sufficient to prove that $`f(x,a,b)`$ is
continuous in $`a`$. Instead we use the Mean Value Theorem to argue that
$`f(x,a,b)`$ is Lipschitz continuous in $`a`$ with a Lipschitz constant
independent of $`x`$. It is crucial to note that this uniform
convergence result holds only in intervals
$`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$ because only then does the learning rate
$`a_{n}`$ converge uniformly to $`a(\cdot)=a^{\star}`$.

We now prove our first volume expansion lemma for the dynamic learning
rate case, namely that neighborhoods of $`b`$ within
$`\mathcal{D}(a_{\min})`$ eventually expand to at least
$`\mathcal{F}(a_{\min})`$. The intuition behind this result is based on
the monotonicity property of $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$. Specifically, for high
enough learning rates $`a_{1}\leq a_{2}`$, we have that
$`\mathcal{F}(a_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{F}(a_{2})`$.

###### Lemma 11.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is an $`z_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a_{\min}>z_{b}`$ it holds that for every $`[\gamma,\delta]`$ such that
$`\{b\}\subset[\gamma,\delta]\subseteq\mathcal{D}(a_{\min})=[x_{\max}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})]`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\ \ x_{n}([\gamma,\delta])\supseteq\mathcal{F}(a_{\min}).
``` |  |

###### Remark 1.

It is worth mentioning that the series of
Lemmas [6](#Thmlemma6 "Lemma 6 (Absorption Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),[5](#Thmlemma5 "Lemma 5 (Forward Invariance Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),[7](#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
and
[9](#Thmlemma9 "Lemma 9. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),[11](#Thmlemma11 "Lemma 11. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
of this section –pertaining to forward invariance, absorption, volume
expansion, and convergence of Césaro means –hold actually regardless of
the choice of the update rule and are of independent interest.

For the case of the fixed learning rate,
Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
makes a more refined prediction compared to
Lemma [11](#Thmlemma11 "Lemma 11. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
as the former provides an equality. In the dynamic learning rate case
there is a gap between the eventual image upper bound, $`\Delta`$ and
the volume expansion lower bound $`\mathcal{F}(a_{\min})`$. In order to
close this gap we will make use of the fact that the learning rate
converges to $`g(0)`$.

Building upon the full range of the developed machinery of this section,
we will strengthen the above volume expansion result by showing that
$`a_{\min}`$ could be actually substituted by any
$`\tilde{a}=a^{\star}-\epsilon`$ for any sufficient small $`\epsilon>0`$
such that $`a^{\star}-\epsilon>a_{\min}>z_{b}`$. Our first observation
is that thanks to the uniform convergence of $`a_{n}\to a^{\star}`$ in
$`[\gamma,\delta]\subset(0,1)`$ there exists a $`n^{{\dagger}}`$ such
that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall n>n^{{\dagger}}\quad\forall x\in[\gamma,\delta]\quad a_{n}(x)>a^{\star}-\epsilon=\tilde{a}
``` |  |

This strengthens the previous volume expansion result to at least the
set $`\mathcal{F}(\tilde{a})`$ for any $`\tilde{a}`$ close to
$`a^{\star}`$.

###### Theorem 3.

For $`a_{\min}>z_{b}`$ and for any sufficient small $`\epsilon>0`$ such
that $`a^{\star}-\epsilon>a_{\min}>z_{b}`$ we have that for all
$`[\gamma,\delta]`$ such that
$`\{b\}\subset[\gamma,\delta]\subset(0,1)`$, it holds that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\quad x_{n}([\gamma,\delta])\supseteq\mathcal{F}(a^{\star}-\epsilon).
``` |  |

In a completely similar fashion we can show that the absorbing set
$`\Delta`$ can also be refined. For a given interval
$`[\gamma,\delta]\subset(0,1)`$, as the learning rates converge to
$`a^{*}`$, the trajectories will tend to be absorbed by
$`\mathcal{F}(a^{\star})`$. Thus the dynamic learning rate volume
expansion behavior matches fixed learning rate case in the long run.

## 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map

The roadmap of this section is our construction of the turbulent sets
and their connection with the symbolic dynamics in order to prove
Li-Yorke chaos in our non-autonomous dynamical system. It should be
noticed that our novel approach is a major departure from the standard
techniques of 3-period orbit arguments that have been extensively used
in the case of fixed learning rates.

We start with some useful definition for our reduction.

###### Definition 2 (\[[40](#bib.bib40)\]).

A continuous map $`f:I\to I`$ is called *turbulent* if there exist
compact subintervals $`J,K`$ with at most one common point such that
$`J\cup K\subseteq f(J)\cap J(K)`$ Additionally, the map is called
*strictly turbulent* if the subintervals can be chosen to be disjoint.
Finally, the corresponding set $`J,K`$ are called turbulent sets.

Delving into the proof of \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], it becomes clear that
the chaotic map $`f(x,a,b)`$ has a periodic orbit of period 3 that
exists within the interior of $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$. We take advantage of
this property to demonstrate that $`f^{2}(\cdot,a,b)`$ is in fact a
strictly turbulent map.

###### Lemma 12.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`u_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>u_{b}`$, there exist closed and disjoint intervals $`K_{a}`$ and
$`J_{a}`$ in the interior of $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ such that
$`f^{2}(K_{a},a,b)`$ and $`f^{2}(J_{a},a,b)`$ are neighborhoods of
$`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$.

We begin by examining the properties of a fixed learning rate MWU map
and its ability to create exponential decaying volume (length) turbulent
sets. Our analysis centers around the key observation that since
$`f^{2}(K_{a})`$ covers $`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$, then there exist
necessarily, by continuity of $`f^{2}`$, at least two distinct
subintervals, $`Z_{1},Z_{2}`$, within $`K_{a}`$ whose $`f^{2}`$-image is
precisely $`K_{a}`$ and $`J_{a}`$, respectively. By repeatedly applying
this principle, we demonstrate that the $`f^{4}`$-image of these
subintervals cover again $`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$, and through induction, we
can extend this property to higher compositions of $`f`$.

###### Lemma 13.

For every $`a>u_{b}`$, there exist closed intervals
$`V_{a}^{k}\subseteq K_{a}`$ and $`U_{a}^{k}\subseteq J_{a}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\textrm{diam}(V_{a}^{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\textrm{diam}(U_{a}^{k})=0`$ |  |

and for every $`k\geq 0`$ it holds that $`f^{2k+2}(V_{a}^{k},a,b)`$ and

The following lemma plays an essential role in our symbolic dynamic
proof of the Li-Yorke chaotic behavior. It allows us to construct a
scrambled set of initial conditions through a set of abstract symbolic
orbits.

On a technical level, we utilize a range of machinery developed in this
paper to prove this lemma. Specifically, we use
Theorem [3](#Thmtheorem3 "Theorem 3. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
to firstly describe turbulent sets $`J_{a^{\star}}\cup K_{a^{\star}}`$
that lie within the interior of $`\mathcal{F}(a^{\star})`$ and, for
small enough $`\epsilon`$, within $`\mathcal{F}(a^{\star}-\epsilon)`$,
and secondly to ensure that the non-autonomous dynamical system covers
$`J_{a^{\star}}\cup K_{a^{\star}}`$. Furthermore, to extend the
implication of
Lemma [13](#Thmlemma13 "Lemma 13. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
for the turbulent map $`f^{2}(\cdot,a^{\star},b)`$ to the non-autonomous
system $`x_{n}`$ map for the sets $`J_{a^{\star}},K_{a^{\star}}`$, we
employ the uniform convergence guarantee provided by
Lemma [10](#Thmlemma10 "Lemma 10. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
which controls the discrepancy between $`x_{n+2}(S)`$ and
$`f^{2}(x_{n}(S),a^{\star},b)`$ for any subinterval $`S`$ of
$`J_{a^{\star}},K_{a^{\star}}`$.

###### Lemma 14 (Tracking Lemma).

If $`b\in(0,1)\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}`$, there exists a $`d_{b}`$ such
that if $`a_{\min}>d_{b}`$, we can construct an increasing sequence
$`n_{i}`$ with the following properties. For every sequence of intervals
$`A_{i}`$ with $`A_{i}=V_{a^{*}}^{i}`$ or $`A_{i}=U_{a^{*}}^{i}`$, there
exists a $`x_{0}\in[0,1]`$ such that for all $`i\geq 0`$ it holds that
$`x_{n_{i}}(x_{0})\in A_{i}`$ .

It is important to note that
Lemma [14](#Thmlemma14 "Lemma 14 (Tracking Lemma). ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
ensures the ability to construct the same sequence of $`n_{i}`$ for any
distinct sequence of $`A_{i}`$. Our approach to demonstrate the Li-Yorke
chaotic behavior through symbolic dynamics is summarized in the
following high-level steps:

- •
  Assume that we can construct an uncountable set $`\mathcal{S}`$ of
  scrambled infinite length binary sequences, i.e., for every pair of
  sequences $`\sigma,\tau\in\mathcal{S}`$ there exist $`(i)`$ an
  infinite length subsequence $`(k_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}`$ where the two
  sequences differ, i.e., $`\sigma_{k_{i}}\neq\tau_{k_{i}}`$ and
  $`(ii)`$ an infinite length subsequence
  $`(\ell_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}`$ where the two sequences are equal,
  i.e., $`\sigma_{\ell_{i}}=\tau_{\ell_{i}}`$.
- •
  For each element of $`\sigma\in\mathcal{S}`$ we construct a sequence
  of sets $`\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}=(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}`$ as follows:
  If the $`k`$-th place element is $`0`$ we use $`V_{a^{*}}^{k}`$,
  whereas if it is $`1`$ we pick $`U_{a^{*}}^{k}`$. We now apply
  Lemma [14](#Thmlemma14 "Lemma 14 (Tracking Lemma). ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
  for each of the sequence of sets to get a corresponding initialization
  $`x_{0}^{\sigma}`$. We call this set of initializations $`Q`$.
- •
  Since every pair of strings $`\sigma,\tau`$ is scrambled, we know that
  we can construct two infinite subsequences
  $`(\mu_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\nu_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}`$ such that
  $`\{x_{\mu_{i}}(x_{0}^{\sigma})\}`$, $`\{x_{\mu_{i}}(x_{0}^{\tau})\}`$
  belong to the same turbulent sets and
  $`\{x_{\nu_{i}}(x_{0}^{\sigma})\}`$, $`\{x_{\nu_{i}}(x_{0}^{\tau})\}`$
  belong to the disjoint turbulent ones. Therefore, we can show that for
  every pair $`\sigma,\tau\in Q`$:
  $`(i)\liminf\|\{x_{n}(x_{0}^{\sigma})-x_{n}(x_{0}^{\tau})\}\|=0\quad(ii)\limsup\|\{x_{n}(x_{0}^{\sigma})-x_{n}(x_{0}^{\tau})\}\|>0`$

Formalizing the outlined proof sketch, our final result follows:

###### Theorem 4.

If $`b\in(0,1)\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}`$, there exists a $`d_{b}`$ such
that if $`a_{\min}>d_{b}`$, the dynamics of
Equation [2](#S2.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 2.4. Dynamic Learning Rate Model ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
are Li-Yorke chaotic.

In this work we have focused on the dynamics of
[Eq. 2](#S2.E2 "In 2.4. Dynamic Learning Rate Model ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates").
But our proof strategy can be readily generalized to any rule
$`a_{n}(\cdot)`$ as long as it uniformly converges to a sufficiently
high constant rate.

###### Corollary 2.

Let $`a_{n}(\cdot)`$ be a sequence of maps uniformly converging in
$`[0,1]`$ to $`a^{*}>d_{b}`$. Then the resulting dynamic learning rate
system is Li-Yorke chaotic.

## 6. Conclusion

We have formally analyzed and established chaotic behavior for a class
of multi-agent learning systems with a heuristically updated, variable
learning rate. At the technical crux of all prior formal analysis of
Li-Yorke chaos in games (e.g., \[[9](#bib.bib9), [17](#bib.bib17),
[27](#bib.bib27), [16](#bib.bib16), [34](#bib.bib34)\]) lied the
celebrated methodology based on period three
orbits \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], which is only applicable in autonomous,
i.e., time-invariant systems. In contrast, we had to delve deeper into
the geometry and structural properties of these dynamics, which itself
evolve with time showing that formal analysis of chaos is still
possible. This opens the possibility of extending prior results to more
realistic time-varying models.

## Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section [3](#S3 "3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

In this section we will focus on the case where the dynamical system has
a fixed learning rate.

|     |                                                                    |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{n+1}=f(x_{n},a,b)=\frac{x_{n}}{x_{n}+(1-x_{n})\exp(a(x_{n}-b))}
       ```                                                                 |     |

The derivative of $`f`$ for a fixed $`a`$ and $`b`$ is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{\prime}(x,a,b)=\frac{(ax^{2}-ax+1)\exp(a(x-b))}{(x+(1-x)\exp(a(x-b)))^{2}}
``` |  |

Critical/stationary points of $`f`$ are solutions of $`ax^{2}-ax+1=0`$.
By taking the determinant, we get than for $`0<a\leq 4`$, there is no
solution so $`f`$ is increasing and no chaos can exist, instead the
system converges to equilibrium. We will thus require $`a>4`$ to enable
chaotic behaviour in the system. Let us study the local minima and
maxima of $`f`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{\max}(a)=\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{a}}\quad x_{\min}(a)=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{a}}
``` |  |

### A.1. The order of $`\{f_{min}(a),x_{\max}(a),b,x_{\min}(a),f_{max}(a)\}`$ in the fixed learning rate regime.

The following facts establish the preliminary necessary observation to
prove structural
Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates").
More precisely, we can show the following straightforward facts:

###### Fact A.1.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`s_{b}>4`$ such that for all
$`a>s_{b}`$

|     |                            |     |
|-----|----------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{\max}(a)<b<x_{\min}(a).
       ```                         |     |

###### Proof.

We have $`x_{\max}(a)`$ is decreasing and
$`\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{\max}(a)=0`$. Symmetrically we have
$`x_{\min}(a)`$ is increasing and $`\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{\min}(a)=1`$.
The fact follows immediately. ∎

###### Fact A.2.

If $`a>4`$, then
$`f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)=f_{\min}(a)<f(x_{\max}(a),a,b)=f_{\max}(a)`$.

###### Proof.

Since the function is decreasing after the local maximum and there is no
stationary point until the local minimum, the local minimum has smaller
value. ∎

###### Fact A.3.

If $`a>s_{b}`$, then $`f_{\min}(a)<b<f_{\max}(a)`$.

###### Proof.

For $`a>s_{b}`$ we know that $`f_{\min}(a)<f(b,a,b)=b`$ since $`f`$ is
decreasing in $`[b,x_{\min}(a)]`$. Symmetrically,
$`f_{\max}(a)>f(b,a,b)=b`$ since again $`f`$ is decreasing in
$`[x_{\max}(a),b]`$. ∎

Having presented the aforementioned intuitive facts, we are ready to
prove
Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

###### Lemma A.1.

\[Restate
Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")\]
For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`a_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>a_{b}`$

|     |                                                 |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f_{min}(a)<x_{\max}(a)<b<x_{\min}(a)<f_{max}(a)
       ```                                              |     |

and $`f_{\min}(a)`$ is decreasing and $`f_{\max}(a)`$ is increasing.

###### Proof.

$`f_{min}(a)<x_{\max}(a)`$ is equivalent to

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{\min}(a)-x_{\min}(a)x_{\max}(a)-x_{\max}^{2}(a)\exp\left[\alpha(x_{\min}(a)-b)\right]<0
``` |  |

Observe that the first two terms are bounded and that for the third term
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{\max}^{2}(a)\exp\left[\alpha(x_{\min}(a)-b)\right]=\infty
``` |  |

So we can pick an $`a_{b}`$ large enough so that $`a>a_{b}`$ implies
that the inequality holds. The case of $`x_{\min}(a)<f_{max}(a)`$ is
symmetric. Moving on to the monotonicity of $`f_{\min}(a)`$, to avoid
overloading notation let us call $`x_{1}`$ the $`x`$ argument of $`f`$
and $`x_{2}`$ its $`a`$ argument

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial f_{\min}(a)}{\partial a}=\frac{\partial f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial x_{\min}(a)}{\partial a}+\frac{\partial f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)}{\partial x_{2}}
``` |  |

Observe that since $`x_{\min}(a)`$ is a local minimum, we have that

|     |                                                      |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \frac{\partial f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)}{\partial x_{1}}=0
       ```                                                   |     |

Additionally we can pick $`a_{b}`$ large enough so that $`a>a_{b}`$
implies $`x_{\min}(a)>b`$. Let us write $`f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)=\frac{x_{\min}(a)}{x_{\min}(a)+(1-x_{\min}(a))\exp(a(x_{\min}(a)-b))}.
``` |  |

Treating $`x_{\min}(a)`$ as a constant independent of $`a`$, the
exponential in the denominator is an increasing function of $`a`$ so
$`f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)`$ is decreasing with respect to $`a`$. This makes

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall a>a_{b}\quad\frac{\partial f(x_{\min}(a),a,b)}{\partial x_{2}}<0
``` |  |

As a result we have that

|       |     |                                                               |     |
|-------|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
| (A.1) |     |
               ``` math
               \forall a>a_{b}\quad\frac{\partial f_{\min}(a)}{\partial a}<0
               ```                                                            |     |

and $`f_{\min}(a)`$ is decreasing. The case of $`f_{\max}(a)`$ is
symmetric. We take the maximum of all the required $`a_{b}`$ to get the
result. ∎

### A.2. Forward invariant, Perpetual & Absorbing sets for fixed learning rate.

Having settled the order among
$`\{f_{min}(a),x_{\max}(a),b,x_{\min}(a),f_{max}(a)\}`$ for high enough
learning rates, we are ready to prove the $`(i)`$ forward invariant,
$`(ii)`$ perpetual and $`(iii)`$ absorbing property of
$`\mathcal{F}(a)=[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$.

For the sake of readability, we recall first the formal definitions of
these properties:

1.  (1)
    A forward invariant set: a set of states such that if the system
    starts in any state in the set, it will remain in the set for all
    future time.
2.  (2)
    A perpetual set: a special case of a forward invariant set, whose
    image consists itself.
3.  (3)
    An (global/local) absorbing set: a forward invariant set that also
    includes (globally/locally) all possible future states of the
    system.

###### Lemma A.2.

\[Restated
Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")\]
For every $`b\in(0,1)`$ there is a $`s_{b}`$ such that
$`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ is forward invariant for all $`a>s_{b}`$, i.e.

|     |                                                          |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x\in\mathcal{F}(a)\Rightarrow f(x,a,b)\in\mathcal{F}(a).
       ```                                                       |     |

###### Proof.

By continuity of $`f`$, we only need to consider four points to
determine the image of $`[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$: $`f_{\min}(a)`$,
$`f_{\max}(a)`$ as well as $`x_{\max}(a)`$, $`x_{\min}(a)`$. Since
$`f(x,a,b)\geq x`$ in $`(0,b)`$ and $`f_{\max}(a)`$ is the maximum in
this interval, we know that

|     |                                                    |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f_{\max}(a)\geq f(f_{\min}(a),a,b)\geq f_{\min}(a)
       ```                                                 |     |

Since $`f(x,a,b)\leq x`$ in $`(b,1)`$ and $`f_{\min}(a)`$ is the minimum
in this interval, we know that

|     |                                                    |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f_{\max}(a)\geq f(f_{\max}(a),a,b)\geq f_{\min}(a)
       ```                                                 |     |

Of course the images of the local optima $`x_{\max}(a)`$,
$`x_{\min}(a)`$ trivially belong to $`[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$. ∎

But even points that are outside of this interval are monotonically
attracted to it without overshooting. We have the following lemma that
shows this monotic attracting property

###### Lemma A.3.

Let $`a>s_{b}`$, then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle x\in(0,f_{\min}(a))`$ | $`\displaystyle\implies f(x,a,b)\in(x,f_{\max}(a)]`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle x\in(f_{\max}(a),1)`$ | $`\displaystyle\implies f(x,a,b)\in[f_{\min}(a),x).`$ |  |

###### Proof.

We will prove the first one, the second one is entirely symmetric. If
$`x<f_{\min}(a)`$ then $`x<b`$ and thus $`f(x,a,b)>x`$ and
$`f(x,a,b)\leq f_{\max}(a)`$. The first implication follows immediately.
∎

Consequently, we have the following lemma:

###### Lemma A.4 (Restated Lemma [3](#Thmlemma3 "Lemma 3. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

For $`a>a_{b}`$, $`\mathcal{F}(a)`$ is surjective, i.e.
$`f(\mathcal{F}(a),a,b)=\mathcal{F}(a).`$

###### Proof.

We already know that the right hand side is a super set of the left hand
side by
[Lemma A.2](#A1.Thmlemma2 "Lemma A.2. ‣ A.2. Forward invariant, Perpetual & Absorbing sets for fixed learning rate. ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates").
But by
[Lemma A.1](#A1.Thmlemma1 "Lemma A.1. ‣ A.1. The order of {𝑓_{𝑚⁢𝑖⁢𝑛}⁢(𝑎),𝑥_max⁢(𝑎),𝑏,𝑥_min⁢(𝑎),𝑓_{𝑚⁢𝑎⁢𝑥}⁢(𝑎)} in the fixed learning rate regime. ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we have that $`x_{\max}(a)`$ and $`x_{\min}(a)`$ belong to
$`[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$. So the left hand is a super set of the
left hand side. Thus the result follows. ∎

More generally we can prove the following absorbing condition:

###### Lemma A.5 (Restated Lemma [4](#Thmlemma4 "Lemma 4. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

Let $`a>s_{b}`$,
$`x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]\Rightarrow\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\text{ s.t }\ x_{n}\in\mathcal{F}(a)`$,
for every $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$.

###### Proof.

If $`x_{0}\in[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$ then the results follows
trivially for $`n_{0}=0`$. Let us take the case

|     |                                                    |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{0}\in\left[\gamma,f_{\min}(a)\right)=\Delta_{0}
       ```                                                 |     |

We know that in $`\Delta_{0}`$ we have that $`f(x,a,b)>x`$. We can
define a uniform bound on their difference

|     |                                                    |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \min_{x\in\Delta_{0}}\left[f(x,a,b)-x\right]=\xi>0
       ```                                                 |     |

We now know that

|     |                                             |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{1}\in\left[\gamma+\xi,f_{\max}(a)\right)
       ```                                          |     |

If $`x_{1}\in\Delta`$ then the result follows trivially for $`n_{0}=1`$.
Otherwise we have that

|     |                                                              |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{1}\in\left[\gamma+\xi,f_{\min}(a)\right)\subset\Delta_{0}
       ```                                                           |     |

Applying recursively, either there is a $`n_{0}`$ such that
$`x_{n_{0}}\in\Delta`$ and the theorem follows trivially or for all
$`n`$ we have that $`x_{n}`$ stays in a subset of $`\Delta_{0}`$

|     |                                                               |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{n}\in\left[\gamma+n\xi,f_{\min}(a)\right)\subset\Delta_{0}
       ```                                                            |     |

But this is impossible since there is a $`n_{0}>0`$ such that

|     |                                             |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \forall n\geq n_{0}:\gamma+n\xi>f_{\min}(a)
       ```                                          |     |

We are now left with the symmetric case of

|     |                                        |     |
|-----|----------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{0}\in\left(f_{\max}(a),\zeta\right]
       ```                                     |     |

which we can handle just like above. ∎

### A.3. Two period trajectories in MWU maps.

We start with a fundamental observation from Calculus of continuous
injective function

###### Claim 1.

Let $`g`$ be a continuous decreasing function on a closed interval $`I`$
and that there exists a $`x_{0}\in I`$ such that
$`\forall n:g^{n}(x_{0})\in I`$. Then either $`g^{n}(x_{0})`$ converges
to a fixed point $`x^{\star}`$ or to a 2-period trajectory.

###### Proof.

Let us take $`g^{n_{j}}(x_{0})`$ a subsequence that converges to
$`\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})`$.

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})\geq\lim_{j\to\infty}g^{n_{j}+1}(x_{0})=g(\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0}))
``` |  |

Now let us take $`g^{n_{k}}(x_{0})`$ a subsequence that converges to
$`\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})`$. Since $`g`$ is decreasing and thus
invertible in $`I`$ we know that $`g^{n_{k}-1}(x_{0})`$ also converges

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})=g(\lim_{k\to\infty}g^{n_{k}-1}(x_{0}))\leq g(\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0}))
``` |  |

The two steps clearly imply that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})=g(\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0}))
``` |  |

Symmetrically, with the same arguments we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})=g(\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0}))
``` |  |

Thus, if we denote
$`x_{1}=g(\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0}))=\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})`$
and
$`x_{2}=g(\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0}))=\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}(x_{0})`$
then either $`x_{1}=x_{2}=x^{\star}`$, which consists a fixed point for
map $`g`$ –$`g(x^{\star})=x^{\star}`$, or $`x_{1}\neq x_{2}`$ consist a
2-period trajectory $`\{x_{1}=g(x_{2}),x_{2}=g(x_{1})\}`$. ∎

Interestingly, if we restrict our attention to the interval
$`\mathcal{D}=[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$, we can show that there is no
2-period trajectory:

###### Lemma A.6.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`\ell_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>\ell_{b}`$, there is no period two trajectory for which both
endpoints belong to $`[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$.

###### Proof.

Endpoints of period two trajectories satisfy the equation
$`f^{2}(x,a,b)=x`$

|     |                                                        |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f^{2}(x,a,b)=\frac{x}{x+(1-x)\exp(a(x+f(x,a,b)-2b))}=x
       ```                                                     |     |

Ignoring $`x=0`$ and $`x=1`$, which are merely fixed points, this is
equivalent to

|     |               |     |
|-----|---------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x+f(x,a,b)=2b
       ```            |     |

After some manipulation, the formula above is equivalent to

|     |                                                     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \gamma_{a,b}(x)=(2b-x-1)x+(2b-x)(1-x)\exp(a(x-b))=0
       ```                                                  |     |

We take the first and second and third derivative of this function

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(x)=2b-2x-1+\exp(a(x-b))\left[(2x-2b-1)+a(2b-x)(1-x))\right]`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(x)=-2+\exp(a(x-b))\left[a^{2}(2b-x)(1-x)+2a(2x-2b-1)+2\right]`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)=a\exp(a(x-b))\left[a^{2}(2b-x)(1-x)+3a(2x-2b-1)+6\right]`$ |  |

Let us define the following finite quantity

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mu_{b}=\frac{\max_{x\in[0,b]}3(2b+1-2x)}{\min_{x\in[0,b]}(2b-x)(1-x)}
``` |  |

For $`a>\mu_{b}`$, we have that
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)>0`$ in $`[0,b]`$. Moving on to
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(x)`$, it is increasing in $`[0,b]`$ and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(0)=-2+\exp(-ab)\left[2ba^{2}-2a(2b-1)+2\right]\quad\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(b)=a^{2}b(1-b)-2a
``` |  |

Clearly we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(0)=-2\quad\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(b)=\infty
``` |  |

We can thus pick an $`\kappa_{b}>\mu_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>\kappa_{b}`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(0)<0\quad\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(b)>0.
``` |  |

Thus $`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(x)`$ has exactly one root in
$`[0,b]`$ given its monotonicity. Moving on to
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(x)`$, it starts of as decreasing and moves to
increasing in $`[0,b]`$ with

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)`$ | $`\displaystyle=2b-1+\exp(-ab)\left[(-2b-1)+2ab\right]`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)`$ | $`\displaystyle=-2+ab(1-b)`$ |  |

To continue our analysis we will study the following cases:
$`b<\frac{1}{2}`$, $`b=\frac{1}{2}`$, $`b>\frac{1}{2}`$.

#### Case: $`b<\frac{1}{2}`$

For the first case

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)=2b-1<0\quad\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)=\infty
``` |  |

Thus we can pick a $`\nu_{b}>\kappa_{b}`$ such that for $`a>\nu_{b}`$,
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)<0`$ and $`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)>0`$. Since
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(x)`$ starts decreasing and moves to increasing,
it has exactly one root. Moving on to $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ we have

|     |                                                     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \gamma_{a,b}(0)=2b\exp(-ab)>0\quad\gamma_{a,b}(b)=0
       ```                                                  |     |

Given that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ starts decreasing and moves to increasing
in $`[0,b]`$, it can have up to two roots in $`[0,b]`$, one of which is
$`b`$. We can observe that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{3b}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)<0.
``` |  |

We can pick a $`\xi_{b}>\nu_{b}`$ such that for all $`a>\xi_{b}`$ it
holds that $`\gamma_{a,b}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)<0`$. For $`a>\xi_{b}`$
we have that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ has exactly one root in $`[0,b)`$ that
is located in $`[0,\frac{b}{2}]`$.

#### Case: $`b=\frac{1}{2}`$

For the second case we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)=\exp\left(-\frac{a}{2}\right)(a-2)\quad\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)=b-1<0\quad\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)=\infty
``` |  |

So we can pick a $`\nu_{b}>\kappa_{b}`$ such that for $`a>\nu_{b}`$,
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)>0`$ and
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)<0`$ and
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)>0`$. Since $`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(x)`$
starts decreasing and moves to increasing, it has exactly two roots.
Moving on to $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ we have

|     |                                                     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \gamma_{a,b}(0)=2b\exp(-ab)>0\quad\gamma_{a,b}(b)=0
       ```                                                  |     |

In $`[0,b]`$, we have that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ starts increasing and
positive, then switches to decreasing and then to increasing. In the
first section it cannot have any root. In the second section it can have
at most one root and in the third section it has exactly one root $`b`$.
Just like above we can observe that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{3}{4}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)<0.
``` |  |

Following the same steps as above, we can pick a $`\xi_{b}`$ such that
for $`a>\xi_{b}`$ we have that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ has exactly one root
in $`[0,b)`$ that is located in $`[0,\frac{b}{2}]`$.

#### Case: $`b>\frac{1}{2}`$

For the last case we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)=2b-1>0\quad\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)=b-1<0\quad\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)=\infty
``` |  |

Just like before we can pick a $`\nu_{b}>\kappa_{b}`$ such that for
$`a>\nu_{b}`$, $`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)>0`$ and
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)<0`$ and
$`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(b)>0`$. Since $`\gamma_{a,b}^{\prime}(x)`$
starts decreasing and moves to increasing, it has exactly two roots.
Moving on to $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$

|     |                                                     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \gamma_{a,b}(0)=2b\exp(-ab)>0\quad\gamma_{a,b}(b)=0
       ```                                                  |     |

In $`[0,b]`$, we have that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ starts increasing and
positive, then switches to decreasing and then to increasing. In the
first section it cannot have any root. In the second section it can have
at most one root and in the third section it has exactly one root $`b`$.
We can observe that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{a,b}\left(\frac{3b-1}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{b-1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{3b-1}{2}\right)<0.
``` |  |

Following the same steps as above, we can pick a $`\xi_{b}`$ such that
for $`a>\xi_{b}`$ we have that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ has exactly one root
in $`[0,b)`$ that is located in $`[0,\frac{3b-1}{2}]`$.

#### Solution pairs

In all cases, we have identified an $`\xi_{b}`$ such that for
$`a>\xi_{b}`$ we have that $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)`$ has exactly one root in
$`[0,b)`$. If $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)=0`$, then we know that
$`\gamma_{a,b}(f(x,a,b))=0`$. Since $`x+f(x,a,b)=2b`$, we know that
$`f(x,a,b)`$ needs to be in $`(b,2b]`$. Symmetrically, any root of
$`\gamma_{a,b}(x)=0`$ with $`x>b`$ can only form a periodic trajectory
with an $`f(x,a,b)=2b-x<b`$ that is also satisfies
$`\gamma_{a,b}(f(x,a,b))=0`$. But since there is only one root in
$`[0,b)`$ and points cannot participate in multiple periodic
trajectories, we have a unique solution for $`\gamma_{a,b}(x)=0`$ in
$`(b,1)`$. Let us define $`x_{l}(a)<b<x_{r}(a)`$ the points of the
unique two-periodic trajectory as functions of $`a`$. These functions
are bounded and thus they need to have at least one limit point. We can
use the following equations

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}(x_{l}(a))=\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a,b}(x_{r}(a))=0
``` |  |

to derive the properties of these limit points. In all three cases
above, we proved that the solution $`x_{l}(a)`$ is bounded away from
$`b`$ for $`a>\xi_{b}`$. As a result all limit points must satisfy

|     |                                             |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       (2b-x-1)x=0\implies x=0\textrm{ or }x=2b-1.
       ```                                          |     |

Similarly, since $`x_{l}(a)+x_{r}(a)=2b`$ and $`x_{l}(a)`$ is bounded
away from $`b`$ , it must be the case that $`x_{r}(a)`$ is bounded away
from $`b`$. As such all limit points of $`x_{r}(a)`$ must satisfy

|     |                                           |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       (2b-x)(1-x)\implies x=2b\textrm{ or }x=1.
       ```                                        |     |

Observe that the limit points of $`x_{l}(a)`$ and $`x_{r}(a)`$ must come
in pairs that sum to $`2b`$ as $`x_{l}(a)+x_{r}(a)=2b`$. We will once
again do a case by case study. For $`b<\frac{1}{2}`$, the only viable
pair is $`(0,2b)`$ since $`2b-1<0`$. For $`b=\frac{1}{2}`$, there is
only one pair $`(0,1)`$. For the case of $`b>\frac{1}{2}`$, the only
viable pair is $`(2b-1,1)`$ since $`2b>1`$. In all of the cases, the
limit points of $`x_{r}(a)`$ and $`x_{l}(a)`$ are unique and thus
$`x_{r}(a)`$ and $`x_{l}(a)`$ converge.

#### Convergence rate

We are now ready to argue why at least one of $`x_{r}(a)`$ and
$`x_{l}(a)`$ do not belong in $`[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$ for
sufficiently large $`a`$. Once again we will do a case by case analysis
on $`b`$. For $`b<\frac{1}{2}`$, we will argue that there is a
$`\ell_{b}`$ such that for $`a>\ell_{b}`$ we have that
$`x_{l}(a)<x_{\max}(a)`$. We know that $`x_{l}(a)=f(x_{r}(a),a,b)`$.
Thus $`x_{l}(a)<x_{\max}(a)`$ is equivalent to

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{r}(a)-x_{\max}(a)x_{r}(a)-x_{\max}(a)(1-x_{r}(a))\exp\left[\alpha(x_{r}(a)-b)\right]<0
``` |  |

Observe that the first two terms are bounded but for the third term we
have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{\max}(a)(1-x_{r}(a))\exp\left[\alpha(x_{r}(a)-b)\right]=\infty
``` |  |

given that $`\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{r}(a)=2b`$ and the exponential goes to
$`\infty`$ much faster than $`x_{\max}(a)`$ goes to 0. Thus we can
choose a $`\ell_{b}`$ such that for $`a>\ell_{b}`$ we have
$`x_{l}(a)<x_{\max}(a)`$. For the case of $`b>\frac{1}{2}`$, we use the
same arguments to prove that there is an is an $`\ell_{b}`$ such that
for $`a>\ell_{b}`$ we have $`x_{r}(a)>x_{\min}(a)`$. For the case of
$`b=\frac{1}{2}`$, we will argue that there is a $`\ell_{b}`$ such that
for $`a>\ell_{b}`$ we have that $`x_{l}(a)<x_{\max}(a)`$. We need study
the convergence rate of $`x_{r}(a)`$ to $`1`$. We have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{a,\frac{1}{2}}(x_{r}(a))=0\implies x_{r}(a)=(1-x_{r}(a))\exp\left[\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(x_{r}(a)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]
``` |  |

We can apply the same argument as in the $`b<\frac{1}{2}`$ case and use
the equation above to prove that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{\max}(a)(1-x_{r}(a))\exp\left[\alpha\left(x_{r}(a)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]=\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{\max}(a)x_{r}(a)\exp\left[\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(x_{r}(a)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]=\infty
``` |  |

because $`\lim_{a\to\infty}x_{r}(a)=1`$ and the exponential goes to
$`\infty`$ much faster than $`x_{\max}(a)`$ goes to 0. The resulting
$`\ell_{b}`$ in all cases satisfy the requirements of the theorem. ∎

### A.4. Volume Expansion & Instability of mixed equilibrium $`x^{\star}=b`$

###### Lemma A.7.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`k_{b}`$ such that for $`a>k_{b}`$
it holds that there exists some neighborhood
$`\mathcal{N}_{\delta}=(b-\delta,b+\delta)`$, such that almost all
initializations from $`\mathcal{N}_{\delta}`$ do not converge to
$`x^{\star}=b`$.

###### Proof.

We can write down $`f^{\prime}(b,a,b)`$ as

|     |                               |     |
|-----|-------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f^{\prime}(b,a,b)=ab^{2}-ab+1
       ```                            |     |

Since $`\lim_{a\to\infty}f^{\prime}(b,a,b)=-\infty`$, thus there is a
$`k_{b}`$ such that for $`a>k_{b}`$ it holds that there exists some
neighborhood $`\mathcal{N}_{\delta}=(b-\delta,b+\delta)`$ where
$`|f^{\prime}(x,a,b)|>1\ \forall\ x\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}`$. Leveraging
Unstable Manifold Theorem (See \[[41](#bib.bib41)\]), the result
immediately follows. ∎

We are now ready to prove that any neighborhood of $`x^{\star}=b`$ lemma

###### Lemma A.8.

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`z_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>z_{b}`$.

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\{b\}\subset[\gamma,\delta]\subseteq[x_{max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]\implies\exists n_{0}:\quad\forall n\geq n_{0}\quad f^{n}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)=[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Let us take the following interval

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle R`$ | $`\displaystyle=f([\gamma,b],a,b)\cap[b,\delta]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=[f(b,a,b),f(\gamma,a,b)]\cap[b,\delta]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=[b,f(\gamma,a,b)]\cap[b,\delta]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=[b,\min\{f(\gamma,a,b),\delta\}]`$ |  |

Let us pick an $`x_{0}\in R`$. Let us assume that

|     |                                                  |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{\max}(a)\leq f^{n}(x_{0},a,b)\leq x_{\min}(a)
       ```                                               |     |

By
Lemma [A.7](#A1.Thmlemma7 "Lemma A.7. ‣ A.4. Volume Expansion & Instability of mixed equilibrium 𝑥^⋆=𝑏 ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
and picking $`a>k_{b}`$ we can always choose a $`x_{0}\in R`$ such that
$`f^{n}(x_{0},a,b)`$ does not converge to $`b`$. Since $`b`$ is the
unique fixed point of $`f(x,a,b)`$ in $`[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$, we
have that $`f^{n}(x_{0},a,b)`$ does not converge at all and as a result

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\liminf_{n\to\infty}f^{n}(x_{0},a,b)\neq\limsup_{n\to\infty}f^{n}(x_{0},a,b)
``` |  |

By
Claim [1](#Thmclaim1 "Claim 1. ‣ A.3. Two period trajectories in MWU maps. ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
on $`f`$, which is decreasing on $`[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$, we have
that the points above form a two-period trajectory of $`f(x,a,b)`$. By
Lemma [A.6](#A1.Thmlemma6 "Lemma A.6. ‣ A.3. Two period trajectories in MWU maps. ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
if we pick $`\ell_{b}`$ such that for $`a>\ell_{b}`$ no period two
trajectory can exist inside $`[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]`$ yielding a
contradiction. As a result

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n^{*}:\quad f^{n^{*}}(x_{0},a,b)\notin[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]
``` |  |

We will first study the case of $`f^{n^{*}}(x_{0},a,b)>x_{\min}(a)`$.
For this case, we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{n^{*}}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)\supseteq f^{n^{*}}([b,x_{0}],a,b)\supseteq[b,x_{\min}(a)].
``` |  |

In the next iteration

|     |                                                           |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f^{n^{*}+1}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)\supseteq[f_{\min}(a),b].
       ```                                                        |     |

Observe though that because $`x_{0}\in R`$, we can pick a
$`x_{1}\in[\gamma,b]`$ such that $`f(x_{1},a,b)=x_{0}`$. We then have

|     |                                                         |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f^{n^{*}+1}(x_{1},a,b)=f^{n^{*}}(x_{0},a,b)>x_{\min}(a)
       ```                                                      |     |

This yields

|     |                                                                    |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       f^{n^{*}+1}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)\supseteq[f_{\min}(a),x_{\min}(a)]
       ```                                                                 |     |

For $`a>a_{b}`$ by
Lemma [1](#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
$`f_{\min}(a)<x_{\max}(a)`$ and by
Lemma [2](#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we have that $`[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$ is forward invariant. As a
result we have the following

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]\supseteq f^{n^{*}+2}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)\supseteq[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]
``` |  |

By
Lemma [A.4](#A1.Thmlemma4 "Lemma A.4 (Restated Lemma ). ‣ A.2. Forward invariant, Perpetual & Absorbing sets for fixed learning rate. ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we know that $`[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]`$ is perpetual. So we can
choose $`n_{0}=n^{*}+2`$ to fullfil the requirements of the theorem. The
case of $`f^{n^{*}}(x_{0},a,b)<x_{\max}(a)`$ is symmetric. ∎

We are ready now to prove the main volume expansion claims of the
section.

###### Theorem A.1 (Restated Theorem [2](#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is a $`v_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a>v_{b}`$ and any interval $`[\gamma,\delta]`$ :
$`\{b\}\subset[\gamma,\delta]\subset(0,1)`$, it holds that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\quad f^{n}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)=\mathcal{F}(a).
``` |  |

###### Proof.

We can apply
Lemma [A.8](#A1.Thmlemma8 "Lemma A.8. ‣ A.4. Volume Expansion & Instability of mixed equilibrium 𝑥^⋆=𝑏 ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
on $`s=[\gamma,\delta]\cap[x_{\max}(a),x_{\min}(a)]\supset\{b\}`$. Then
there is a $`n^{*}_{1}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall n\geq n^{*}_{1}\quad f^{n}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)\supseteq f^{n}(s,a,b)=[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]
``` |  |

But by
Lemma [4](#Thmlemma4 "Lemma 4. ‣ 3. Refinements in Fixed Learning rates ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
for $`a>r_{b}`$ there is a $`n^{*}_{2}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall n\geq n^{*}_{2}\quad[f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a)]\supseteq f^{n}([\gamma,\delta],a,b)
``` |  |

Taking $`v_{b}=\max\{z_{b},r_{b}\}`$ and
$`n_{0}=\max\{n^{*}_{1},n^{*}_{2}\}`$ satisfies the theorem
requirements. ∎

## Appendix B Omitted Proofs of Section [4](#S4 "4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

### B.1. Forward & Absorbing Set in Dynamic Learning Rates.

In this first part of this section we prove the forward invariance and
absorption property of set

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Delta=\left[\displaystyle\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a),\displaystyle\max_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\max}(a)\right].
``` |  |

###### Lemma B.1 (Restated Lemma [5](#Thmlemma5 "Lemma 5 (Forward Invariance Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

For all $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$, then for every $`n`$ it holds that
$`\Delta`$ is forward invariant, i.e.

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle x_{n}(x_{0})\in\Delta\implies x_{n+1}(x_{0})\in\Delta.`$ |  |

###### Proof.

We have three cases to consider. The first one

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n}(x_{0})\in[f_{\min}(a_{n}(x_{0})),f_{\max}(a_{n}(x_{0}))]\implies x_{n+1}(x_{0})\in[f_{\min}(a_{n}(x_{0})),f_{\max}(a_{n}(x_{0}))]
``` |  |

and the result follows trivially because of the perpetual set
$`\mathcal{F}(a_{n}(x_{0}))`$. For the second case we get

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n}(x_{0})\in\left[\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a),f_{\min}(a_{n}(x_{0}))\right)\implies x_{n+1}(x_{0})\in\left[\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a),f_{\max}(a_{n}(x_{0}))\right]
``` |  |

and thus the result follows for this one as well. The last case is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n}(x_{0})\in\left(f_{\max}(a_{n}(x_{0})),\max_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\max}(a)\right]\implies x_{n+1}(x_{0})\in\left[f_{\min}(a_{n}(x_{0})),\max_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\max}(a)\right]
``` |  |

Clearly the result holds for all cases. ∎

###### Lemma B.2 (Restated Lemma [6](#Thmlemma6 "Lemma 6 (Absorption Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

For all $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ and $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$, there is
an $`n_{0}\geq 0`$ so that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall n\geq n_{0}\quad x_{n}([\gamma,\zeta])\subseteq\Delta.`$ |  |

###### Proof.

Let us define the three following sets

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle U_{0}=\left[\gamma,\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a)\right)\quad\Delta_{0}=\Delta\cap[\gamma,\zeta]\quad V_{0}=\left(\max_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\max}(a),\zeta\right]`$ |  |

It suffices to prove the theorem for each of them (if they are
non-empty) because we can pick

|     |                                                           |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       n_{0}=\max\{n_{0}(U_{0}),n_{0}(\Delta_{0}),n_{0}(V_{0})\}
       ```                                                        |     |

(excluding any empty sets) to satisfy the theorem. Based on
Lemma [5](#Thmlemma5 "Lemma 5 (Forward Invariance Property). ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
it is clear that $`n_{0}(\Delta_{0})=0`$. Moving on to the case of
$`U_{0}`$, we know that in $`U_{0}`$ we have that $`f(x,a,b)>x`$. We can
define a uniform bound on their difference

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\min_{x\in U_{0}}\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}\left[f(x,a,b)-x\right]=\xi>0
``` |  |

We now know that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{1}(U_{0})\in\left[\gamma+\xi,\max_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\max}(a)\right)
``` |  |

If $`x_{1}(U_{0})\subset\Delta`$ then the result follows trivially for
$`n_{0}=1`$. Otherwise we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{1}(U_{0})\in\left[\gamma+\xi,\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a)\right)\subset U_{0}
``` |  |

Applying recursively, either there is a $`n_{0}`$ such that
$`x_{n_{0}}(U_{0})\subset\Delta`$ and the theorem holds for $`U_{0}`$ or
for all $`n`$ we have that $`x_{n}(U_{0})`$ stays in a subset of
$`U_{0}`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n}(U_{0})\in\left[\gamma+n\xi,\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a)\right)\subset U_{0}
``` |  |

But this is impossible since there is a $`n_{0}>0`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall n\geq n_{0}:\gamma+n\xi>\min_{a\in[a_{\min},a_{\max}]}f_{\min}(a)
``` |  |

The case of $`V_{0}`$ can be handled symmetrically. ∎

### B.2. Uniform Convergence of Césaro means & Learning Rate.

In this section, we prove the uniform convergence of learning rate,
pseudo-regret and the average iteration while $`n\to\infty`$. We start
with the asymptotic behavior of the introduced notion of pseudo-regret

###### Lemma B.3 (Restated Lemma [7](#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ and $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)=0.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Observe that $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, so by
Lemma [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
there is a $`\delta>0`$ such that $`\delta<x_{n}<1-\delta`$. Thus we
have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{x_{0}}{1-\delta}<x_{0}+(1-x_{0})\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)\right)<\frac{x_{0}}{\delta}
``` |  |

so we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta^{2}<\frac{x_{0}\delta}{1-\delta}<(1-x_{0})\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)\right)<\frac{x_{0}(1-\delta)}{\delta}<\frac{1}{\delta}
``` |  |

Clearly we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta^{2}<\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)\right)<\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}
``` |  |

By talking the logarithm and diving by $`n`$ we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{n}<\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)<-\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{n}
``` |  |

By taking the limit, the result follows easily. ∎

We proceed now to the uniform convergence of the adaptively changing
learning rate

###### Lemma B.4 (Restated Lemma [8](#Thmlemma8 "Lemma 8. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$. The sequence of functions $`a_{n}(\cdot)`$ is
converging uniformly to the constant function $`a^{\star}(\cdot)=g(0)`$
in every interval $`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$.

###### Proof.

By
Lemma [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
$`x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]`$ there is a $`\delta>0`$ such
$`\delta<x_{n}<1-\delta`$. Following the steps of
Lemma [7](#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{n}<\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)<-\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{n}
``` |  |

Clearly this implies that for any $`n>0`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
a_{n}(x_{0})=g\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)\right)\in g\left(\left[\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{n},-\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{n}\right]\right)
``` |  |

Since $`g`$ is continuous, for every $`\epsilon>0`$ there is a $`n_{0}`$
such that $`\forall n\geq n_{0}`$ we have that

|     |                                             |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \lvert a_{n}(x_{0})-g(0)\rvert\leq\epsilon.
       ```                                          |     |

Uniform convergence follows immediately. ∎

Next, we can prove the following uniform convergence result for the
Césaro mean of the iterations of our non-autonomous dynamical system

###### Lemma B.5 (Restated Lemma [9](#Thmlemma9 "Lemma 9. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$ and $`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, then

|     |                                                             |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}(x_{0})=b.
       ```                                                          |     |

###### Proof.

Let us define $`\rho_{n}=a_{n}(x_{0})-a^{*}`$. Obviously
$`\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{n}=0`$. Then applying
Lemma [7](#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(x_{0})(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{a^{*}}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)+\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)\right]
``` |  |

It is easy to prove the following statement:

###### Fact B.1.

Let $`\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}\to\gamma`$. Then
$`\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\gamma_{i}}{n+1}=\gamma`$.

Clearly we have that $`\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}(x_{n}(x_{0})-b)=0`$
since $`x_{n}(x_{0})`$ is bounded. And thus we obviously have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{a^{*}}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(x_{i}(x_{0})-b)\right]
``` |  |

Given that $`a^{*}`$ is positive, the theorem follows. ∎

### B.3. Uniform Convergence of varying step-size MWU map for uniform convergent update step rule.

###### Lemma B.6 (Restated Lemma [10](#Thmlemma10 "Lemma 10. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

Let $`a_{\min}>r_{b}`$. For every $`k`$, $`\epsilon>0`$ and
$`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\forall n\geq n_{0}\quad\max_{x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]}\lvert x_{n+k}(x_{0})-f^{k}(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{*},b)\rvert\leq\epsilon.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

For the sake of readability, we will present the case $`k=2`$ and with a
completely similar fashion we can prove the result for any iterate
$`k`$.

###### Lemma B.7.

Let $`a_{\min}>s_{b}`$. For every $`\epsilon>0`$ and
$`[\gamma,\zeta]\subset(0,1)`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\forall n\geq n_{0}\quad\max_{x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]}\lvert x_{n+2}(x_{0})-f^{2}(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{*},b)\rvert\leq\epsilon.
``` |  |

###### Proof.

By
Corollary [1](#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
there is a $`\delta>0`$ such that for any $`x_{0}\in[\gamma,\zeta]`$, we
have that $`x_{n}(x_{0})\in(\delta,1-\delta)`$. Let us define the
following function that takes an $`x`$ and applies $`f`$ with learning
rates $`a_{1}`$ and $`a_{2}`$

|     |                                            |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       q(x,a_{1},a_{2},b)=f(f(x,a_{1},b),a_{2},b)
       ```                                         |     |

With this definition in mind we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
D(x_{0})=x_{n+2}(x_{0})-f^{2}(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{*},b)=q(x_{n}(x_{0}),a_{n}(x_{0}),a_{n+1}(x_{0}),b)-q(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{*},a^{*},b)
``` |  |

By the mean value theorem we have that there exists an $`c\in(0,1)`$
such that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2})`$ | $`\displaystyle=c(a_{n}(x_{0}),a_{n+1}(x_{0}))+(1-c)(a^{*},a^{*})`$ |  |

and also

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle D(x_{0})=\frac{\partial q(x_{n}(x_{0}),a_{1},a_{2},b)}{\partial a_{1}}(a_{n}(x_{0})-a^{*})+\frac{\partial q(x_{n}(x_{0}),a_{1},a_{2},b)}{\partial a_{2}}(a_{n+1}(x_{0})-a^{*})`$ |  |

The following value is finite

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\phi=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in(\delta,1-\delta),\\
\ a_{1},a_{2}\in(a_{\min},a_{\max})\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial q(x,a_{1},a_{2},b)}{\partial a_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial q(x,a_{1},a_{2},b)}{\partial a_{2}}\right)^{2}}.`$ |  |

Then we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\lvert x_{n+2}(x_{0})-f^{2}(x_{n}(x_{0}),a^{*},b)\rvert\leq\phi\sqrt{(a_{n}(x_{0})-a^{*})^{2}+(a_{n+1}(x_{0})-a^{*})^{2}}
``` |  |

By the uniform convergence of $`a_{n}(x_{0})`$ and $`a_{n+1}(x_{0})`$ to
$`a^{*}`$, the result follows immediately. ∎

∎

###### Lemma B.8 (Restated Lemma [11](#Thmlemma11 "Lemma 11. ‣ 4. Chaos in Uniformly Convergent Non-autonomous Dynamical Systems ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

For every $`b\in(0,1)`$, there is an $`z_{b}`$ such that for all
$`a_{\min}>z_{b}`$ it holds that for every $`[\gamma,\delta]`$ such that
$`\{b\}\subset[\gamma,\delta]\subseteq\mathcal{D}(a_{\min})=[x_{\max}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})]`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\exists n_{0}:\ \forall n\geq n_{0}\ \ x_{n}([\gamma,\delta])\supseteq\mathcal{F}(a_{\min}).
``` |  |

###### Proof.

Our first observation is that for two $`a_{1}>a_{2}>0`$

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| (B.1) |  | $`\displaystyle 1>x>b`$ | $`\displaystyle\implies f(x,a_{1},b)<f(x,a_{2},b)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle 0<x<b`$ | $`\displaystyle\implies f(x,a_{1},b)>f(x,a_{2},b)`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle x=b`$ | $`\displaystyle\implies f(x,a_{1},b)=f(x,a_{2},b)=b`$ |  |

Let us define the following subset of $`[\gamma,\delta]`$

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
[\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}]=[\gamma,\min\{f(\gamma,a_{\min},b),\delta\}]
``` |  |

Using the same arguments as in
[Lemma A.8](#A1.Thmlemma8 "Lemma A.8. ‣ A.4. Volume Expansion & Instability of mixed equilibrium 𝑥^⋆=𝑏 ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we know that there is a $`x^{\prime}\in(b,\delta^{\prime}]`$ and a
minimal $`n^{*}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b)\not\in[x_{\max}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})]
``` |  |

We are going to assume
$`f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b)>x_{\min}(a_{\min})`$. The case of
$`f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b)<x_{\max}(a_{\min})`$ is entirely
symmetric. Since $`n^{*}`$ is minimal we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall 0\leq n<n^{*}\quad f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b)\in[x_{\max}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})]
``` |  |

As a result we have that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall 0\leq 2n<n^{*}\quad f^{2n}([b,x^{\prime}],a_{\min},b)=`$ | $`\displaystyle[b,f^{2n}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b)]`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\forall 0<2n+1<n^{*}\quad f^{2n+1}([b,x^{\prime}],a_{\min},b)=`$ | $`\displaystyle[f^{2n+1}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b),b]`$ |  |

Using the above equations as well as
[Equation B.1](#A2.E1 "In Proof. ‣ B.3. Uniform Convergence of varying step-size MWU map for uniform convergent update step rule. ‣ Appendix B Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we can recursively prove that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall 0\leq n<n^{*}\quad x_{n}([b,x^{\prime}])\supseteq f^{n}([b,x^{\prime}],a_{\min},b)
``` |  |

We can deduce that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{*}}([b,x^{\prime}])\supseteq[b,f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},a_{\min},b)]\supseteq[b,x_{\min}(a_{\min})]
``` |  |

With one more iteration we have

|     |                                                            |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       x_{n^{*}+1}([b,x^{\prime}])\supseteq[f_{\min}(a_{\min}),b]
       ```                                                         |     |

We can also pick a $`x^{\prime\prime}\in[\gamma^{\prime},b]`$ such that
$`f(x^{\prime\prime},a_{\min},b)=x^{\prime}`$. By construction, we know
that $`n^{*}+1`$ is the first iteration such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{n^{*}+1}(x^{\prime\prime},a_{\min},b)\not\in[x_{\max}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})].
``` |  |

By similar arguments as above we can prove that

|  |  |  |

As a result we now have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{*}+1}([x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}])\supseteq[f_{\min}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})]\supseteq[x_{\max}(a_{\min}),x_{\min}(a_{\min})]
``` |  |

It follows directly that

``` |  |

so $`n_{0}=n^{*}+2`$ satisfies the requirements of the theorem. ∎

###### Proof.

$`a_{\min}`$ could be substitute by any $`\tilde{a}=a^{*}-\epsilon`$ for
any sufficient small $`\epsilon>0`$ such that
$`a^{*}-\epsilon>a_{\min}>z_{b}`$. Our first observation is that thanks
to the uniform convergence of $`a_{n}\to a^{*}`$ in
$`[\gamma,\delta]\subset[0,1]`$ there exists a $`n^{{\dagger}}`$ such
that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall n\geq n^{{\dagger}}\quad a_{n}([\gamma,\delta])\subseteq(a^{*}-\epsilon,a^{*}+\epsilon)
``` |  |

which implies that there exists a $`n^{{\dagger}}`$ such that

|  |  |  |  |

Again notice that for any $`a_{n}(x)>\tilde{a}>0`$

Let us define the following intervals

|  |  |  |  |

|  | $`\displaystyle[\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}]`$ | $`\displaystyle=[r,\min\{f(r,\tilde{a},b),q\}]`$ |  |

Using the same arguments as in
[Lemma A.8](#A1.Thmlemma8 "Lemma A.8. ‣ A.4. Volume Expansion & Instability of mixed equilibrium 𝑥^⋆=𝑏 ‣ Appendix A Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
we know that there is a $`x^{\prime}\in(b,\delta^{\prime}]`$ and a
minimal $`n^{*}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},\tilde{a},b)\not\in[x_{\max}(\tilde{a}),x_{\min}(\tilde{a})]
``` |  |

We are going to assume
$`f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},\tilde{a},b)>x_{\min}(\tilde{a})`$. The case of

``` |  |

As a result we have that

|  |  |  |  |

Using the above equations as well as
[Equations B.2](#A2.E2 "In Proof. ‣ B.3. Uniform Convergence of varying step-size MWU map for uniform convergent update step rule. ‣ Appendix B Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

we can recursively prove that

\forall 0\leq n<n^{*}\quad x_{n+n^{{\dagger}}}([b,x^{\prime}])\supseteq f^{n}([b,x^{\prime}],\tilde{a},b)
``` |  |

We can deduce that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}}([b,x^{\prime}])\supseteq[b,f^{n^{*}}(x^{\prime},\tilde{a},b)]\supseteq[b,x_{\min}(\tilde{a})]
``` |  |

With one more iteration we have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+1}([b,x^{\prime}])\supseteq[f_{\min}(\tilde{a}),b]
``` |  |

We can also pick a $`x^{\prime\prime}\in[\gamma^{\prime},b]`$ such that
$`f(x^{\prime\prime},\tilde{a},b)=x^{\prime}`$. By construction, we know
that $`n^{*}+1`$ is the first iteration such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
f^{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+1}(x^{\prime\prime},\tilde{a},b)\not\in[x_{\max}(\tilde{a}),x_{\min}(\tilde{a})].
``` |  |

By similar arguments as above we can prove that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+1}([x^{\prime\prime},b])\supseteq[b,f^{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+1}(x^{\prime\prime},\tilde{a},b)]=[b,f^{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}}(x^{\prime},\tilde{a},b)]\supseteq[b,x_{\min}(\tilde{a})]
``` |  |

As a result we now have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+1}([x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}])\supseteq[f_{\min}(\tilde{a}),x_{\min}(\tilde{a})]\supseteq[x_{\max}(\tilde{a}),x_{\min}(\tilde{a})]
``` |  |

It follows directly that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x_{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+2}([\gamma,\delta])\supseteq x_{n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+2}([x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}])\supseteq[f_{\min}(\tilde{a}),f_{\max}(\tilde{a})]\supseteq[x_{\max}(\tilde{a}),x_{\min}(\tilde{a})]
``` |  |

Applying the above step recursively we get

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall n\geq n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+2\quad x_{n}([\gamma,\delta])\supseteq[f_{\min}(\tilde{a}),f_{\max}(\tilde{a})]
``` |  |

so $`n_{0}=n^{{\dagger}}+n^{*}+2`$ satisfies the requirements of the
theorem. ∎

### C.1. Decaying Volume Turbulent sets in Fixed Learning Rate Regime

1.  (1)
    $`d`$ between $`x`$ and $`f(x,a,b)`$, so that $`f(d,a,b)=x`$ and
    hence $`d<f^{2}(d,a,b)`$.
2.  (2)
    $`z`$ between $`f(x)`$ and $`x`$, so close that $`f^{2}(z,a,b)>d`$.
3.  (3)
    $`q`$ between $`z`$ and $`x`$, so close to $`x`$ that

4.  (4)

Then, $`J_{a}=[z,q]`$ and $`K_{a}=[c,d]`$ are disjoint and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |

Moreover we can prove the following claim for the sets $`K_{a},J_{a}`$

###### Lemma C.2 (Restated Lemma [13](#Thmlemma13 "Lemma 13. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

For every $`a>s_{b}`$, there exist closed intervals
$`V_{a}^{k}\subseteq K_{a}`$ and $`U_{a}^{k}\subseteq J_{a}`$ such that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\textrm{diam}(V_{a}^{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\textrm{diam}(U_{a}^{k})=0`$ |  |

and for every $`k\geq 0`$ it holds that $`f^{2k+2}(V_{a}^{k},a,b)`$ and
$`f^{2k+2}(U_{a}^{k},a,b)`$ are neighborhoods of $`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$.

###### Proof.

We will prove the lemma by induction. Choosing $`V_{a}^{0}=K_{a}`$ we
know by

that $`f^{2}(V_{a}^{0},a,b)`$ is a neighborhood of $`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$.
Now let us assume that we have a $`V_{a}^{k}\subseteq K_{a}`$ such that
$`f^{2k+2}(V_{a}^{k},a,b)`$ is a neighborhood of $`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$.
Since $`K_{a}`$ and $`J_{a}`$ are disjoint closed intervals , there are
two disjoint closed intervals $`Z_{1}`$ and $`Z_{2}`$ of $`V_{a}^{k}`$
such that $`f^{2k+2}(Z_{1},a,b)`$ is a neighborhood of $`K_{a}`$ and

$`f^{2k+4}(Z_{1},a,b)`$ and $`f^{2k+4}(Z_{2},a,b)`$ are neighborhoods of
$`K_{a}\cup J_{a}`$. Given that $`Z_{1}`$ and $`Z_{2}`$ are disjoint
intervals of $`V_{a}^{k}`$ it must be the case that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math

|  |  |  |

Choosing $`U_{a}^{0}=J_{a}`$ we can follow the same arguments for the

### C.2. Tracking properties, make chaos explicit via symbolic dynamics

In this section, we will demonstrate how to construct a scrambled set of
initial conditions via a scrambled set of abstract symbolic orbits.
Symbolic dynamics is a mathematical method used in dynamical systems
theory to study the long-term behavior of a system. It involves
representing the states of a system as a sequence of symbols, typically
taken from a finite alphabet –in our case $`\{0,1\}^{*}`$ –. In our
proof, these symbols are chosen based on position of the trajectory of a
point in strategy space. The resulting sequence of symbols is called a
symbolic orbit, and the study of these orbits will provide the necessary
insight to establish the long-term chaotic behavior of MWU.

Below we present the tracking lemma that translates a binary sequence to
the trajectory of an initial condition inside the decaying sequence of
turbulent sets $`V_{a^{*}}^{i},U_{a^{*}}^{i}`$:

###### Lemma C.3 (Restated Lemma [14](#Thmlemma14 "Lemma 14 (Tracking Lemma). ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

If $`b\in(0,1)\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}`$, there exists a $`d_{b}`$ such
that if $`a_{\min}>d_{b}`$, we can construct an increasing sequence
$`n_{i}`$ with the following properties. For every sequence of intervals
$`A_{i}`$ with $`A_{i}=V_{a^{*}}^{i}`$ or $`A_{i}=U_{a^{*}}^{i}`$, there
exists a $`x_{0}\in[0,1]`$ such that for all $`i\geq 0`$ it holds that
$`x_{n_{i}}(x_{0})\in A_{i}`$ .

###### Proof.

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math

Lemma [12](#Thmlemma12 "Lemma 12. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

$`[f_{\min}(a^{*}),f_{\max}(a^{*})]`$. As a result there is a

properties hold

``` math

``` math

Theorem [B.1](#A2.Thmtheorem1 "Theorem B.1 (Restated Theorem ). ‣ B.3. Uniform Convergence of varying step-size MWU map for uniform convergent update step rule. ‣ Appendix B Omitted Proofs of Section ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")

###### Theorem C.2 (Restated Theorem [4](#Thmtheorem4 "Theorem 4. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")).

If $`b\in(0,1)\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}`$, there exists a $`d_{b}`$ such
that if $`a_{\min}>d_{b}`$, the dynamics of
Equation [2](#S2.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 2.4. Dynamic Learning Rate Model ‣ 2. Problem setup and preliminaries ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
are Li-Yorke chaotic.

###### Proof.

We first prove that there is an uncountable set $`S`$ of infinite length
binary sequences with the following property: For every pair of
sequences $`i,j\in S`$ there is an infinite length subsequence where the
two sequences differ, i.e., one of $`i,j`$ is $`0`$ and the other is
$`1`$.

We first define the equivalence relation $`R`$ over infinite length
binary sequence such that two binary sequences are equivalent if and
only if they differ in finitely many places. The relation is clearly
reflexive, a sequence differs with itself in $`0`$ places, it is by
definition symmetric and it is transitive, if a sequence $`i`$ differs
in $`k`$ places with sequence $`j`$ and $`j`$ differs in $`m`$ places
with a sequence $`t`$ then $`i`$ and $`t`$ differ in up to $`m+k`$
places which is also finite. Thus we can partition all binary sequences
in equivalence classes where all pairs of all elements in the same class
differ in finite places.

We now prove that each equivalence class has countably infinite binary
sequences. Let us pick an element $`\sigma`$ of the equivalence class.
For each member of the equivalence class $`w`$ we can construct a finite
subset of $`\mathbb{N}`$ by picking the indices where
$`s_{t}\neq w_{t}`$. Inversely, for each finite subset of $`\mathbb{N}`$
we can construct a member of the equivalence by flipping the
corresponding indices of $`\sigma`$. Thus there is a bijection between
the members of the equivalence class and the finite subsets of
$`\mathbb{N}`$. Because the finite subsets of $`\mathbb{N}`$ are
countably infinite, so is each equivalence class.

This allows us to prove that the relation $`R`$ has uncountably infinite
number of equivalence classes. We proceed by contradiction. If we had
countably finite equivalence classes each having countably many elements
then all of the binary sequences would be countable which is false.

To construct $`S`$ we only need to pick one element from each
equivalence class. Since each pair belongs in a different equivalent
class then they differ in infinite number of places which forms an
infinite length subsequence. The set $`S`$ is uncountable because there
are uncountably many equivalence classes.

We now prove that there is an uncountable set $`S^{\prime}`$ of infinite
length binary sequences that has the following two properties: First,
for every pair of sequences $`i,j\in S^{\prime}`$ there is an infinite
length subsequence where the two sequences differ, i.e., one of $`i,j`$
is $`0`$ and the other is $`1`$. Second, for every pair of sequences
$`i,j\in S^{\prime}`$ there is an infinite length subsequence where the
two sequences are equal, i.e., they are either both $`0`$ or $`1`$.

The construction works as follows: For each element of $`i\in S`$ we
construct a new sequence. In its even places the sequence is $`0`$ and
in its odd places we use the elements of $`i`$. Clearly by construction
$`S^{\prime}`$ remains uncountable and satisfies the first property as
we can pick the subsequences from the odd places. The second property
also holds because all sequences have the same elements in the even
places.

For each element of $`S^{\prime}`$ we construct a sequence of sets $`T`$
as follows: If the $`k`$th place element is $`0`$ we use
$`V_{a^{*}}^{k}`$, whereas if it is $`1`$ we pick $`U_{a^{*}}^{k}`$. We
now apply
Lemma [14](#Thmlemma14 "Lemma 14 (Tracking Lemma). ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
for each of the sequence of sets to get a corresponding initialization
$`x_{0}`$ and a subsequence of iteration indices $`n_{i}`$. We call this
set of initializations $`Q`$. By the construction in
Lemma [14](#Thmlemma14 "Lemma 14 (Tracking Lemma). ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates")
all initializations use the same subsequence $`n_{i}`$.

Let us pick two initializations $`\mu,\nu\in Q`$. By the construction of
$`T`$ and $`S^{\prime}`$ we have the following: There is an infinite
subsequence of $`n_{i}`$, which we call $`h_{t}`$, where
$`x_{n_{h_{t}}}(\mu)\in V_{a^{*}}^{h_{t}}`$ and
$`x_{n_{h_{t}}}(\nu)\in U_{a^{*}}^{h_{t}}`$ or vice versa. Because
$`V_{a^{*}}^{h_{t}}`$ and $`U_{a^{*}}^{h_{t}}`$ are disjoint we have
that there is an infinite subsequence where the trajectories of $`\mu`$
and $`\nu`$ are bounded away from each other. In other words, we have
that

|     |                                               |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \limsup_{n\to\infty}|x_{n}(\mu)-x_{n}(\nu)|>0
       ```                                            |     |

Again by construction of $`T`$ and $`S^{\prime}`$ we also have that
there is an infinite subsequence of $`n_{i}`$, which we call $`f_{t}`$
such that either $`x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\mu)\in V_{a^{*}}^{f_{t}}`$ and
$`x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\nu)\in V_{a^{*}}^{f_{t}}`$ or
$`x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\mu)\in U_{a^{*}}^{f_{t}}`$ and
$`x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\nu)\in U_{a^{*}}^{f_{t}}`$. Thus we have that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall t\geq 0:|x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\mu)-x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\nu)|\leq\max\{\textrm{diam}(U_{a^{*}}^{f_{t}}),\textrm{diam}(V_{a^{*}}^{f_{t}})\}
``` |  |

By
Lemma [13](#Thmlemma13 "Lemma 13. ‣ 5. Turbulent Sets and Chaos in MWU map ‣ Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learningdespite adaptive learning rates"),
we have that the right hand side converges to $`0`$. As a result

|     |                                                            |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \lim_{t\to\infty}|x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\mu)-x_{n_{f_{t}}}(\nu)|=0
       ```                                                         |     |

which directly implies that

|     |                                                |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \liminf_{n\to\infty}|x_{n}(\nu)-x_{n}(\nu)|=0.
       ```                                             |     |

Technically our dynamics are expressed in terms of two state variables
$`x_{n}`$ and $`r_{n}`$ so distances in orbits need to account for both
dimensions. Because $`r_{n}\to 0`$ for all initializations
$`x_{0}\in(0,1)`$, in all cases above we have
$`\lim_{n\to\infty}|r_{n}(\mu)-r_{n}(\nu)|=0`$ so the distances in the
limit are not affected by the $`r_{n}`$ dimension.

Picking $`\{(x,r):x\in Q\text{ and }r=0\}`$ as our scrambled set, which
is equivalent to choosing $`a_{0}=g(0)`$ for all $`x\in Q`$, we show
that our dynamic learning rate dynamics are Li-Yorke chaotic.
````
