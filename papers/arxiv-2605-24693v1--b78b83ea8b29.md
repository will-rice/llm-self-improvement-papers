###### Abstract.

Large language models still struggle with contest-level programming,
while many agentic remedies rely on massive inference-time sampling or
expensive multi-stage post-training. We study when execution feedback
reliably helps an LLM CP solver and which mechanisms govern the gains.
We model feedback-driven solving as a calibrated stopped process and
identify three quantities: false-admission risk, program-level evidence
against bad programs, and the active-state success hazard. Under
held-out trace calibration and selection from a pre-declared finite
controller manifest, the resulting structural certificate lower-bounds
the clean success probability before false admission. We instantiate
mechanisms targeting these quantities as Dual-Granularity Verification,
Test Augmentation, and Experience-Driven Self-Evolving, yielding
CP-Agent. Without updating any parameters, CP-Agent raises Pass@1 from
25.8% to 48.5% on LiveCodeBench Pro and improves Refine@5 by 11.0% on
ICPC-Eval. Across three LLM backbones, CP-Agent lies on the
cost–accuracy efficiency frontier, and ablations show that each
component primarily affects its corresponding certificate quantity.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) now perform well across a broad range of
downstream tasks, including code generation and everyday
software-engineering assistance. Contest-level competitive programming
(CP), however, remains a sharp failure case: on ICPC/IOI-style
benchmarks ([Jain et al., 2025](#bib.bib23); [Xu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib2); [Zheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib3)), even strong LLMs
reach below 30% Pass@1
(Section [4](#S4 "4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
CP is judged by hidden correctness and asymptotic feasibility under
strict time and memory limits, so plausible implementations earn no
credit when the algorithm or an edge case is wrong, and
software-engineering virtues such as maintainability or modular design
do not compensate.

Recent work has explored two directions: (i) tackling competitive
programming problems through massive code generation combined with
heuristic-based inference and execution-based pruning, exemplified by
AlphaCode ([Li et al., 2022](#bib.bib25)), AlphaCode2 ([Leblond et al.,
2023](#bib.bib39)), and o1-ioi ([El-Kishky et al., 2025](#bib.bib12));
(ii) enhancing the intrinsic reasoning ability of LLMs, including large
reasoning models ([Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib16); [El-Kishky et al.,
2025](#bib.bib12); [OpenAI, 2025b](#bib.bib38); [Liu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib26)) that are often trained with reinforcement learning.
While these directions improve performance, they often come with
substantial computational cost-large-scale sampling during inference, or
expensive rollouts during training.

CP offers rich, executable feedback: compilation errors, test failures,
and counterexamples directly expose concrete failure modes ([Shinn et
al., 2023](#bib.bib30)). This makes agentic solving a natural fit. Two
gaps, however, remain for agentic CP. First, the literature disagrees on
which mechanisms reliably help. MapCoder ([Islam et al.,
2024](#bib.bib21)) reports that additional test-case generation is
unhelpful while AlphaCodium ([Ridnik et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)) reports
gains; MapCoder and Oi-Assistant ([Yuhua et al., 2025](#bib.bib37)) find
retrieval of similar problems useful, while AlgoSimBench ([Li and
Mooney, 2025](#bib.bib24)) finds it unstable. Second, recent strong
agentic CP systems increasingly invest in multi-stage agentic
post-training ([Team et al., 2026](#bib.bib32)). In contrast, for
training-free LLM agents on CP, it remains unclear whether tool
orchestration alone improves performance and which mechanisms drive its
gains.

These gaps converge to one essential question: which controllable
mechanisms turn execution feedback into measurable gains on
contest-level CP? We answer it through a calibrated risk-control view of
feedback-driven solving. We analyze feedback-driven CP solving as a
calibrated, risk-controlled stopped process. Its state reduces to three
certificate quantities: the residual bad-candidate envelope
$`\rho_{t}`$, the accumulated program-level evidence $`I_{t}`$, and the
active-state success hazard $`\underline{h}_{t}`$. Each is moved by
exactly one abstract action class: RiskProbe on $`\rho_{t}`$,
EvidenceAcquire on $`I_{t}`$, and ContextAcquire on
$`\underline{h}_{t}`$. The calibrated false-admission UCB
$`\bar{q}_{t}\approx\bar{\rho}_{t}\exp(-\mathcal{I}_{t})`$ follows as a
derived quantity. This one-to-one reduction yields a unified
design-and-selection framework: for any pre-declared finite manifest of
controllers, it supplies a post-selection-valid lower bound on
clean-before-false success. Guided by this framework, we design CP-Agent
with one controller per action class: Dual-Granularity Verification
contracts $`\rho_{t}`$, Test Augmentation adds to $`I_{t}`$, and
Experience-Driven Self-Evolving raises $`\underline{h}_{t}`$. A frozen
admission gate and a refinement loop complete the agent. Extensive
empirical results across three backbones on two contest-level
benchmarks, together with theory-grounded mechanism ablations on the
five-controller calibration class, jointly validate CP-Agent’s
effectiveness and its calibrated certificate.

Our contributions are threefold:

- •
  A calibrated risk-control theory for feedback-driven CP. We prove an
  admission-risk theorem and a simultaneously valid finite-manifest
  selection rule over five abstract action classes
  (Section [2](#S2 "2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
- •
  A theory-induced agent. We instantiate this controller as CP-Agent, a
  tool-using framework with no parameter updates
  (Section [3](#S3 "3. Method ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).¹¹
  1 Our implementation is open-sourced at

## 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control

A feedback-driven CP agent repeatedly faces the same decision: admit the
current candidate, refine it, or acquire more information. We specify
certificate quantities that audit this decision, abstract action classes
that act on them, and a calibration rule that turns them into a frozen
controller; the takeaway box summarizes the role of the analysis and
Appendix [B](#A2 "Appendix B Notation Glossary ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
consolidates the
Section [2](#S2 "2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
notation.

###### Definition 2.1 (Controlled stopped process and certificate state).

Fix a horizon $`T`$, false-admission budget $`\alpha`$, and a frozen
policy $`\pi`$. Throughout, “step $`t`$” denotes a _refinement step_—one
response–revision attempt (one Refine advance, equivalently one
outer-loop iteration of
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix A Tool-Orchestrated Pseudocode ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")),
matched to Refine@$`K`$ and Pass@$`1`$. Within-trajectory interaction
events such as individual LLM outputs or tool calls are termed
_interaction steps_ and appear only in diagnostic figures (e.g.,
Figure [3(a)](#acmlabel3 "Figure 3(a) ‣ Figure 3 ‣ Test Augmentation. ‣ 4.6. Tool-Use Analysis ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
Before refinement step $`t`$, the trajectory is active when
$`A_{t}=\{\tau_{S}>t-1,\tau_{F}>t-1\}`$. On active trajectories, the
implementation state is $`X_{t}`$ (problem text, candidate code,
feedback, retrieved memory, and tool outputs). The certificate exposes a
lower-dimensional summary state

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       Z_{t}:=\phi(X_{t})=(\rho_{t},I_{t},\underline{h}_{t},t),
       ```                                                       |     |

where $`\rho_{t}`$ is an upper envelope on residual bad-candidate mass,
$`I_{t}`$ is accumulated program-level evidence, and
$`\underline{h}_{t}`$ is a lower envelope on clean-success hazard. The
residual admission-risk score is

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (1) |     |

             ``` math
             r_{t}(Z_{t}):=\rho_{t}\exp(-I_{t}).
             ```                                  |     |

Here $`\rho_{t}`$ upper-bounds the bad-candidate probability after
upstream screening and $`\exp(-I_{t})`$ upper-bounds the probability
that such a candidate survives the evidence gate, so their product
upper-bounds false admission; $`I_{t}`$ is in nats so evidence
accumulates additively as $`I_{t}=\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta I_{s}`$. Let
$`\Gamma_{t}\in\{0,1\}`$ denote the controller’s observable admission
gate at step $`t`$, the binary admit/reject signal logged on every
active trace. If the controller admits a candidate through gate
$`\Gamma_{t}`$, hidden adjudication defines

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
F_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{A_{t},\Gamma_{t}=1,B_{t}^{\epsilon}\},\qquad S_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{A_{t},\Gamma_{t}=1,\neg B_{t}^{\epsilon}\},
``` |  |

where $`B_{t}^{\epsilon}`$ denotes the event that the admitted candidate
fails the deterministic hidden-judge on at least one hidden test, which
we treat as the $`\epsilon`$-significant error event under the
benchmark’s hidden-test distribution. The stopping times are
$`\tau_{F}:=\inf\{t:F_{t}=1\}`$ and $`\tau_{S}:=\inf\{t:S_{t}=1\}`$; the
certified event is $`\{\tau_{S}\leq T,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}`$.

###### Definition 2.2 (Abstract action classes).

We choose action classes to span the certificate: each certificate
quantity in $`Z_{t}`$ admits one action that can move it in the
direction of a larger lower bound—RiskProbe on $`\rho_{t}`$,
EvidenceAcquire on $`I_{t}`$, and ContextAcquire on
$`\underline{h}_{t}`$—together with Stop and Refine for the
commit/advance decision. The controller chooses actions from

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| (2) |  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(Z_{t})=\{`$ | $`\displaystyle\textsf{Stop},\textsf{Refine},\textsf{RiskProbe},`$ |  |
|  |  |  | $`\displaystyle\textsf{EvidenceAcquire}(m),\textsf{ContextAcquire}(k)\}.`$ |  |

Each action acts on the certificate as follows:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| (3) |  | $`\displaystyle\textsf{Stop}:\ `$ | $`\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}\},`$ |  |
| (4) |  | $`\displaystyle\textsf{RiskProbe}:\ `$ | $`\displaystyle\rho_{t+}\leq\lambda_{t}(Z_{t})\,\rho_{t},\quad\lambda_{t}(Z_{t})\in[0,1],`$ |  |
| (5) |  | $`\displaystyle\textsf{EvidenceAcquire}(m):\ `$ | $`\displaystyle I_{t+}=I_{t}+\Delta I_{t}(m,Z_{t}),`$ |  |
| (6) |  | $`\displaystyle\textsf{ContextAcquire}(k):\ `$ | $`\displaystyle\underline{h}_{t+}\geq\underline{h}_{t}+\Delta h_{t}(k,Z_{t}),`$ |  |

where $`\rho_{t+},I_{t+},\underline{h}_{t+}`$ denote the post-action
certificate components, and Refine advances to the next candidate
distribution without admitting the current candidate. The integers $`m`$
and $`k`$ are action intensities, e.g., evidence budget and
context-retrieval budget.

###### Assumption 2.3 (Held-out exchangeability).

Calibration and evaluation problem trajectories are independent draws
from the same benchmark distribution. Independence is required across
problem trajectories at a fixed step $`t`$; it is *not* required across
refinement steps within a trajectory.

###### Definition 2.4 (Controller manifest).

The controller manifest is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi:=\bigl(\Gamma^{\rm obs}_{1:T},\,\mathcal{U}_{\rm probe},\,m,\,\gamma_{\rm evid},\,k_{\rm gen},\,\ell_{\rm alg},\,R_{\rm route},\,D_{\rm dedup},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\rm prompt},\,d_{\rm decode},\,T,\,M^{\star},\,\omega_{\rm write}\bigr)`$ |  |

containing: the observable admission gate $`\Gamma^{\rm obs}_{1:T}`$;
the RiskProbe tool interface $`\mathcal{U}_{\rm probe}`$ and its
model-controlled invocation policy; the EvidenceAcquire intensity $`m`$
and aggregation rule $`\gamma_{\rm evid}`$; the two ContextAcquire
retrieval channels—general-bank injection budget $`k_{\rm gen}`$ and
algorithmic-bucket retrieval budget $`\ell_{\rm alg}`$ (so the action
intensity $`k`$ in
Eq. ([6](#S2.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ Definition 2.2 (Abstract action classes). ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"))
is realized as the pair $`(k_{\rm gen},\ell_{\rm alg})`$); the retrieval
routing $`R_{\rm route}`$; the deduplication rule $`D_{\rm dedup}`$; the
prompt template $`p_{\rm prompt}`$; the decoding parameters
$`d_{\rm decode}`$; the horizon $`T`$; the memory snapshot
$`M^{\star}`$; and the write mode $`\omega_{\rm write}`$. For every
certificate-bearing result, $`\omega_{\rm write}=\textsf{audit-only}`$,
i.e., $`M^{\star}`$ is read-only and evaluation-time experiences are
written only to the audit log $`\widetilde{M}`$. Each entry of $`\pi`$
is either pre-declared or selected on held-out traces, and all entries
are frozen before final evaluation under the split
$`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}\to D_{\rm cal}\to D_{\rm test}`$ with
$`M^{\star}`$ built from $`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}`$.

##### Calibration estimators.

For each fixed $`\pi`$ and step $`t`$, let $`n_{t}^{\pi}`$ active
calibration traces contain $`f_{t}^{\pi}`$ false admissions and
$`s_{t}^{\pi}`$ clean successes. Under
Assumption [2.3](#S2.Thmtheorem3 "Assumption 2.3 (Held-out exchangeability). ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
the counts are binomial conditional on $`n_{t}^{\pi}`$, so we use the
exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson intervals ([Clopper and Pearson,
1934](#bib.bib11))

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (7) |  |
``` math
\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}:=\mathrm{UCB}_{\rm Binom}\!\left(f_{t}^{\pi},n_{t}^{\pi},\tfrac{\delta}{2T}\right),\quad\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}:=\mathrm{LCB}_{\rm Binom}\!\left(s_{t}^{\pi},n_{t}^{\pi},\tfrac{\delta}{2T}\right)
``` |  |

on the deployment active-state hazards
$`q_{t}^{\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}{=}1\mid A_{t})`$ and
$`h_{t}^{\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(S_{t}{=}1\mid A_{t})`$, jointly valid for
$`t\leq T`$ by a union bound (proof of
Theorem [2.6](#S2.Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 2.6 (Simultaneously valid finite-manifest calibration). ‣ Controller certificate. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"));
operationally, both bounds share the same active-row denominator
$`n_{t}^{\pi}`$ on $`D_{\rm cal}`$.

###### Theorem 2.5 (Calibrated admission gate).

Let $`\Theta_{t}`$ be a finite grid of admission thresholds for the risk
score in
Eq. ([1](#S2.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ Definition 2.1 (Controlled stopped process and certificate state). ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
On held-out calibration traces, let $`n_{t}(\theta)`$ be the number of
active candidate admissions with $`r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\theta`$, and let
$`f_{t}(\theta)`$ be the number of such admissions that hidden
evaluation rejects. Define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{R}_{t}(\theta)=\mathrm{UCB}_{\rm Binom}\!\left(f_{t}(\theta),n_{t}(\theta),\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T|\Theta_{t}|}\right)
``` |  |

and select

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (8) |  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}=\max\{\theta\in\Theta_{t}:\bar{R}_{t}(\theta)\leq\alpha_{t}\},
``` |  |

with $`\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}{:=}-\infty`$,
$`\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}{\equiv}0`$ if the set is empty, and the
conservative conventions $`\mathrm{UCB}_{\rm Binom}(0,0,\eta){:=}1`$ and
zero-probability conditioning evaluated as $`0`$. Then, with probability
at least $`1-\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}`$ over calibration, the gate
$`\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}\}`$
satisfies

|     |                                                            |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \Pr(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t},\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=1)\leq\alpha_{t}
       ```                                                         |     |

simultaneously for all $`t\leq T`$ and all data-dependent
$`\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}\in\Theta_{t}`$.

##### Controller certificate.

Theorem [2.5](#S2.Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 2.5 (Calibrated admission gate). ‣ Calibration estimators. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
controls risk-threshold stopping when a score threshold is part of the
manifest, and audits the frozen observable gate otherwise. To select
information-acquisition actions, we compare two separately calibrated
UCBs on the active false-admission hazard — the *raw* bound from
admitted-failure counts and the *mechanism-factorized* bound that
multiplies probe-survival and evidence-gate-survival rates — and take
their minimum (the mechanism factor wins when the probe and evidence
channel together reject most of the bad mass; the raw bound wins when
calibration data is sparse, since unfit mechanism factors fall back to
$`1`$):

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\exp\!\bigl[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}\bigr],`$ |  |  |
| (9) |  | $`\displaystyle\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\min\!\left\{\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi},\,\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}\right\}.`$ |  |

Here $`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}`$ is the Clopper–Pearson UCB on
$`q_{t}^{\pi}`$ from active calibration trajectories. For the mechanism
factor, let $`W_{t}^{\rm probe},W_{t}^{\rm evid}\in\{0,1\}`$ denote
survival through the upstream risk-probe stage and the evidence gate,
respectively. Since $`\{F_{t}{=}1\}\subseteq\{B_{t}^{\epsilon},`$
$`W_{t}^{\rm probe}{=}1,`$ $`W_{t}^{\rm evid}{=}1\}`$ on $`A_{t}`$, the
chain rule of conditional probabilities (not independence) gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}{=}1\mid A_{t})\leq\underbrace{\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon},W_{t}^{\rm probe}{=}1\mid A_{t})}_{\leq\,\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\ \text{(Prop.~\ref{prop:probe-odds})}}\cdot\underbrace{\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\rm evid}{=}1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},W_{t}^{\rm probe}{=}1,A_{t})}_{\leq\,\exp[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}]\ \text{(Prop.~\ref{prop:evidence-acquire})}}.
``` |  |

We split
$`\delta=\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}+\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}+\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}+\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}`$,
allocating $`\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}/(2T)`$ to each of
$`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}`$ and $`\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}`$
(Eqs. ([7](#S2.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ Calibration estimators. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")), ([10](#S2.E10 "Equation 10 ‣ Controller certificate. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"))),
$`\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}`$ to the threshold grid
(Theorem [2.5](#S2.Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 2.5 (Calibrated admission gate). ‣ Calibration estimators. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")),
$`\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}/T`$ to
$`\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}`$, and
$`\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}/T`$ to $`\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)`$. A single union
bound makes all UCBs/LCBs jointly valid with calibration probability
$`\geq 1-\delta`$, so $`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi}`$ is a valid UCB

The product form follows from the chain rule over stopped events, not

## 3. Method

##### Motivation & Overview.

CP-Agent is the instantiation of the selected controller
$`\pi^{\star}`$: each abstract action class maps to a concrete tool
(Table [1](#S2.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
All manifest entries—prompts, the observable admission gate, the HP and
TA interfaces, the retrieval budget, the historical memory snapshot
$`M^{\star}`$, the write mode, and the maximum horizon—are frozen before
benchmark evaluation, either pre-declared or audited on held-out traces
$`D_{\rm cal}`$ via
Theorem [2.6](#S2.Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 2.6 (Simultaneously valid finite-manifest calibration). ‣ Controller certificate. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").

### 3.1. Dual-Granularity Verification

RiskProbe and $`\textsf{EvidenceAcquire}(m)`$ act at different
granularities: the former screens a reasoning state before it is
admitted, the latter gates a complete candidate before it is trusted.
CP-Agent realizes them as a Hypothesis Prober for the logged upstream
gate $`G_{t}^{\mathrm{HP}}`$ and a Solution Validator for the
program-level admission screen inside $`\Gamma_{t}`$. Separating the two
roles prevents the upstream screen from being double-counted as
calibrated program-level evidence.

##### Hypothesis Prober.

During early-stage reasoning, the agent often needs to verify local
claims that are hard to establish purely in natural language—e.g.,
whether a recurrence holds on random small instances, whether a greedy
rule fails on a constructed counterexample, or whether an invariant is
preserved by a transition. CP-Agent exposes a Hypothesis Prober that
compiles and runs short C++ snippets for this purpose. Under the frozen
prompt and decoding policy the backbone itself decides when to call HP
from its observable reasoning state—no hand-written trigger grid—and
every call/pass/fail outcome is logged for calibration. A surviving HP
call contracts the posterior odds of an upstream reasoning error
(Proposition [2.7](#S2.Thmtheorem7 "Proposition 2.7 (RiskProbe bad-and-survive UCB). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"))
and tightens the residual envelope
$`\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}`$ entering
Eq. ([9](#S2.E9 "Equation 9 ‣ Controller certificate. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"));
HP thus moves $`\rho_{t}`$ (and the aggregate $`\bar{q}_{t}`$) alone,
not the program-level evidence channel.

##### Solution Validator.

Once the agent produces a complete candidate implementation, the
Solution Validator compiles and runs it against the local test suite:
any public/local failure rejects the candidate and returns
counterexample-level feedback. By default SV is a deterministic hard
screen and contributes no calibrated statistical evidence; only when the
suite uses fresh sampled tests or an externally calibrated set is an
all-pass event additionally folded into $`\mathcal{I}_{t}`$ as the
binary survival channel of
Proposition [2.8](#S2.Thmtheorem8 "Proposition 2.8 (Candidate-level evidence from EvidenceAcquire). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").

### 3.2. Test Augmentation

The action class $`\textsf{EvidenceAcquire}(m)`$ makes the admission
gate more informative against public-test-passing errors and exposes the
evidence-acquisition intensity as a frozen controller entry. Public test
suites $`\mathcal{T}_{\text{basic}}`$ are fixed and shallow, so passing
them is a weak certificate: candidates routinely clear
$`\mathcal{T}_{\text{basic}}`$ yet fail hidden tests in corner regimes.
Test Augmentation (TA) instantiates EvidenceAcquire by appending
consensus-filtered checks beyond $`\mathcal{T}_{\text{basic}}`$; an
all-pass on the augmented suite enters $`\mathcal{I}_{t}`$ as the binary
survival channel
of Proposition [2.8](#S2.Thmtheorem8 "Proposition 2.8 (Candidate-level evidence from EvidenceAcquire). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").

##### Augmentation pipeline.

CP-Agent implements test augmentation via three stages. Input Synthesis.
Given the statement, the agent synthesizes a C++ input generator based
on testlib.h²² 2
[https://github.com/MikeMirzayanov/testlib](https://github.com/MikeMirzayanov/testlib)
and command suites that sweep corner regimes such as min/max values,
degenerate structures, adversarial patterns, and random sizes. Oracle
Labeling. Ignoring time and memory limits often makes it easier to write
a correct brute-force solver than an efficient one ([Ridnik et al.,
2024](#bib.bib29); [He et al., 2025](#bib.bib17)); CP-Agent therefore
executes independently generated brute-force solvers on synthesized
inputs to obtain candidate reference outputs. Consensus Filtering.
Because brute-force programs can still be imperfect, CP-Agent runs
multiple solvers in parallel and majority-votes their outputs.
Candidates are evaluated on augmented tests only after passing public
tests, so augmentation serves as additional diagnostic evidence after
the basic admission screen. Budget and retries. The frozen TA manifest
caps synthesis at 32 candidate augmented inputs per problem. This is an
upper bound rather than a fixed suite size: the generator does not keep
sampling until a nonempty suite is obtained, and there is no separate
retention cap after filtering. Instead, CP-Agent retains all valid
inputs that pass synthesis, compilation/execution, and consensus
labeling within the cap; generator synthesis/compilation/execution may
be retried up to five times. Consequently, GenRate measures the fraction
of problems for which this capped protocol yields a nonempty suite,
while the candidate-level evidence calibration treats the resulting
suite as one binary survival channel.

### 3.3. Experience-Driven Self-Evolving

The action class $`\textsf{ContextAcquire}(k)`$ calls for a compact
context prior $`M`$ that raises the active-state success hazard
$`\underline{h}_{t}(M)`$ in
Corollary [2.10](#S2.Thmtheorem10 "Corollary 2.10 (Stopped clean-before-false certificate). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").
A naive instantiation—continuously updating $`M`$ across the entire
problem stream—would let target-split outcomes leak into $`M`$ and
invalidate the calibration premise of
Theorem [2.6](#S2.Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 2.6 (Simultaneously valid finite-manifest calibration). ‣ Controller certificate. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"):
the hazard lift would no longer be data-independent of the evaluation
split. CP-Agent resolves this with a temporal frozen-memory protocol,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}\rightarrow D_{\rm cal}\rightarrow D_{\rm test}.
``` |  |

In *memory-building mode*, validated fail-to-pass traces from
$`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}`$ are written into a retrievable memory bank.
In *certificate-evaluation mode*, the resulting snapshot $`M^{\star}`$
is frozen before $`D_{\rm cal}`$ and $`D_{\rm test}`$; both read
$`M^{\star}`$ read-only, and target-split experiences are written only
to the audit log, never retrieved by subsequent target problems. Dataset
instantiation of $`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}/D_{\rm cal}/D_{\rm test}`$ is
reported in
§[4](#S4 "4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").

##### Memory-building stage.

*Notation.* On $`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})`$ we index
problems by $`r`$ and within-problem attempts by $`t`$; the target-split
index $`i\in D_{\rm cal}\cup D_{\rm test}`$ is disjoint from $`r`$.

The design question is what evidence each experience should carry so
that the resulting prior can move the hazard but not the calibration
premise. We require each retrievable item to be a self-contained
*Fail$`\to`$Pass* triplet—a failed attempt, its observable failure
evidence, and a later observable passing correction on the *same*
problem. The triplet anchors every learnt pattern to an observable
counterexample, so retrieval at evaluation time substitutes for an
additional probe rather than supplying labels; and because it is built
only on $`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}`$, it leaves the active-row denominator
$`n_{t}^{\pi}`$ on $`D_{\rm cal}`$ untouched.

Concretely, on problem $`q_{r}`$ the agent maintains a build-time memory
state $`M^{\rm build}_{r}`$ distilled from validated fixes. Candidate
solutions are generated as

|      |     |                                                          |     |
|------|-----|----------------------------------------------------------|-----|
| (16) |     |
              ``` math
              s_{r,t}=\mathrm{Gen}(q_{r},M^{\rm build}_{r},h_{r,t-1}),
              ```                                                       |     |

where $`h_{r,t-1}`$ summarizes within-problem history. The Solution
Validator returns a pass indicator and public/local feedback,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (17) |  |
``` math
(v_{r,t},\,f_{r,t})=\Phi(q_{r},s_{r,t}),\qquad v_{r,t}\in\{0,1\},
``` |  |

with $`f_{r,t}`$ the actionable failure feedback (unused when
$`v_{r,t}{=}1`$), matching the $`(v,f)\leftarrow\Phi(\cdot)`$ call in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix A Tool-Orchestrated Pseudocode ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").
For any failing attempt $`t`$, define the first later observable pass

|      |     |                                        |     |
|------|-----|----------------------------------------|-----|
| (18) |     |
              ``` math
              t^{+}(r,t):=\min\{u>t\mid v_{r,u}=1\},
              ```                                     |     |

when it exists. The extracted experience is

|      |     |                                                               |     |
|------|-----|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
| (19) |     |
              ``` math
              e_{r,t}=\mathrm{Exp}(q_{r},s_{r,t},s_{r,t^{+}(r,t)},f_{r,t}),
              ```                                                            |     |

realising the Fail$`\to`$Pass triplet above as a reusable memory item.
The build-time memory update is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (20) |  |
``` math
M^{\rm build}_{r+1}=\mathrm{Update}(M^{\rm build}_{r},e_{r,t}).
``` |  |

Because both $`s_{r,t^{+}(r,t)}`$ and $`f_{r,t}`$ are observable on
$`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}`$, no hidden label from
$`D_{\rm cal}\cup D_{\rm test}`$ enters $`\mathrm{Exp}(\cdot)`$,
$`\mathrm{Update}(\cdot)`$, prompts, or routing.

##### Frozen certificate-evaluation stage.

After historical memory construction, calibration chooses a snapshot
$`M^{\star}`$ and controller manifest $`\pi^{\star}`$ using
$`D_{\rm cal}`$ with memory fixed. For every target calibration and test
problem,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (21) |  |
``` math
M_{i}^{\rm target}\equiv M^{\star},\qquad i\in D_{\rm cal}\cup D_{\rm test}.
``` |  |

An extractable target-split experience is recorded only in an audit log
$`\widetilde{M}`$ for optional post-benchmark deployment:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
| (22) |  |
``` math
\widetilde{M}_{i+1}=\widetilde{M}_{i}\cup\{e_{i}\},\qquad M_{i+1}^{\rm target}=M^{\star}.
``` |  |

Thus the calibrated certificate’s memory prior is data-independent of
the target evaluation outcomes—exactly the i.i.d.-given-controller
premise that
Theorem [2.6](#S2.Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 2.6 (Simultaneously valid finite-manifest calibration). ‣ Controller certificate. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
requires of the held-out rows.

##### Experience Curation.

The hazard lift attributable to $`M`$ is bounded by the description
length of the retrieved evidence
(Proposition [2.9](#S2.Thmtheorem9 "Proposition 2.9 (ContextAcquire as calibrated hazard envelope). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")),
so curation is the part of the manifest that directly determines how
much $`\underline{h}_{t}`$ can move. We design three controllers, one
per MDL component, and freeze all three into the manifest
(Definition [2.4](#S2.Thmtheorem4 "Definition 2.4 (Controller manifest). ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")):

*(i) Routing* restricts the active hypothesis bucket by splitting the
snapshot into two repositories,
$`M^{\star}:=(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{gen}},\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{alg}})`$,
where $`\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{gen}}`$ collects task-agnostic debugging
patterns and $`\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}`$ is
indexed by a two-level taxonomy $`(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})`$ with 8 primary
categories and 68 secondary tags.

*(ii) LLM-mediated deduplication* merges redundant traces: on each
insertion the curation prompt compares the candidate against existing
summaries in the target bank and rejects entries judged duplicate or
covered by an existing generalization, so retrieved memory carries
non-redundant evidence rather than near-copies of the same pattern.

*(iii) Capacity* controls the description length of retrieved memory by
capping each bank, $`|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{gen}}|\leq 20`$ and
$`|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}|\leq K{=}10`$; once a
bank is at capacity the same semantic rule keeps at most the allowed
number of representative experiences,

|     |                                                     |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathcal{B}\leftarrow\mathrm{Dedup}(\mathcal{B};K).
       ```                                                  |     |

##### Experience Utilization.

At evaluation time the two repositories play distinct roles in the
hazard. The general bank supplies a *task-agnostic prior shift* applied
to every problem: a compact summary is appended to the system prompt,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\textsf{SysPrompt}_{i}={}`$ | BaseSysPrompt |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\|\ \mathrm{Format}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{gen}},k_{\text{gen}}),`$ |  |

raising the baseline component of $`\underline{h}_{t}(M^{\star})`$
uniformly across $`q_{i}`$. The algorithmic bank supplies a
*task-conditional lift* that fires only when $`q_{i}`$ is routed to a
populated bucket: CP-Agent predicts a taxonomy label and retrieves the
corresponding frozen bucket,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  |  |  |  |
| (23a) |  | $`\displaystyle(\hat{\tau}_{1},\hat{\tau}_{2})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{ClassifyAlg}(q_{i}),`$ |  |
| (23b) |  | $`\displaystyle E_{i}^{\mathrm{alg}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathrm{RetrieveAlgExp}(\hat{\tau}_{1},\hat{\tau}_{2};\ell,M^{\star}),`$ |  |

contributing the residual, problem-specific portion of the lift. Both
pathways read $`M^{\star}`$ read-only, so the realized hazard
$`\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})`$ remains the calibrated quantity
entering
Corollary [2.10](#S2.Thmtheorem10 "Corollary 2.10 (Stopped clean-before-false certificate). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"),
and the refinement-step-$`1`$ ($`t{=}1`$) acceptance gain in
Table [5](#S4.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 4.4. Trace-Calibrated Certificate and Mechanism Evidence ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
is its empirical instantiation.

### 3.4. Tool Orchestration

CP-Agent runs a stage-wise refinement loop coordinating the three
components. For each problem $`q_{i}`$, the system prompt is initialized
with the general bank of $`M^{\star}`$ and, when helpful, retrieved
algorithmic experiences $`E_{i}^{\mathrm{alg}}`$. The frozen backbone
then drafts a solution and may call the Hypothesis Prober to falsify
local hypotheses before committing; the Solution Validator compiles and
runs each draft on public tests, whose feedback drives subsequent edits;
once all public tests pass, Test Augmentation generates
consensus-filtered cases. In deployment mode, attempts may be appended
to retrievable memory via
Eq. ([20](#S3.E20 "Equation 20 ‣ Memory-building stage. ‣ 3.3. Experience-Driven Self-Evolving ‣ 3. Method ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"));
in certificate-evaluation mode they are written only to the audit log
$`\widetilde{M}`$ via
Eq. ([22](#S3.E22 "Equation 22 ‣ Frozen certificate-evaluation stage. ‣ 3.3. Experience-Driven Self-Evolving ‣ 3. Method ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).

Section [2](#S2 "2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")

$`\to\textsf{EvidenceAcquire}(m)`$, Experience
$`\to\textsf{ContextAcquire}(k)`$.

## 4. Experiment

### 4.1. Experiment Setup

##### Backbone LLMs

To evaluate the general applicability of our method across different
LLMs, we conducted experiments using three distinct backbone LLMs:
GPT-4o ([Hurst et al., 2024](#bib.bib20)),
Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct ([Yang et al., 2025](#bib.bib33)), and
DeepSeek-V3.2-Chat ([Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)) for all multi-agent
workflows and CP-Agent variants.

##### Baselines

We compare against two baseline groups: (i) Frontier LLMs:
DeepSeek-R1 ([Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib16)),
DeepSeek-V3.2-Reasoner ([Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)),
Gemini-3-pro ([Pichai et al., 2025](#bib.bib14)), and
o4-mini-high ([OpenAI, 2025b](#bib.bib38)). (ii) Agent
frameworks/workflows: For a fair comparison, we evaluate three agent
frameworks: Reflexion ([Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib30)),
CodeSim ([Islam et al., 2025](#bib.bib22)), and AlphaCodium ([Ridnik et
al., 2024](#bib.bib29)), each with the same three backbones, all
targeting C++.

##### Evaluation datasets.

We evaluate all baselines and CP-Agent variants on two
competition-style, contamination-controlled benchmarks: LiveCodeBench
Pro ([Zheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib3)) and ICPC-Eval ([Xu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib2)). The target split for LCB-Pro is the held-out 2025Q2
subset. The temporal partition that Experience uses on LCB-Pro and the
controller transfer to ICPC-Eval are described in
§[4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
(summarized in
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).

##### Metrics

For ICPC-Eval, we use Refine@K ([Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)) to measure
iterative refinement under execution feedback, i.e., whether the model
passes the hidden judge within $`K`$ response–revision attempts; we
report Refine@5 ($`K{=}5`$) to reflect the interactive nature of
competitive programming. For LiveCodeBench Pro, we report Pass@1 to
match its standard single-shot setting and prior work, and use
per-refinement-step analyses ($`t{=}1,2,3`$) when studying the role of
experience.

##### Implementations.

The full frozen controller manifest—refinement horizon $`T`$, retrieval
budgets $`(k_{\rm gen},\ell_{\rm alg})`$, TA intensity $`m`$ and
consensus rule $`\gamma_{\rm evid}`$, decoding parameters, and prompt
template—is given in
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").
Baselines (Reflexion, CodeSim, AlphaCodium) use their official prompts
and control flow with the same three backbones. HP, TA, and Experience
are added only inside CP-Agent variants and never touch baselines.

### 4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability

The controller is calibrated before final benchmark reporting and is
never recomputed after observing evaluation outcomes. We use a temporal
three-stage protocol,

``` |  |

First, the 387 historical pre-test problems in biannual_2024_7_12

Eq. ([20](#S3.E20 "Equation 20 ‣ Memory-building stage. ‣ 3.3. Experience-Driven Self-Evolving ‣ 3. Method ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
Second, for each candidate policy $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$, we run the
frozen agent with a fixed snapshot $`M^{\star}`$ on the disjoint

##### Experience-Driven Self-Evolving

Certificate-bearing benchmark results use the temporal frozen snapshot
protocol in
Section [4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"):
historical problems build $`M^{\star}`$, calibration audits the
controller with $`M^{\star}`$ fixed, and final testing reads the same
snapshot. Deployment-mode growth diagnostics in the full study show that
general experiences saturate quickly while algorithmic memories keep
expanding as new problem types appear; the curriculum effect follows our
write-in criterion, because experiences are stored only after a failure
is repaired. These diagnostics explain snapshot construction on
$`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}`$, while all reported certificates freeze the
resulting snapshot before $`D_{\rm cal}`$ and $`D_{\rm test}`$.

### 4.7. Ablation Study

Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.4. Trace-Calibrated Certificate and Mechanism Evidence ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
also serves as the component ablation on DeepSeek-V3.2-Chat: SV and HP
constitute Dual-Granularity Verification, TA generates extra tests
beyond public cases, and Exp retrieves reusable repair knowledge from
past successful trajectories. Vanilla LLM yields $`22.9`$ Refine@5 on
ICPC-Eval and $`25.8`$ Pass@1 on LCB-Pro at \$0.002 per problem. Adding
SV markedly improves both ($`31.4/43.7`$ at \$0.023); SV+HP further
improves to $`32.2/44.3`$, supporting their complementarity. Adding TA
on top of Dual-Granularity Verification brings additional Pass@1 gains
at higher cost ($`33.1/46.1`$ at \$0.043). Finally, historical frozen
Experience achieves the best performance ($`33.9/48.5`$) and reduces
per-problem cost back to \$0.035, indicating that retrieved repair
experiences reduce ineffective trial-and-error without using
calibration- or test-split write-ins. The certificate column
$`C_{5}^{\rm ctrl}`$ rises monotonically with the same component
additions, so the ablation ranking reproduces the certificate ranking on
$`D_{\rm cal}`$.

##### Additional diagnostics.

TA reliability on 117 public-pass candidates: TA flags 40/51
hidden-wrong programs while letting 47/66 hidden-correct survive,
leaving the $`11/58{=}19.0\%`$ post-survival bug rate used by
Prop. [2.8](#S2.Thmtheorem8 "Proposition 2.8 (Candidate-level evidence from EvidenceAcquire). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming");
sampling $`k`$ tests per suite raises detection to $`74.5\%`$ at
$`k{=}15`$.

## 5. Related Work

##### Competitive programming agents.

Among tool-augmented LLM agents that ground reasoning on deterministic
execution feedback ([Yao et al., 2022](#bib.bib36); [Madaan et al.,
2023](#bib.bib27); [Gao et al., 2023](#bib.bib13); [Chen et al.,
2022b](#bib.bib9); [Gou et al., 2024](#bib.bib15); [Wu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib35); [Das et al., 2024](#bib.bib1)), work on CP
benchmarks ([Hendrycks et al., 2021](#bib.bib18); [Chen et al.,
2021](#bib.bib4); [Jain et al., 2025](#bib.bib23); [Zheng et al.,
2026](#bib.bib3); [Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib2)) mostly raises accuracy
by scale: dedicated training or massive sampling ([Li et al.,
2022](#bib.bib25); [Leblond et al., 2023](#bib.bib39); [El-Kishky et
al., 2025](#bib.bib12); [Guo et al., 2025](#bib.bib16); [Liu et al.,
2025](#bib.bib26)). Inference-time pipelines such as MapCoder,
AlphaCodium, and CodeSim avoid retraining by iterating generation and
verification, but are session-local and discard repair experience after
each problem ([Islam et al., 2024](#bib.bib21); [Ridnik et al.,
2024](#bib.bib29); [Islam et al., 2025](#bib.bib22); [Yuhua et al.,
2025](#bib.bib37); [Li and Mooney, 2025](#bib.bib24)). Reflexion-style
self-refinement ([Shinn et al., 2023](#bib.bib30)) keeps verbal memory
across problems but offers no admission rule for when retrieved memory
should enter the loop. CP-Agent adds neither training nor sampling: a
calibrated controller ties generation, verification, and a frozen memory
snapshot to a single held-out audit, closing the gap to a stronger
reasoner ([Liu et al., 2025](#bib.bib26)) on ICPC-Eval Refine@5.

##### Testing, risk control, and frozen memory.

Randomized testing and generated checks have long driven program repair
and synthesis ([Claessen and Hughes, 2000](#bib.bib10); [Solar-Lezama et
al., 2006](#bib.bib31); [Chen et al., 2022a](#bib.bib8); [He et al.,
2025](#bib.bib17)), but are usually consumed as raw signals. We treat SV
and TA as calibrated evidence channels via binomial calibration,
conformal-style risk control, and anytime-valid evidence ([Clopper and
Pearson, 1934](#bib.bib11); [Angelopoulos et al., 2024](#bib.bib6);
[Howard et al., 2021](#bib.bib19); [Vovk and Wang, 2021](#bib.bib34)),
so coverage and post-survival risk are read off held-out traces.
Experience enters as a temporal frozen memory prior with PAC-Bayes/MDL
complexity control ([McAllester, 1999](#bib.bib28); [McAllester,
1999](#bib.bib28)): tools and memory are fixed on
$`D_{\rm hist}^{\rm mem}`$ before $`D_{\rm cal}`$ and $`D_{\rm test}`$
(Section [4.2](#S4.SS2 "4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"))
so the audit remains valid; memory may still grow in deployment.

## 6. Conclusion

A calibrated risk-controlled controller substantially improves
contest-level programming without additional training. The theory
derives certificate quantities and abstract action classes that are
audited on held-out traces and frozen before evaluation; CP-Agent
instantiates them as Dual-Granularity Verification, Test Augmentation,
and Experience-Driven Self-Evolving, matches DeepSeek-V3.2-Reasoner on
ICPC-Eval Refine@5, and provides trace-calibrated mechanism evidence for
each action class.

## Appendix C Section 2 Proofs

This appendix contains the proofs for Section 2 ("A Calibrated

### 0. Setting and Conventions

#### 0.1 Probability space and stopped events

Fix a frozen controller $`\pi`$. A trajectory is generated by sampling
one problem from the benchmark distribution and running $`\pi`$ for at
most $`T`$ refinement steps. All probabilities $`\Pr_{\pi}(\cdot)`$ are
taken with respect to a fresh evaluation trajectory; calibration
probabilities are taken with respect to held-out calibration
trajectories.

The stopping times are

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tau_{F}:=\inf\{t:F_{t}=1\},\hskip 18.49988pt\tau_{S}:=\inf\{t:S_{t}=1\},
``` |  |

and the active event before step $`t`$ is

|     |                                         |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       A_{t}:=\{\tau_{S}>t-1,\;\tau_{F}>t-1\}.
       ```                                      |     |

By Definition 2.1,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
F_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{A_{t},\,\Gamma_{t}=1,\,B_{t}^{\epsilon}\},\hskip 18.49988ptS_{t}:=\mathbf{1}\{A_{t},\,\Gamma_{t}=1,\,\neg B_{t}^{\epsilon}\},
``` |  |

so $`F_{t}`$ and $`S_{t}`$ are mutually exclusive at every fixed step
$`t`$ via $`B_{t}^{\epsilon}`$. Hence $`\tau_{S}`$ and $`\tau_{F}`$
cannot both be finite and equal (they may, however, both equal
$`+\infty`$ when neither stop occurs by $`T`$, with the convention
$`\inf\varnothing:=+\infty`$). The certified event is

|     |                                         |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \{\tau_{S}\leq T,\;\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}.
       ```                                      |     |

We adopt the stopped-process convention: once a clean success or false
admission occurs at some step $`t^{\star}`$, $`S_{t}`$ and $`F_{t}`$ are
set to $`0`$ at all subsequent steps $`t>t^{\star}`$ and the trajectory
is inactive. As a consequence, if $`\tau_{S}<\infty`$ then $`F_{t}=0`$
for all $`t>\tau_{S}`$, so $`\tau_{F}=+\infty`$ on
$`\{\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}`$; symmetrically, $`\tau_{S}=+\infty`$ on
$`\{\tau_{F}<\tau_{S}\}`$. We further assume $`\Pr_{\pi}(A_{1})=1`$,
i.e., the trajectory begins active at step $`1`$.

#### 0.2 Admission-gate semantics required by the mechanism factorization

Eq. (9) and Section 5 below use the containment

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\{F_{t}=1\}\subseteq\{B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\;W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\;W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on }A_{t}.
``` |  |

This is equivalent to the requirement that the observable admission gate
satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Gamma_{t}=1\;\Longrightarrow\;W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\;\text{and}\;W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on }A_{t},
``` |  |

i.e., any candidate released by $`\Gamma_{t}`$ has first passed the
risk-probe and evidence stages (with the convention
$`W^{\mathrm{probe}}=W^{\mathrm{evid}}=1`$ when the corresponding stage
is not invoked). We treat this implication as part of the manifest
contract on $`\pi`$; if $`\Gamma_{1:T}^{\mathrm{obs}}`$ does not chain
the upstream screens, the mechanism UCB is invalid and only the raw UCB
$`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}`$ applies.

#### 0.3 Held-out exchangeability

Under Assumption 2.3, calibration and evaluation trajectories are
independent draws from the same benchmark distribution. Independence is
required across problem trajectories at a fixed step $`t`$, not across
refinement steps within a trajectory. For fixed $`t`$ and $`\pi`$, the
active calibration rows yield conditional Bernoulli samples; a random
active denominator does not invalidate the bounds, because we condition
on the denominator first and then mix.

#### 0.4 Zero-probability conditioning

For any conditional probability $`\Pr(H\mid E)`$ with $`\Pr(E)=0`$, we
set $`\Pr(H\mid E):=0`$. This convention never inflates an upper bound;
in chain-rule decompositions, the corresponding event has probability
$`0`$ and contributes nothing.

#### 0.5 Clopper–Pearson UCB / LCB

Let
$`Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)`$
and $`K=\sum_{i}Y_{i}`$. Write

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
U(K,n,\eta)=\mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(K,n,\eta),\hskip 18.49988ptL(K,n,\eta)=\mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(K,n,\eta)
``` |  |

for the exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson bounds, so that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{p}\{p\leq U(K,n,\eta)\}\geq 1-\eta,\hskip 18.49988pt\Pr_{p}\{p\geq L(K,n,\eta)\}\geq 1-\eta.
``` |  |

If the denominator $`N`$ is random with
$`K\mid N=n\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)`$, then

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\{p>U(K,N,\eta)\}=\sum_{n}\Pr(N=n)\,\Pr\{p>U(K,n,\eta)\mid N=n\}\leq\eta,
``` |  |

and the analogous bound holds for the LCB. We use the conservative
conventions

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(0,0,\eta)=1,\hskip 18.49988pt\mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}(0,0,\eta)=0.
``` |  |

#### 0.6 Pre-declaration of score-defining objects

The proof of Theorem 2.5 requires that the score function
$`r_{t}(Z_{t})=\rho_{t}\exp(-I_{t})`$, the finite threshold grid
$`\Theta_{t}`$ (with $`1\leq|\Theta_{t}|<\infty`$), and the gate rule
$`\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}\leq\theta\}`$ be determined by $`\pi`$ alone, using
only the observable trajectory state. Concretely:

1.  (1)
    The measurable map
    $`\phi_{\pi}:X_{t}\mapsto Z_{t}=(\rho_{t},I_{t},\underline{h}_{t},t)`$
    depends only on observable mechanism outputs (probe survival,
    evidence-gate survival, retrieval slots, step index), not on hidden
    labels $`B_{t}^{\epsilon}`$.
2.  (2)
    The finite grid $`\Theta_{t}`$ is determined by $`\pi`$ with
    $`1\leq|\Theta_{t}|<\infty`$.
3.  (3)
    The gate rule $`\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}\leq\theta\}`$ is determined by
    $`\pi`$.

If $`\phi_{\pi}`$, $`\Theta_{t}`$, the aggregation rule
$`\gamma_{\mathrm{evid}}`$, the memory snapshot $`M^{\star}`$, or any
calibrated scalar in $`r_{t}`$ (such as
$`\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}`$ or $`\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}`$) is
tuned on $`D_{\mathrm{cal}}`$, every candidate value must be enumerated
in the finite, pre-declared manifest class $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$ (with
$`1\leq|\Pi_{\alpha}|<\infty`$) in advance, so that the union bound of
Theorem 2.6 absorbs both the score selection and the gate threshold
selection. Throughout Theorem 2.5 and Theorem 2.6 we treat this
pre-declaration as a standing hypothesis.

### 1. Validity of the Calibration Estimators in Eq. (7)

For fixed $`\pi`$ and step $`t`$, let $`n_{t}^{\pi}`$ be the number of
active calibration rows, $`f_{t}^{\pi}`$ the count of false admissions,
and $`s_{t}^{\pi}`$ the count of clean successes. Define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}:=\mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(f_{t}^{\pi},n_{t}^{\pi},\tfrac{\delta}{2T}\right),\hskip 18.49988pt\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}:=\mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(s_{t}^{\pi},n_{t}^{\pi},\tfrac{\delta}{2T}\right).
``` |  |

The deployment hazards are $`q_{t}^{\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t})`$
and $`h_{t}^{\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(S_{t}=1\mid A_{t})`$.

Proof. Fix $`t`$ and $`\pi`$. Let $`A_{i,t}`$ be the active indicator
for calibration trajectory $`i`$. On $`\{A_{i,t}=1\}`$, $`F_{i,t}`$ is
Bernoulli with parameter $`q_{t}^{\pi}`$. By Assumption 2.3, different
trajectories are independent at fixed $`t`$, so the active rows yield
i.i.d. $`\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q_{t}^{\pi})`$ samples; the random
denominator $`n_{t}^{\pi}`$ is handled by Section 0.5. The
Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\{q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}\}\geq 1-\tfrac{\delta}{2T}.
``` |  |

The same argument applied to $`S_{i,t}`$ gives
$`\Pr\{h_{t}^{\pi}\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}\}\geq 1-\tfrac{\delta}{2T}`$.
A union bound over the $`2T`$ one-sided events $`t=1,\dots,T`$ yields

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\!\left\{\forall t\leq T:\;q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}\;\text{and}\;h_{t}^{\pi}\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}\right\}\geq 1-\delta.\hskip 18.49988pt\square
``` |  |

The argument requires only across-trajectory independence at fixed
$`t`$, not within-trajectory step independence.

### 2. Theorem 2.5: Calibrated Admission Gate

Statement. Assume the pre-declaration of Section 0.6: $`\phi_{\pi}`$,
the score function $`r_{t}(Z_{t})=\rho_{t}\exp(-I_{t})`$, the finite
non-empty grid $`\Theta_{t}`$ (with $`1\leq|\Theta_{t}|<\infty`$), and
the gate rule are fixed by $`\pi`$ before label counting; any calibrated
scalar entering $`r_{t}`$ is enumerated in the finite, non-empty
manifest $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$ (with $`1\leq|\Pi_{\alpha}|<\infty`$). On
held-out calibration traces, let $`n_{t}(\theta)`$ be the number of
active candidate admissions with $`r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\theta`$ and
$`f_{t}(\theta)`$ the count of such admissions rejected by hidden
evaluation. Define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{R}_{t}(\theta):=\mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(f_{t}(\theta),\,n_{t}(\theta),\,\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T|\Theta_{t}|}\right)
``` |  |

and select

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}=\max\{\theta\in\Theta_{t}:\bar{R}_{t}(\theta)\leq\alpha_{t}\},
``` |  |

with $`\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}:=-\infty`$ and
$`\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}\equiv 0`$ when the set is empty. Then, with
calibration probability at least $`1-\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}`$, the gate
$`\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=\mathbf{1}\{r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}\}`$
satisfies

|     |                                                              |     |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \Pr(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t},\,\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=1)\leq\alpha_{t}
       ```                                                           |     |

simultaneously for all $`t\leq T`$ and all data-dependent
$`\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}\in\Theta_{t}`$.

Proof. Section 0.6 asserts that $`\phi_{\pi}`$, $`\Theta_{t}`$, and the
gate rule are pre-declared (or absorbed into $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$ by Theorem
2.6). Fix $`t`$ and $`\theta\in\Theta_{t}`$, and define the deployment
conditional rejection rate

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{t,\theta}:=\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t},\,r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\theta).
``` |  |

The conditioning event $`\{A_{t},\,r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\theta\}`$ is
determined by observables. By Assumption 2.3, the calibration rows
satisfying this event yield i.i.d. $`\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_{t,\theta})`$
samples for hidden rejection, with a random denominator
$`n_{t}(\theta)`$ handled by Section 0.5. The Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\{p_{t,\theta}\leq\bar{R}_{t}(\theta)\}\geq 1-\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T|\Theta_{t}|}.
``` |  |

Union-bounding over $`t\leq T`$ and $`\theta\in\Theta_{t}`$ defines the
good event

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{\mathrm{gate}}:=\{\forall t\leq T,\,\forall\theta\in\Theta_{t}:\;p_{t,\theta}\leq\bar{R}_{t}(\theta)\},\hskip 9.24994pt\Pr(G_{\mathrm{gate}})\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}.
``` |  |

On $`G_{\mathrm{gate}}`$, consider the data-dependent choice
$`\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}`$.

*Case 1: the selection set is non-empty.* Then
$`\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}\in\Theta_{t}`$ and
$`\bar{R}_{t}(\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha})\leq\alpha_{t}`$. Since
$`G_{\mathrm{gate}}`$ holds at every grid point,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{t,\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}}\leq\bar{R}_{t}(\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha})\leq\alpha_{t},
``` |  |

and $`\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=1`$ is equivalent to
$`r_{t}(Z_{t})\leq\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}`$, so

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t},\,\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=1)=p_{t,\hat{\theta}_{t,\alpha}}\leq\alpha_{t}.
``` |  |

*Case 2: the selection set is empty.* Then
$`\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}\equiv 0`$, and
$`\Pr(A_{t},\,\Gamma_{t}^{\alpha}=1)=0`$; by Section 0.4 the conditional
probability is $`0\leq\alpha_{t}`$. $`\hskip 18.49988pt\square`$

The post-selection validity follows from establishing the UCB
simultaneously over the finite grid before any data-dependent choice is
made.

### 3. Proposition 2.7: RiskProbe Bad-and-Survive UCB

Statement. Let $`W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}\in\{0,1\}`$ be the
probe-survival indicator at step $`t`$ (set to $`1`$ when the probe is
not invoked). On held-out calibration traces, let $`n_{t}^{\pi}`$ be the
number of active rows at step $`t`$ under $`\pi`$, and let
$`f_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}`$ count those rows where both
$`B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1`$ and $`W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1`$. Define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}:=\mathrm{UCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(f_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi},\,n_{t}^{\pi},\,\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{T}\right).
``` |  |

Then, with calibration probability at least
$`1-\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}`$, simultaneously for $`t\leq T`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\geq\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t}),
``` |  |

and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t})=\sigma_{t}^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}\,\rho_{t}^{\pi},
``` |  |

where
$`\sigma_{t}^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,A_{t})`$
and $`\rho_{t}^{\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon}\mid A_{t})`$.

Proof. Fix $`t`$. For calibration trajectory $`i`$, let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Y_{i,t}^{\mathrm{probe}}:=\mathbf{1}\{A_{i,t}=1,\,B_{i,t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{i,t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\}
``` |  |

and consider it only on active rows. On $`\{A_{i,t}=1\}`$,

|     |                                                                 |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \mathbf{1}\{B_{i,t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{i,t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\}
       ```                                                              |     |

is Bernoulli with parameter
$`p_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t})`$.
By Assumption 2.3, the active rows yield i.i.d. samples; Section 0.5
handles the random denominator. The Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\{p_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\leq\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\}\geq 1-\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{T},
``` |  |

and a union bound over $`t=1,\dots,T`$ yields the simultaneous statement
with confidence $`1-\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}`$.

The factorization follows from the chain rule of conditional probability
(no independence is required):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,A_{t})\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon}\mid A_{t})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}\,\rho_{t}^{\pi}.\hskip 18.49988pt\square`$ |  |

If $`\sigma_{t}^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}<1`$, the probe contracts the
bad-and-survive mass strictly below the bad prior; if additionally
$`\sigma_{t}^{\mathrm{bad},\pi}<\sigma_{t}^{\mathrm{good},\pi}`$, the
probe also shrinks the bad-versus-good odds.

### 4. Proposition 2.8: Candidate-Level Evidence from EvidenceAcquire

Statement. Let $`W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}\in\{0,1\}`$ denote the
program-level evidence-gate survival at step $`t`$ (set to $`1`$ when
the gate is not invoked). On calibration rows that are simultaneously
active, bad, and probe-surviving, let $`\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)`$ be the
one-sided Clopper–Pearson UCB on

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t})
``` |  |

at level $`\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}/T`$. Set
$`\Delta I_{t}(m):=-\log\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)`$. Then, with calibration
probability at least $`1-\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}`$, simultaneously for
$`t\leq T`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t})\leq\exp[-\Delta I_{t}(m)].
``` |  |

Proof. Fix $`t`$ and restrict the calibration set to rows with
$`A_{t}=1`$, $`B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1`$, and $`W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1`$.
Let $`n_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}`$ be the number of such rows and
$`k_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}`$ the number with
$`W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1`$. Conditional on this restriction,
$`W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}`$ is Bernoulli with parameter

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
b_{t}^{\pi}(m):=\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t}).
``` |  |

By Assumption 2.3 and Section 0.5, the Clopper–Pearson UCB gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\{b_{t}^{\pi}(m)\leq\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)\}\geq 1-\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}}{T},
``` |  |

and a union bound over $`t=1,\dots,T`$ produces the good event

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G_{\mathrm{evid}}:=\{\forall t\leq T:\;b_{t}^{\pi}(m)\leq\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)\},\hskip 9.24994pt\Pr(G_{\mathrm{evid}})\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}.
``` |  |

On $`G_{\mathrm{evid}}`$,
$`\exp[-\Delta I_{t}(m)]=\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)`$, so

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t})=b_{t}^{\pi}(m)\leq\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)=\exp[-\Delta I_{t}(m)].
``` |  |

If the conditioning subset is empty, $`\bar{b}_{t}^{\pi}(m)=1`$ by
Section 0.5 and the bound is trivial. $`\hskip 18.49988pt\square`$

#### 4.1 Suite-level interpretation

The bound treats the all-pass event over the consensus-filtered suite as
a single Bernoulli channel,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\iff{}`$ | $`\displaystyle\text{a bad-and-probe-surviving candidate}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\text{passes the entire suite}.`$ |  |

We calibrate the suite-level rate $`b_{t}^{\pi}(m)`$ directly; the $`m`$
generated tests need not be independent, and $`m`$ is not treated as a
sample size.

#### 4.2 Channel-level accumulation

If multiple evidence channels $`j=1,\dots,J_{t}`$ act on the same
admission decision at step $`t`$, with survival events $`E_{t,j}`$
calibrated under sequential conditional survival

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(E_{t,j}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t},\,E_{t,1}=1,\dots,E_{t,j-1}=1)\leq b_{t,j},
``` |  |

where each $`b_{t,j}`$ is a Clopper–Pearson UCB at level
$`\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}/(T\cdot J_{\max}\cdot|\Pi_{\alpha}|)`$ on its
conditional subset (with $`J_{\max}=\max_{t}J_{t}`$), then the chain
rule gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t})\leq\prod_{j=1}^{J_{t}}b_{t,j}.
``` |  |

In this setting we define the cumulative evidence by channel index,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}:=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\Delta I_{t,j},\hskip 18.49988pt\Delta I_{t,j}:=-\log b_{t,j},
``` |  |

yielding

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t})\leq\exp[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}].
``` |  |

The accumulation is over channels acting on the current step’s admission
decision, not over refinement steps $`s\leq t`$. Across refinement
steps, the candidates differ, no sequential conditional survival is
calibrated, and the chain-rule decomposition does not apply.

For CP-Agent, the implementation uses a single TA channel per admission
decision ($`J_{t}\equiv 1`$), and
$`\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}=\Delta I_{t}(m)`$.

### 5. Validity of the Mechanism Factorization in Eq. (9)

Eq. (9) defines

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}=\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\,\exp[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}],\hskip 18.49988pt\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi}=\min\{\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi},\,\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}\}.
``` |  |

We show that on the good events of Propositions 2.7 and 2.8,
$`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi}`$ is a valid UCB on
$`q_{t}^{\pi}:=\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t})`$.

Proof. Under the gate-semantics requirement of Section 0.2, on
$`A_{t}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\{F_{t}=1\}\subseteq\{B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\}.
``` |  |

The chain rule of conditional probability gives

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle q_{t}^{\pi}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid A_{t})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t})`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\times\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t}).`$ |  |

On the good event of Proposition 2.7,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t})\leq\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}.
``` |  |

On the good event of Proposition 2.8 (or Section 4.2 when multiple
channels are present),

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1\mid B_{t}^{\epsilon}=1,\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1,\,A_{t})\leq\exp[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}],
``` |  |

where $`\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}`$ accumulates over channels
acting on the step-$`t`$ admission decision (Section 4.2). Combining,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\exp[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}]=\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}.
``` |  |

Section 1 gives $`q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}`$ on its
own good event. On the joint good event,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\min\{\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi},\,\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}\}=\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi}.\hskip 18.49988pt\square
``` |  |

If a mechanism factor is not separately calibrated, we set it to $`1`$;
this can only relax $`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}`$, so Eq. (9)
falls back to $`\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}`$ without losing
validity.

### 6. Proposition 2.9: ContextAcquire as a Calibrated Hazard Envelope

Statement. Under the split
$`D_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{mem}}\to D_{\mathrm{cal}}\to D_{\mathrm{test}}`$
and a frozen memory snapshot $`M^{\star}`$, ContextAcquire$`(k)`$
assigns
$`\underline{h}_{t+}\leftarrow\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})`$,
where

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})=\mathrm{LCB}_{\mathrm{Binom}}\!\left(s_{t}^{M^{\star},\pi},\,n_{t}^{M^{\star},\pi},\,\eta_{h}\right)
``` |  |

is a Clopper–Pearson LCB estimated on calibration traces using
$`M^{\star}`$. The increment
$`\Delta h_{t}(k,Z_{t}):=\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})-\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi,\varnothing}`$
is the snapshot-on/off LCB gap, used only as a deployment diagnostic;
the certificate uses $`\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})`$ directly.

Proof. Fix $`\pi`$, $`t`$, and $`M^{\star}`$. The split ensures that
$`M^{\star}`$ is constructed from $`D_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{mem}}`$
and used read-only on $`D_{\mathrm{cal}}`$ and $`D_{\mathrm{test}}`$, so
the evaluation rows do not leak into memory construction. If
$`M^{\star}`$ or any other manifest entry is selected from a finite set
on $`D_{\mathrm{cal}}`$, all candidates are absorbed into the union
bound of Theorem 2.6; the present statement is for a fixed
$`M^{\star}`$.

Let $`n_{t}^{M^{\star},\pi}`$ be the number of active calibration rows
using $`M^{\star}`$ and $`s_{t}^{M^{\star},\pi}`$ the count of clean
successes. The deployment hazard is

|     |                                                                   |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       h_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star}):=\Pr_{\pi}(S_{t}=1\mid A_{t},\,M^{\star}).
       ```                                                                |     |

By Assumption 2.3 and Section 0.5, on $`\{A_{t}=1\}`$ the indicator
$`S_{t}`$ is i.i.d. Bernoulli with parameter $`h_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})`$,
and the Clopper–Pearson LCB satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr\{h_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})\}\geq 1-\eta_{h}.
``` |  |

Union-bounding over steps and any manifest-level memory/controller
choices yields the simultaneous LCB. Assigning
$`\underline{h}_{t+}\leftarrow\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})`$ gives
$`h_{t}^{+}\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M^{\star})`$. The certificate
(Corollary 2.10, Theorem 2.6) uses this LCB directly, not the gap
$`\Delta h_{t}(k,Z_{t})`$. $`\hskip 18.49988pt\square`$

### 7. Corollary 2.10: Stopped Clean-Before-False Certificate

Statement. Fix a frozen controller $`\pi`$. Assume the stopped-process
convention of Section 0.1 and the initial-activity condition
$`\Pr_{\pi}(A_{1})=1`$. On a calibration good event giving simultaneous
active-step bounds $`q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t}^{\pi}`$ and
$`h_{t}^{\pi}\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}`$ for all $`t\leq T`$, a fresh
evaluation trajectory satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})\geq C_{T}(\pi):=\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\bar{q}_{t}^{\pi})-\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi})\right]_{+}.
``` |  |

Proof. The argument uses only the chain rule on stopped events; no
across-step independence is required.

*Step 1 (no-false survival).* The event $`\{F_{t}=1\}`$ implies the
trajectory is active at $`t`$, hence
$`\{F_{t}=1\}\subseteq A_{t}\subseteq\{\tau_{F}>t-1\}`$. For events
$`E\subseteq B\subseteq C`$ with $`\Pr(B)>0`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr(E\mid C)=\frac{\Pr(E)}{\Pr(C)}\leq\frac{\Pr(E)}{\Pr(B)}=\Pr(E\mid B),
``` |  |

so

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}=1\mid\tau_{F}>t-1)\leq\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}=1\mid A_{t})=q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t}^{\pi}.
``` |  |

If $`\Pr(A_{t})=0`$, Section 0.4 sets the conditional to $`0`$ and the
inequality remains valid. Iterating the chain rule on
$`\{\tau_{F}>t\}=\{\tau_{F}>t-1\}\cap\{F_{t}=0\}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(\tau_{F}>T)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\Pr_{\pi}(F_{t}=0\mid\tau_{F}>t-1)\geq\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\bar{q}_{t}^{\pi}).
``` |  |

*Step 2 (still-active upper bound).* Since
$`A_{t+1}=A_{t}\cap\{S_{t}=0,\,F_{t}=0\}\subseteq A_{t}\cap\{S_{t}=0\}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(A_{t+1}\mid A_{t})\leq\Pr_{\pi}(S_{t}=0\mid A_{t})=1-h_{t}^{\pi}\leq 1-\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}.
``` |  |

Iterating, and using $`\Pr_{\pi}(A_{1})=1`$ from the Section 0.1
initial-activity assumption together with the inclusion
$`A_{T+1}\subseteq A_{T}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{1}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(A_{T+1})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\Pr_{\pi}(A_{t+1}\mid A_{t})\leq\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}).
``` |  |

*Step 3 (decomposition).* We claim the disjoint identity

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\{\tau_{F}>T\}=\{\tau_{S}\leq T,\;\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}\;\dot{\cup}\;A_{T+1}.
``` |  |

To verify it, fix a trajectory in $`\{\tau_{F}>T\}`$ and split on
whether $`\tau_{S}\leq T`$ or $`\tau_{S}>T`$:

(i) If $`\tau_{S}\leq T`$ then $`\tau_{S}\leq T<\tau_{F}`$, so the
trajectory lies in $`\{\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}`$.

(ii) If $`\tau_{S}>T`$ then $`\tau_{S}>T`$ and $`\tau_{F}>T`$, hence the
trajectory lies in $`A_{T+1}=\{\tau_{S}>T,\,\tau_{F}>T\}`$.

For the reverse containment, note that the two right-hand events are
disjoint: in $`A_{T+1}`$ we have $`\tau_{S}>T`$, contradicting
$`\tau_{S}\leq T`$. To see that every trajectory in
$`\{\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}`$ also lies in
$`\{\tau_{F}>T\}`$, we invoke the stopped-process convention of Section
0.1: on $`\{\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}\}`$, $`\tau_{S}`$ is the first stop, so
$`F_{t}=0`$ at every active step $`t<\tau_{S}`$ (by minimality of
$`\tau_{S}`$) and $`F_{t}=0`$ at every step $`t>\tau_{S}`$ (by the
stopped-process zeroing); the within-step exclusivity of
$`F_{\tau_{S}}`$ and $`S_{\tau_{S}}`$ via $`B_{t}^{\epsilon}`$ (Section
0.1) gives $`F_{\tau_{S}}=0`$. Hence
$`\tau_{F}=\inf\{t:F_{t}=1\}=+\infty>T`$, and the trajectory is in
$`\{\tau_{F}>T\}`$. Without this convention the identity fails (e.g.,
$`\tau_{S}=2,\,\tau_{F}=3,\,T=5`$ would satisfy
$`\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F}`$ but $`\tau_{F}\leq T`$).

Therefore

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})=\Pr_{\pi}(\tau_{F}>T)-\Pr_{\pi}(A_{T+1}).
``` |  |

Combining (14) and (15) and taking the positive part,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi}(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})\geq\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\bar{q}_{t}^{\pi})-\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi})\right]_{+}.\hskip 18.49988pt\square
``` |  |

*Specialization to $`T=1`$.*
$`C_{1}(\pi)=[\underline{h}_{1}^{\pi}-\bar{q}_{1}^{\pi}]_{+}`$,
recovering the Pass@1 form.

### 8. Theorem 2.6: Simultaneously Valid Finite-Manifest Calibration

Statement. Let $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$ be a finite, pre-declared, non-empty
class of frozen controllers with $`1\leq|\Pi_{\alpha}|<\infty`$,
declared before $`D_{\mathrm{cal}}`$ is opened. Each
$`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$ specifies the stop/refine rule, risk-probe
invocation policy, evidence intensity $`m`$, context intensity $`k`$,
prompts, routing and deduplication rules, decoding parameters,
retrievable memory snapshot $`M^{\star}`$, write mode
$`\omega_{\mathrm{write}}`$, and (when a threshold gate is used) a
finite non-empty grid $`\Theta_{t}`$ with $`1\leq|\Theta_{t}|<\infty`$
per step. With probability at least $`1-\delta`$ over calibration, the
UCB/LCB quantities entering

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C_{T}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi):=\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi})-\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1-\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M))\right]_{+}
``` |  |

are simultaneously valid for every $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$, so the
data-dependent selection

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi^{\star}\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}}C_{T}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi)
``` |  |

inherits a post-selection-valid lower bound
$`C_{T}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi^{\star})`$ on
$`\Pr_{\pi^{\star}}(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})`$.

#### 8.1 Confidence budget

Choose non-negative
$`\delta_{\mathrm{raw}},\delta_{\mathrm{gate}},\delta_{\mathrm{probe}},\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}`$
with

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}+\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}+\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}+\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}\leq\delta.
``` |  |

For each $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$, allocate per-row Clopper–Pearson
confidences as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}}{2T|\Pi_{\alpha}|},\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{T|\Theta_{t}||\Pi_{\alpha}|},\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{T|\Pi_{\alpha}|},\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}}{T|\Pi_{\alpha}|\cdot J_{\max}}
``` |  |

for the raw, gate, probe, and evidence bounds, respectively, with
$`J_{\max}=\max_{t}J_{t}`$ (which equals $`1`$ in the single-channel
implementation). Substituting $`\delta=0.10`$,
$`\delta_{\bullet}=0.025`$, $`|\Pi_{\alpha}|=5`$, $`T=5`$,
$`|\Theta_{t}|=1`$, $`J_{\max}=1`$ gives the per-row raw confidence
$`0.025/(2\cdot 5\cdot 5)=5\times 10^{-4}`$ used in the main text.

#### 8.2 Proof

For each $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$ define the good events

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)`$ | $`\displaystyle:\forall t,\;q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}\text{ and }h_{t}^{\pi}(M)\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M),`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi)`$ | $`\displaystyle:\forall t,\,\forall\theta\in\Theta_{t},\;p_{t,\theta}^{\pi}\leq\bar{R}_{t}^{\pi}(\theta),`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi)`$ | $`\displaystyle:\forall t,\;\Pr_{\pi}(B_{t}^{\epsilon},\,W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=1\mid A_{t})\leq\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)`$ | $`\displaystyle:\forall t,\,\forall j\leq J_{t},\;\Pr_{\pi}(E_{t,j}=1\mid\cdots)\leq b_{t,j}^{\pi}.`$ |  |

The per-row allocations of Section 8.1 give

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\Pr(G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)^{c})`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}}{|\Pi_{\alpha}|},`$ | $`\displaystyle\Pr(G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi)^{c})`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}}{|\Pi_{\alpha}|},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\Pr(G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi)^{c})`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}}{|\Pi_{\alpha}|},`$ | $`\displaystyle\Pr(G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)^{c})`$ | $`\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}}{|\Pi_{\alpha}|}.`$ |  |

Let

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
G:=\bigcap_{\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}}\!\bigl(G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)\cap G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi)\cap G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi)\cap G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)\bigr).
``` |  |

A union bound over $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$, steps, threshold grids, mechanism
factors, and channels gives

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr(G)\geq 1-(\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}+\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}+\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}+\delta_{\mathrm{evid}})\geq 1-\delta.
``` |  |

On $`G`$, for any $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$ and any $`t\leq T`$:

1.  (1)
    $`q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi}`$ from
    $`G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)`$.
2.  (2)
    $`q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe},\pi}\exp[-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}]=\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}`$
    from Section 5 using
    $`G_{\mathrm{probe}}(\pi)\cap G_{\mathrm{evid}}(\pi)`$.
3.  (3)
    Therefore
    $`q_{t}^{\pi}\leq\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\pi}:=\min\{\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{raw}}^{\pi},\,\bar{q}_{t,\mathrm{mech}}^{\pi}\}`$.
4.  (4)
    $`h_{t}^{\pi}(M)\geq\underline{h}_{t}^{\pi}(M)`$ from
    $`G_{\mathrm{raw}}(\pi)`$.

Corollary 2.10 then gives
$`\Pr_{\pi}(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})\geq C_{T}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi)`$
for every $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$. Since $`G`$ holds simultaneously for
all $`\pi\in\Pi_{\alpha}`$, any data-dependent $`\pi^{\star}`$ in this
finite class also satisfies

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\Pr_{\pi^{\star}}(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})\geq C_{T}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\pi^{\star}).
``` |  |

If $`\pi^{\star}`$’s manifest includes a score-threshold admission rule,
$`G_{\mathrm{gate}}(\pi^{\star})`$ provides the Theorem 2.5 guarantee at
the corresponding threshold. $`\hskip 18.49988pt\square`$

### 9. Monotonicity of the Certificate

In the unclipped regime, define

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C_{T}=\prod_{s=1}^{T}(1-\bar{q}_{s})-\prod_{s=1}^{T}(1-\underline{h}_{s}),\hskip 18.49988pt\bar{q}_{t}=\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}\exp(-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}).
``` |  |

We have

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial C_{T}}{\partial\bar{q}_{t}}=-\prod_{s\neq t}(1-\bar{q}_{s})\leq 0,
``` |  |

so any reduction in $`\bar{q}_{t}`$ does not decrease $`C_{T}`$. Since

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial\bar{q}_{t}}{\partial\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}}=\exp(-\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}})\geq 0,\hskip 18.49988pt\frac{\partial\bar{q}_{t}}{\partial\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}}=-\bar{q}_{t}\leq 0,
``` |  |

it follows that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{\partial C_{T}}{\partial\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}}\leq 0,\hskip 18.49988pt\frac{\partial C_{T}}{\partial\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}}\geq 0,\hskip 18.49988pt\frac{\partial C_{T}}{\partial\underline{h}_{t}}=\prod_{s\neq t}(1-\underline{h}_{s})\geq 0.
``` |  |

RiskProbe contracts $`\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}`$, EvidenceAcquire
increases $`\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}`$, and ContextAcquire raises
$`\underline{h}_{t}`$, each weakly increasing the certificate. The same
conclusions hold in the clipped regime by subgradient monotonicity of
$`[\cdot]_{+}`$.

### 10. Summary of Structural Conditions

1.  (1)
    Across-trajectory independence at fixed $`t`$ is required;
    within-trajectory step independence is not (Section 0.3).
2.  (2)
    The suite-level Bernoulli treatment does not require independence
    across the $`m`$ generated tests (Section 4.1); test-level
    independence is needed only for sequential channel accumulation
    (Section 4.2).
3.  (3)
    The Hypothesis Prober contributes only to
    $`\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}`$; Solution Validator and Test
    Augmentation contribute only to $`\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}`$
    (Sections 3, 4, 5).
4.  (4)
    Finite grids and finite manifests give post-selection validity
    (Theorems 2.5, 2.6).
5.  (5)
    Empty cells use the conservative conventions
    $`\mathrm{UCB}(0,0,\eta)=1`$ and $`\mathrm{LCB}(0,0,\eta)=0`$
    (Section 0.5).
6.  (6)
    Memory must be frozen, or all candidate snapshots, routes, and
    retrieval budgets must be absorbed into $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$ (Section
    6).
7.  (7)
    Corollary 2.10 lower-bounds
    $`\Pr(\tau_{S}\leq T,\,\tau_{S}<\tau_{F})`$, which is strictly
    stricter than aggregate Refine@$`T`$ or Pass@1.
8.  (8)
    $`F_{t}`$ and $`S_{t}`$ are mutually exclusive by construction;
    $`\tau_{S}=\tau_{F}`$ does not occur (Section 0.1).
9.  (9)
    The mechanism containment requires
    $`\Gamma_{t}=1\Rightarrow W_{t}^{\mathrm{probe}}=W_{t}^{\mathrm{evid}}=1`$
    on $`A_{t}`$; without it, only the raw UCB applies (Section 0.2).
10. (10)
    The score map $`\phi_{\pi}`$, threshold grid $`\Theta_{t}`$,
    aggregation rule $`\gamma_{\mathrm{evid}}`$, memory snapshot
    $`M^{\star}`$, and any calibrated scalar in $`r_{t}`$ must be
    pre-declared or absorbed into $`\Pi_{\alpha}`$ before label counting
    (Section 0.6).
11. (11)
    Evidence accumulates over channels acting on the same admission
    decision, not over refinement steps. The CP-Agent implementation
    uses a single channel per step, so
    $`\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\mathrm{evid},\pi}=\Delta I_{t}(m)`$ (Section
    4.2).
12. (12)
    The confidence budget satisfies
    $`\delta_{\mathrm{raw}}+\delta_{\mathrm{gate}}+\delta_{\mathrm{probe}}+\delta_{\mathrm{evid}}\leq\delta`$
    (Section 8.1).

### D.2. Dual-Granularity Verification Robustness

This analysis targets the main failure mode exposed by the TA
diagnostic: verifier-level false positives. A false positive occurs when
a correct candidate fails generated augmented tests because the
generated expected outputs or test conditions are noisy.

##### Counterfactual study.

We examine the $`19`$ verifier-level false-positive cases from the TA
label-accuracy diagnostic. The comparison starts from the same
intermediate candidate/state and runs the full CP-Agent refinement loop
with TA versus without TA.

|                                         |       |
|-----------------------------------------|-------|
| Counterfactual outcome on 19 FP cases   | Count |
| Successful with or without TA           | 14    |
| Failed with or without TA               | 5     |
| Succeeded without TA but failed with TA | 0     |

Table 9. End-to-end counterfactual analysis on the $`19`$ verifier-level
TA false-positive cases.

In these cases, verifier-level TA false positives do not produce
observed end-to-end harm: no case succeeds without TA but fails with TA.
Among the $`14`$ cases that remain successful in both conditions, TA
introduces $`5.64`$ additional steps on average to recover.

##### Why recovery is possible.

Dual-Granularity Verification (DGV) gives CP-Agent two independent
executable feedback channels:

- •
  Solution Validator (SV) and TA surface program-level failure signals.
- •
  Hypothesis Prober (HP) checks local claims, brute-force comparisons,
  edge cases, and alternative failure hypotheses.

When these channels disagree, CP-Agent arbitrates the evidence rather
than blindly trusting a single noisy verifier.

##### Case study: Codeforces 2098B.

![Refer to caption](2605.24693v1/figs/extra/case_study_2098B.png)

Figure 4. Problem 2098B (“Sasha and the Apartment Purchase”) case study:
a noisy TA alarm triggers extra HP validation but does not cause the
agent to discard the correct formula-based solution.

In Problem 2098B, “Sasha and the Apartment Purchase”, a correct
formula-based solution passes the public/basic case but fails several
generated TA cases. The agent treats the signal as a possible bug and
performs multiple independent HP checks:

- •
  Step 18: first brute-force check on small instances.
- •
  Step 19: second brute-force round with duplicates, edge cases, and
  re-derivation.
- •
  Steps 20–21: stress testing and extended counterexample search.
- •
  Step 22: additional hypotheses such as ceil-vs-floor behavior and
  arithmetic issues.
- •
  Step 29: no genuine counterexample is found, so the agent retains the
  formula-based solution.
- •
  Step 30: the final submission is accepted.

This illustrates the practical role of DGV: a noisy TA alarm can trigger
extra validation without causing the agent to discard a correct
solution.

### D.4. Efficiency and BCC Derivation

We analyze cost–performance trade-offs with Data Envelopment Analysis
(DEA) ([Charnes et al., 1978](#bib.bib7)), using the
variable-returns-to-scale Banker–Charnes–Cooper (BCC) model ([Banker et
al., 1984](#bib.bib5)). The input is average API cost per problem, and
the output is Pass@1. A method is BCC-efficient when no observed method,
nor any convex combination of observed methods, can achieve at least the
same output with no more input. The multi-backbone Pass@1-versus-cost
view supporting this analysis is shown in the main-text
Figure [1](#acmlabel1 "Figure 1 ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming").

##### Input-oriented BCC program.

For each decision-making unit (DMU) $`k`$ with $`(x_{k},y_{k})`$, the
input-oriented BCC efficiency is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\min_{\theta,\{\lambda_{j}\}}\quad`$ | $`\displaystyle\theta`$ |  |
|  | s.t. | $`\displaystyle\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}x_{j}\leq\theta\,x_{k},`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}y_{j}\geq y_{k},`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}=1,`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\ \forall j.`$ |  |

The optimum $`\theta_{k}^{\star}\in(0,1]`$. $`\theta_{k}^{\star}=1`$
means the method lies on the BCC frontier; $`\theta_{k}^{\star}<1`$
means its input could be proportionally reduced by
$`(1-\theta_{k}^{\star})`$ while preserving at least the same output.

Let $`(x_{j},y_{j})`$ denote $`(\text{Cost},\text{Pass@1})`$.

##### Closed-form one-input one-output computation.

With one input, one output, and the BCC convexity constraint, any
feasible reference point is a convex combination of observed DMUs. For a
target output $`y`$ between two frontier outputs
$`y_{1}\leq y\leq y_{2}`$, the minimal attainable input on the frontier
is obtained by linear interpolation:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\lambda(y)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{y-y_{1}}{y_{2}-y_{1}},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle x^{\star}(y)`$ | $`\displaystyle=(1-\lambda(y))\,x_{1}+\lambda(y)\,x_{2},`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\theta_{k}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{x^{\star}(y_{k})}{x_{k}}.`$ |  |

### D.6. Tool Use vs. Problem Difficulty

Figure [3(a)](#acmlabel3 "Figure 3(a) ‣ Figure 3 ‣ Test Augmentation. ‣ 4.6. Tool-Use Analysis ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
analyzes verification-tool usage along the *interaction-step* axis on a
single backbone. This subsection complements that view with the
orthogonal *difficulty* axis: how much tool-mediated effort CP-Agent
spends per problem as problems become harder. The diagnostic uses the
DeepSeek-V3.2-Chat backbone on LCB-Pro and counts the average number of
HP/SV/TA invocations per problem, broken down by the benchmark’s
Easy/Medium/Hard difficulty bands.

Figure 5. Average tool-use frequency (calls per problem) on LCB-Pro by
difficulty band (Easy/Medium/Hard), broken down by tool channel (HP, SV,
TA). DeepSeek-V3.2-Chat backbone. See body for the resulting per-band
totals and their interpretation.

##### Adaptive scaling on Easy/Medium.

Compared with a fixed-pipeline baseline (each method calls every tool a
constant number of times), CP-Agent issues $`5.5`$ tool calls per
problem on Easy and $`5.1`$ on Medium. The slight drop from Easy to
Medium reflects two competing effects under the certificate machinery of
Section [2](#S2 "2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"):
(i) Medium problems trigger longer chain-of-thought before an executable
candidate is produced, so HP and SV start firing later in the
trajectory; (ii) once an executable candidate is produced, the admission
gate $`\Gamma_{t}`$ continues to require evidence, so tool calls remain
non-trivial. Empirically the two effects roughly cancel, and CP-Agent
still relies heavily on external feedback on Easy and Medium problems.

##### Why Hard drops to $`3.3`$.

On Hard problems, average tool calls drop to $`3.3`$. This is consistent
with two structural facts already documented in the main paper:

- •
  *Few executable candidates are reached.* Hard problems often consume
  the budget on high-level algorithmic discovery before any candidate is
  produced. SV is only meaningful on executable candidates, and TA is
  only invoked when SV first returns pass on public tests; both channels
  stay neutral on trajectories that never reach this state. This is the
  same structural barrier that produces the unchanged Hard column in
  Table [10](#A4.T10 "Table 10 ‣ D.3. Frontier-Backbone Transfer ‣ Appendix D Additional Empirical Analyses ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"):
  refinement-loop tools cannot substitute for missing high-level ideas.
- •
  *Public-test-only filter.* The augmented suite
  $`\mathcal{T}_{\rm aug}`$ is generated at most once per problem
  (Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix A Tool-Orchestrated Pseudocode ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")),
  conditional on the candidate first passing public tests. On Hard
  problems this trigger fires rarely, suppressing the TA contribution to
  the per-problem call count.

In other words, the drop on Hard is not a controller-level decision to
invoke fewer tools, but a downstream consequence of the candidate stream
itself becoming sparse: the gates
$`G^{\mathrm{HP}}_{i,t},G^{\mathrm{SV}}_{i,t},G^{\mathrm{TA}}_{i,t}`$
from
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ Appendix A Tool-Orchestrated Pseudocode ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
record neutral on inactive channels
(Definition [2.1](#S2.Thmtheorem1 "Definition 2.1 (Controlled stopped process and certificate state). ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
Combined with
Fig. [3(a)](#acmlabel3 "Figure 3(a) ‣ Figure 3 ‣ Test Augmentation. ‣ 4.6. Tool-Use Analysis ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"),
which shows HP concentrated in early interaction steps, this gives a
coherent picture: CP-Agent spends tool budget where evidence is
actionable, and remains efficient elsewhere by leaving inactive channels
neutral rather than firing them spuriously.

### D.7. Experience-Driven Efficiency

Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3. Experience-Driven Self-Evolving ‣ 3. Method ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
attributes the Experience module to $`\textsf{ContextAcquire}(k)`$,
whose effect in
Proposition [2.9](#S2.Thmtheorem9 "Proposition 2.9 (ContextAcquire as calibrated hazard envelope). ‣ Numerical instantiation. ‣ 2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
is to lift the active-state success hazard $`\underline{h}_{t}(M)`$.
Beyond the per-step acceptance lift reported in
Table [5](#S4.T5 "Table 5 ‣ 4.4. Trace-Calibrated Certificate and Mechanism Evidence ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"),
a higher $`\underline{h}_{t}`$ should also let CP-Agent commit earlier
in the refinement loop, with downstream effects on both the average
number of refinement steps and the per-problem cost. We isolate this
efficiency channel by an internal ablation of $`\pi^{\star}`$ that
toggles only the frozen Experience snapshot $`M^{\star}`$ on the LCB-Pro
target split (DeepSeek-V3.2-Chat backbone, manifest of
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).

Figure 6. Average refinement steps and average per-problem API cost on
LCB-Pro with and without the frozen Experience snapshot $`M^{\star}`$
(DeepSeek-V3.2-Chat backbone, $`\pi^{\star}`$ from
Table [3](#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ 4.2. Calibration Protocol and Auditability ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
Both axes are averaged over all $`167`$ LCB-Pro problems.

##### Both axes move in the right direction.

With Experience, the average refinement step count drops and the average
per-problem cost drops jointly
(Fig. [6](#A4.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ D.7. Experience-Driven Efficiency ‣ Appendix D Additional Empirical Analyses ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")).
The same direction shows up in the component ablation of
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.4. Trace-Calibrated Certificate and Mechanism Evidence ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"):
adding Experience on top of $`+\textsf{SV}+\textsf{HP}+\textsf{TA}`$
moves CP-Agent from \$$`0.043`$ to \$$`0.035`$ per problem and improves
the only-solved $`\#T`$ on ICPC-Eval from $`2.2`$ to $`1.8`$, while
raising LCB-Pro Pass@1 from $`46.1`$ to $`48.5`$. Two measurement
axes—LCB-Pro all-problem average in
Fig. [6](#A4.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ D.7. Experience-Driven Efficiency ‣ Appendix D Additional Empirical Analyses ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
and ICPC-Eval only-solved $`\#T`$ in
Table [4](#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ 4.4. Trace-Calibrated Certificate and Mechanism Evidence ‣ 4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")—thus
agree on the sign of the effect, while differing in absolute magnitude
due to the averaging convention.

##### Mechanism interpretation.

The cost reduction is not driven by Experience being a cheaper module:
Table [13](#A4.T13 "Table 13 ‣ D.5. Per-Component Token and Cost Breakdown ‣ Appendix D Additional Empirical Analyses ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
shows Experience itself consumes only $`\sim\!3.4\%`$ of the total
tracked API cost. Instead, the savings act through the certificate
state. A higher $`\underline{h}_{t}(M)`$ lets the admission gate
$`\Gamma_{t}`$ close earlier on the trajectories that would otherwise
have continued, which in turn truncates the HP/SV invocations downstream
of those refinement steps. Since HP and SV together account for
$`87.3\%`$ of tracked cost
(Table [13](#A4.T13 "Table 13 ‣ D.5. Per-Component Token and Cost Breakdown ‣ Appendix D Additional Empirical Analyses ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")),
the saved HP/SV calls dominate the additional retrieval cost incurred by
Experience. In the language of
Section [2](#S2 "2. A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Theory of Feedback Control ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming"),
ContextAcquire converts hazard lift into refinement-step savings, and
the savings on the expensive verification channels outweigh the
retrieval overhead—giving the qualitative claim in
Section [4](#S4 "4. Experiment ‣ CP-Agent: A Calibrated Risk-Controlled Agent for Feedback-Driven Competitive Programming")
that “retrieved repair experiences reduce ineffective trial-and-error” a
concrete per-problem efficiency signature.

## Appendix E Datasets

##### ICPC-Eval ([Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib2))

is a competitive-programming benchmark designed to reflect real contest
settings. It is built from problems drawn from $`11`$ recent ICPC
contests (collected via QOJ and VJudge): starting from $`139`$ problems,
the dataset is cleaned and finalized to $`118`$ problems. It filters out
problems that rely on crucial non-textual figures, interactive problems,
or problems without an official reference solution. For tasks requiring
special judging, ICPC-Eval retains them and additionally implements
$`12`$ custom special judges (e.g., for floating-point outputs or
multiple valid answers). All problem statements are standardized into
LaTeX for consistent parsing and model input. The benchmark matches
ICPC-style problem diversity and difficulty, and provides annotations
over eight major algorithmic areas (e.g., DP, mathematics, graph
algorithms, and data structures), with many problems spanning multiple
areas to capture the compositional and challenging nature of ICPC
problems.

##### LiveCodeBench Pro ([Zheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib3))

is a challenging benchmark for evaluating large language models on
competitive programming. Its central goal is to mitigate overestimation
caused by training-data contamination by maintaining a continuously
updated problem set. The benchmark curates problems from top-tier
contests such as Codeforces, ICPC, and IOI. Problems are collected as
soon as possible after they appear in real contests, further reducing
the risk of data leakage.

## Appendix F Metrics

##### Refine@K ([Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib2))

is an execution-feedback-based evaluation metric designed to measure an
LLM’s ability to iteratively improve a solution under a bounded turn
budget. In ICPC-Eval, each problem is solved through up to $`K`$
response–refinement chances, and the metric asks whether the model can
“pass the test within response and refinement chances” (i.e., succeed
within $`K`$ turns). Concretely, the evaluation is multi-turn: in the
first turn, the model receives the full problem statement; in subsequent
turns, the model is additionally provided with its previous response and
the corresponding evaluation feedback. Thus, a model is counted as
successful under Refine@K if any of its first $`K`$ attempts
(conditioned on prior code + feedback) produces a correct program.
Compared with Pass@K (independent resampling), Refine@K better captures
the behavior of reasoning/reflection-capable models because it
explicitly evaluates whether a model can use feedback to correct
mistakes rather than merely getting lucky with another sample.

##### \# Turn ([Xu et al., 2026](#bib.bib2))

represents the average number of correct response turns, computed only
over problems that are successfully solved within the refinement budget.
Specifically, for each solved problem, we record the turn index at which
the model first produces a correct solution, and then average this index
across the solved subset. A smaller \#T indicates the model typically
fixes its solution within fewer turns, suggesting more efficient use of
execution feedback, whereas a larger \#T implies the model often needs
more trial-and-error refinement rounds before arriving at a correct
program.

##### Pass@1

measures the single-try success rate of code generation: for each
problem, the model produces exactly one candidate solution (one
response/submission). If this first solution passes the evaluation
(e.g., all test cases), the problem is counted as solved. Pass@1 is the
fraction of problems solved on the first attempt:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{Pass@1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}\big[`$ | the model’s first solution for |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\text{problem }i\text{ passes}\big].`$ |  |

Intuitively, Pass@1 reflects the model’s *initial answer quality*
without any retries or feedback-driven refinement, in contrast to
iterative metrics such as Refine@K.

## Appendix I Extended Discussion on Related Work

##### LLM Agents.

Large Language Models have been increasingly deployed as autonomous
agents capable of interacting with external environments and solving
multi-step tasks. In the domain of web navigation, researchers have
developed agents to autonomously browse and interact with online
interfaces ([Ning et al., 2025](#bib.bib41); [He et al.,
2024](#bib.bib42); [Lai et al., 2024](#bib.bib43); [Zhang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib44)). In the field of software engineering, agents such as
SWE-Agent have been proposed to handle repository-level maintenance and
development tasks ([Luo et al., 2024](#bib.bib45); [Gao et al.,
2025](#bib.bib46)). Furthermore, the application of agents has extended
to high-level intellectual domains, including automated scientific
discovery ([Toledo et al., 2026](#bib.bib47); [Baek et al.,
2025](#bib.bib48); [Lu et al., 2024](#bib.bib49)) and data science
analysis, where agents assist in data visualization and
interpretation ([Zhu et al., 2025](#bib.bib50); [Yang et al.,
2024](#bib.bib51); [Jiang et al., 2024](#bib.bib52)). These advancements
collectively demonstrate the potential of LLM agents to generalize
across diverse and complex workflows.

In contrast to the extensive body of work on autonomous agents
interacting with dynamic external environments, a more focused stream of
research investigates Agentic Reasoning—the use of deterministic tools
to enhance the model’s internal cognitive processes. Unlike autonomous
agents that use tools as “effectors” to change the environment state,
this paradigm employs tools—primarily code interpreters (e.g., Python)
and calculators—as “cognitive prosthetics” to ground the reasoning
process itself. Early works such as PAL ([Gao et al., 2023](#bib.bib13))
and PoT ([Chen et al., 2022b](#bib.bib9)) demonstrated that decoupling
reasoning from computation significantly reduces hallucinations in
mathematical and symbolic tasks. Advancing this trajectory, recent
frameworks ([Wu et al., 2025](#bib.bib35); [Gou et al.,
2024](#bib.bib15); [Das et al., 2024](#bib.bib1); [Shang et al.,
2025](#bib.bib40)) establish a more sophisticated interleaved
reasoning–action loop. By integrating code execution directly into the
chain of thought, these methods utilize deterministic feedback for
iterative step verification and error correction, enabling the model to
navigate complex logical paths with higher reliability. Although fewer
in number compared to general agent papers, these studies highlight a
critical distinction: for challenging reasoning tasks, the value of a
tool lies not in its complexity or connectivity, but in its ability to
provide deterministic, verifiable feedback that constrains the model’s
probabilistic output.

##### Competitive Programming.

Program synthesis has evolved significantly from early deductive ([Manna
and Waldinger, 1980](#bib.bib53)) and inductive ([Spector and Robinson,
2002](#bib.bib54)) synthesis to neural approaches like DeepCoder ([Balog
et al., 2016](#bib.bib55)). However, the release of challenging
benchmarks ([Hendrycks et al., 2021](#bib.bib18); [Jain et al.,
2025](#bib.bib23); [Zheng et al., 2026](#bib.bib3); [Xu et al.,
2026](#bib.bib2); [Sun et al., 2026](#bib.bib57)) revealed a harsh
reality: early models failed near-completely on competition-level
problems. AlphaCode ([Li et al., 2022](#bib.bib25)) marked a major
milestone in this domain by leveraging massive sampling for competitive
programming problems, while AlphaCode2 ([Leblond et al.,
2023](#bib.bib39)) and OpenAI’s o1-ioi ([El-Kishky et al.,
2025](#bib.bib12)) later adopted reinforcement learning and heuristic
inference strategies to achieve gold-medal performance. Most recently,
OpenAI o3 ([OpenAI, 2025a](#bib.bib56)) demonstrated that
general-purpose reasoning models could reach Grandmaster status on
Codeforces without domain-specific hand-crafting.

Since training such reasoning models is computationally prohibitive,
recent research has pivoted to inference-time enhancements using
hand-crafting LLM workflows like MapCoder ([Islam et al.,
2024](#bib.bib21)), AlphaCodium ([Ridnik et al., 2024](#bib.bib29)), and
CODESIM ([Islam et al., 2025](#bib.bib22)). These methods simulate human
problem-solving through iterative “generate-and-verify” cycles. However,
they mainly rely on fixed, stateless workflows, which suffer from
rigidity: they lack the adaptivity to scale reasoning effort within a
problem, and crucially, they fail to transfer experience across
problems, discarding valuable debugging insights after each session.
This limitation highlights the need for more dynamic and long-term
evolving mechanisms.
